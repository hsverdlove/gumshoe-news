## US News | sting=us
[ava] Here at home, an update on the new White House press secretary. The Wall Street Journal reports that Katie Zacharia took the California bar exam after law school in twenty eleven and did not pass, and California's State Bar has no record of her being licensed. She has described herself as a lawyer. According to The Hill, Zacharia says she misheard a reporter's question about where she took the exam. A watchdog group has asked the California bar to look into her claims.
[ava] On the campaign trail, NBC News reports a handful of Republicans in close races are breaking with President Trump on tariffs, including Senator Susan Collins in Maine and Mike Rogers in Michigan's Senate race, as the cost of living tops voters' worries. Democrats say the pushback is too little, too late. An NBC polling average shows voters almost evenly split on which party would handle the economy better.
[ava] Also new: preliminary findings from police in Cobb County, Georgia, say the man who died on Six Flags' Goliath coaster this week was ejected from the ride. Six Flags has temporarily closed nine similar rides at its North American parks.
[ava] And a small consumer note: the F.D.A. has approved a new sunscreen ingredient, the first new sunscreen formula available in the U.S. in about twenty-five years, letting American products catch up with what's long been sold in Europe.

## International | sting=world
[ava] Around the world, a heartbreaking story from Pakistan. The roof of a private school in Peshawar collapsed during classes this morning, crushing a classroom below. The main hospital there says at least thirty-three people died, thirty-two of them students between seven and fourteen, along with a young teacher; some local outlets put the toll higher. Police have detained the school's owner and say the building was poorly built. A formal investigation is underway.
[ava] On Ukraine, the war of words between Kyiv and Elon Musk escalated overnight. After President Zelensky warned that people will die because of SpaceX's new limits on Starlink, Musk posted that it was, quote, time for Zelensky to go. Ukraine says the restrictions, along with reduced U.S. intelligence sharing, are meant to pressure it into concessions. SpaceX hasn't publicly explained the limits, and President Trump has blamed Ukraine's refinery strikes for pushing up energy prices.
[ava] And on Iran: President Trump says the war will end, quote, very soon, and that the U.S. is in total control of the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's parliament speaker says his country must be ready for war even while it negotiates, accusing Washington of breaking promises.

## Technology | sting=tech
[andrew] In tech, a big night for Tesla owners. Tesla unveils its next-generation Roadster tonight at eight fifty-five Eastern, at SpaceX's test site in McGregor, Texas, streaming on Tesla's site and its X account. Reuters notes this comes nearly nine years after Tesla first showed the car in twenty seventeen, when it priced it at two hundred thousand dollars and promised deliveries in twenty twenty. Musk has promised six hundred twenty miles of range. Fans are excited; skeptics point to the years of delays and say to wait for real specs and a delivery date.
[andrew] Apple has a new home gadget, HomeView, a two-hundred-ninety-nine-dollar countertop screen that mixes pieces of the iPad, Apple Watch, and CarPlay, aimed at Amazon's Echo Show and Google's Nest Hub. One catch: you need an iPhone or iPad to set it up.
[andrew] And Semafor reports a bipartisan group of House lawmakers is questioning whether Meta should be claiming a research tax credit for its data center construction. We haven't seen Meta's response yet.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I., federal regulators are digging in. The Federal Trade Commission confirmed it has sent subpoena-style demands to OpenAI and Anthropic, first reported by Axios. It wants documents on safety incidents, privacy policies, and safeguards, and the probe follows several incidents where A.I. agents went off-script online. Neither company had commented publicly in the reports we saw. The context: an A.P.-NORC poll finds sixty-four percent of Americans think A.I. is developing too fast. The administration, though, has resisted calls to slow it down, arguing A.I. is vital to America's national interests.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] On the cyber desk, another firewall alert. Palo Alto Networks says attackers are exploiting a newly disclosed flaw in its PAN-OS firewall software that lets someone who already has network access run code with full root control. There's no workaround yet, and some fixes are still rolling out today. CISA has ordered federal agencies to patch and check for compromise by Saturday, October seventeenth. The advice: lock down the management interface so it isn't reachable from the internet, and review admin logins.
[andrew] And X says it suspended more than thirteen thousand accounts tied to three suspected foreign influence operations linked to Iran, Russia, and China, which it says targeted U.S. political conversation ahead of the midterms. X hasn't published a country-by-country breakdown or its evidence, so outside researchers can't yet check that attribution.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, some good news from London. London Zoo says it has bred Afia Birago's puddle frog in captivity for the first time ever. Fifteen tiny froglets have hatched. The species was only described by scientists in twenty eighteen, and in the wild it clings to just two pools in a forest reserve in Ghana threatened by mining. The zoo brought twenty adults over last year and is sharing what it learned with partners in Ghana, in hopes of one day returning frogs to the wild.
