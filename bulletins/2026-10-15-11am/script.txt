## US News | sting=us
[ava] Here at home, President Trump has called off the Pentagon's plan to livestream the execution of Nidal Hasan, the former Army psychiatrist who killed thirteen people in the twenty oh-nine Fort Hood shooting. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had announced the execution, set for December third by firing squad, would be streamed for the public. That drew criticism from both parties, and Vice President JD Vance said he wouldn't watch it.
[ava] This morning, the president said many well-meaning people would rather not watch, though he still believes streaming it could have been a strong deterrent. He says the execution will go ahead as scheduled, and victims' families can watch in person. According to the BBC, it would be the first U.S. military execution in more than sixty years. Supporters of the original plan argued transparency would send a message; critics warned it could turn into propaganda for extremists.

## International | sting=world
[ava] Around the world, shipping near Iran is getting more dangerous. Two cargo ships have sunk near the Strait of Hormuz in the past day or so. The Singapore-flagged container ship G F S Pride caught fire and sank after being hit by unknown projectiles on Tuesday. Singapore says all its crew got off safely. A second ship, the Cape Dao, also went down after being hit back in September; one Indian crew member was killed in that attack.
[ava] Iranian state media says the G F S Pride hit a sea mine on an unauthorized route. Experts quoted by CNN doubt that and say a drone strike is more likely. A Greek maritime monitor warned today that ships face what it called indiscriminate attacks there, even if they switch off their trackers. Data firm Kpler says oil moving through the strait on Monday was less than a quarter of pre-war levels.
[ava] In Ukraine's war, Ukraine's military says it damaged six Russian Su-thirty-four bombers in an overnight missile strike on an airfield in Russia's Voronezh region. Russia hasn't confirmed that. The Kremlin, meanwhile, says Ukraine's latest strikes on Russian refineries go against the spirit of U.S. peace efforts. Ukraine says it never agreed to the energy truce President Trump announced on Sunday, and Russia never publicly committed to it either.

## Technology | sting=tech
[andrew] In tech, Google's Universal Cart is now live in the U.S. It lets you save products you find in Google Search or the Gemini app, from any retailer, in one place. It can alert you when prices drop or when sold-out items come back, and it can suggest promo codes and which credit card perks to use.
[andrew] Nvidia says its new RTX Spark laptop chip is three to five times faster than any Windows chip ever made. The Verge's early take: impressive for thin-and-light laptops, but Nvidia is comparing it to Intel and A.M.D. chips, not Apple's, and those numbers use frame generation. Full reviews are coming.
[andrew] And if your Windows eleven P C has had black screens or File Explorer crashes since the August updates, BleepingComputer says Microsoft has fixed it in updates released October thirteenth or later.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I., a European push. In Stockholm, Spotify founder Daniel Ek sat down with Nvidia C.E.O. Jensen Huang and argued Europe should focus on open-source A.I. models rather than trying to copy the U.S. giants. At the same time, a group of Swedish companies, including Ericsson, AstraZeneca, Saab, and SEB, launched Sferical One, a shared supercomputer with more than eleven hundred Nvidia chips, so they can build their own A.I. at home.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] On the cyber desk, the F.B.I. has raided laptop farms in Texas tied to North Korea's fake I.T. worker schemes. In an operation called Lonestar Roundup, agents searched forty homes and seized about a hundred eighty-seven laptops. These were company laptops that North Koreans, posing as remote U.S. workers, used to get hired at American firms. Two Americans were arrested, and the Justice Department charged fourteen people in total. CrowdStrike, which tracked the group, says the raids will likely have a limited effect on their own, but calls them the start of a bigger crackdown.
[andrew] A reminder for I.T. teams: CISA's deadline is Saturday for federal agencies to patch actively exploited flaws, including two in Ahsay's backup software that can be chained to take over a server.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a simple invention that came out of a loss. In twenty twenty-two, Julie Roth was killed in a forty-seven-car pileup on the Ohio Turnpike during a winter storm. Her two young children survived in their car seats, but in the chaos, no one could tell who they were or what medical needs they had, and it was hours before their father was reached.
[ava] Julie's sister created the Roth I.D. Tag, a sticker that goes on a child's car seat with emergency and medical information written on the back. In a crash, first responders peel it off and it becomes a hospital wristband. Good News Network reports it's been approved by child passenger safety technicians, endorsed by police and fire departments in a number of states, and is sold at major stores like Target and Walmart.
[andrew] Small idea, big difference.
