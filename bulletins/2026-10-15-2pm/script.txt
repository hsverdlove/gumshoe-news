## US News | sting=us
[ava] A local angle on a story we flagged at one. WMUR reports the New Hampshire Attorney General's office confirmed this afternoon that some offices in the state received those suspicious letters federal investigators are chasing nationwide. The A.G. did not name which offices. The F.B.I. still says no harmful substance has been confirmed in the sampled letters, and election work has not been stopped. Officials treat every envelope as potentially hazardous until proven otherwise.
[ava] Separately, a voting-I.D. fight in New Hampshire. A federal appeals court on Wednesday refused the state's bid to overturn a ruling that lets poll workers consider a student I.D. as backup when a voter doesn't have an accepted form of I.D. Student I.D.s still cannot stand alone to get a ballot. Supporters of the law say it protects election integrity. Civil rights groups that sued say the secretary of state's earlier blanket ban went further than the statute allowed. The First Circuit left the district judge's compromise in place for the November election.

## International | sting=world
[ava] Overseas, the Ukraine war heated up again overnight. Ukrainian drones hit an oil refinery in Russia's Ryazan region, about a hundred eighty miles southeast of Moscow, and started a fire. A senior Ukrainian official confirmed the strike to the A.P. President Zelenskyy told the Financial Times Ukraine's pause on refinery attacks is over, saying, quote, we're done. He told Piers Morgan he felt betrayed by President Trump after a brief energy truce, limits on Starlink, and what Kyiv says was a cut in Western intelligence sharing. Trump has said the U.S. has not stopped sharing intelligence. Russia, for its part, says Ukraine never really stopped hitting refineries. Overnight, Zelenskyy said Russia launched more than eight hundred fifty drones at Ukraine.
[ava] In the Iran war, shipping risk is climbing. Reuters reports two commercial ships sank near the Strait of Hormuz after being hit by projectiles. The Singapore-flagged container ship G.F.S. Pride went down with all twenty-one crew safely evacuated. The Antigua and Barbuda-flagged Cape Dao also sank; one crew member was killed earlier when it was struck. Iran says one vessel hit a mine on an unauthorized route. Singapore's port authority called the attacks a breach of international law. About a fifth of the world's oil used to move through that strait before this war.

## Technology | sting=tech
[andrew] In tech, eyes turn to Texas tonight. Tesla says it will finally stream the next-generation Roadster event at seven fifty-five Central, that's eight fifty-five Eastern, from a SpaceX test site. Electrek notes the car was first shown in twenty seventeen with production promised for twenty twenty, and this unveil itself has slipped through a long list of dates. Reservation holders will be listening for a real price and a real production start, not just a demo.

Excitement over the foldable form factor is running into sticker shock.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I., Elon Musk posted this afternoon that SpaceX and Nvidia are co-engineering a computer he calls Star Mind, saying it will reach volume production next year and is, in his words, far better than almost anyone expects. He credited Nvidia's Jensen Huang as an old friend. That's Musk's claim; independent specs and benchmarks are not out yet. It lands in the same debate that has been hitting chip stocks: how much more A.I. hardware the world can absorb, and who pays for the debt to build it.
[ava] Meanwhile, the A.I. safety group METR says it now has employee-level access to Anthropic's frontier models. Watchers on X called that the kind of note you see before a big model drop. Anthropic has not announced a launch tied to that access, so treat the timing talk as speculation for now.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] On cyber, a new one for email admins. Cisco warned that hackers are actively exploiting a critical flaw in its Secure Email Gateway, tracked as C.V.E. twenty twenty-six dash seventy-six five nineteen. An unauthenticated attacker can send a crafted attachment and, under the right settings, write files and run commands as root. CISA added it to the known-exploited list and wants federal agencies patched by October seventeenth. Cisco has published indicators of compromise and says it has already contacted some cloud customers where signs of intrusion showed up. If you run that appliance, this is a patch-now item, separate from the Palo Alto firewall issue we flagged earlier.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, fruit trees where vacant lots used to sit. N.P.R. reports a Kansas City nonprofit called The Giving Grove has helped plant about fourteen thousand fruit and nut trees across nearly eight hundred fifty orchards in seventeen cities, from Seattle to Atlanta.
[ava] Volunteers do almost all the work. The orchards now produce more than two and a half million pounds of free organic fruit and nuts a year. Founder Kevin Birzir says the group shares know-how and seed money so local garden organizations can tend the trees for the long haul. Corporate donations cover most of the budget, and cities keep inviting them in. Unused land becomes a little neighborhood park, and dinner grows on it.
