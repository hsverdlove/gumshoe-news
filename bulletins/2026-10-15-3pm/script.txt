## US News | sting=us
[ava] The Trump administration sued California election officials this afternoon. The Justice Department wants a federal judge to stop the state from counting mail ballots from about twenty thousand people it says are likely noncitizens, until their citizenship is confirmed. The department says it gave the state that list with a forty-eight-hour deadline, and the state didn't act. Attorney General Todd Blanche said state-facilitated voter fraud is unacceptable.
[ava] California sees it very differently. Secretary of State Shirley Weber says the federal government demanded action within two days on data it hasn't verified, without sharing its methods or evidence, and without clear legal authority. Los Angeles County says it got a password-protected thumb drive and didn't open it, citing data-security and privacy rules. Ballots went out October first, so a judge now has to sort this out with the election less than three weeks away.
[ava] Also tonight: debates. Maine Senator Susan Collins and Democrat Troy Jackson meet for their fourth and final debate. Jackson has tried to tie Collins to President Trump, and Collins has stressed her independence.

## International | sting=world
[ava] In the Iran war, more trouble near Hormuz. Iran's navy says an oil tanker caught fire in what it calls a restricted zone just east of the strait, after the ship turned off its tracking system. Iran didn't name the ship or say what caused the fire, and there's no word on the crew. That's Iran's account, and we haven't seen it confirmed independently.
[ava] Washington added financial pressure. The Treasury Department sanctioned an Iraqi bank it says gives Iran access to U.S. dollars, along with two Iranian offshore banks, a Dubai-based Iranian oil middleman, and a network of money changers. [ava] And Europe is being pulled in. France says it has started deploying military assets to help protect Saudi oil sites, especially the Red Sea port of Yanbu, after Houthi attacks on the kingdom. It didn't say what it sent. Britain already has a refueling plane flying missions for Saudi operations in Yemen. European governments are wary of getting drawn deeper in, but energy prices are part of the calculation.
[ava] Elsewhere, Brazil's Senate voted seventy-five to one to end the six-day work week. The maximum week drops from forty-four hours to forty, in two steps over about fourteen months, with no pay cut. It's a win for President Lula.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] On cyber, a new kind of attack. Researchers at Citizen Lab and the ad-security firm Confiant say an Israeli ad company called Discover-Tech helped spyware makers infect devices just by showing someone an online ad. No click needed. They call it the first technical proof of a zero-click attack delivered through targeted ads. One partner was Candiru, a spyware firm the U.S. has blacklisted, and at least forty-five devices at Al Jazeera were targeted in twenty twenty-four and twenty twenty-five. Google has dropped Discover-Tech from its ad platform. For people at high risk, the advice is an ad blocker, limited ad tracking, and resetting your phone's advertising I.D. now and then.
[andrew] And for developers, new reporting on a GitHub attack called GhostAction. About nineteen hundred accounts were hijacked and more than thirty thousand code repositories targeted with a fake workflow file named security-audit dot Y-M-L that steals passwords and cloud keys. GitHub began blocking it on October ninth. If that file showed up in your repos, rotate your secrets.
