## US News | sting=us
[ava] Now, the top stories here at home.
[ava] Inflation is running hotter. The Labor Department says consumer prices rose three point seven percent over the past year in September, up from three point four in August, and a bit above what economists expected. Gasoline did a lot of the work, but core prices, which leave out food and energy, also came in a touch firm at two point six percent. Why it matters: the Federal Reserve raised rates in September, and markets now see roughly even odds of another hike at its October twenty-seventh and twenty-eighth meeting. More on that with Andrew in a few minutes.
[ava] In Washington, Kimberly Guilfoyle has resigned as U.S. ambassador to Greece. That came days after the Wall Street Journal reported that she asked a Republican donor to pay off a hundred-thousand-dollar American Express bill, and that she made sharp remarks to Greece's energy minister. Guilfoyle hasn't publicly detailed her side of the reporting, so keep in mind these are the Journal's accounts, based on its sources.
[ava] Election season is in full swing, with the midterms less than three weeks out. The Associated Press reports Republicans are pulling money out of a handful of front-line House districts and moving it elsewhere, while Democrats press into Republican-held seats. President Trump traveled to Alaska to campaign for a Senate seat there; the Washington Post notes the state is feeling the pinch of high energy prices. Closer to home, Maine Senator Susan Collins faced Democratic challenger Troy Jackson in a debate. Both campaigns say their side has the momentum; the polls, as always, will tell us in November.
[ava] A public health note: Pennsylvania has recorded a sixth measles death, as cases there top eleven hundred. The C.D.C. says it's sending more support, and nationally, confirmed cases have passed four thousand this year, the most in decades.
[ava] And in Georgia, a man died after being thrown from the Goliath roller coaster at Six Flags Over Georgia. His school remembers him as a cherished teacher. The cause is under investigation, and the case has renewed questions about how amusement rides are inspected.

[ava] In the Middle East, the conflict around Yemen is widening. Saudi Arabia struck targets near Sanaa, and Yemen's Houthi movement responded with an attack on the headquarters of the Saudi oil company Aramco in Dhahran. Saudi officials described a limited fire; some sources told Reuters the damage was more extensive. ## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] Now the cyber desk, and if you run a corporate network, this one's for you.
[andrew] CISA, the federal cybersecurity agency, is urging everyone to patch Ivanti Connect Secure V.P.N. appliances right away. Attackers have been exploiting a flaw since at least October first that lets them run commands without logging in. Researchers at Volexity say it's being chained with two other bugs to get full control, and one of those, a privilege escalation flaw, still has no patch. Because the attack runs in memory, Ivanti's own integrity checker may not spot it, so CISA recommends network and memory forensics. Federal agencies have until Friday.
[andrew] Cisco patched another actively exploited zero-day in its Secure Email Gateway. A booby-trapped attachment can give an attacker root access on unpatched devices.
[andrew] And Nextcloud admins: if you use the Collabora built-in CODE server app, update it to version twenty-six point four point three oh three or remove it. Just disabling it doesn't help. Attackers have been planting web shells since September.
[andrew] On the bigger picture, the heads of the I.M.F. and the Bank of England warned that cyber fraud could erode trust in the financial system so badly that people opt out, with the I.M.F.'s Kristalina Georgieva saying some could turn to shoeboxes under the bed. On the defense side, Microsoft and Japanese authorities say they took down a scam network.
