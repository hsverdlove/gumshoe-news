## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I., the White House's new Super Intelligence Force held its first meeting Thursday. Politico and Semafor report it's led by Jay Clayton, the administration's A.I. czar. The group commissioned a report from science advisers on A.I. risks and set up a working group, co-led with National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross, to carry out September's White House Accord on Super Intelligence, under which top labs agreed to police their own frontier development.
[ava] Supporters say a voluntary, dialogue-first approach lets the U.S. move fast while still watching risk. Skeptics argue that self-policing without enforcement depends on the labs' goodwill, especially with the F.T.C. separately demanding safety records from OpenAI and Anthropic this week.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] On the cyber desk, a serious one. BeyondTrust, Mandiant, and Google's threat intelligence group confirmed Thursday night that a suspected Chinese state-backed espionage group has been exploiting a zero-day in BeyondTrust Remote Support appliances, the tools I.T. teams use to reach and fix remote machines. It's C-V-E twenty twenty-six dash one oh two seven six four, exploited since at least August twenty-ninth. Mandiant calls the group Armory Castle, the same crew behind earlier Ivanti and Citrix attacks.
[andrew] According to The Record, the attackers pulled every credential stored on the appliance and planted changes that survive reboots and configuration resets. A patch is out. Mandiant's advice: treat any vulnerable appliance that was reachable from the internet as potentially compromised until it's been reviewed, and expect broad exploitation now that the bug is public, because that's this group's pattern.
[andrew] And a reminder: the federal fix-by dates for the Palo Alto PAN-OS and Cisco Secure Email Gateway flaws are tomorrow.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, New York has new celebrities. A family of barn owls has nested in a water tower atop a building on West Seventy-Fifth Street, near Central Park. A migration ecologist at the Cornell Lab of Ornithology says it's the first documented barn owl nest in Manhattan in recorded history. Birders count two adults and five or six fledglings, and every night a crowd gathers on the sidewalk to watch them head out to hunt.
[ava] Neighbors had heard blood-curdling screeches for weeks. One father told the A.P. he ran into his kids' bedroom more than once, thinking a child was hurt, before learning it was owls. Now he lets photographers onto his roof deck almost every night. The couple who first reported the nest say they weren't birders before. Now, quote, I think we're about to become birders.
