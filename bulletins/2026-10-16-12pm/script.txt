## US News | sting=us
[ava] In the midterms, meat is on the menu. The Associated Press reports that beef has turned into a surprising campaign battleground. Bureau of Labor Statistics figures show a pound of ground beef averaged six dollars and seventy-nine cents last month, up from five fifty-five in January of last year, when President Trump returned to office.
[ava] Democrats are mostly using beef as a symbol of rising costs. In Mississippi, Democratic Senate candidate Scott Colom is running an ad built around a clip of Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith saying shoppers have, quote, so many proteins to choose from. Her campaign counters that Colom backs the Biden-era policies it says drove up inflation.
[ava] Republicans, for their part, are painting some Democrats as anti-meat. In Texas they've seized on an old interview in which Senate nominee James Talarico said a past campaign was going vegan, and in Colorado they're targeting House candidate Manny Rutinel over his past activism against factory farming. Rutinel, who's been photographed eating steak tacos, says voters care more about being able to afford what's on their plates. The A.P. also notes the president's push to import more beef, especially from Argentina, hasn't brought prices down and has angered some American ranchers.

## International | sting=world
[ava] Overseas, the European Union summit in Brussels wrapped its Ukraine session with strong words but no new money. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the bloc will not lift any sanctions on Russia unless it stops the war, and that work is under way on a twenty-second sanctions package. Summit Chair António Costa told President Zelenskyy, quote, this is no time to withdraw support.
[ava] According to draft conclusions reported by Euronews, leaders formally regret the U.S. decision to temporarily lift sanctions on Russian diesel. But Brussels Signal and the Kyiv Independent report there were no new funding decisions, and using frozen Russian assets wasn't on the formal agenda. That matters, because Ukraine's finance minister says Kyiv still faces a budget gap of more than thirty-two billion dollars next year. The Trump administration's view is that the diesel arrangement and its energy ceasefire were steps toward ending the war, and it blames Ukraine's refinery strikes for higher oil prices.
[andrew] And in Asia, a market shock. Indonesian stocks plunged about eleven and a half percent today, their worst single day since nineteen ninety-eight, according to Nikkei Asia and The Jakarta Post. The trigger: index provider M.S.C.I. proposed reclassifying Indonesia from an emerging market to a so-called standalone market, which would drop its shares from the big emerging-market indexes that trillions of dollars in funds track.
[andrew] M.S.C.I. cites slow progress on transparency around who owns Indonesian shares, and concerns about suspected coordinated trading. Nothing is final yet. The consultation runs until the end of next April, with a decision due by June thirtieth, which gives Jakarta time to respond. Reuters quotes observers calling it a wake-up call.

## Technology | sting=tech

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I., a look at how ads inside ChatGPT actually work. 404 Media bought ads in ChatGPT to test the system and reports that advertisers can aim at you two ways: by what you're asking about, and by uploading lists of email addresses or phone numbers that OpenAI then matches to real accounts. OpenAI says topic hints guide matching but aren't exact targeting rules, that small lists can only be used to exclude people, and that uploaded files are usually deleted within a day.
[ava] The catch, according to 404 Media: turning off ads personalization in settings doesn't stop ads or all tracking. OpenAI's position is that ads help keep a free tier available to hundreds of millions of people. If privacy matters to you, it's worth checking that setting and knowing what it does and doesn't do.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] Cyber desk, two quick ones. Burger King Japan says attackers got into a server holding records on eleven point one eight million app users, with names, birth dates, email addresses, phone numbers and order history, which were later leaked online. The company says no card numbers were stored there and it found no sign passwords were taken. Expect phishing that impersonates the brand.
[andrew] And GreyNoise found a campaign hijacking at least a hundred forty-three internet-connected devices in thirty-four countries, including D-Link storage boxes and old Ubiquiti video recorders, using known, years-old flaws. The attackers quietly installed a legitimate bandwidth-sharing app and pocketed the earnings, so your internet connection becomes their product. The fix is the boring one: update or retire old gear that faces the internet.

## Local News & Events | sting=local
[ava] Closer to home. New Hampshire Attorney General John Formella's office has ruled that five state troopers were legally justified when they shot and killed fifty-seven-year-old David Ward in Keene in August of last year. The Concord Monitor reports Ward was armed with a loaded handgun during a six-hour standoff after a traffic stop on a drug-trafficking warrant, and that he told officers he would make them shoot him before lunging from his car with the gun in hand. No charges will be filed.
[ava] The Monitor also notes that of the nine people shot by police in New Hampshire last year, the attorney general found the use of force justified in every case, a record that police-accountability advocates have long questioned, while law enforcement says it reflects how dangerous these encounters are.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally. Dennis Darnes of Atlanta started losing his sight at fifty to glaucoma and cataracts. CBS Atlanta reports he lost his job, then his home, and was afraid to cross the street. A chance conversation on a bus led him to the Center for the Visually Impaired in Midtown, where he learned to navigate by touch and sound.
[ava] This week, on White Cane Awareness Day, he and more than fifty other veterans received free Meta smart glasses and training. Paired with the Be My Eyes app, the glasses read signs aloud and describe what's around him. Now seventy, he says he can ask them to find the lemon pepper seasoning at the grocery store, or a quarter he dropped on the floor. His words: there's no box. You can almost do anything.
