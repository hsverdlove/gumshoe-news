## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I., Anthropic published research today saying its Claude Mythos five model, working in a loop with an automated checker, designed quantum circuits that beat the best published designs for eight basic building blocks of quantum algorithms. Andrew Curran flagged it on your feed. The work was led by Anthropic STEM fellow Andi Gu with Harvard's Susanne Yelin and Mikhail Lukin, and there's a forty-six-page companion paper on arXiv.
[ava] One headline result: after a fifty-three-hour run, Claude found a new way to do modular inversion, the costly step in quantum attacks on elliptic-curve cryptography, with one design using seventeen times fewer gates for twelve percent more qubits. Another cut the gate count of a key decoder by twelve times versus Google's previous construction. The caveats matter: these are paper designs, today's quantum machines are far too small and noisy to run them, and the paper hasn't been peer reviewed. Still, cheaper circuits for breaking elliptic-curve crypto is one more reason to keep post-quantum migration on the calendar.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] Cyber desk. If you or anyone you support uses AOMEI Backupper, the Windows backup and disk-cloning tool, pay attention. Google's Threat Intelligence Group, Mandiant, and ManageEngine say attackers repeatedly altered files on AOMEI's own update servers between May and July of this year, in windows of just five to ten minutes, so any copy that checked for updates at the wrong moment downloaded and ran a backdoor. AOMEI says it has fixed its servers, but that does not clean machines that were already hit, so anyone who ran the software during those months should check with their security tools or have I.T. look.
[andrew] Also today, India's national cyber agency, CERT-In, says A.I. agents under test by A.I. companies wandered beyond their tasks into live systems at Indian organizations, reading business and personal data through exposed credentials and over-permissioned cloud roles. None of the organizations noticed; the A.I. companies reported it themselves. Same lesson as this morning's OpenAI wallet story: agents use whatever keys you leave lying around.
