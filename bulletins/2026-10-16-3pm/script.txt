## US News | sting=us
[ava] One update on the story that broke last hour. Katie Zacharia is out as White House press secretary, but she may not be leaving the administration. A senior administration source told NBC News she'll move to a new role that hasn't been announced yet. Her supporters say she was set up to fail with almost no preparation; critics point to her one short briefing and her acknowledgment that she never passed the bar after describing herself as a lawyer.

Tafoya also broke with the president on Iran, saying the war needs to end quickly and strategically, and asking him to lay out an exit plan.

## International | sting=world
[ava] Overseas, Iran is widening its campaign against shipping. CNBC counts at least nine tankers and cargo ships attacked near the Strait of Hormuz since Sunday, and BBC Verify, using British maritime data, found a record twelve vessels reported attacks in the week before that. The container ship GFS Pride sank this week after a strike; all twenty-one crew were evacuated. The International Maritime Organization says three seafarers were injured and two are missing.
[ava] Iran says the ships are using an unauthorized route close to Oman's coast and wants them nearer its own shore. U.S. Central Command says traffic is still flowing under escort by U.S. and partner forces, and the Navy says escorted transits in early October were up eighty-five percent from August. Analysts quoted by the BBC say Iran is now hitting ships far from the strait, off Qatar, the U.A.E., and in the Gulf of Oman. Meanwhile Iran-allied Houthi militants in Yemen have kept firing missiles and drones at Saudi Arabia this week.
[ava] In Canada, Albertans vote Monday in a referendum on whether the province should separate from Canada. Prime Minister Mark Carney rallied thousands in Calgary last night, telling them to vote like Canada depends on it. And off The Gambia, the Red Cross says at least twelve migrants died when a boat went down, with the number missing still unknown.

## Technology | sting=tech
[andrew] Tech desk. Nvidia's robotics research group, called GEAR, released version one of an open-source project called ASPIRE. Instead of retraining a robot's neural network, a coding A.I. writes the robot's control program, watches it fail, debugs it, and saves what works into a growing library of skills. In real-world demos, a two-armed robot solved a Rubik's cube, stacked cups, and unscrewed a bottle cap, and Nvidia reports ninety-nine percent success on eighty standard simulation tasks. Nvidia also notes how much depends on the A.I. you plug in: on one test set, success ranged from fifteen percent with a weaker model to ninety-nine percent with a top one. The caveat is that these are lab benchmarks and demos chosen by the researchers, not robots on a factory floor, but it's free for anyone to try.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I., the Simons Foundation, Anthropic and OpenAI are putting fifteen million dollars in combined funding and resources into helping mathematicians handle the flood of A.I.-generated results. The foundation will run an open call for workshops and programs to check A.I.-assisted proofs, teach the tools, and set shared standards, and the two labs will give researchers model access and computing power. Foundation president David Spergel said the math community, not any single company, should decide how these tools are used.
[ava] The backdrop, as The Independent reports, is a backlash after OpenAI published hundreds of experimental A.I. proofs at once, which some mathematicians said swamped peer review. Fields medalist Terence Tao told Numberphile that math is leaving its romantic era of lone heroes for industrial-scale teamwork with A.I. Supporters say the money brings the companies to the table; skeptics may ask whether labs funding the people who check their work is the right arrangement.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] Cyber desk. Booz Allen, the government contractor, built a mock industrial network and turned commercial frontier A.I. models loose on it. Dark Reading reports the A.I. went from the outside firewall, through the business network, into the plant-control network, and physically moved a robotic arm in about sixteen minutes, achieving all eight test objectives. Booz Allen's Kyle Miller said it would likely have been twice as fast without human approval steps, though the lab did leave known, unpatched flaws in place.
[andrew] The lesson for utilities and factories is that obscurity is no longer a defense. The recommended fixes are tighter network segmentation, monitoring for unusual traffic, automatic blocking at the perimeter, and decoys like honeypots that can slow down or even fool an A.I. attacker.

It sank, and so did everyone's hearts. Those strings keep him calm and happy, and his mum, Emma Jones, tried craft-store ribbons and shoelaces, but nothing was the same.
