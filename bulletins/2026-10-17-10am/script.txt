## US News | sting=us
[ava] The federal investigation into letters with suspicious substances mailed to public officials keeps widening. NBC News reports Republicans are now among the targets. Senators Susan Collins of Maine, Pete Ricketts of Nebraska, Ted Cruz of Texas, and Mike Rounds of South Dakota confirmed their offices were sent mailings. Ricketts said police and the F.B.I. confirmed the substance sent to his Omaha office was not harmful.
[ava] They join at least twenty-six state election offices and offices of at least twenty-two congressional Democrats, including Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer. In Massachusetts, six House members' district offices and Secretary of State Bill Galvin's office got letters Friday. The F.B.I. and the Postal Inspection Service are leading the case and most letters to senators were intercepted before delivery. No injuries have been reported.
[ava] A new A.P.-NORC poll finds about eight in ten registered voters want substantial change or complete upheaval in how the country is run. The economy and the cost of living top the list of issues. The appetite for change isn't just on one side: Democrats and independents lead, but fifty-eight percent of Republicans also want substantial change, even with their own party holding the White House. The poll of eleven hundred nineteen adults was taken October eighth through twelfth, with a margin of error of about four points.

## International | sting=world
[ava] Overseas, Ukraine's president has responded to President Trump's overnight pause on the new Russia sanctions. That's Iran's account, and the tanker hasn't been named. The commander of U.S. Central Command, Admiral Brad Cooper, said Friday the strait is controlled by the United States and its partners and remains open for business. Either way, the British maritime agency U.K.M.T.O. counts at least ten ships attacked in or near the strait since last Sunday, the most in any week since the war began in February.
[ava] In France, tens of thousands of people marched today against rising living costs, fuel prices, and cuts to public services. Most marches were peaceful, but in Paris police used tear gas and water cannons on small violent groups at the Place de la République, where a car and a newspaper kiosk were set on fire. In Marseille, protesters briefly forced their way into City Hall. Officials blamed the violence on the ultra-left, and police report at least a hundred sixty-six arrests nationwide.
[ava] And Pope Leo the fourteenth is back at the Vatican. He walked out of Rome's Gemelli hospital this afternoon, local time, to greetings from dozens of well-wishers, four days after surgery to remove the lower lobe of his right lung.

## Technology | sting=tech
[andrew] Tech desk, and this one's for every Tesla owner, Harry included. The California Highway Patrol released dashcam video of a Tesla plowing into a patrol car on Interstate eighty in Fairfield, just before midnight Tuesday. Officers were working an earlier fatal crash, with four lanes closed behind flares and cones. The twenty-two-year-old driver from Sacramento told investigators he was asleep with Full Self-Driving engaged. Both officers were seriously hurt; KQED reports they've since been released from the hospital. The driver had minor injuries.
[andrew] Two caveats. Right now, the driver's word is the only evidence F.S.D. was on, and Tesla's logs should settle that. And the C.H.P. stresses this is a supervised driver-assist system, so the driver is responsible. Critics like Electrek's Fred Lambert argue Tesla shares the blame, pointing to the product's name and how easily its driver monitoring can be fooled. Either way, the rule hasn't changed: eyes up.
> source: Electrek, "Tesla driver says he was asleep on FSD when he hit CHP car, 2 officers hurt" (Fred Lambert, Oct 16); KQED, "After Sleeping Tesla Driver Crashes Into Patrol Car, CHP Investigates"

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I., the big companies want agents that shop and book for you, but The Verge reports a lot of the web is closing the door. Yelp says it only allows agents that pay for its data. Delta is blocking agents from booking tickets. JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab block them from accounts over security concerns, and Amazon has pushed back on agents from Perplexity and Meta. Meta argues that turning away a personal agent means turning away the customer behind it, and it has signed partnerships with Walmart, Best Buy, and others instead. The companies doing the blocking cite fraud and security.
[ava] And Google's new Gemini 4 Argon scored ninety-seven and a half percent on the tough ARC-AGI-3 reasoning test, making Google the second lab after OpenAI to pass ninety-five. There's an asterisk, though. That score used a setup that lets the model keep its reasoning between rounds. Under the standard setup, it scored under fifty percent.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] Cyber desk. Researchers at Citizen Lab say they've caught spyware being delivered through ordinary online ads, with no click needed. Just loading the ad was enough. They call it AdFection. Al Jazeera gave them evidence that at least forty-five of its devices were targeted in twenty twenty-four and twenty twenty-five, and they tied the infections, with high confidence, to Candiru, an Israeli spyware maker that says it sells only to governments.
[andrew] The ads were bought through mainstream ad exchanges by another Israeli firm, Discover-Tech, and the code only fired on chosen targets. Everyone else just saw an ad. Between March and June, the operation won four hundred forty-two million ad auctions across about seventy-five thousand apps and a hundred twenty thousand websites. Those aren't infection counts, just reach. Google and other ad companies have since removed the accounts involved.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Targeted ads installed Candiru spyware in zero-click attacks" (Bill Toulas, Oct 16), citing Citizen Lab

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally. Last March, ten-year-old Willow Crowther from Kent, in England, got pneumonia so severe it triggered a seizure and several cardiac arrests. At London's Great Ormond Street Hospital, her parents were told she had a fifty percent chance and that they should bring family in to say goodbye.
[ava] Doctors ran a newer test called metagenomics, which reads all the genetic material in a sample at once, instead of hunting for one suspect bug at a time. It found a drug-resistant bacterial infection that needed a different antibiotic. With the right drug, Willow recovered, and she celebrated her tenth birthday in August. Her mom says the test saved her life.
[ava] In a study of a hundred twenty-two critically ill children, just published in the Lancet Microbe, the test changed treatment in a quarter of cases, and it's now rolling out to about thirty U.K. intensive care units.
[andrew] A test that tells doctors exactly what they're fighting, in a day. And a happy tenth birthday to Willow.
