## US News | sting=us
[ava] The fight between the Justice Department and California over mail ballots escalated this morning. President Trump posted that California election officials will all go to jail for a long period of time if they count ballots from more than twenty thousand people the department says are likely noncitizens. He was sharing a Friday letter from Acting Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter, who warned Los Angeles County that knowingly counting those ballots could make officials aiders and abettors to a federal crime.
[ava] The department says its list came from immigration records and data matching, and that it's asking only to set those ballots aside until citizenship is verified, not to purge anyone. California's Secretary of State, Shirley Weber, says the department hasn't shown its evidence or method, and that federal law bars systematic voter-roll removals this close to an election. She says she'll review any real proof, but won't disenfranchise thousands of voters on a belief that they're likely noncitizens. For context, documented noncitizen voting is rare. A Reuters review found a hundred twenty-nine prosecutions since the federal ban took effect in nineteen ninety-six. A federal court in Los Angeles will hear the department's lawsuit.
[ava] Also new: lawyers for Minnesota Representative Ilhan Omar asked the Justice Department to return her phone, computers, notebooks, and passport, seized when Homeland Security agents searched her Washington home on Monday. They call the search neither justified nor lawful, say investigators ignored limits the judge set, and say the probe breaks the department's policy against actions that could sway an election. Omar is on the ballot November third. A department spokesperson called the letter riddled with false allegations and says agents followed every court order. The New York Times reports agents seized the phone of Omar's sister before the search. The investigation reportedly concerns immigration paperwork Omar's father filed when she was a child. Omar denies wrongdoing, and no charges have been filed.
[ava] And across the country today, the group behind the No Kings protests is holding what it calls Vote Early events, thousands of gatherings focused on getting people to the polls. Organizers bill them as nonpartisan, though the movement grew out of opposition to the president.

## Technology | sting=tech
[andrew] Tech desk, and a car story with a Tesla angle. France and Germany struck a deal to soften Europe's planned twenty thirty-five end of new gas and diesel cars. Instead of a full hundred percent cut in fleet emissions, carmakers would need ninety percent, with no requirement to offset the rest. That leaves room for some hybrids and combustion models. In return, Germany backs France's Made in Europe push: seventy percent of a car's parts, not counting the battery, would have to be built in Europe to qualify for public money and contracts.
[andrew] German carmakers welcome the flexibility, saying electric demand is weaker than planned and Chinese competition is fierce. Environmental groups, and even Germany's own environment minister, wanted a tougher ninety-five percent target. It isn't law yet. European lawmakers vote on the proposal November twenty-third.
[andrew] And if you know someone with a Honda Accord Hybrid or C.R.-V. Hybrid from twenty twenty-three on, there's a redo recall. About forty-eight hundred cars got a defective tire repair kit nozzle during the first fix, so the sealant bottle cap can still pop off under pressure. Dealers will swap the nozzle for free, and V.I.N. lookup opened today.
> source: Euronews, "France and Germany strike car-industry deal linking 2035 engine rules to 'Made in Europe' push" (Oct 16); Élysée, Joint Franco-German press statement (Oct 16); Car and Driver, "Honda Issues Redo Recall for Accords and CR-Vs Prone to Flying Bottle Caps"; Honda news release and NHTSA recall 26V663

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I. policy, Politico reports Anthropic has been quietly pitching lawmakers its own draft federal safety bill. It would cover only the biggest developers, those training models above ten to the twenty-fifth computing operations that also have at least half a billion dollars in A.I. revenue or a billion in A.I. research spending. They'd have to test their models, publish safety reports, and be checked by independent evaluators registered with the Commerce Department.
[ava] The government could halt a model only through a federal judge in Washington, and only for a foreseeable and imminent risk of more than a billion dollars in damage or more than fifty deaths or serious injuries, from bioweapons, cyberattacks, or a model escaping its developer's control. The draft would also bar other agencies from stopping a model on their own.
[ava] The White House A.I. adviser, David Sacks, posted that some of the ideas could be reasonable if limited to true frontier companies, but that as written the computing threshold would eventually sweep in most of Silicon Valley and hurt startups. Supporters say the revenue and spending tests are there to keep small companies out. And critics on the other side may say a billion-dollar bar for stopping a model is set far too high.
> source: Politico, "Anthropic quietly pitches its own AI safety bill to lawmakers" (Oct 16), via Reuters and AI Affairs, "Anthropic pitches draft AI safety bill with Commerce-led enforcement" (Oct 17); AI Weekly; David Sacks on X (Oct 17)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] Cyber desk, and if you run Microsoft 365 for a business, this one matters. ConnectWise shut down its Cloud Backup service, the former SkyKick, after attackers took over the app's identity inside customers' Microsoft 365 accounts. Huntress says it has found at least a hundred sixty-two compromised tenants, and Microsoft calls it an active supply-chain attack.
[andrew] Because the attacker signed in as the backup app itself, multi-factor login never came into play, and the app already had broad permission to read mail, OneDrive and SharePoint files, and Teams chats. In some tenants, the attacker gave itself admin roles and planted extra credentials. ConnectWise says it has no sign backup data was taken. The advice: disable the ConnectWise and SkyKick apps, and check every app's secrets and certificates for anything added since October thirteenth. Shutting off the service doesn't remove what was already planted.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Hackers breach Microsoft 365 tenants via ConnectWise supply-chain attack" (Ionut Ilascu, Oct 17), citing ConnectWise, Huntress, Microsoft and Todyl

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally. Hero is a working cocker spaniel from Clevedon, in the west of England. Glaucoma cost him one eye in January, and the other in July. His vet warned that about one dog in ten can't cope with losing their sight, and at first Hero shut down. He was anxious and barked whenever his owners left the room.
[ava] So his longtime trainer, Lucy Shipway, went back to basics with scent work: hunting for a tiny piece of clove buried in gravel, or an oily cloth hidden behind a flower pot. Now Hero finds his way around using his nose, almost like a new pair of eyes. His owners say the other night he went off on his own, found his favorite toy, and dropped it in their laps, as if to say thank you.
