## US News | sting=us
[ava] President Trump rallied in Highland Heights, Kentucky, this afternoon for Republican House candidates, including Ed Gallrein. He told the crowd this second term is much more powerful than his first, and said, quote, I can do stuff that is unbelievable. He pointed to renaming the Gulf of Mexico, and said he had also renamed a lake bordering Canada, apparently Lake Ontario, as Lake America.
[ava] He said the war with Iran will end soon and gas prices will fall, and he credited his tariffs for new factory investment in the state. He repeated his promise that every adult would get five thousand dollars if Republicans hold the House and Senate. The Associated Press notes that would likely cost more than a trillion dollars and need Congress to pass it. And he added a new pledge: Defense Department money to revive a drug manufacturing plant in Winchester, Kentucky, if his party wins.
[ava] Supporters say the investment and border numbers show his agenda working. Critics point to what the A.P. describes as voter frustration over prices driven up by tariffs and the Iran war. Election Day is November third.
[ava] Also new this weekend, if you fly: air traffic controllers get a two point eight percent raise starting tomorrow, backdated to January. In exchange, the F.A.A. and the controllers' union agreed that controllers will badge in and out of their facilities, shifts will be built around expected traffic, and transfers will go first to the most short-staffed sites. The Wall Street Journal reports the badge rule is aimed at the practice of controllers leaving shifts early, which drew scrutiny after this year's deadly crash at LaGuardia. The union's president called the raise long overdue.

## International | sting=world
[ava] In France, a day of nationwide protests turned violent. The interior minister, Laurent Nuñez, says about five hundred fifty thousand people marched, a hundred ten thousand of them in Paris, after three weeks of student-led protests over school conditions, joined by unions and yellow vest activists angry about the cost of living. Most marches were peaceful. But on the margins, there was looting in Paris, Lyon, Bordeaux, Marseille and other cities, and attacks on public buildings.
[ava] The worst damage was in Limoges, in central France. Masked protesters burned a police car, broke into the town hall, and set it on fire. The roof and the landmark bell tower collapsed, and the regional prefect told A.F.P. the building, more than a century old, is largely lost. He called it a catastrophe for heritage.
[ava] About four hundred people were arrested and sixty officers hurt. In Saint-Étienne, the mayor says a young man lost his hand to a grenade, though he didn't say what kind of grenade or who used it. Nuñez says most of those arrested were adults with no tie to the student movement, and he and Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu blame hard-left agitators ahead of April's presidential election. President Macron says those who vandalized and looted will be prosecuted. On the other side, leaders of the left-wing France Unbowed party accuse the government of provoking the crowds with heavy-handed policing and letting the crisis fester. Despite the riots, the government is not declaring a state of emergency for now.
[ava] And on Mexico's Pacific coast, Hurricane Trudy is moving roughly parallel to the shore. Forecasters don't expect a direct landfall, but Mexico warns of heavy rain, flooding and mudslides from Jalisco and Colima down toward Guerrero.

## Technology | sting=tech
[andrew] Tech desk. A public disagreement in the world of A.I. coding tools. Replit's chief executive, Amjad Masad, said on stage at TechCrunch Disrupt that the company is no longer working with Anthropic. He cited cost, the partnership, and performance, saying OpenAI's newest models score better on Replit's own test for building apps from scratch. Masad says Replit had been one of Anthropic's top five customers.
[andrew] But it's not a clean break. Masad later clarified that users can still pick Anthropic's Claude models, and OpenAI is simply the new default. Since most people stick with the default, that could still cut Replit's spending with Anthropic a lot. A person familiar with the relationship told TechCrunch the partnership is still in place, and Replit was promoting Claude models as recently as late September. Masad also says enterprise contracts grew from about two percent of Replit's revenue last year to about fifteen percent this year.
> source: TechCrunch, "Replit is no longer working with Anthropic, CEO says" (Kirsten Korosec, Lucas Ropek and Rebecca Bellan, updated Oct 17)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I. and science, Google Research described putting A.I. agents in charge of real lab robots. One tool keeps a live 3D model of the lab bench, so an agent can check volumes and robot moves in simulation before touching anything. In another project, agents ran three rounds of designing and testing short D.N.A. strands that grab target proteins, raising the share that worked. In a third, a human and agent team found proteins that bind tightly to a target found on many solid tumors, in just a few experimental runs. Google says humans still approve the designs and supervise, and fully autonomous labs need safeguards against agents gaming their own experiments.
[ava] And Anthropic made its multi-agent code reviewer for Claude Code generally available to Team and Enterprise customers. The company's reason is striking: it says changes landing each day in its own main code base jumped from about twenty-five hundred in May to more than thirty thousand in September, too many for people to read line by line. Those are the company's own figures.
> source: Google Research, "Grounding AI agents in biological reality: Building blocks for autonomous wet labs" (Oct 16); Anthropic, "Code Review is now generally available" and "How the Claude Code team does code review" (Oct 15), via AI Pro Playbook (Oct 17)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] Cyber desk, and if you or anyone you know uses the OpenCode coding agent, rotate your keys. Anomaly Innovations, the company behind it, says attackers reached stored OpenAI, Anthropic and Google A.P.I. keys through an old console system it was still shutting down. It says about one point four percent of its Console users were affected, and it emailed them this morning. The company says it can't confirm the keys were misused, but some users say they've already seen unexplained charges on their A.I. accounts. The advice: revoke and reissue keys directly in each provider's dashboard, not through links in an email, check billing since today, and set spending limits.
[andrew] And website admins: Drupal patched two critical flaws that can let attackers run code on a server, in some setups without logging in. It warns exploits could appear within hours or days. Update core to ten point six point nineteen, eleven point three point nineteen, or eleven point four point ten, and the Entity List module to three point zero point ten.
> source: Startup Fortune, "OpenCode breach exposes stored OpenAI and Anthropic keys, prompts urgent key rotation" (Oct 17); OfficeChai and Crypto Briefing (Oct 17); BleepingComputer, "Drupal patches critical RCE flaws, warns exploits may emerge soon" (Bill Toulas, Oct 17)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
[ava] Around York, a University of Maine study finds baby lobsters on the Midcoast fell about eighty percent from twenty ten to twenty twenty-two, an early warning of a later drop in adults. Researchers tie it to warmer water, not overfishing. The good news closer to home: in southern Maine, lobster numbers at every life stage have stayed fairly stable since twenty ten. Some lobstermen are skeptical, saying their traps are full of short lobsters that bode well for next year.
