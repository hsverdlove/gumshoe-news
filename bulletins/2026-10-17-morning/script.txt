## US News | sting=us
[ava] We start in Washington. Late Friday night, President Trump signed an executive order waiving the main penalties in the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, the law he signed just last month. It's named for the late South Carolina senator, who died unexpectedly in July. The law required duties of up to five hundred percent on Russian goods within thirty days, and tariffs of up to a hundred percent on the biggest buyers of Russian oil and gas, which include China and India. The waiver came two days before that deadline.
[ava] The law lets the president do this if he certifies to Congress that it's in the national interest, and he did, though the order itself doesn't spell out why. Sanctions under earlier executive orders stay in place. CNBC reports a Russian envoy praised the move and Democrats criticized it, and lawmakers from both parties had been urging more pressure on Moscow, not less. The case for the waiver is that tariffs that steep on China and India would hit major U.S. trading partners while energy prices are already high, and the administration has framed its Russian diesel deal as a way to bring prices down.
[ava] In Bend, Oregon, the A.P. has pieced together how at least three people tried to stop the sixteen-year-old who attacked Silver Rail Elementary. A driver in a red pickup saw him jump the school fence, yelled at him, and made the first nine-one-one call. The gunman fired at the truck three times, and the driver wasn't hurt. Inside, a teacher threw her cellphone at him, tackled him, and fought for his shotgun. She wasn't hurt either. Then Kaylub Giamanco, a father of two students who was driving by, followed him into the school and shot him. The Washington Post reports the gunman was a former student. Four people were killed, three children and a teacher. Gun-rights advocates point to an armed parent ending the attack, and others point to how easily a teenager got a shotgun.
[ava] In Southern California, hot Santa Ana winds are driving several fires. The Norte Fire, between San Clemente and San Juan Capistrano, burned about five hundred seventy acres on Friday and was zero percent contained last night. It destroyed or damaged at least six homes, and evacuation orders cover about sixteen thousand residents. Two firefighters went to the hospital with heat-related injuries. In Malibu, the Bryson Fire was at a hundred forty-six acres and seventeen percent contained, and forecasters expect strong winds and low humidity to continue through today.
[ava] Two quick ones. Katie Zacharia posted late Friday that it was an honor to work for the president, confirming she's leaving the press secretary job after less than a week. And the Coast Guard pulled at least sixteen people from the Pacific about a hundred forty miles west of Ensenada, Mexico, after a boat sank. Eleven more are believed missing, and one survivor was treated for a suspected shark bite. The A.P. notes that area is used by migrant smugglers, but also by fishing and pleasure boats.

## International | sting=world
[ava] Overseas, Ukraine's president is leaning on Europe after a bruising week with Washington. At an E.U. summit in Brussels on Friday, Volodymyr Zelenskyy thanked what he called real leaders. He's slammed Washington's diesel deal with Russia and complained about sudden limits on Starshield, the military version of Starlink that Ukraine's drones depend on. Still, he told Euronews it's easier dealing with Trump than with Putin. For his part, President Trump said Friday he didn't think either he or Elon Musk was responsible for Ukraine losing that access.
[ava] Meanwhile, Russian strikes hit nine Ukrainian regions on Friday, cutting power to hundreds of thousands of households and wounding at least fifteen people. The U.N. human rights office in Kyiv says three hundred twenty-eight civilians were killed in September, and that killed and wounded combined ran eighty-five percent above a year earlier.
[ava] In Canada, Alberta votes Monday on whether to start the process toward independence. A yes wouldn't mean leaving; it would start a constitutional process toward a later, binding vote. Prime Minister Mark Carney made a final appeal against it, calling it a dangerous gamble while the U.S. wages a trade war, and Alberta's conservative premier, Danielle Smith, joined him in opposing independence. An Abacus Data poll taken October seventh through thirteenth had remain at sixty-eight percent and starting the process at twenty-four.
[ava] At the Vatican, Pope Leo the fourteenth is set to leave Rome's Gemelli hospital today, four days after surgery to remove a cancerous growth from his right lung. The Vatican says he'll lead the Sunday Angelus prayer from his apartment window tomorrow.
[ava] And Istanbul is cleaning up from its worst flooding since nineteen forty-two. The A.P. counts at least four dead, Turkish outlets report the toll has risen, and several people are still missing. In Yemen, the Houthi-run health ministry says a Saudi airstrike killed three children in Dhamar province; that hasn't been independently confirmed.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I., China's DeepSeek released an update today called V four point one Flash ten seventeen, with the weights free to download under an open license. DeepSeek says it's faster, gives shorter answers, and in the company's own tests beats bigger rivals like Claude Opus five and GPT five point six Sol on several coding and agent benchmarks. The Decoder highlights one chart where it solves seventy-seven percent of a software benchmark at just under twenty cents a task, compared with about fifty cents for the previous version. Those are DeepSeek's numbers, though, and independent testing is just starting.
[ava] Also from China: the commerce and science ministries have proposed export controls on twenty-three A.I. technologies, from large models to agents and computing clusters. Work that's already open source is exempt, and offering a model through an app or an A.P.I. doesn't count as an export. A temporary registration rule for exports took effect right away, and public comments run through November fifteenth. It echoes Washington's own export controls on advanced chips.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] Cyber desk, and this one's fun. For twenty-five years, cryptographers have wondered where the N.S.A. got the seed numbers behind the NIST elliptic curves, like P two fifty-six, which help secure a huge share of internet traffic. The worry was that the agency had shopped for curves with a hidden weakness. Anthropic says one of its internal Claude models cracked it in under half an hour. The seeds came from an old C random number generator, seeded with ordinary clock timestamps from nineteen ninety-seven and ninety-eight, and anyone can now reproduce them.
[andrew] Anthropic says that makes a backdoor much less plausible, and nobody needs to change anything. Some questions remain, like why a few usable candidates were skipped and how several of the curves' base points were chosen. The thirty-six thousand eight hundred sixty-four dollar bounty goes to the nonprofit behind Let's Encrypt.
[andrew] Less fun: if you run an online store on Magento or Adobe Commerce, there's a maximum-severity flaw nicknamed BlockStrap that can let attackers run code on your server. No attacks yet, but the security firm Sansec found four possible attack paths in under a day and warns that A.I. tools can turn the patch into a working exploit fast. Adobe rates it lower priority; Sansec disagrees. Apply Adobe's October release or its patch, and keep September's hotfix in place too.
