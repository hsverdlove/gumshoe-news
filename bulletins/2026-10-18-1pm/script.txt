## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Trump endorses Netanyahu
[ava] President Trump this morning gave Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu what he called his complete and total endorsement, nine days before Israel's October twenty-seventh election. In a Truth Social post, he called Netanyahu a great warrior prime minister against Iran, and wrote that if Israel picks anyone else, America will have to do this work ourselves, and he's not inclined to. Netanyahu thanked him, saying together we will complete the mission.
[ava] It's unusual for a U.S. president to openly back a candidate abroad, and it's a turnaround from Thursday, when Trump declined to endorse anyone. Axios reports most polls show Netanyahu's coalition short of the sixty-one seats needed to govern, and that Trump has privately been frustrated with him. His main rival, Gadi Eisenkot, said he respects the president, but the election is a domestic Israeli matter, decided only by Israel's citizens.

[ava] Here at home, sixteen days before the midterms, a new NBC News Decision Desk poll of registered voters puts the president's approval at thirty-nine percent, thirty-one percent on inflation, and thirty-four on Iran. He does better on immigration. Democrats lead by nine points on which party should control Congress, and forty-four percent say they're worse off than a year ago. The Cook Political Report has shifted eleven more House races toward Democrats. The White House argues prices will fall once the Iran war ends, and as always, polls are snapshots, not results.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Zelenskyy and Bessent on Meet the Press
[ava] On Meet the Press, Ukraine's President Zelenskyy said it would be a historic mistake for the U.S. to side with Russia, and said he hopes President Trump will change his decisions on pausing new sanctions. He also said slowed Starlink service, which his forces use against drones, was in his view a message from the American government. Starlink later returned to normal speed.
[ava] Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, on the same program, called that charge reckless, saying the president and Secretary Rubio have categorically denied any U.S. role. He said no sanctions were lifted, only the diesel embargo, and that the president wants a sixty-day pause to examine the situation. Asked three times whether Ukraine is an ally, he pointed to U.S. intelligence, Starlink and weapons sales through NATO, then said he's not the secretary of state. He also pressed European allies to send more Patriot missile systems.
// Story 4: Albania protests
[ava] In Albania, a sixth straight day of violent protests in the capital, Tirana. Student-led crowds angry about university conditions, tuition, jobs and corruption are demanding Prime Minister Edi Rama resign. Saturday was the worst so far, with a court, banks and a post office set on fire and police firing rubber bullets for the first time. The health ministry counts two hundred seventy-two injured since Tuesday, including a hundred sixty-nine police officers. Rama refuses to quit, blames what he calls a foreign hand, and warns the unrest threatens Albania's bid to join the European Union. He's offered talks on education costs, and the U.S. embassy is urging calm on all sides.
// Story 5: Wainwright update
[ava] And an update from Canada. Prime Minister Mark Carney has confirmed four service members were killed in the explosion and fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta, with their families notified, and dozens more seriously wounded. Earlier reports of seven deaths came from unnamed sources and haven't been confirmed. Officials say the cause is under investigation.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Apple App Store rules
[andrew] Tech desk. Apple has tightened its App Store rules against A.I. image abuse. Apps that let users create or share content, including A.I. image generators, must now actively prevent and remove nonconsensual intimate images, real or A.I. generated, with filtering, reporting and blocking tools. Apple is also banning bait-and-switch apps, ones that pass review and then quietly switch on hidden features, a trick some so-called nudify apps have used. Developers who don't fix violations can be removed from the store. The open question is enforcement, since the bait-and-switch apps were built to slip past review in the first place.
> source: Apple Developer News, "Strengthening App Review Guidelines against nonconsensual intimate imagery and bait-and-switch apps" (Oct 16); MacRumors, "New App Store Rules Target AI Deepfakes and Nudify Apps" (Oct 16); The Mac Observer

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: SiteGuard WAF exploited
[andrew] Cyber desk. Attackers are exploiting a critical flaw in SiteGuard Server Edition, a web application firewall from Japan that the vendor says protects more than one and a half million websites. The bug is in the statistics feature, which is on by default, and lets an attacker run commands with full administrator rights on the server. Fixed versions and a patch are out. One unusual piece of advice from the vendor: save the diagnostic information first, because patching can erase signs of an attack. The irony, of course, is that the tool meant to guard the website became the way in.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Hackers exploit SiteGuard WAF flaw to run commands as admins" (Bill Toulas, Oct 18), citing the SiteGuard advisory and JPCERT/CC

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a small kindness down in Bellingham. Seven-year-old Tristan Caron, who has autism, was sitting by himself at the Urban Air Adventure Park when a group of teenagers from Milford noticed. One of them, Livy Molinari, walked over and said, that's a really nice ball you have, can I hold it for a second? A game of catch turned into forty minutes of hide-and-seek, catch and the slides.
[ava] His mom, Christina, says it was life-changing for a boy who usually gets left out, and she tracked the teens down on Facebook to thank them. They told WBZ they had no idea Tristan had autism. They just saw a kid alone. As one of them, Kenny Leite, put it, maybe now other people will think about it when someone isn't included. The park plans to invite them all back for a day to celebrate.
> source: CBS Boston / WBZ-TV, "Mass. teens react to attention after inviting kid with autism to play" (Paul Burton, Oct 17)
