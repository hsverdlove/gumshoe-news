## US News | sting=us
[ava] Reuters reports that President Trump is privately blaming senior aides and political operatives as Republicans' midterm prospects slip. Three people familiar with internal talks say he has questioned campaign spending, faulted the redistricting push, and second-guessed candidate choices. Much of the friction centers on James Blair, who runs the super PAC MAGA Inc., and on White House chief of staff Susie Wiles. Public records put the super PAC's spending at about two hundred million dollars so far.
[ava] The White House disputes the picture. A spokesman called the sources armchair experts peddling baseless speculation. Blair called the reporting mudslinging and said he and the president are focused on helping Republicans win. Polls suggest Democrats are favored to win the House, while the Senate looks too close to call.
[ava] One reason voters are frustrated: prices. An Associated Press review finds the Iran war's effects are uneven but adding up. Regular gas averaged four thirty-three a gallon on Friday, about forty-five percent above where it was before the war, and AAA says it's the first October the average has topped four dollars. Diesel is up sixty-eight percent, and airfares in September were about twenty-two percent higher than a year earlier. So far, though, many stores have absorbed costs rather than raising prices across the board.
[ava] Moody's chief economist, Mark Zandi, estimates the war has cost the average household the equivalent of almost two thousand dollars, counting energy, higher interest rates and military costs. That's one economist's estimate. And here in the Northeast, the government expects heating oil bills to run about twenty-one percent higher this winter, even if the weather is mild. The administration's argument is that prices will fall once the war ends, which the president says will be soon.
> source: Reuters, "Trump pointing fingers at aides as GOP's midterm prospects dim" (Nandita Bose, Bo Erickson and Steve Holland, Oct 18); AP, "Retail price impacts from the Iran war are uneven but add up for US households before midterms" (Oct 17); AAA and EIA figures via AP; Fortune on Moody's Mark Zandi estimate (Oct 17)

## International | sting=world
[ava] In Canada, a deadly explosion and fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright, one of the country's busiest army training bases, in eastern Alberta. Military sources told Global News at least seven people were killed and dozens hurt, and that a live-fire training exercise struck a gas line or gas well Saturday afternoon. The armed forces confirm deaths and injuries but haven't released numbers while families are notified, and the Mounties are investigating. High winds pushed the fire beyond the base, and hospitals as far away as Edmonton declared a mass casualty incident. Local officials said overnight that the fire was being held. Defence Minister David McGuinty said he could not recall an incident like it.
[ava] Ukraine launched one of its biggest drone attacks of the war overnight. Russia's defense ministry says it downed more than a thousand drones. In St. Petersburg, the governor says fires broke out at the city's oil export terminal and an oil depot, one person was killed and five hurt, and flights were disrupted. Russian officials also reported hits on an Ozon warehouse and a VK data center. Ukraine says it's done with the energy ceasefire President Trump announced last week, and President Zelenskyy says Russia attacked Ukraine's energy system seventy-six times since then, with Russian strikes killing two people overnight.
[ava] In the Gulf, Iran's Revolutionary Guard says two supertankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz and caught fire, a claim that hasn't been independently verified. Iran's foreign minister says Tehran's seven-day plan to reopen the strait, passed through Qatar, is still on the table if Washington accepts its conditions. U.S. Central Command offers a different picture, saying ninety-seven percent of ships trying to cross have made it without being hit.
[ava] And in France, the morning after. The interior minister says more than eighteen hundred people were arrested, and the prime minister says more than a hundred fifty police were hurt. Videos also show a synagogue on Rue Burnouf in Paris ransacked, with holy books thrown to the floor; officials haven't said who did it. Marine Le Pen blames the far left for legitimizing the violence. Jean-Luc Mélenchon, who backed the marches, told protesters to stay away from looting. Unions and organizers stress that most marchers were peaceful and angry about the cost of living.

## Technology | sting=tech
[andrew] Tech desk. Shoppers are uneasy about A.I. setting prices, and the A.P. took a look at what's real. Digital shelf tags are spreading at big chains like Walmart and Whole Foods, and many companies use software to predict how shoppers will react to prices. On social media, people worry that different people get different prices for the same thing, sometimes called surveillance pricing.
[andrew] The industry's side: Walmart's chief executive says its tags save labor and are not used to change prices by the weather, time of day, or shopper, in his words, we price the product, not the person. A retail tech firm says changing grocery prices through the day is exceedingly rare, and one study found virtually no surge pricing after digital tags arrived. But the skeptics have a point too. Companies aren't always clear about the data they collect, Maryland, New Jersey and Connecticut have now limited using personal data to set prices, and the Federal Trade Commission has warned that failing to disclose it could break consumer protection law. McDonald's is also facing a lawsuit over its pricing tool, which it says is filled with inaccuracies.
> source: AP, "Consumer apprehension grows as US retailers adopt AI-assisted pricing tools" (Dee-Ann Durbin and Anne D'Innocenzio, Oct 18)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
[ava] In A.I., OpenAI is offering university labs free access to its life sciences model, GPT-Rosalind. Under a new academic plan, eligible U.S. labs can get up to thirty seats at no cost for up to a year. Because the model can reason about sensitive biology, access goes through a vetting process that checks the institution, the lab lead and the intended research, with monitoring afterward. OpenAI says research data shared through the plan isn't used to train its models by default.
[ava] And if you talk to ChatGPT out loud, OpenAI is rolling out a voice model called GPT-Live one point one, with new voices for languages including French, German, Hindi, Japanese, Korean and Mexican Spanish, plus more natural accents. Availability depends on your plan, region and app version.
> source: OpenAI, "Bringing GPT-Rosalind to academic research labs" (Oct 15); OpenAI Help Center, ChatGPT release notes; Notebookcheck, "ChatGPT Voice: GPT-Live-1.1 brings new voices for six languages"

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
[andrew] Cyber desk. In California, hackers stole records on millions of current and former public school students from the state's testing contractor, ETS. Officials say someone used a single stolen login to copy files between May and September. The data includes names, addresses, birthdates, and sensitive details such as disability and English learner status, but not Social Security numbers or financial information. ETS says it has added safeguards. Families get two years of free identity protection, and officials suggest a credit freeze for kids under sixteen. A Johns Hopkins security expert asked why one stolen password could open months of access.
[andrew] And Florida says it will start mailing letters to drivers whose records may have been exposed in last month's breach of its driver and vehicle database, offering a year of free credit monitoring. The state blames one police user's login that was stored on a personal device. A hacking group claims more than two hundred thousand records, which the state hasn't confirmed. Wherever you live, the lesson is the same: watch for scams that quote your real details back to you.
> source: Los Angeles Times, "Hackers steal personal data of millions of California students in massive breach" (Howard Blume, Andrew Khouri and Jaweed Kaleem, Oct 16); The Florida Press, citing Florida Politics and FLHSMV (Oct 17)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a record from just down the road in Rhode Island. Ron Wallace, a sixty-year-old grower, brought a pumpkin to the Southern New England Giant Pumpkin Weigh-Off in Warren that tipped the scales at three thousand one hundred fifty-six pounds. Guinness World Records has confirmed it as the heaviest pumpkin ever, and the first ever over three thousand pounds.
[ava] It beat a British record set just one week earlier by almost three hundred pounds. And it's Wallace's third world record. He was also the first past fifteen hundred pounds, in two thousand six, and the first past two thousand, in twenty twelve. He's been growing giants for about thirty-five years, inspired by his dad. His advice for beginners: start small, a hundred fifty square feet is enough, and join a local growers' club.
[andrew] Guinness says it's more than twice the weight of a dairy cow. That's a lot of pie.
