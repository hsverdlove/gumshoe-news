## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Castro warrant
[ava] In Florida, the state has issued a no-bond arrest warrant for former Cuban leader Raúl Castro, now ninety-five. It charges him with conspiracy to commit first-degree murder in the nineteen ninety-six shootdown of two Brothers to the Rescue planes, which killed four men over international waters. A Miami-Dade judge found probable cause that the order came down a chain of command with Raúl and Fidel Castro as the final decision-makers. Attorney General James Uthmeier and Governor Ron DeSantis are announcing it this morning. Castro already faces federal murder charges filed in May. The practical catch: he lives in Cuba, beyond the reach of U.S. courts, so the warrant is largely symbolic unless he travels.
// Story 2: East Potomac golf course
[ava] In Washington, President Trump says the historic East Potomac Golf Course will close November fifteenth for fifteen months of renovations, and he says the finished course will stay public and host major tournaments. It's contested: a federal judge has halted further tree removal there until October twenty-sixth, and the Interior Department says the plan isn't final while the Park Service review continues.
// Story 3: AAPI poll
[ava] And a new A.P.-NORC and AAPI Data poll of Asian American, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander adults finds a quarter approve of the president's job performance and about three in four disapprove. On the economy, which nearly nine in ten call a top issue, forty-one percent trust Democrats more and twenty-one percent Republicans. Republicans would note that nearly four in ten don't pick either party on the economy, so plenty of these voters are still up for grabs.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: France
[ava] Overseas, France says it could declare a state of emergency if needed after a weekend of the worst rioting in years. For now, Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu prefers local, partial curfews, starting in some cities tonight, though not a blanket curfew in Paris. Marseille has banned gatherings today. About twenty-four hundred people have been arrested, nearly a thousand officers injured, and retailers say almost three thousand shops were hit.
[ava] Lecornu blamed part of the violence on a far-left movement he says wants to disrupt April's presidential election. Hard-left leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon told him to show evidence or apologize. President Macron refused his interior minister's offer to resign, and the centre-left mayors of Paris, Marseille and Lyon wrote that whole neighborhoods were left without protection. President Trump posted that migrants have taken over France; French officials have not linked the unrest to immigration. Unions have called new strikes for November fifth.
// Story 5: UK school hoax threats
[ava] In the U.K., up to three hundred schools in Northern Ireland and many across Scotland received emails this morning claiming explosives had been planted. Police in both places are treating them as hoaxes sent from outside the country, and nothing has been found, though some schools closed or sent staff home.
// Story 6: West Bank arrests
[ava] And in Israel, police say they've arrested ten suspects over weekend settler attacks in the West Bank, one on a delegation of European diplomats near Taybeh and one on Washington Post photographer Heidi Levine during the olive harvest. Israel's president had called the attacks despicable.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: MiniMax M3.1 Flash
[andrew] In A.I., the price war from China keeps going. Days after DeepSeek's update, Shanghai's MiniMax launched M3.1 Flash, an agent and coding model it says runs nearly three times faster, cuts usage costs by seventy percent, and costs fifteen cents per million input tokens. MiniMax says an early version had already processed more than two hundred trillion tokens anonymously, under the name Space Bunny. The benchmark scores are MiniMax's own and haven't been independently reproduced, and the open weights are promised later. Still, it's another sign that capable A.I. keeps getting cheaper.
> source: MiniMax, "MiniMax M3.1 Flash: Redefining Agent Performance and Cost" (Oct 19); MarkTechPost (Oct 19)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Apex Accelerator npm
[andrew] Cyber desk, and a warning for anyone who uses A.I. coding agents. Elastic Security Labs exposed a fake crypto venture accelerator called Apex that courted startup founders with investment offers and free A.I. credits, then had them run its npm packages: a "copilot" and a "security scanner." The tools installed info-stealers and backdoors on Windows and Macs, produced fake audit results, and quietly rerouted the coding agent's A.I. traffic through the attackers' servers, exposing prompts and code and letting them slip in instructions. The packages are gone now, but the lesson stands: be wary of any tool a would-be investor asks you to install.
> source: The Hacker News, "Fake Web3 Accelerator Uses npm Packages to Spread Malware and Hijack AI Agent Traffic" (Ravie Lakshmanan, Oct 19); Elastic Security Labs, "Auditing the auditor: Unmasking Apex Accelerator"

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, in Braintree, Massachusetts, sixteen-year-old Tatiana Calvillo remembered feeling helpless during her sister's hospital stay. So this spring she started the Little Heroes Foundation, a student-run group that delivers monthly comfort kits, with small toys, coloring books and stickers, to kids in local hospitals. Friend Brynn Hso, whose sister was diagnosed with cancer last year, signed on right away. Since April, Little Heroes has grown to nineteen chapters across the country, plus chapters in France and Uganda, and volunteers now add handwritten cards. Tatiana's message for other kids: we can make an impact.
> source: Boston 25 News, "Massachusetts teen creates Little Heroes Foundation to bring comfort to children in hospitals" (Kelly Sullivan, Oct 18)
