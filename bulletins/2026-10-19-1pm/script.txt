## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: California noncitizen-voter sweep
[ava] In California, federal agents arrested more than two dozen people this morning in what the Justice Department calls Operation Protect the Vote. It says all are lawful permanent residents, not citizens, charged with crimes such as fraudulent voter registration. The administration's case: noncitizen voting is a federal crime, and every illegal vote cancels a legal one. California's Democratic leaders call the federal push an attempt to undermine the state's elections, and A.P. notes the problem is rare: before today, the department had charged about fifty people with noncitizen voting this year, against more than two hundred twenty million registered voters. It follows last week's federal lawsuit over twenty thousand mail ballots the government says went to likely noncitizens; state officials say they've seen no evidence for that list.
// Story 2: Supreme Court orders
[ava] At the Supreme Court, the justices turned away appeals from accused nine-eleven mastermind Khalid Sheikh Mohammed and two co-defendants seeking to revive plea deals that would have spared them the death penalty. Then-Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin threw out those deals in twenty twenty-four, and an appeals court backed him. Their military trial is set for June twenty twenty-eight. The court also agreed to decide whether an eighteen sixty-eight federal ban on distilling spirits at home is constitutional. Hobbyists call it a crime against a harmless pastime; the government says it prevents tax evasion.
// Story 3: Women's health order signed (update)
[ava] And an update: in the Rose Garden, with the first lady at his side, President Trump signed the women's health executive order. It creates a women's health division at the National Institutes of Health within ninety days and a White House task force co-chaired by Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Junior and Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, who the president noted recently beat breast cancer. No funding figure yet, and N.I.H. already has an Office of Research on Women's Health that the new division will work with.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 10: DeepSeek ban
[andrew] In A.I., the Department of Homeland Security has ordered all federal civilian agencies to remove China's DeepSeek from their systems and stop buying it, including any outside services built on DeepSeek models. The order cites the risk of sensitive data reaching China, plus malware and data collection concerns. Agencies can seek exceptions only with a compelling justification. DeepSeek hasn't commented publicly on the order.
> source: Federal Register public inspection, "Exclusion and Removal of DeepSeek Products from Federal Civilian Executive Branch Information Technology Systems and Procurements" (Oct 19); WSJ (Oct 19); Bloomberg (Oct 19); Reuters (Oct 19); CyberScoop (Oct 19)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 11: Cairn AI-agent hacking
[andrew] Cyber desk, and a vivid example of A.I. agents doing a hacker's work. Reuters reports that a researcher at the security firm NorForge, Snorre Fagerland, watched a Chinese-speaking hacker use an open-source agent platform called Cairn to break into websites at more than a hundred companies, including Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins. The agents tried more than twelve hundred sites, with each successful break-in taking about twenty-three hours and roughly fifty dollars of A.I. usage. Deere says it found no sign sensitive data was accessed, and SharkNinja says only a standalone support site was hit. Cairn's author says it wasn't built for attacks.
// Story 12: OpenAI bug case
[andrew] And prosecutors, ahead of a detention hearing today, say the former OpenAI engineer accused of hiding a recording device in an executive conference room, Abdulrahman Alfozan, was in frequent contact with a Saudi royal urging him to join a Saudi A.I. effort, and was days from a one-way flight to Riyadh when arrested. These are allegations he has yet to answer in court.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, ninety-nine-year-old Helga Duffy of Wiltshire, England, is the world's oldest wing walker, after riding strapped to the top of a small plane over an airfield in Gloucestershire. She got the idea watching ninety-seven-year-old Betty Bromage do it on the news. Helga said she wasn't frightened because she was so excited, and she raised more than thirteen hundred pounds for an air ambulance charity. Betty sent congratulations, and a warning: she can't wait to turn a hundred and do it again.
