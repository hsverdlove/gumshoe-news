## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Judge Reyes and the golf course case
[ava] In Washington, the fight over the East Potomac golf course moved into the courtroom. Federal Judge Ana Reyes rejected a Justice Department request that she step aside, calling it baseless in a thirteen-page opinion. Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward had argued she showed deep-seated antipathy toward the administration at an earlier hearing. Reyes answered with transcripts in which she praised government lawyers and ruled for them at earlier stages. At today's hearing she asked the other government lawyers at the table to raise their hands if they'd sign Woodward's filing; he told them not to answer, and she ordered a brief on the dispute. Hours earlier, President Trump called Reyes a crazed lunatic online and said work on the course would start immediately. The administration's case: a judge is micromanaging routine park maintenance. Preservationists counter that the government cut down hundreds of trees with little notice to the court.
// Story 2: Trump on Ukraine and a possible summit
[ava] Speaking to reporters before leaving for a campaign rally near Wichita for Kansas Senator Roger Marshall, President Trump said it may be time for Ukraine's President Zelenskyy to get somebody else to negotiate, and said Zelenskyy should have settled the war long ago. Zelenskyy said earlier today that Washington is in denial about Russia's position. The president also said he would join a meeting next month in China with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping if it happens, though he expects it to focus on nuclear weapons and denuclearization. He said he hasn't agreed to it yet.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: France curfews update
[ava] In France, overnight curfews spread tonight. In Paris, people may not enter or stay on the Place de la République or around Les Halles from eleven p.m. to six a.m., which is five p.m. to midnight our time; residents, workers and shopkeepers are exempt, and demonstrations are banned overnight near the Élysée, the National Assembly and other sites. Lyon and Nice have curfews from nine p.m., with Nice's applying only to minors through Sunday. The justice minister says more than twenty-five hundred people have been taken into custody since the weekend. And the U.S. Embassy is telling Americans to avoid protest areas.
[ava] On the cause, the accounts differ. President Trump again said today that France's trouble is immigration. Prime Minister Lecornu blames the ultra-left and criminals for hijacking protests, and the protests themselves began a month ago with high school students over crumbling classrooms, then grew into anger about living costs. Many analysts and French officials dispute the immigration link, while critics of France's migration policy say it's part of the picture.
// Story 4: Gaza commercial shipments
[ava] And in Gaza, Israel says it will resume letting commercial goods in after a days-long halt. COGAT, the Israeli agency that oversees aid, says merchants report Hamas dropped a demand for about thirty-two hundred dollars per truck; Hamas calls it a voluntary donation. Israel says it hasn't verified that, and payments must now go through documented bank transfers. Local health officials say Israeli strikes killed at least five Palestinians today; Israel's military didn't comment right away.
> source: AP, "Paris police issue overnight curfew in 2 areas of French capital" (Oct 19); BBC News live (Oct 19); The Guardian Europe live (Lucy Campbell, Oct 19); Libération via The Guardian; U.S. Embassy Paris alert (Oct 19); Reuters via StreetInsider, "Israel to resume commercial shipments to Gaza after Hamas fee dispute" (Nidal al-Mughrabi, Oct 19)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Gemini agents beat Minecraft
[andrew] In A.I., Google DeepMind says four Gemini 4 Argon agents beat Minecraft, defeating the Ender Dragon, with no human help in about twenty-seven hours. They wrote more than twenty-seven hundred small programs, traded messages and died six hundred five times along the way. The researchers estimate the run would cost about sixteen hundred dollars at the model's planned price. The caveats: it was one successful run, on easy difficulty, with structured game data and pathfinding tools rather than reading the screen like a person.
> source: arXiv, "A team of Gemini 4 Argon agents beats Minecraft" (2610.22319); Engadget (Oct 19); TEXXR roundup (Oct 19)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: BeyondTrust
[andrew] Cyber desk. If your company uses BeyondTrust Remote Support, patch now; federal agencies hit their deadline today. The critical flaw lets attackers into the appliance without logging in. BeyondTrust says two systems were breached as far back as late August, before the flaw was public, and Mandiant ties the attacks to a suspected Chinese espionage group. Experts say patching isn't enough: check for intruders and change any stored passwords.
// Story 9: ProPublica on CISA
[andrew] ProPublica reports that early in the administration, political appointees at Homeland Security asked its cyber agency, CISA, to secretly get a voting machine, hack it, report results to the White House, and not tell the manufacturer about any flaws. Two former officials say career staff called it impossible, partly because the needed specialists had been laid off, and the request was dropped. The account comes from unnamed former officials.
// Story 10: Alfozan held
[andrew] And an update: the former OpenAI engineer accused of planting a recording device in an executive conference room, Abdulrahman Alfozan, will stay in jail without bail. A magistrate judge called the Saudi consulate's offer to pay any bail a blank check that didn't ensure he wouldn't flee. His lawyer says he has lived here fifteen years and isn't a flight risk.
> source: Rapid7, "CVE-2026-102764: Critical BeyondTrust Remote Support Authentication Bypass Exploited in the Wild" (Oct 19); CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog; ProPublica, "How the Trump Administration Politicized the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency" (Andy Mannix and Doug Bock Clark); Metacurity (Oct 19); CNBC, "OpenAI bugging suspect Alfozan ordered held without bail" (Dan Mangan and Jordan Novet, Oct 19); WIRED (Oct 19)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a story from just over the border in Massachusetts. Seven-year-old Tristan Caron, who has autism, was sitting by himself at the Urban Air Adventure Park in Bellingham when a group of teenagers from Milford noticed. One of them, Livy Molinari, simply said, that's a really nice ball you have, can I hold it? That turned into forty minutes of catch, hide-and-seek and the slides. His mom, Christina, says it was life-changing for a boy who usually gets left out, and tracked the teens down on Facebook to thank them. They say they didn't know he had autism; they just didn't want him alone. Urban Air plans to invite them all back for a day celebrating their kindness.
> source: CBS Boston, "Mass. teens react to mother's praise after befriending boy with autism" (Paul Burton, Oct 19)
