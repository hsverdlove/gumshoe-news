## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 9: World ID for Zoom
[andrew] Tech desk. As deepfake scams get better, a company co-founded by OpenAI's Sam Altman is pitching banks a way to prove who's really on the call. World, formerly Worldcoin, launched a bundle for financial firms this weekend. Its centerpiece, World ID for Zoom, matches a live selfie and the meeting video against a person's earlier iris scan at one of World's Orb devices, then shows a verified-human badge. Bain Capital and VanEck are among the early partners. Supporters see a real answer to impersonation fraud; privacy advocates have long objected to collecting iris data, and several countries have restricted World's scans.
> source: World, "World ID for banking and finance" and "World ID for Zoom" (Oct 18); Fortune, "Zoom and Bain Capital add 'Proof of Human' badges" (Oct 18); Biometric Update (Oct 2026)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 10: Anthropic workforce study
[andrew] In A.I., Anthropic published a rare look at how A.I. is changing its own workforce. Junior employees' share of technical hires fell from twenty-seven percent to nine percent since early twenty twenty-five, and juniors now make up fourteen percent of technical staff, down from twenty-one. Technical hiring still grew overall, so it's a shift in the mix, not fewer engineers. Anthropic says managers now value judgment over implementation skills. It's one unusual company, but a data point for anyone worried about entry-level tech jobs.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a small kindness from Massachusetts. At the Urban Air adventure park in Bellingham, seven-year-old Tristan Caron, who has autism, was sitting by himself. A group of teenagers from Milford noticed. One of them, Livy Molinari, asked if she could hold the ball he'd just won, and a game of catch turned into forty minutes of hide-and-seek and slides.
[ava] Tristan's mom, Christina, says her son usually gets left out, and called it life-changing. She tracked the teens down on Facebook to thank them. They say they had no idea he had autism; they just didn't want him sitting alone. And the park plans to invite them all back for a day to celebrate.
> source: CBS Boston and WBZ-TV, "Mass. teens react to mother's praise after befriending boy with autism" (Paul Burton, Oct 18)
