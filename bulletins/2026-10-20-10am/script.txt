## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Reuters/Ipsos poll + Bessent
[ava] In Washington, a new Reuters/Ipsos poll puts President Trump's approval at thirty-one percent, the lowest in that survey's tracking across both of his terms. Democrats lead the generic congressional ballot by twelve points, their widest margin this cycle. Polling averages are less stark, with his approval in the high thirties. And Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said this morning that even a Democratic House could do, in his words, "very little" to stop the administration's agenda.
// Story 2: Fraud whistleblower program
[ava] This afternoon at one-thirty, the president and Vice President Vance plan to unveil a twenty-five-million-dollar fraud whistleblower program, according to a White House official quoted by Fox News. Tipsters could collect up to thirty thousand dollars if their information leads to a fraud conviction or a major enforcement action, with tips going through fraud dot gov. The president also plans to hand a ten-thousand-dollar check to Nick Shirley, the independent journalist whose videos on Minnesota day care centers drew national attention. The White House credits his work with spurring federal probes; critics dispute parts of it.
// Story 3: Visas
[ava] The State Department says it has now revoked more than three hundred thousand visas in the president's second term, up from two hundred fifty thousand at the end of September. It cites crimes, overstays, fraud and security risks.
// Story 4: SCOTUS magnet schools
[ava] And the Supreme Court will hear a challenge to the lottery-based admissions at four magnet middle schools in Montgomery County, Maryland. Mostly Asian-American parents argue the policy looks neutral but was meant to reduce their children's numbers. Lower courts sided with the board, noting Asian-American students are admitted above their share of applicants.

// Story 5: Hormuz mines
[ava] Overseas, the shipping war around Iran escalated again. Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two tankers they call non-compliant hit Iranian mines south of the Strait of Hormuz and caught fire, and that Iran shot down an American drone overnight. ## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 10: Citrini antivenom
[andrew] In A.I., an experiment worth hearing about. Citrini Research, an investment research firm with no drug-development background, published results this morning from working with Anthropic's internal research model to design new proteins against scorpion toxins. Over sixty-four hours, the model spun up more than three hundred sub-agents, designed two hundred eleven candidates with only a handful of human messages, and sent them off to a lab. The two best bound tightly to one toxin in test-tube experiments. The authors are candid: binding is not the same as neutralizing venom, and there are no animal tests yet.
// Story 11: China on DeepSeek ban
[andrew] And China is hitting back at the U.S. order removing DeepSeek from federal civilian agencies. Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning says it ignores the understanding reached by Presidents Xi and Trump and politicizes technology, and that Beijing will defend Chinese companies. U.S. officials say the point is keeping Chinese-controlled software out of government systems.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 12: Ahold Delhaize / Hannaford
[andrew] Cyber desk, with a local angle. If you shop at Hannaford, its website, app and To Go pickup and delivery are still down about a week after a cyberattack on parent company Ahold Delhaize, which also owns Stop and Shop. Stores are open, and Hannaford is pointing online shoppers to Instacart, DoorDash or Uber Eats. Ahold says U.S. online services should be back in the coming days, and so far it has found no evidence customer data was stolen, though the investigation continues.
// Story 13: OVHcloud CLI
[andrew] And for developers: OVHcloud says an attacker hijacked a developer's GitHub account and slipped info-stealing malware into versions zero point eleven through zero point fifteen of its command-line tool on Monday. Version zero point sixteen is clean. If you installed a bad version, clean the machine and change every secret stored on it.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a friendship that started with a cotton swab. David Nichols was living in New York in twenty twenty when he was diagnosed with lymphoblastic lymphoma and needed a stem cell transplant from an anonymous donor. Five years later, back home in Norfolk, England, and feeling ready, he wrote to that donor: Tobias Killie, from Germany. Killie's wife wrote back that he was overwhelmed, and arranged a meeting. They bonded over football, watched Norwich City together this weekend, and they now say they're like family. Today Nichols is urging people to join the donor register; it takes a few painless mouth swabs.
