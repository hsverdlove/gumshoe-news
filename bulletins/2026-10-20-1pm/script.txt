// Story 2: Florida execution
[ava] In Florida, the state plans to execute seventy-seven-year-old William Reaves at six this evening for the nineteen eighty-six shooting death of Indian River County sheriff's deputy Richard Raczkowski. The Florida Supreme Court rejected his final appeals last week, and a last request was pending at the U.S. Supreme Court as of this morning. It would be Florida's eighteenth execution this year, one short of last year's state record. Governor DeSantis says he's bringing justice to families who have waited decades. Reaves' lawyers argue jurors were never told the V.A. rates him fully disabled by post-traumatic stress from Vietnam, and more than a hundred veterans asked the governor to stop it.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Mediation, Hormuz
[ava] Overseas, a possible opening in the Iran standoff. The New Arab, citing unnamed sources, reports that Qatar and Pakistan delivered a new compromise proposal to Tehran and Washington today, carried to Iran by Pakistan's army chief, Asim Munir. As described, Iran would allow an unannounced visit by U.N. nuclear inspectors, and in return could collect the interest on six billion dollars of its money frozen in Qatar. The sources say both sides reacted positively, but neither government has confirmed the details, and the core gap remains: Washington wants Iran's nuclear program dismantled, Tehran wants all its frozen assets released. Meanwhile, U.S. Central Command calls Iran's claim that mines set two tankers ablaze false, saying it has cleared the international shipping lanes of mines. Iran's Revolutionary Guards are now threatening undersea internet cables in the Gulf.
// Story 5: Abyei
[ava] In Africa, a drone strike on a market in Abyei, the region disputed by Sudan and South Sudan, hit a fuel depot and killed at least one hundred fifty-three people on Monday, local officials told the A.P.; a South Sudanese radio station now puts the toll above two hundred twenty. Abyei's administration blames Sudan's military. That hasn't been independently verified, and Khartoum hasn't responded.

And in Yemen, officials say the toll from a Houthi missile strike on a government army camp has risen to seventy-three, while the U.N. human rights chief warns the Houthis' encirclement of Taiz puts civilians at grave risk.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: Anthropic draft bill
[andrew] In A.I., Anthropic is on the defensive over its own draft safety bill, which Politico published Friday. The draft would let the Commerce Department ask a federal court to block a dangerous model, but only for imminent risks above high thresholds, such as more than a billion dollars in damage or more than fifty deaths, and it would override some state A.I. rules. Safety advocates and California State Senator Scott Wiener say that's weaker than the bipartisan FRONTIER Act already in Congress. From the other side, former White House A.I. adviser David Sacks says it would sweep in too many startups. Anthropic's federal affairs lead says the preemption language was "not precise enough" and will be rewritten, and that proposals on independent evaluators are coming.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a kindness that kept going. In Maricopa, Arizona, eighteen-year-old Jordan Hope noticed an older man with a walker struggling outside a Walgreens, and gave him the size-thirteen slides off his own feet. A witness posted the moment online. What the camera didn't catch: Hope went into the store with him, helped him pick up a prescription, bought him a bag of peanut M&Ms, called him a ride and followed it home to make sure he got inside. The man, whom he calls Mr. Roy, is eighty and recently lost his wife. Now the two are friends, with plans for meals and church, and a fundraiser Hope started for Mr. Roy's groceries and medicine has passed nineteen thousand dollars.
