## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Whistleblower program, what was announced
[ava] We promised you what the White House actually announced at one-thirty. It's called the Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program, a twenty-five-million-dollar fund run by the Justice Department. Anyone can send a tip through fraud dot gov, and if it leads to a fraud conviction or a significant federal enforcement action, the written program pays up to thirty thousand dollars. A tip alone doesn't qualify.
[ava] In the Oval Office, though, President Trump described something bigger: a one percent commission on the fraud uncovered. He also said he'd personally act as the binding arbiter when several people claim the same reward, and promised to be "very generous." So the president's description and the written terms don't match yet. The first award, ten thousand dollars, went to YouTuber Nick Shirley, whose video last year alleged fraud at Minneapolis day cares. Supporters point to real fraud there, including a guilty plea in July from the owner of a day care in his video. Critics say the video fueled anti-Somali posts, and question a president personally deciding who gets paid.
// Story 2: Reaves
[ava] Following up on Florida: the U.S. Supreme Court rejected William Reaves' final appeal this afternoon without comment. His execution for the nineteen eighty-six killing of Deputy Richard Raczkowski remains set for six o'clock, which would make it Florida's eighteenth this year.
// Story 3: Measles, campaign money
[ava] In health news, the C.D.C. has confirmed a third measles death in the U.S. this year, with cases now past forty-four hundred. The federal count runs behind some states; Pennsylvania alone reports five deaths.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Iran
[ava] Overseas, the Iran mediation shuttle kept moving, but the positions look as far apart as ever. Pakistan's army chief, Asim Munir, met President Pezeshkian, parliament speaker Qalibaf and security chief Mohsen Rezaei, and as he left, a Qatari delegation arrived. Rezaei said none of Iran's enriched nuclear material will leave the country, a core U.S. demand. Qatar says contact with Washington continues, but warned that Iranian threats to cut undersea internet cables would hurt the whole region. Treasury Secretary Bessent says economic pressure is working, with oil loadings at Iran's Kharg Island export terminal halted. And Iran's Revolutionary Guards now claim a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels exploded after hitting a mine south of Hormuz. That's unverified, and the U.S. called earlier mine claims today false.
// Story 5: Ukraine
[ava] On Ukraine, U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner wrapped up talks in Oslo with President Zelenskyy and Nordic leaders on security guarantees; Witkoff takes the proposals to St. Petersburg tomorrow. Zelenskyy says U.S. early warnings of Russian missile attacks are working again after a call with Secretary of State Rubio. Rubio says there was never a directive to withhold them, and that missed warnings on October thirteenth and eighteenth were a technical problem; Democratic Congressman Eugene Vindman calls that false. President Trump, for his part, says Russia keeps grinding forward and Zelenskyy should make a deal.
// Story 6: North Korea
[ava] And North Korean state media released photos of Kim Jong Un touring a Pyongyang exhibition with his wife and daughter, after viral posts claimed he'd had a brain hemorrhage and died. Seoul says nothing backed those rumors, though the photos themselves haven't been independently dated.

[andrew] Tech desk. Google Chrome is adding a second, softer tier of website warnings. ## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: Anthropic draft prospectus
[andrew] In A.I., a story Kimmonismus flagged on your X feed: the Wall Street Journal has seen a draft of Anthropic's I.P.O. prospectus. It shows revenue of sixteen point three billion dollars in the first half of this year, up from one point one billion a year earlier. It spent nearly nine billion on computing in those six months, and has roughly four hundred fifty billion dollars in cloud commitments over the next decade with Google, Amazon and Microsoft that it largely can't cancel. The reported net loss is eighty point seven billion, but seventy-two billion of that is a non-cash accounting charge tied to its financing. Bulls see the fastest revenue growth in tech; skeptics point out that about a fifth of revenue came from just two customers, and that the filing warns damages from a catastrophic A.I. incident could exceed its insurance. The draft is confidential, and Anthropic hasn't commented.
> source: Wall Street Journal via Investing.com, "Anthropic revenue hits $16.3 billion as preliminary S-1 outlines IPO risks" (Luke Juricic, Oct 20); Reuters via InvestmentNews, Anthropic prospectus (Sep 28-29); @kimmonismus on X (Oct 20)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: Palo Alto GlobalProtect
[andrew] Cyber desk. If your company's remote access runs through Palo Alto Networks' GlobalProtect, make sure it's patched. Palo Alto's Unit Forty-Two says the Qilin and INC ransomware gangs are exploiting an authentication bypass, C.V.E. twenty twenty-six dash zero two five seven, to open unauthorized V.P.N. sessions, then jumping to VMware servers to steal credentials.
// Story 11: OVHcloud, Muse links
[andrew] French cloud provider OVHcloud says a hijacked developer account was used to plant info-stealing malware in versions zero point eleven through zero point fifteen of its command-line tool. Anyone who ran one should treat every secret on that machine as stolen and update to version zero point sixteen. And Meta has paused sharing in its Muse A.I. app after security firm Claroc found more than sixty-eight hundred share links discoverable through public internet records, some exposing business finances and a loan application.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a band that brought the show to its biggest fan. Four-year-old Remi Floro of Port Clinton, Ohio, loves Twenty One Pilots, and he and his mom had tickets for the band's hometown show at Ohio Stadium on Saturday. Then his leukemia relapsed, a bone marrow transplant was scheduled two days before the concert, and they had to sell the tickets. Word reached Josh Dun and Tyler Joseph, who surprised Remi at Nationwide Children's Hospital, each staying about an hour and a half. Remi gave Dun a necklace with his name on it, and on Saturday, at the biggest U.S. headline show of the band's career, Dun wore it on stage. Fans have been sending friendship bracelets and concert confetti to Remi's hospital room ever since.
> source: WBNS via abc10.com, "Twenty One Pilots honor 4-year-old superfan fighting cancer in biggest US show of their career" (Carla Rogner, Oct 20)
