## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Harvest Fuel Relief
[ava] We start with farm country. The Agriculture Department announced an eight-billion-dollar Harvest Fuel Relief Program, one-time payments to help farmers with the soaring cost of diesel and other supplies this harvest. President Trump unveiled it at his Kansas rally last night. Payments will follow a formula based on planted acres and government cost and price estimates, not farmers' actual fuel bills, and rates for the major crops are due in November. Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins says it brings certainty to farmers at harvest. Skeptics note it lands two weeks before the midterms, and that fuel prices are high largely because of the war with Iran.
// Story 2: Golf course hearing
[ava] In Washington, the East Potomac golf course hearing ran seven hours. Judge Ana Reyes signaled she'll likely extend her order blocking more tree cutting, saying she doesn't buy the government's argument that the felling of over four hundred trees was unrelated to the president's renovation plan. Government lawyers told her there's no plan to close the course, and the acting Park Service director testified she first learned of a closure from the president's social media post. President Trump calls the course old and tired and says Interior can renovate it without approval.
// Story 3: $5,000 checks
[ava] On the campaign trail, the Associated Press finds even some Trump supporters doubt the five-thousand-dollar checks he's promised every adult citizen if Republicans keep Congress. The president says the payments will happen. Congress would have to approve the roughly one-point-two-trillion-dollar cost, and a Treasury official has acknowledged there's no funding plan yet.

## International | sting=world
// Story 5: Houthis vs Saudi Arabia
[ava] Overseas, the war between Yemen's Houthi rebels and Saudi Arabia is widening. The Iran-backed Houthis claim six attacks in two days on Saudi targets, including Aramco oil facilities at Yanbu, the Shaybah oil field and the main airport in Riyadh. Saudi Arabia hasn't commented, and the damage claims aren't independently confirmed. The Houthis say they're retaliating for more than two hundred Saudi air strikes. The kingdom has now postponed its big Future Investment Initiative conference to next year. And Bloomberg reports Britain will send Royal Air Force Typhoon jets to help defend Saudi cities and infrastructure; the British defense ministry hasn't confirmed.
// Story 6: Reuters on Iran's strategy
[ava] On Iran itself, Reuters quotes three senior Iranian officials who say Tehran is deliberately dragging out talks with Washington to deny President Trump a win before the midterms, and is preparing for renewed attacks rather than a deal. The White House didn't respond. The president has said the U.S. won't bomb Iran again before November third, and that oil prices will fall after the election.
// Story 7: Ukraine
[ava] In Ukraine, Russia launched another overnight barrage of drones and missiles. Officials say at least seven people were killed, including three at a warehouse near Kyiv, and power went out in parts of twelve regions. Today President Zelenskyy is in Oslo meeting U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff and Nordic leaders, with Jared Kushner joining by video, ahead of a planned Witkoff trip to Moscow. Zelenskyy accuses Washington of holding up Patriot interceptors that allies paid for. The administration says its recent moves are meant to keep Russia at the bargaining table.
// Story 8: France, Alberta
[ava] In France, Paris had a calmer night under its first curfew, and the prime minister says the restrictions helped. Local officials decide tonight whether to renew them. A suspect has been arrested over the fire at the city hall in Limoges, according to French media.
[ava] And in Canada, Alberta voters rejected starting down the road to independence, about seventy-six percent to twenty-four with most votes counted. Premier Danielle Smith welcomed the result.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 13: AOMEI
[andrew] Cyber desk. If your company uses AOMEI Backupper, Google's Mandiant and ManageEngine say attackers secretly tampered with AOMEI's own update servers between May and July, for windows of just five to ten minutes, to push a backdoor to computers checking for updates. Victims were found in the U.S. and five other countries. AOMEI says its C.T.O. is investigating. Researchers stress that securing the servers doesn't clean machines that already got the malware, so check the published indicators.
// Story 14: Rank Math and briefs
[andrew] And WordPress site owners: Rank Math PRO version three point oh point one twenty-three, downloaded during an eleven-hour window from last Thursday afternoon into Friday morning, carried a self-restoring backdoor. The free version isn't affected. Rank Math says to restore a clean backup, change all passwords, and update to the fixed release.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a teenager who was ready when it counted. Fifteen-year-old Ethan, from Mansfield Woodhouse in England, has wanted to be a paramedic from a young age, and he'd just bought three CPR practice dummies so he could teach others. Two weeks later, walking home from a gardening job, he found a seventy-five-year-old man collapsed in the road in cardiac arrest. Ethan took charge of chest compressions, with an off-duty carer and a teacher taking turns, until paramedics arrived with a defibrillator. He's now recovering, and his family says Ethan helped save his life. Ethan is already running free CPR classes for scout groups, and his head teacher wants more pupils trained.
> source: BBC News, "My dream of being a paramedic helped me save a life at 15" (Rebecca Brice, Oct 19)
