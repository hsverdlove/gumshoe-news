## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Marriage fraud ring, charges
[ava] We now know who's charged in that Massachusetts marriage-fraud case. U.S. Attorney Leah Foley says six women, five from Fall River and one from Attleboro, are charged with marriage fraud and harboring. Prosecutors say the alleged ringleader, forty-three-year-old Jennifer Gonzalez, a U.S. citizen, charged thirty-eight to forty thousand dollars per sham marriage, arranged eighteen to twenty a year for about twelve years, and invested the money in land in the Dominican Republic. Most of the men who paid were Brazilian nationals seeking green cards, officials say. Prosecutors also allege she collected about a hundred twenty-four thousand dollars in food-stamp benefits without reporting the income. Five of the six are in custody, the investigation is ongoing, and all are presumed innocent.
// Story 2: SCOTUS transgender inmate policy
[ava] At the Supreme Court, the justices turned down the Trump administration's emergency request to enforce a Bureau of Prisons policy ending hormone therapy and other gender-transition care for transgender federal inmates. A lower-court block stays in place while the case goes on. The order gave no reasoning; Justices Thomas and Alito would have sided with the administration. The government argues the care is medically questionable and creates safety and fairness problems in prisons. The A.C.L.U., which represents about a thousand inmates diagnosed with gender dysphoria, says the prisons have provided hormone therapy since nineteen ninety-two and changed course without evidence.
// Story 3: Wedgewood fake polls
[ava] And a polling scandal ahead of the midterms. A firm called Wedgewood Polls has admitted that most of its published surveys were fabricated, after the data site FiftyPlusOne found its raw data didn't add up. Wedgewood calls it an experiment to show how easily a fake pollster can win credibility. The New York Times pulled fifteen of its polls and Decision Desk H.Q. pulled seventeen. Its numbers had boosted progressive candidates, including Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan's Senate race. Democrat Mallory McMorrow, who lost that primary, says the polls dried up her donations. Commentators on the right are using it to question polling in general; pollsters reply that the vetting process is what caught it.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Iran standoff
[ava] Overseas, the U.S.-Iran standoff has hardened. Secretary of State Marco Rubio says a peace deal will be extremely difficult and that economic pressure continues until Iran stops targeting commercial shipping. Tehran says the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until Washington accepts its conditions, including easing the naval blockade, and that enriched nuclear material won't leave the country. // Story 5: Macron and the riots
[ava] In France, President Macron accused foreign leaders of exploiting last weekend's riots for their own elections. Without naming them, he appeared to aim at Israel's Prime Minister Netanyahu, who called it an invasion of extremist Islam, and President Trump, who blamed mass immigration. Macron says the riots grew out of student and cost-of-living protests, blames ultra-left agitators, and says some leaders were misled by fake A.I. videos. The B.B.C. has traced a number of fake news clips to a Russian disinformation operation. Critics counter that Macron is dodging real tensions over integration. Nearly a hundred fifty people have been jailed, and troops will back police for Saturday's protests.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: SafePay advisory
[andrew] Cyber desk. C.I.S.A., the F.B.I., the Secret Service and British and German partners are warning about the SafePay ransomware gang, which they say has hit at least six hundred critical-infrastructure organizations since August twenty twenty-four. SafePay gets in through stolen or guessed passwords, phishing, and holes in V.P.N.s and firewalls, then deletes backups, encrypts systems and threatens to leak stolen data. The advice is familiar but urgent: multifactor login everywhere, patch internet-facing gear first, limit admin rights, and keep offline backups. C.I.S.A. also added six actively exploited flaws to its must-patch list, including four chained bugs in Plex Media Server, so if you run Plex at home, update it.
> source: CISA, "#StopRansomware: SafePay Ransomware" (AA26-293A); BleepingComputer, "CISA: SafePay ransomware hit over 600 critical infrastructure orgs" (Sergiu Gatlan, Oct 21); CISA, "CISA Adds Six Known Exploited Vulnerabilities to Catalog" (Oct 20)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a swim for the record books. Thirty-three-year-old Catherine Breed of the Bay Area has become the first person to swim the length of California, about nine hundred miles from Oregon to Mexico, over a hundred eleven days. She lived aboard a fifty-two-foot sailboat, swam through the edges of tropical storms and hurricanes, and sometimes swam stretches backward, south to north, to dodge the currents. Sharks were spotted, but far off. Dolphins and sea lions kept her company. Would she recommend it? "Absolutely not," she laughed. Her advice instead: just get in the ocean, however you're comfortable. The next day, she went surfing.
> source: Los Angeles Times, "California woman makes history by swimming state's 900-mile length" (Iris Kwok, Oct 21)
