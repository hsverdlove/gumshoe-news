## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Toledo chemical leak
[ava] In Ohio, about a thousand people in north Toledo are out of their homes after a chemical leak at an industrial site on Progress Avenue. City officials say the chemical is glycidyl methacrylate, used to make plastics and coatings, and that it escaped during a transfer error. There's a one-mile evacuation zone and a three-mile shelter-in-place zone covering more than fifty thousand people, who are being told to stay inside, seal windows and doors, and shut off outside air. The E.P.A. is monitoring the air.
// Story 2: Musk and the Senate
[ava] In Washington, Senate Democrats led by Adam Schiff want Elon Musk to testify under oath, after the Wall Street Journal reported he personally ordered the slowdown of Starshield, the SpaceX military satellite service Ukraine's forces rely on. Schiff says either the President or Musk hasn't told the full story; Musk had suggested any limits came from the White House, and President Trump denies that. Republicans control the Senate, so Democrats can't force a hearing on their own, and Musk's supporters call the push political targeting ahead of the midterms.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Gulf embassy departures
[ava] Overseas, Reuters reports the State Department is preparing to order some staff and families out of U.S. embassies across the Gulf, citing three U.S. officials. Two of them say it could mean evacuating everyone from the U.S. mission in Saudi Arabia. The worry is a fresh round of Houthi attacks; the U.S. embassy in Riyadh issued a new alert today after recent deadly strikes, including on Riyadh's airport. The order hasn't been issued yet, and the State Department hasn't confirmed the plan.
// Story 4: Zelensky on a freeze
[ava] On Ukraine, President Zelensky told Fox News that freezing the fighting along the current front line is, in his words, the real way to end this war, as a first step toward a ceasefire. He says Kyiv must be at the table and wants security guarantees from Washington and Europe. Critics say a freeze would leave Russia holding the land it has taken, and it's not clear Moscow would agree. Zelensky also says Ukraine's satellite service is still limited, though Secretary Rubio told him he'd work to unblock it.
// Story 5: El Salvador in Haiti
[ava] In Haiti, El Salvador has launched a military operation to rescue four of its aviation technicians, part of the U.N.-backed gang suppression force, who went missing after their armored vehicle was ambushed Sunday outside Port-au-Prince. President Bukele, in a message translated into Haitian Creole, told the gangs the men are medevac crew, not combatants, and warned that today's operation is nothing compared with what's coming if they aren't returned unharmed. The force says several suspected gang members were killed. The four have not been found.
> source: Reuters via Al-Monitor and The Straits Times, "US preparing to issue ordered departure of some personnel from Gulf embassies" (Oct 21); ABC News (Oct 21); Euronews, "Zelenskyy backs freezing front line as first step towards Ukraine ceasefire" (Simon Ormiston, Oct 21); Infobae (Thania Urías, Oct 21); Noticias SIN (Oct 21)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Meta $25B
[andrew] Tech. A New Mexico judge has ordered Meta to pay twenty-five billion dollars, the biggest penalty the company has ever faced. A jury found last month that Facebook misled users in the state about who could reach their data, in a case that grew out of the Cambridge Analytica scandal, and about how it handles harmful content. Judge Francis Mathew said state law would have allowed more than two hundred nineteen billion, but cut it to twenty-five, with fifteen percent yearly interest until paid. The state attorney general calls it a warning to Big Tech. Meta has argued a penalty that size violates its constitutional protections against excessive fines, and an appeal is expected.
// Story 8: BioWare closes
[andrew] And a sad day for gamers. Electronic Arts is closing BioWare, the studio behind Mass Effect, Dragon Age and Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, after thirty years, and canceling the next Mass Effect. It's part of about four hundred job cuts reported by Bloomberg, after E.A.'s fifty-five-billion-dollar sale to investors led by Saudi Arabia's sovereign fund. E.A. says it's betting on fewer, bigger games like Marvel's Iron Man and the next Star Wars Jedi.
> source: NBC News / Reuters, "Meta ordered to pay $25 billion to New Mexico" (Oct 21); CNBC (Jonathan Vanian, Oct 21); AP (Oct 21); The Verge, "EA is shutting down BioWare" (Jay Peters, Oct 21); BioWare Blog, "BioWare Update" (Oct 21); Variety (Oct 21)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: Claude Duet
[andrew] In A.I., Anthropic has joined the race for always-on assistants. It's opening a research preview of Claude Duet, a separate app you message like a person. ## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: White House cybercrime roundtable
[andrew] Cyber desk. President Trump held a White House roundtable this afternoon on scams run from overseas, with the attorney general, the F.B.I. director and the Homeland Security and Defense secretaries. The administration says its Homeland Security Task Force has frozen about eight hundred thirty-three million dollars in crypto tied to foreign scam centers, seized five hundred three fake crypto-investment websites, and warned nearly nine thousand Americans who were actively being scammed. The President put the losses prevented this year at one point six billion dollars or more. Critics point to a Reuters investigation finding the Trump family has made at least two point three billion dollars from crypto ventures, and say that's a conflict as the government shapes crypto enforcement. The White House says every action is in the public's interest.

## Local News & Events | sting=local
[ava] Closer to home, some relief on heating oil. The Energy Department will loan out up to three hundred fifteen thousand barrels, about a third of the Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve, from storage in South Portland, Boston and New Haven, with deliveries by November thirteenth. Companies have to return the oil later, plus a premium. Maine's Senators Collins and King, who pushed for it, say it adds about thirteen percent to New England's supply; a Maine state study found heating oil prices up seventy-four percent in a year. The reserve has only been tapped twice in its twenty-six years, and never before just to lower prices.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, the right person in the right place. Peter Jones cleans the beach in Sheerness, on England's Kent coast. He was driving his seafront truck along the promenade when he saw a man, one of the regulars, collapse while chatting with two women who didn't know what to do. Jones jumped out and started C.P.R., and kept it up for ten minutes until the ambulance came. Paramedics got the man's heart going again after three shocks from a defibrillator, and he's made a full recovery. He's back riding his bike along the seafront, and he's thanked the man who saved him. Now Jones is getting a national lifesaving award from the Royal Humane Society.
> source: BBC News, "Beach cleaner honoured for saving man's life" (Daniel Sexton, Oct 21)
