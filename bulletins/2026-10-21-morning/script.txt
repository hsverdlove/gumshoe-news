## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Reaves executed
[ava] We start with a follow-up. Florida executed seventy-seven-year-old William Reaves last night. He was pronounced dead at six-twelve p.m. at Florida State Prison, for the nineteen eighty-six killing of Indian River County Deputy Richard Raczkowski. It's Florida's eighteenth execution this year, one short of last year's state record, with another scheduled for November. The sheriff called it justice for the deputy's family; more than a hundred military veterans had asked Governor DeSantis for mercy, noting the V.A. rated Reaves, a Vietnam veteran, one hundred percent disabled by P.T.S.D., something his jurors were never told.
// Story 2: Starlink
[ava] On Ukraine's satellite internet: the Wall Street Journal reported Tuesday that Elon Musk ordered a slowdown of the military-grade Starlink service Ukraine relies on against Russian missiles, and that Musk and President Trump discussed it. Speaking in India this morning, Secretary of State Rubio said he wasn't aware of the decision beforehand, and that access is governed by a private contract and is up to SpaceX. That's a shift from Saturday, when he said nobody ordered a slowdown. Reuters, citing U.S. and Western officials, says the decision ultimately came from the White House, which the administration denies. Musk and SpaceX haven't commented.
// Story 3: Data center dividend
[ava] President Trump, on Truth Social, said companies that want data centers to be popular should pay a "dividend" to the community or to residents, saying projects have flipped from unpopular to popular once a financial deal is struck. Opposition to data centers crosses party lines, including here in New Hampshire. Skeptics say cash payments don't address the core worries about power bills, water and noise.
// Story 4: NBA opening night
[ava] And the N.B.A. is back. The Pistons erased a twenty-two-point deficit to beat the Celtics one twenty-one to one thirteen, despite thirty-eight from Paul George in his Boston debut. And the Knicks raised their first title banner in fifty-three years, then lost to LeBron James and the Sixers, one oh six to one hundred.

## International | sting=world
// Story 5: Hubbard, Witkoff-Putin
[ava] Overseas, Russia has freed an American. Seventy-four-year-old Stephen Hubbard, a retired Michigan teacher living in Ukraine, was captured in twenty twenty-two and sentenced to nearly seven years on mercenary charges, which he and the U.S. deny. He was the last American in Russia the State Department had formally declared wrongfully detained. His release came as envoy Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin in St. Petersburg this morning. Putin said Hubbard was already at the airport, and Witkoff thanked him, saying, "Because of you, he's going home to his family." CBS says he's in poor health and headed to a military hospital in Texas.
[ava] Witkoff handed Putin papers he described as ideas for elements of a peace deal, after Ukraine brought changes to the draft framework in Oslo. Putin suggested he and President Trump could meet in China. He said the first Russian diesel tanker for the U.S. sails within days. Supporters of the outreach call this momentum. Critics, including many in Kyiv and Europe, see Washington tilting toward Moscow, noting the diesel deal and waived sanctions, while Russia hasn't budged on its demand for all of the Donbas region.
// Story 6: Iran
[ava] On Iran, Foreign Minister Araghchi says talks with Washington continue through Pakistan and Qatar. He says Iran has offered a seven-day plan under which the Strait of Hormuz would fully reopen on day six, if the U.S. meets its conditions, reportedly including ending the blockade and easing oil sanctions. Secretary Rubio says the U.S. welcomes mediation but won't accept any deal leaving Iran a path to a nuclear weapon. Reports, not yet confirmed by the Pentagon, say a third U.S. aircraft carrier is heading to the region.
// Story 7: Climate
[ava] And a new U.N. report says the world is losing ground on climate. Under current policies, the Earth is on track for about two point eight degrees Celsius of warming this century. U.S. emissions rose the most of any major economy last year, driven by electricity demand and a rebound in coal power. The administration argues affordable, reliable power has to come first.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, meet Olly, a hedgehog with no prickles. Olly caught ringworm in the wild in Devon, England, and lost every one of his spines. For four months he's been at a rescue called Prickles in a Pickle, getting antifungal baths and moisturizing creams; his vet calls them spa treatments. One person said he looks like a rolled-up sock, and his rescuer says he's more like a very large potato, about two pounds. Now he's the charity's "poster hog." A healthy hedgehog has about seven thousand spines, so they're raising seven thousand pounds, about one pound per missing prickle. He can't go back to the wild without them, but as his rescuer puts it, his only real problem is that he hasn't got any clothes on.
> source: BBC News, "Hedgehog without prickles made charity ambassador" (Chris Baker and Hattie McCann, Oct 20)
