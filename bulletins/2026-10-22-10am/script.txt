## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Louise Lucas indictment
[ava] Breaking this morning: one of Virginia's most powerful Democrats, State Senate President pro tempore L. Louise Lucas, has surrendered to federal authorities. An indictment accuses the eighty-two-year-old of leading a scheme since at least twenty nineteen to sell more than a hundred kilograms of illegal marijuana through the medical cannabis shop she co-owns in Portsmouth, and of using her office to shield it. Six others were charged, including two daughters and a grandson. Attorney General Todd Blanche says she put her own finances ahead of her constituents. The investigation began under the Biden administration, but critics, including Virginia Congressman Suhas Subramanyam, call the timing, twelve days before the midterms, political. Lucas, a vocal Trump critic, hasn't commented yet.
// Story 2: AP-NORC priorities poll
[ava] A new A.P.-NORC poll finds voters in both parties want the next Congress focused on everyday problems. About eight in ten registered voters say lowering the cost of living should be a top priority, and about seven in ten say ending the war with Iran. Only about four in ten put investigating the administration at the top, and about two in ten prioritize advancing President Trump's agenda, including just four in ten Republicans.
// Story 3: Big Bend wall
[ava] The administration has asked the Supreme Court to let it resume border wall construction in Texas's Big Bend region, including parts of the national park. A federal judge in El Paso paused the work, finding the area has the lowest rate of illegal crossings on the border and that Homeland Security likely lacked authority to waive environmental laws there. Solicitor General John Sauer calls that order baseless.
// Story 4: New Mexico Epstein suit
[ava] And New Mexico's attorney general has sued Jeffrey Epstein's longtime lawyer Darren Indyke and accountant Richard Kahn, accusing them of helping conceal abuse at Epstein's Zorro Ranch and seeking to block their payouts from his estate. Both have told Congress they knew nothing of it.

## International | sting=world
// Story 5: Aramco HQ evacuation
[ava] Overseas, Saudi Aramco has told the remaining essential staff at its Dhahran headquarters to evacuate and work from home, Reuters and A.F.P. report, citing people familiar with it. It's the first full evacuation of the oil giant's headquarters since the Iran war began. Bloomberg says it followed an alarm in the building; the cause isn't clear, and Aramco hasn't commented. Houthi fire damaged a tower there earlier this month, and Dhahran is only about nine miles from the Dammam airport hit last night. Sources say production is unaffected.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 10: AI agents in bank accounts
[andrew] In A.I., Axios reports big U.S. banks are finding unvetted A.I. agents logging into customer accounts with the customers' own passwords. Some have called customer service lines or tried things users never asked for. The core problem, as Andrew Curran put it on your feed, is that banks can't tell whether the customer authorized the agent. Banks are tightening their terms and favoring read-only access; a Morgan Stanley spokesperson said it hasn't seen rogue agents, and Reuters couldn't independently verify the report.
// Story 11: Flapping Airplanes
[andrew] And a year-old lab called Flapping Airplanes raised one billion dollars at a six billion dollar valuation, led by Index Ventures and Lux, with Sequoia and Nvidia in. Its pitch is efficiency: training strong models with far less data and computing power.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 12: LiteLLM
[ava] Cyber desk. If you or your developers use LiteLLM, the popular tool for routing requests to A.I. models, check your version. Attackers hijacked its release pipeline and published a poisoned version, one point one oh seven point oh, to the Python package index and Docker for about two hours yesterday. It steals credentials and API keys. The fix: pin version one point one oh six point two, rebuild, and rotate every key that machine could reach.
// Story 13: eMudhra
[ava] India's eMudhra is quitting the global website-certificate business after a researcher found weak, guessable private keys in eight of its certificate authorities. Google, Mozilla, Apple and Microsoft have already blocked them. The company says nothing was breached.
// Story 14: Capcom
[ava] And game maker Capcom says hackers accessed more than eleven million Capcom I.D. accounts, mostly email addresses. No passwords or card numbers leaked, but watch for phishing.
> source: LiteLLM, "Oct 21, 2026 LiteLLM release v1.107.0 Compromised" (Oct 21); Datadog Security Labs (Oct 2026); Reuters via Yahoo Finance, "Indian firm eMudhra exits global digital certificates business" (Oct 22); eMudhra statement (Oct 2026); Capcom, "Notice and Apology Regarding Customer Information" (Oct 22); The Verge (Oct 22)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a reporter who became the story. A.B.C. Australia's Will Murray was covering flash floods north of Brisbane this morning, their time, when a driver sped into a flooded road in Morayfield. His four-wheel drive stalled and began to float away, then sink. Murray called emergency services, handed the phone to his camera operator, and swam out. With a passerby, Daniel Hainey, he climbed onto the car, braced his feet and yanked open a rear door, and they pulled the driver out just before the vehicle disappeared. His camera operator says the pair definitely saved the man's life. Murray deflected the hero talk, adding: you can't tell how deep it is, so please don't drive into floodwater.
> source: ABC News (Australia), "ABC reporter and bystander rescue man from sinking car in floodwater near Brisbane" (Oct 22); Al Jazeera (Oct 22)
