## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Lucas court appearance
[ava] We start with Virginia State Senator Louise Lucas. She made her first appearance in federal court in Norfolk this afternoon, alongside family members and others charged in the case. Prosecutors didn't ask to hold her, and she was released on her own recognizance, with conditions that include no contact with co-defendants about the case. Her arraignment is set for November sixteenth in Newport News. N.B.C. News reports the indictment quotes an intercepted call from February in which Lucas allegedly said, I've gotten too old to not be greedy. Lucas hasn't publicly addressed the specific charges, and her supporters call the timing, twelve days before the midterms, political; prosecutors say the case began before this administration.
// Story 2: Housing executive order
[ava] President Trump signed an executive order today aimed at lowering the cost of buying and refinancing a home. It directs the Federal Housing Finance Agency to expand pilots that let some refinances skip title insurance, to cancel mortgage insurance automatically once borrowers have enough equity, and to cut paperwork and appraisal hurdles. It also tells the Agriculture and Veterans Affairs departments to lower rates on their home loans, and sets up a task force on manufactured housing. The White House says it will save buyers money. Semafor reported earlier that some in the administration wanted to slow down, and the title insurance industry warns skipping title coverage could leave owners less protected.
// Story 3: BBC deposition
[ava] And in Miami, a federal magistrate judge ordered President Trump to sit for a deposition by December eleventh in his ten billion dollar defamation suit against the BBC, or face sanctions that could include dismissal. The BBC's lawyer said Trump canceled an agreed October sixteenth date for what was described as a matter of national importance, and was campaigning that day. Trump's side says the deadline can move if the trial schedule does. The suit is over a documentary that spliced parts of his January sixth speech; the BBC apologized for the edit but denies defamation.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Hormuz claims vs Trump
[ava] Overseas, a war of words over the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's Revolutionary Guard says four ships doing oil transfers in the northern Persian Gulf were struck and set on fire, and three more hit mines. None of that has been independently confirmed, and Iran didn't name the ships. President Trump posted that Iran is, quote, making up fake hits, mines and everything else to drive up oil prices before the midterms. The full picture is murky: the British maritime monitor says many attacked ships no longer report incidents, and it logged twelve attacks in the week to October sixteenth, while earlier Iranian mine claims were rejected by U.S. Central Command. Central Command marked a hundred days of its renewed blockade of Iranian ports, which it says has kept twenty-five billion dollars of Iranian oil off the market.
// Story 5: Italy truckers
[ava] In Italy, truckers protesting high fuel prices have disrupted deliveries, leaving some supermarket shelves empty. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's government approved an aid package today for transport, farming and fishing.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Apple Australia
[andrew] Tech. Australia's Federal Court has ordered Apple to allow rival app stores, app downloads from websites, and outside payment options for Australian users starting March first, twenty twenty-seven, following its finding last year that Apple misused its market power. Apple must also let Epic's Fortnite back into the Australian App Store. Apps from outside the store still have to pass a security scan. Apple says it will appeal and ask for a pause, citing security and its intellectual property. Australia joins the European Union, Japan and Brazil in forcing this kind of opening.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Nadella insider threat
[andrew] In A.I., Microsoft C.E.O. Satya Nadella told Semafor that companies should treat A.I. models like potential insider threats, saying alignment seems elusive, and even understanding how the models work is tough. It's a notable admission from one of the biggest sellers of A.I.
// Story 9: Skynet mode agent
[andrew] And security firm Symantec says its honeypot caught an autonomous A.I. agent its creators call Skynet mode. It hunts for A.I. models left open on the internet without passwords, hijacks them, and uses them to scan for victims, steal credentials and plant crypto miners, sticking the owner of the hijacked model with the computing bill. Symantec reports no confirmed real-world victims yet. If you run your own models, lock them down.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: ConnectWise / Storm-0940
[ava] Cyber desk. Microsoft says a Chinese state-aligned group it calls Storm 0940 was behind the breach of ConnectWise's cloud backup service. Using stolen credentials that link ConnectWise to its customers' Microsoft 365 accounts, the hackers, helped by an open-source A.I. hacking tool called ARTEX, tried to get into more than twenty-two hundred organizations, at times nearly three thousand attempts a minute. Most failed, but Microsoft says the group gained lasting privileged access at numerous companies, in some cases in under three hours. It hasn't said how many, or whether data was taken. ConnectWise has paused the affected services and is restoring them in phases. If you use ConnectWise, review app sign-ins and any unexpected permission changes.
> source: Microsoft Security Blog, "AI-driven, large-scale nation-state campaign using ARTEX enables supply chain compromise" (Oct 21); TechCrunch, "Chinese hackers spied on thousands of companies after breaching IT giant ConnectWise" (Zack Whittaker, Oct 22); The Register (Carly Page, Oct 22)
