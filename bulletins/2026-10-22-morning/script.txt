## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: DHS voter-check injunction
[ava] A federal judge in San Francisco has ordered the Department of Homeland Security to pause door-to-door election checks in nine states, including California, New York, Pennsylvania and Georgia. Agents have been asking residents about their immigration status and voting history, part of a push against noncitizen voting. Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín found the operation likely violates the Voting Rights Act's ban on intimidating people who help others vote, and pointed to a high likelihood of errors in the lists agents work from. She stressed that investigating illegal voting is lawful; her order covers visits to people officials know or should suspect are citizens. The administration says it's protecting election integrity, and the Justice Department this week charged dozens of green card holders in California with illegally registering. The order is on hold until five o'clock Friday so the government can appeal.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Saudi ordered departure + attacks
[ava] Overseas, the State Department has now ordered non-emergency staff and their families to leave the U.S. embassy in Riyadh and the consulates in Saudi Arabia. Iran's Revolutionary Guard, meanwhile, claims seven tankers were hit, four set on fire in what it calls illegal oil transfers and three striking mines near the Strait of Hormuz. ## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Fortnite Australia
[andrew] Tech. An Australian federal court has ruled that Apple broke competition law by blocking rival app stores and payment systems on the iPhone, and ordered Fortnite back on the App Store there. Epic says it returns Friday. Apple must also allow rival app stores and payments there by March. Apple says it will seek a stay and appeal, arguing its rules protect users' security and privacy.

[andrew] And Walmart is rolling out the welcome mat for A.I. shopping assistants. You can set spending caps or require approval on every order, and agents never see your card numbers. Amazon, by contrast, has been blocking outside agents.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: ConnectWise / Storm-0940
[ava] Cyber desk. Microsoft says a Chinese state-aligned hacking group it calls Storm-zero-nine-four-zero was behind the breach of ConnectWise's cloud backup service, and that it used an open-source A.I. agent framework to automate the attack. Using stolen ConnectWise app credentials, the group reached more than two thousand two hundred fifty Microsoft three-sixty-five customer accounts, firing thousands of sign-in attempts a minute. Kimmonismus on your feed flagged it as a sign A.I.-driven attacks are now hitting real organizations. Microsoft is telling affected customers to keep the ConnectWise connections disabled and review activity since October fifteenth.
// Story 10: Plex2Shell
[ava] And if you run a Plex media server at home, update it. CISA says attackers are actively chaining four Plex flaws, dubbed Plex2Shell, to take over servers. The fix has been out since August, in version one point four three point three.
