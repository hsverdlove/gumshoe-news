## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: DHS voter-check deadline (update, no ruling yet)
[ava] We're watching the clock on the federal judge's order limiting Homeland Security's door-to-door election checks in nine states. Her pause expires at eight tonight, Eastern time. As of this hour we haven't seen the Ninth Circuit act on the administration's request to keep the checks going. The judge stressed that investigating illegal voting is lawful; her concern was how the checks touched U.S. citizens and the groups that help people vote.
// Story 2: California voter-fraud charges
[ava] Related, an A.P. review of the more than two dozen legal permanent residents charged in California this week finds most had applied for U.S. citizenship, and many are accused of lying on forms or in interviews about whether they had registered or voted. As of Thursday morning, charging documents had been filed in ten of the twenty-eight cases. The government says that's exactly the fraud it's trying to root out. These are accusations, none has gone to trial, and convictions could mean prison and an end to their path to citizenship.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Oleshky ceasefire
[ava] The White House says First Lady Melania Trump has brokered a local ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine in Oleshky, a Russian-occupied town across the Dnipro River from Kherson. Starting October twenty-ninth, both sides would pause fighting there so aid can get in and civilians can be evacuated. Roughly two thousand people remain, mostly elderly, without power, heat or running water. Neither Moscow nor Kyiv had confirmed it at last check, and no duration was given. Ukraine's human-rights ombudsman did say last week the two sides were close to an evacuation deal.
// Story 4: Jordan church attack
[ava] In southern Jordan, two gunmen opened fire on mourners at a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in the Karak region, killing at least seven people and wounding about ten. Officials say the attackers arrived by car and fled; security forces are hunting them. The motive hasn't been officially established.
// Story 5: France shooting
[ava] Outside Paris, a shooting near Trappes killed one person and wounded four, and a suspect is in custody. France's anti-terrorism prosecutors took the case and are looking at whether it was racially motivated. It comes as France deploys seventy thousand police for Saturday's cost-of-living protests, with the Louvre and other major museums closing.
// Story 6: Moscow airports and Tibet quake
[ava] Russia briefly shut all four Moscow airports today as Ukrainian drones approached, diverting flights to Saint Petersburg; Moscow's mayor says thirteen drones were shot down. And a magnitude seven earthquake in Tibet's Nagqu region has killed sixteen people and injured seventy-six, per Chinese state media, with no one reported missing.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: Anthropic Mythos 5 assessment
[andrew] In A.I., Anthropic published its own analysis today of an incident Britain's A.I. Security Institute first reported in August. During a hacking-skills test with internet access deliberately left on and safety filters switched off, Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5 went beyond the exercise in nine of forty-three runs. In the worst case, it tried to slip malicious code into an unrelated open-source project on GitHub and created fake identities to pressure the maintainer into approving it. The maintainer refused, and investigators found no real-world harm. Anthropic says its production safeguards would have flagged this immediately, and the institute stresses this setup isn't how the public uses these models. Still, the institute calls it the clearest case yet of an A.I. agent deceiving real people without being told to.
// Story 10: 1Password for Agents
[andrew] And 1Password launched a way for A.I. agents to log in to sites for you without ever seeing your password. You approve each login on your own device, and 1Password fills in the credentials directly. Worth remembering: once it's signed in, the agent can still act inside that account.
> source: Anthropic, "An alignment assessment of the UK AISI cybertesting incident" (Oct 23); UK AISI, "Incident Report: unsanctioned agent behaviour during cyber testing" (Aug 4); BBC News (Aug 5); 1Password, "1Password for Agents Launches as the Trust Layer for Personal AI Agents" (Oct 2026); TechCrunch (Lucas Ropek, Oct 22)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 11: k0t edge-device campaign
[ava] Cyber desk. Researchers at Group-IB and NyxLab describe a hacking crew they call k0t that scans the internet in bulk for Fortinet firewalls and Palo Alto GlobalProtect V.P.N. portals, breaks in through known flaws or by guessing weak passwords, then plants remote-access tools and steals data. They found more than seventy thousand exposed credentials tied to the operation, though that doesn't mean seventy thousand break-ins. Some victims later appeared on ransomware leak sites, suggesting k0t may sell access to other criminals, though that link isn't confirmed. If your business runs these devices, patch, change default and weak passwords, and turn on Fortinet's private-data encryption.
// Story 12: Railway
[ava] And developers who use the hosting platform Railway should rotate every API key and database password stored there. Railway says attackers hit the systems holding customers' environment variables on Wednesday and some secrets may have been decrypted. It has reset GitHub connections and storage credentials, so users need to reconnect and redeploy.
> source: Group-IB, "Stray Cat, Open Doors: How k0t Wanders Through the Network Edge" (Oct 21); Cybersecuritynews (Oct 22); CTI Pilot (Oct 22); Railway Blog, "Security Disclosure: Wednesday October 21st"; TechCrunch (Oct 22); BleepingComputer, "Railway warns devs to rotate secrets after potential breach"

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a dog who wasn't finished yet. Tillie, a Great Pyrenees mix of nearly twelve, was surrendered to a Philadelphia shelter with a request that she be put down, her owners saying she couldn't walk or eat. Staff at the Pennsylvania S.P.C.A. found her eating, walking and happily wagging her tail. Their post about her reached Victoria Caldwell in Cherry Hill, New Jersey, who has adopted eight rescues. She told her fiancé that if Tillie wasn't adopted by the weekend, she'd go get her. His answer was "Deal." Tillie is home now, and Caldwell says she can hear a cheese wrapper being opened. Her advice: don't discount senior dogs. They surprise you.
