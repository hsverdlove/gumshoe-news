## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: DHS voter-check deadline, still no ruling
[ava] The clock is running down on the federal judge's order limiting Homeland Security's door-to-door election checks in nine states. Her pause ends at eight tonight, Eastern time, and as of midday we still haven't seen the Ninth Circuit rule on the administration's request to keep the checks going. One wrinkle: the order also required the challengers to post a one-hundred-dollar bond by Thursday afternoon, and we haven't seen public confirmation that it was posted. If the order does take effect, it runs until polls close on November third. The government says it only wants to find noncitizens who vote illegally; the challengers say its target lists are error-prone and the visits intimidate citizens.
// Story 2: Fifth Circuit en banc on immigration detention
[ava] Separately, the full Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled eleven to six that the administration can hold immigrants arrested inside the country without a bond hearing for as long as their deportation cases last. Judge Cory Wilson wrote that due process didn't entitle the three men in the case to bond hearings, and the length of their detention raised no constitutional problem. That reverses a July panel ruling that would have required a hearing after ninety days. Supporters call it a win for enforcing immigration law; the dissenters and immigrant advocates warn it allows open-ended detention of people who haven't been convicted of anything. Courts elsewhere have read the Constitution differently, so this could be headed to the Supreme Court.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Kremlin confirms Oleshky ceasefire
[ava] An update on this morning's Oleshky story. The Kremlin now confirms the local ceasefire there. Spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Russian and Ukrainian military officials are working out the dates, that every civilian who wants to leave will be allowed to, and that the evacuation will take at least several days. He credited Melania Trump with a significant role and said it came up in a recent call between Presidents Putin and Trump. The White House says the pause starts October twenty-ninth. We haven't yet seen Kyiv's formal confirmation.
// Story 4: Nairobi building collapse
[ava] In Kenya, a building of up to eighteen floors that was still under construction collapsed in Nairobi's busy Eastleigh district this afternoon local time. Many people are feared trapped. Rescuers pulled one woman out, and the governor says they could hear people calling from inside the rubble. At one point, crews paused the excavators after those trapped warned that the digging was cutting off their air. The governor says the building had approval but no certificate allowing anyone to occupy it, yet shops were operating on its lower floors. Police are looking for the owner, and there's no confirmed casualty count yet.
// Story 5: Hormuz claims vs data
[ava] And in the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's Revolutionary Guard claims traffic by what it calls violating ships has been completely stopped, after it says it hit three tankers Thursday and destroyed another overnight. The shipping data tell a different story. The tanker owners' group Intertanko says transits on the U.S.-protected southern route are averaging more than thirty a day, and it calls many of Iran's claims of ships hit unsupported. Still, attacks are real and rising. The Washington Institute counts twenty-eight attacks in and around the strait this month, a record for the war.
> source: Reuters via Internazionale, "Kremlin confirms Oleshky ceasefire, says working with Ukraine on evacuation timing" (Dmitry Antonov, Oct 23); Anadolu (Oct 23); AP, "Many feared trapped as a tall building collapses in Kenya" (Evelyne Musambi, Brian Inganga, Oct 23); Kenyans.co.ke (Frankline Oduor, Oct 23); Daily Nation (Hilary Kimuyu, Oct 23); Newsquawk, citing Fars (Oct 23); Intertanko, "Advice for Members regarding situation in Gulf Region" (Oct 23); The Straits Times, citing Reuters (Oct 23)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: SpaceX cell towers
[andrew] Tech. Bloomberg reports SpaceX is in talks to get access to at least eighty thousand cell sites, roughly half the towers in the country, as it builds Starlink Mobile into a rival to Verizon, T-Mobile and AT and T. The talks are reportedly with Crown Castle, one of the biggest tower owners. Crown Castle's CEO wouldn't discuss specific customers, saying only that it's talking to all parties. The idea is to pair ground towers with SpaceX's direct-to-phone satellites.

Meta says sites can still signal their preferences through the standard robots file; publishers say that's not enough protection against A.I. scraping.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: OpenAI fires safety researchers
[andrew] In A.I., three OpenAI safety researchers, Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni and Jasmine Wang, tell the Wall Street Journal they were fired after the company found disappearing Signal messages in a chat with Ryan Greenblatt, formerly chief scientist at the safety group Redwood Research. OpenAI reportedly accused Greenblatt of leaking details of its Astra model to the tech site The Information, which he denies. OpenAI says the firings were for breaking clear rules on handling sensitive information, not for raising safety concerns or speaking out. Expect this to fuel the debate over how much room A.I. labs give their own safety staff.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: HPE Aruba patches
[ava] Cyber desk. If you manage business Wi-Fi from H.P.E. Aruba, patch now. H.P.E. fixed seventy-two flaws in the Aruba operating system that runs its access points, controllers and gateways, including eight rated maximum severity that let attackers take over devices remotely without logging in. H.P.E. says it hasn't seen exploit code or attacks yet, and advises keeping management interfaces on a separate, firewalled network.
// Story 10: Arista VeloCloud zero-day
[ava] And Arista has patched a critical flaw in self-hosted VeloCloud Orchestrator, the management portal for its SD-WAN networks, that attackers are already exploiting. Anyone with a basic login can escalate to a super-user operator. It's Arista's fourth exploited zero-day this year. Until you patch, restrict the web interface to admin networks and check your logs.
> source: BleepingComputer, "HPE patches eight max severity RCE and auth bypass AOS flaws" (Sergiu Gatlan, Oct 23); BleepingComputer, "Arista patches new VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day exploited in attacks" (Sergiu Gatlan, Oct 22)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
[ava] Closer to home, Governor Kelly Ayotte and Democratic nominee Cinde Warmington held their second debate Thursday night at the Harpoon Brewery in Manchester, a calmer exchange than their first. On data centers, Ayotte said she lacks the authority to ban them by executive order but promised to get a moratorium done; Warmington said Ayotte signed a twenty twenty-five law that made them easier to build and only shifted when it became convenient. Libertarian Stephen Villee is also on the ballot. They debate twice more next week, Tuesday morning in Nashua and Thursday at nine p.m. on WMUR.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a birthday party with some very special guests. Graham Stockwell of Florence, Kentucky, was born nearly four months early, weighing one pound nine ounces, and his dad says he had less than a ten percent chance of surviving. He spent a year and a day in the hospital. Before he came home, local firefighters visited through a Cincinnati Children's Hospital program that introduces crews to kids with complex medical needs, so they'd know his equipment and history. Two days after he got home, his family called nine-one-one, and those same crews arrived within minutes and helped save his life. This week Graham turned four, free of his breathing tube, talking up a storm, and celebrating with the firefighters. He loves his toy fire trucks, and his dad says he wants to be a firefighter himself.
> source: Hoodline, "Florence Boy Who Had Less Than 10% Survival Odds Turns 4, Reunites With Firefighters Who Saved Him" (Hazel Ramirez, Oct 23), citing WCPO 9 News and NKyTribune
