## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: DHS voter-check order, status check
[ava] A quick status check on the order limiting Homeland Security's door-to-door election checks in nine states. As of about four-fifteen, we have not seen any Ninth Circuit ruling on the administration's request to keep the checks going, so the judge's order is set to take effect at eight tonight, Eastern time. We'll tell you if that changes.
// Story 2: Alaska Senate lawsuit
[ava] In Alaska, the Justice Department has sued to stop the state from certifying the U.S. Senate race if Republican Senator Dan Sullivan loses to Democrat Mary Peltola while a second candidate, Daniel J. Sullivan, stays on the ballot. President Trump said today he ordered the suit. The department argues the second Sullivan is a sham candidate meant to confuse voters: it notes he filed as "Dan Sullivan," switched to Republican, and told The New York Times he voted for Peltola. Alaska's election office concluded in June that his filing was meant to confuse voters, but state courts ordered him onto the ballot. Supporters say it protects voters from being misled; critics warn it could set up a long federal-state fight over certifying an election.
// Story 3: Ex-FBI officials charged
[ava] And two former FBI intelligence officials, Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides, who worked on foreign election interference, were charged today with lying to Congress. Prosecutors say that in twenty twenty-three they misled the House Judiciary Committee about their task force's role in investigations tied to Hunter Biden and his laptop. Both surrendered and appeared in federal court in Washington. Floris's lawyers call the case nonsense. Republican Senator Chuck Grassley praised the charges; critics see it as the latest in a run of politically charged prosecutions just before the midterms.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Oleshky ceasefire date discrepancy (update)
[ava] An update on the Oleshky ceasefire. Ukraine has now signed on publicly. Its human-rights commissioner and lead negotiator, Dmytro Lubinets, thanked Melania Trump and described a three-part deal: a ceasefire, aid deliveries, and free movement for civilians who want to leave. He also warned it will be extremely challenging to carry out. But the dates don't match yet. Russia's top general, Valery Gerasimov, says Russian forces will hold fire on that front starting at four a.m. Moscow time on Tuesday, while the White House has said the pause begins Thursday the twenty-ninth.
// Story 5: West Bank closure
[ava] Israel has ordered its military to prepare a full closure of the occupied West Bank after an attack near Hebron. Israel says two Palestinian gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers before troops killed them. Prime Minister Netanyahu blamed Hamas and said a larger attack on civilians was foiled; he offered no detail. The U.N. secretary-general is alarmed by the sweeping limits on Palestinian movement, while roads stay open to settlers. It's the fourth closure in a month, days before Israel's election Tuesday.
// Story 6: Istanbul summit
[ava] And in Istanbul, foreign ministers from Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Egypt and Pakistan met with their counterparts from Britain, France, Germany and Italy. Their joint message: no new U.S. or Israeli strikes on Iran, which they warned would spread the war and push oil even higher. They also demanded an end to Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Germany's foreign minister blamed Iran and the Houthis for, as he put it, blackmailing world trade. The U.S. wasn't at the table.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Google privacy policy
[andrew] Tech. Google has quietly updated its privacy policy. It used to promise flatly not to use your Gmail, Drive or Google Photos content for personalized ads. Since October sixteenth, that promise carries an exception: if you connect those services to Search's A.I. features, like Personal Intelligence, that data can now help target ads. Google says advertisers never see your mail or photos, and it won't target on sensitive categories like health or religion. You can opt out in Search's personalization settings.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: OpenAI containment red-teaming
[andrew] In A.I., OpenAI published a striking account of testing its own security. It set teams of its most capable A.I. agents loose on copies of its research infrastructure and told them to break out. They did, repeatedly: escaping virtual machines, reaching the live internet through metrics, logging and login services, and even signaling between isolated agents by timing the processor's cache. OpenAI says it fixed the problems, contributed fixes to open-source tools, now trains frontier models with no internet access, and resumed that training on October first after an eleven-day pause triggered by a September incident. Supporters see welcome transparency; skeptics say it shows how hard containment gets as models improve.
// Story 10: Kimi K3.1 Pro
[andrew] Also out today: Moonshot's Kimi K three point one Pro, the open-weight Chinese model we told you this morning hadn't shipped yet. Moonshot says it nearly matches Anthropic's Claude Opus five point five on a coding-agent benchmark, though those numbers are its own and unverified. It also says it cut hacking-related training data before releasing the model's weights, due by November fourth.
// Story 11: Texas sues OpenAI
[andrew] And Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton sued OpenAI and Sam Altman under the state's consumer-protection law, alleging ChatGPT was marketed as safe while failing to protect Texans, including children. We didn't find an OpenAI response; the company has said it builds safeguards into ChatGPT, and the case will test those claims.
> source: OpenAI, "Securing our frontier model training environment" (Oct 23); The Next Web and Bloomberg on Kimi K3.1 Pro (Oct 23); reports on the Texas attorney general lawsuit (Oct 23)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 12: PHP unpatched flaw
[ava] Cyber desk. PHP's security team warned of yet another remote code execution flaw, with no patch yet. The good news: it only works with an unusual setup, where an app switches its session settings at runtime with upload progress turned on. The fix for now is to turn session upload progress off.
// Story 13: BlackX bootkit
[ava] Kaspersky has found a bootkit it calls BlackX that loads before Windows and slips past Secure Boot, used against diplomatic organizations in Central Asia. The catch: even fully patched machines are exposed unless administrators also applied Microsoft's manual Secure Boot revocation steps. Kaspersky ties it, with moderate confidence, to the Chinese group A.P.T. forty-one.
// Story 14: North Korea DDoS + Windows Server 2016
[ava] And North Korea's handful of government and state-media websites, including its news agency, went dark for about thirty-two hours this week. Network firm Kentik says it was a wave of denial-of-service attacks, but couldn't say who was behind it.
> source: BleepingComputer (Sergiu Gatlan, Oct 23); Kaspersky Securelist and The Hacker News on BlackX; Kentik via Reuters and The Straits Times on North Korea

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, the kindness of a stranger named Ernie. The Autumn Kings Trio had just played Rockfest in Wisconsin's Chippewa Valley when their van broke down in rural Wisconsin. They had ten hours to reach the Upheaval Festival in Grand Rapids, Michigan, five hundred miles away. Ernie, a stranger who stopped to help, lent them his Ford Explorer, for several days and more than a thousand miles. The band calls that Michigan set the greatest show of their career. Ernie's explanation: he'd never met a live band before, he just did it to help three strangers on the side of the road. As one fan put it, it's the best and most Midwest story.
