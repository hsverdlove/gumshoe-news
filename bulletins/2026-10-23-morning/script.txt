## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: DHS voter-check order deadline (update)
[ava] An update on the ruling limiting Homeland Security's door-to-door election checks in nine states. The judge's own pause on her order runs out at five p.m. Pacific today, eight Eastern. Unless the Ninth Circuit steps in, the limits take effect tonight and last through Election Day. The administration says the checks protect legitimate votes; the judge found they relied on error-prone data and intimidated voter-assistance groups. Separately, the Justice Department asked the same appeals court to intervene in its bid to stop California from counting more than twenty thousand mail ballots it says likely came from noncitizens.
// Story 2: Early voting surge
[ava] Meanwhile, early voting is running well ahead of the last midterm. An A.P. analysis counts more than thirteen million early ballots, versus nine million at this point in twenty twenty-two. Texas is up seventy-four percent, Kansas has more than doubled, and Maine, North Carolina and Ohio are up more than thirty. Caveats: some states expanded early voting or publish more data now, and early turnout doesn't predict winners. In Michigan and Georgia it's about even with twenty twenty-two, and in Iowa it's down.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: Microsoft diagnostic AI
[andrew] In A.I., Microsoft says its Diagnostic Orchestrator, a system that works a case step by step like a panel of doctors, asking questions and ordering tests, got about eighty-three percent of difficult New England Journal of Medicine cases right in a study published in Science, versus thirty-six percent for general physicians. Mustafa Suleyman calls it a step toward medical superintelligence. The caveats matter: the cases were text-only, the doctors worked alone without specialists or references, and there's no data yet on real patients. TechCrunch notes much of the gain may come from the underlying A.I. models themselves.
// Story 10: METR / OpenAI monitoring
[andrew] And outside evaluators from METR and Redwood Research spent three weeks inside OpenAI testing the system that watches its internal A.I. agents for misbehavior. They found several holes, including a way agents could keep hundreds of recent actions unmonitored. OpenAI says the flaws affecting production are patched; the evaluators haven't verified that.
> source: Science, "Sequential diagnosis with language models" (Oct 2026); TechCrunch, "Microsoft's Mustafa Suleyman says its AI outperforms doctors on hard cases" (Oct 22); The Verge (Oct 22); METR, "Red-Teaming OpenAI's Internal Agent Monitoring Systems" (David Rein, Adam Kaufman, Oct 22); CNBC (Oct 22); Reuters via Investing.com (Oct 22)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 11: Quest Diagnostics
[ava] Cyber desk. Quest Diagnostics, the lab company that tests roughly one in three American adults a year, says hackers got into its Quanum lab services system, which some clinics use to order tests and look up results. Quest believes patient health and identifying information was accessed, but doesn't yet know how many people are affected. It found the intrusion October twenty-first, paused the system, and says its core testing operations are running. It will notify patients as required. If you've used Quest, watch for scam messages that reference real test results.
// Story 12: PHP2Shell and Node2Shell
[ava] On the PHP flaws we've been following, Amazon says attackers began probing them on October seventeenth, four days before patches came out, and that exploitation attempts are now widespread, though no successful break-ins have been confirmed. And Node.js has its own critical bug, nicknamed Node2Shell, where crafted compressed data can let attackers run code. If you or your developers run Node, update to the October twenty-first releases.
> source: Quest Diagnostics Form 8-K (Oct 21); Cybernews, "Quest Diagnostics confirms breach of lab systems" (Stefanie Schappert, Oct 23); AP (Oct 22); Washington Post (Oct 22); BleepingComputer, "Amazon says PHP2Shell exploitation began 4 days before patches" (Sergiu Gatlan, Oct 22); Node.js, "October 2026 Security Releases" (Oct 21); Imperva, "Node2Shell: From Brotli Data to Remote Code Execution"

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, an island bouncing back. Nu'utele, off Samoa, has one of the country's most pristine native forests, but invasive rats and feral pigs were eating eggs and tearing up seedlings. Samoan officials, nearby villagers and the nonprofit Island Conservation removed them, with former New Zealand rugby star Glen Osborne joining the hunt for the last pigs. A year on, seedlings carpet the forest floor, people are harvesting giant taro where crops used to fail, and researchers have found nests of the rare shy ground dove. Next up: bringing back an endangered forest bird called the mao.
> source: Good News Network, "Samoan Island Witnesses Explosion of Native Wildlife Following Removal of Invasive Rats and Pigs" (Andy Corbley, Oct 22), citing Island Conservation and SPREP
