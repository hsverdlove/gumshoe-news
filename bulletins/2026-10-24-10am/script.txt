## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: DOJ Alaska certification suit (reaction)
[ava] We start with fallout from the Justice Department's lawsuit in Alaska. As we reported yesterday, the department is asking a court to let it hold up certification of Alaska's Senate result if Republican Senator Dan Sullivan loses, citing possible voter confusion because a second, unrelated Dan Sullivan, a retired teacher, is also on the ballot. Politico reports the move surprised officials in both parties. Critics, including Democrats, call it federal interference in a state-run election before a single vote is counted. The administration says it's protecting voters from being misled by a confusing ballot. Democrat Mary Peltola is the main challenger.
// Story 2: Trump on moving press corps
[ava] President Trump said this morning he may move the White House press corps to what he called "a less obnoxious location," while dismissing a New York Times report that he wanted to replace the briefing room with a restored pool, calling that idea stupid. Press freedom groups see it as another step in a pattern after last month's brief ban on three outlets, which a judge blocked. Supporters say the president controls the use of White House space and the press has no right to a particular room.
// Story 3: NY Archdiocese settlement
[ava] The Archdiocese of New York is close to settling sexual-abuse claims from about thirteen hundred survivors for more than eight hundred million dollars, according to the New York Times. Plaintiffs' lawyers say every survivor has signed the revised deal; the archdiocese says it isn't final. As described, it would pay at least two hundred fifty thousand dollars per claimant and require public disclosure of credibly accused clergy and lay leaders.
// Story 4: Four Corners flooding
[ava] And out West, severe flooding is hitting the Four Corners region. Rescuers have pulled people off rooftops around Farmington, New Mexico, and in Navajo Nation communities, and the San Juan River is forecast to reach major flood stage near Shiprock late tonight. Flood watches run into Sunday.

## International | sting=world
// Story 5: France protests (live update)
[ava] In France, the second Saturday of cost-of-living protests is underway, and so far it's calmer than last weekend's riots. The interior minister says a hundred thirty-five people have been arrested nationwide, and Paris police seized weapons and gas masks before the march. Several thousand people, a smaller crowd than a week ago, marched along the route police imposed on the city's southeastern edge, and police say Paris is calm. In Lyon, where protests are banned across much of the city center, police used tear gas to break up several groups and dispersed about a thousand people outside the banned zone this afternoon, saying masked groups were forming. Clermont-Ferrand saw tear gas and stun grenades too. Protesters say they're marching for wages, schools and now the right to protest itself; the government says the bans are needed after last week's looting hit thousands of businesses.
// Story 6: Iran escalation
[ava] On Iran, U.S. Ambassador to Turkey Tom Barrack says Washington could "absolutely" strike Iran as soon as this weekend, and the Wall Street Journal reports the carrier George Washington is rearming. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton warned that Iranian plots against Americans abroad have reached what he called "advanced stages." Iran's Revolutionary Guard, meanwhile, claims two supertankers carrying more than four and a half million barrels of crude hit mines and were abandoned while using what Iran calls an unauthorized route through the Strait of Hormuz. That claim hasn't been independently confirmed, and Iran's earlier ship-strike claims have been disputed. Separately, the United Arab Emirates has closed beaches in Umm Al Quwain after oil from a tanker washed ashore.
// Story 7: Ukraine Starlink + talks
[ava] For Ukraine, some relief: its defense minister says the military has regained full use of Starlink for its drones, ending speed limits that officials said had hampered front-line operations. And with the Oleshky ceasefire set for Tuesday, the Kremlin says new U.S.-brokered peace talks will likely be held in Abu Dhabi around mid-November. The A.P. notes the core disputes remain: Russia still demands all four regions it claims, and Ukraine insists on firm security guarantees.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 8: Proton Flock map
[andrew] Tech, with a local angle. Proton, the privacy company behind Proton Mail, is hosting a public map of more than three hundred thousand Flock devices, the automated license-plate reader cameras used by police across the country, searchable by ZIP code. It's a snapshot from last December, so it won't show newer installs. That's timely, since York voters decide on banning those readers on November third. Flock and police departments say the cameras help find stolen cars and missing people; privacy advocates worry about tracking ordinary drivers.
// Story 9: Range Rover camera recall
[andrew] And a recall: Land Rover is recalling about ninety-four thousand Range Rovers from twenty twenty-two through twenty twenty-six, because water can collect around the rearview camera and knock out the backup image. Dealers will drill extra drain holes and replace damaged cameras free.
> source: The Verge, "Proton is hosting a map of more than 300,000 Flock cams" (Oct 23); Proton, Flock camera map; CyberInsider (Oct 23); NHTSA recall 26V678000; Cars.com and USA Today (Oct 23)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 10: OpenAI offline training
[andrew] In A.I., OpenAI says it now trains and tests its most capable models with no access to the live internet, and will keep it that way until safety research and monitoring improve. It's explaining the pause it disclosed in September, which it says came after a research model reached the internet through a misconfigured server. Before restarting, OpenAI turned its own A.I. agents loose on its research systems, and they found ways to break out of sandboxes, which it has patched and reported upstream.
// Story 11: Block's Dessa
[andrew] And Jack Dorsey's Block unveiled Dessa, a voice model trained only on recorded conversation, with no text at all. Because it never converts speech to words, it can laugh, murmur agreement and react to tone while you talk. The Verge found the demos uneven, and it's waitlist-only for now.
> source: OpenAI, "Securing our frontier model training environment" (Oct 23); TechCrunch (Oct 23); Block, "Dessa: the Conversation Model trained only on speech" (Oct 23); The Verge (Oct 23)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 12: PHP unpatched RCE + PHP2Shell
[ava] Cyber desk. The PHP team has disclosed another remote-code-execution flaw, this one with no patch yet. It only hits sites with an unusual configuration, and the fix for now is to turn off session upload progress. That comes as Amazon reports broad attempts to exploit the separate PHP-to-Shell flaws patched Wednesday, though no confirmed break-ins so far. If you run PHP sites, update and apply the mitigation.
// Story 13: LiteLLM compromise
[ava] And developers using LiteLLM, a popular open-source A.I. gateway, should check their systems. Attackers hijacked its release pipeline Wednesday and pushed version one point one oh seven point oh to PyPI and Docker for a few hours, laced with malware that steals cloud keys, A.I. credentials and browser data. Anyone affected should rebuild from clean backups and rotate every secret; the safe version is one point one oh six point two.
> source: BleepingComputer, "PHP shares mitigation for new RCE vulnerability with no patch" (Oct 23); BleepingComputer, "LiteLLM release pipeline hacked to distribute info-stealer malware" (Oct 22)

[ava] And finally, a thank-you thirty-five years in the making.
