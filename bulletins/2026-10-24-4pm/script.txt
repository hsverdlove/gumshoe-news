## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Britt McHenry resigns
[ava] A State Department press adviser, Britt McHenry, has resigned after a confrontation at a Washington Wizards game this week. Video from the arena shows the former ESPN reporter throwing a drink toward fans in the stands as some of them chanted "Get her out." Her side says hecklers had insulted model Winnie Harlow, who was sitting with her, and threw a drink first. McHenry stepped down Friday; the State Department declined to comment. Critics say an official spokesperson should have walked away; supporters say she was defending a friend from harassment.
// Story 2: Screwworm in wildlife
[ava] In Texas, federal and state officials have confirmed the first New World screwworm case in U.S. wildlife: a female elk taken by a hunter on a game ranch in Pecos County. Screwworm larvae feed on the living flesh of animals, and the pest had been working its way north through Mexico. Texas has widened its quarantine zone and is asking hunters to inspect their harvests and report any wounds with maggots. Officials stress that the U.S. food supply remains safe and that human cases are rare.
// Story 3: Six Flags seat belts
[andrew] Six Flags says it will add seat belts to the Goliath roller coaster at Six Flags Over Georgia and similar rides, after forty-two-year-old Hassan Jihad Ibn Kariem, an Atlanta school employee, was ejected and killed on October twelfth. Goliath stays closed, along with eight other rides at five parks, while independent engineers review safety across the chain. C.E.O. John Reilly wrote that trust is earned through actions, not words. Georgia's investigation is still open, so the cause hasn't been determined.

## International | sting=world
// Story 5: France protests evening
[ava] France's second Saturday of cost-of-living protests is winding down with nearly a thousand arrests nationwide, according to Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez. As usual, the counts differ widely: the ministry says about four hundred ten thousand marched across the country, and the C.G.T. union says eight hundred thousand. This evening brought clashes at Porte de Vincennes in Paris, barricades in Marseille and Bordeaux, and water cannon in Rennes, where fifty-two people were detained. In Nice, about fifty masked far-right activists attacked people marching despite a ban; the prefecture reports more than sixty arrests, including eleven from the far-right group. An outlet called Blast reports the prime minister asked mobile carriers to be ready to cut off TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat and X if things escalated. That hasn't been confirmed; publicly, the government has only asked platforms to tighten their crisis procedures. The violence was well below last weekend's. Unions say the bans and arrests criminalize protest; the government says they kept a lid on rioting.
// Story 6: Iran
[ava] On Iran, there's still no sign of renewed U.S. strikes this evening, but plenty of signals in both directions. President Masoud Pezeshkian said on state TV that the Americans, in his words, "messed everything up," but that he still thinks Iran should go back and conclude a deal. Saudi-owned Al Arabiya reports, without confirmation, that Tehran told Pakistan's mediators it would reopen the Strait of Hormuz if Washington lifts its blockade of Iranian ports. Meanwhile, the Wall Street Journal reports the U.S.S. George Washington carrier group is loading munitions and readying aircraft. Russia denied reports it's evacuating staff from Iran's Bushehr nuclear plant, and Pezeshkian is expected in Moscow on Tuesday to meet Vladimir Putin.
// Story 7: Israel election
[ava] That same Tuesday, Israelis vote. The final polls from public broadcaster Kan and Channel Twelve show Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc at about fifty seats and the opposition, now led in the polls by Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party, at about fifty-two. Neither reaches the sixty-one needed to govern, which leaves the Arab parties and a small centrist list as potential kingmakers, and about a third of voters say they're still undecided. Netanyahu used a Saturday campaign video to warn that Iran has restarted its nuclear program; his opponents say he's leaning on fear after failing to deliver security.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 9: Tesla FSD Vallejo
[andrew] Tech. California Highway Patrol is investigating a fatal crash in Vallejo involving a Tesla whose driver says Full Self-Driving was engaged. Seventy-one-year-old Domingo Gonzales was crossing westbound Interstate eighty on foot at about two-thirty Thursday morning when he was hit; a second vehicle then struck him as well. Dashcam video shows the Tesla braking and swerving left in the final moment. Tesla's system is officially "supervised," so the driver is responsible at all times, and a pedestrian on a dark interstate is hard for any driver to avoid. Investigators haven't said whether the software played a role.
// Story 10: Getty Images Chapter 11
[andrew] And Getty Images has filed a prepackaged Chapter eleven bankruptcy in Delaware. Getty says photographers, customers and day-to-day business won't be affected. The company was squeezed by heavy debt and by A.I.-generated imagery, even as it licenses its own library for A.I. training. Creditors vote at the end of November, with a confirmation hearing set for mid-December.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 13: Popcorn Time updater
[ava] Cyber desk. Researchers at Eye Security found that Time4Popcorn, a version of the old Popcorn Time streaming app, checked for updates over plain, unencrypted web traffic with no signature check, so anyone controlling its update server could push any software they liked. The project's domain had lapsed, so the researchers registered it themselves and found about three million Windows and three hundred fifty thousand Mac installs still calling home, including some on corporate, government and military networks. Instead of anything malicious, they pushed a harmless update that turned the updater off, cutting requests by ninety-eight percent, and then handed the domain to the nonprofit Shadowserver. Android versions are still exposed, and the app runs a browser engine from twenty fourteen. If it's on any machine you manage, uninstall it.
> source: BleepingComputer (Oct 23); The Hacker News (Oct 24); Eye Security research, CVE-2026-30612

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a quick-thinking bystander in St. Paul, Minnesota. On Monday, twenty-seven-year-old Ebro Ahmed was outside a corner store when a school bus rolled past with its doors open and the driver having a seizure. He ran after it, jumped aboard, fumbled briefly into reverse, got it into neutral and set the parking brake. Then he called for help and stayed with the driver until paramedics came. About eighteen middle schoolers were on board, and nobody was hurt. The driver is home resting. Ahmed has no medical training; he credits watching Grey's Anatomy for keeping him calm. He was given a thousand-dollar reward and says he didn't need the recognition. He's just glad the kids are okay.
