## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: DHS voter-check limits in effect (update)
[ava] We start with the update we promised. The judge's order limiting Homeland Security's door-to-door election checks in nine states took effect at eight last night, Eastern time. As of this morning we found no report of an appeals court stepping in, so through the close of polls on November third, agents in those states can't run election-related field interviews likely to involve U.S. citizens, or act solely on the government's citizenship-matching lists. The judge stressed that investigating illegal voting itself remains lawful. DHS says it targets only noncitizens who vote; the groups that sued say the lists are error-prone and the visits intimidate voter-outreach workers.
// Story 2: Ninth Circuit National Guard ruling
[ava] The same appeals court did rule in another big case. A divided Ninth Circuit panel held, two to one, that President Trump exceeded his authority when he federalized four thousand California National Guard troops during last year's Los Angeles protests. Judge Jennifer Sung wrote that the administration hadn't shown it met the legal conditions the Supreme Court set out last year; Judge Mark Bennett dissented. Governor Newsom and Attorney General Rob Bonta called it a win for the rule of law. Supporters of the deployment argue presidents need wide latitude to protect federal officers and property, and the administration can seek further review.
// Story 3: Trump on Cuba
[ava] And at a rally in Fort Myers, Florida, last night, President Trump revived the idea of pressuring Cuba militarily, saying warships coming back from the Iran war could stop about fifty yards off Cuba's coast before Secretary of State Rubio, quote, finishes up the deal. He has previously said he didn't think military intervention would be needed. Backers see pressure on a hostile government ninety miles away; critics warn against opening another front while the Iran conflict is unresolved.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: France - Trappes attack update + protests
[ava] Overseas, France is on edge. About seventy thousand police and gendarmes are deployed today for nationwide protests over living costs and school conditions, a week after riots and looting hit cities across the country. The Louvre, the Orsay and other Paris museums are closed, Paris's march was rerouted away from the center, and police reportedly fired tear gas in Lyon this morning. The tension follows Friday's shooting in Trappes and La Verrière, west of Paris, where a gunman firing from his car killed a twenty-six-year-old martial-arts athlete and wounded five, including a twelve-year-old girl. Prosecutors are treating it as a racist terrorist attack. President Macron called it a turning point, the first planned terror attack to target people for their skin color or perceived religion. France's main Jewish umbrella group objected to the word "first," pointing to past attacks on a Jewish school and a kosher grocery. The suspect remains in custody, and three people close to him, including his mother, are being questioned.
// Story 5: Oleshky ceasefire date settled (update)
[ava] On the Oleshky ceasefire: the start-date mismatch is resolved. Ukraine's General Staff and Russia now both say guns fall silent at three a.m. Kyiv time on Tuesday, the twenty-seventh. The humanitarian mission itself, with demining, aid deliveries and voluntary evacuations, follows two days later, on Thursday. Ukraine's negotiator still warns it will be extremely hard to carry out.
// Story 6: Iran - embassies + Gulf warning
[ava] On Iran, the State Department is cutting staff at the U.S. embassies in Jordan, Lebanon, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates, citing intelligence that Iran and the Houthis are stepping up efforts to target Americans. The posts stay open. Iran's armed forces chief, Major General Ali Abdollahi, said today that U.S. warships will never be allowed back into the Persian Gulf. We haven't seen a U.S. response.
// Story 7: Jordan update
[ava] In Jordan, the death toll from the attack on a church funeral in Karak province has risen to eight, with forty wounded; the gunmen are still being hunted, and no group has claimed it.
// Story 8: Eagle's Nest fire
[ava] Finally, in Bavaria, a fire has largely destroyed the Eagle's Nest, the mountaintop house built for Hitler and now a state-owned tourist site and documentation spot. No one was hurt. Police suspect arson and detained a suspect, though they haven't confirmed a link to the blaze.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 9: NHTSA cyan lights
[andrew] Tech, and one for drivers. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration has proposed letting fully driverless cars wear cyan, a blue-green light, to signal when the computer is driving with no human watching the road. The lamps would be optional, but if a carmaker uses them they'd need to be visible from the front, sides and rear, steady rather than flashing, and meet set color and brightness rules. The proposal also loosens some requirements for manual light controls in vehicles without a driver. It's set to be published in the Federal Register Monday, opening public comment.
// Story 10: iPhone Duo durability
[andrew] And if you're eyeing Apple's two-thousand-dollar foldable: YouTuber JerryRigEverything's torture test found that dust worked into the iPhone Duo's hinge and kept it from closing, and that bending eventually killed the inner screen. The outer screen held up well. These are deliberately extreme tests, not everyday use.
> source: NHTSA press release, "Vehicle Lighting Updates for Automated Vehicles" (Oct 2026); Federal Register, FMVSS No. 108 modernization for ADS-equipped vehicles (scheduled Oct 26); The Verge (Oct 23); GSMDome and Kursiv on the JerryRigEverything iPhone Duo test (Oct 23-24)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 13: CISA KEV Collabora + VeloCloud
[ava] Cyber desk. CISA says attackers are actively exploiting two flaws, and federal agencies have until Monday to fix them. One is a command-injection bug in Collabora Online, the document editor often paired with Nextcloud, which is being used to plant web shells. The other hits on-premises Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator, the third exploited VeloCloud flaw since July. If you run either, patch now and check for signs of intrusion.
// Story 14: Brotli flaw widens
[ava] The compression bug behind the Node.js flaw we told you about this week turns out to be much wider. The same Brotli decoder flaw also affects Bun, Python's Brotli packages, and the Envoy and Istio proxies when Brotli decompression is on. Fixed versions are out for all of them.
> source: CISA, KEV catalog additions (Oct 23); Arista Security Advisory 0199; The Hacker News (Oct 24); Bun, Envoy and Istio security advisories for CVE-2026-111497; Aikido Security blog
