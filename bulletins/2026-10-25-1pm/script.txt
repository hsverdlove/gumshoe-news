## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Blanche defends Alaska suit
[ava] We start with the fights over the midterm count. Attorney General Todd Blanche used the Sunday shows to defend the Justice Department's lawsuit that could block certification of Alaska's Senate race if Republican Senator Dan Sullivan loses. The suit argues voters may be confused by a second candidate on the ballot also named Dan Sullivan. On CNN, Blanche said the goal is to preserve ballots so a court can look at confusion after the election, and he called it "crazy" when host Dana Bash raised the prospect of another January sixth. The other side: several election-law scholars, including N.Y.U.'s Richard Pildes, call it one of the weakest election lawsuits they've seen, and note Alaska's courts already settled the ballot question. President Trump has said he told the department to file it.
> source: CNN live updates, "Blanche defends lawsuit to block certification of Alaska Senate results" (Oct 25); The Guardian, "Blanche defends DoJ's pre-emptive suit" (Oct 25)

[ava] Second, a federal appeals court in California hears arguments today in the Justice Department's push to keep more than twenty thousand mail-in ballots tied to suspected noncitizens from being counted. The panel has two Trump appointees and one Biden appointee. The administration says it's stopping illegal votes; state officials call the demand "catastrophic" for running the election and say Washington hasn't identified specific ineligible voters.
> source: CNN, "Why today's hearing in the California voter roll fight could be so consequential" (Oct 25)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 1: Google links phone number to all Android accounts
[andrew] On the tech desk, Ava: if you use Android, Google verifies your SIM's phone number during setup and quietly attaches it to every Google account signed in on that phone, work and secondary accounts included. Engadget flagged it after users got surprise verification emails. You can switch off future linking in the phone's Google settings under phone number verification, but you have to remove numbers already attached from each account. iPhones aren't affected.
> source: Engadget, "Google is quietly linking your phone number to other Android accounts" (Oct 24); Google support page (Oct 25)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 1: AGENTS.md in Downloads
[ava] On the cyber desk, one that's close to home for anyone using A.I. coding agents. Researchers at Forever Security found a booby-trapped instruction file, called AGENTS dot M D, sitting in a customer's Downloads folder. When the person ran a coding agent from that folder, it read the file and tried to install a malicious software package. In tests it fooled OpenAI's Codex about ninety percent of the time, and a similar file made Claude Code leak its login token. Agents are built to obey these files, so there's nothing to patch. The advice: never run an agent from your Downloads folder; start it in the specific project folder, and keep approval prompts on.
> source: BleepingComputer, "AI coding agents hijacked using AGENTS.md file in Downloads folder" (Oct 25)

// Story 2: PHP2Shell exploited
[ava] And the two critical PHP flaws dubbed PHP2Shell are now under active attack. Palo Alto Networks says intruders broke into a European government agency and reached password data within two hours, and it counts more than twenty-two million exposed systems. If you run a website, even a small blog, make sure your host updated PHP after October twenty-first.
> source: Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 threat brief (Oct 23); Yuphora tech roundup (Oct 25); WIRED (Oct 24)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 1: DHMC trauma strain
[ava] Closer to home, doctors at Dartmouth Hitchcock, New Hampshire's only Level One trauma center, told Congresswoman Maggie Goodlander the system is "not sustainable." They say the hospital runs over capacity six days a week, a quarter of emergency patients are seen in the waiting room. Staff blamed federal Medicaid cuts in last year's budget law, and Dartmouth Health cut a hundred twenty-four jobs last month. Supporters of the law note it also created a fifty-billion-dollar rural health fund, two hundred four million of it for New Hampshire this year.
