## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: DOJ noncitizen voting charges
[ava] The Justice Department this morning announced charges against forty-eight noncitizens in Pennsylvania, California, Florida, New York and Nevada, for voting illegally, falsely claiming citizenship to register, and related crimes. All but one are described as lawful permanent residents, meaning green-card holders. Most of the alleged votes were cast in twenty twenty-two or twenty twenty-four, and many cases surfaced when the defendants later applied to become citizens and allegedly lied about their voting history. The department says the cases show the problem is real. Skeptics note that prosecutions like these remain rare next to the more than one hundred fifty million ballots cast in twenty twenty-four. All of the defendants are presumed innocent.
// Story 2: Crime victimization survey
[ava] Also from the Justice Department: its annual survey asking about a hundred thousand households whether they were victims of crime. Preliminary results show violent victimization, not counting simple assaults, fell about twenty-five percent from twenty twenty-four to twenty twenty-five, from nine point nine to seven point four per thousand people. The administration calls it a historic drop. The Bureau of Justice Statistics cautions that these come from a redesigned survey that can't be directly compared with older years, and final numbers are due in twenty twenty-seven.
// Story 3: Campaign trail
[ava] On the campaign trail, eight days out, President Trump heads to Mississippi today to rally for Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith, while Vice President Vance campaigns in Montana and Kansas. And the California ballot fight returns to a lower court tomorrow, with the Ninth Circuit's ruling still pending.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Tehran talks under way
[ava] Overseas, those talks in Tehran are now under way. Saudi Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman, Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, and Turkey's intelligence chief, Ibrahim Kalin, sat down with Iranian leaders. It's Iran's first high-level meeting with all three members of the Mecca defense pact signed in August, under which an attack on one member counts as an attack on all. ## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Anthropic files publicly for IPO
[andrew] In A.I., it's official. Anthropic publicly filed its I.P.O. paperwork this morning and plans to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker A-N-T, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading the deal. The filing shows revenue of sixteen point three billion dollars in the first half of this year, up from one point two billion a year earlier, but a net loss of eighty point seven billion, mostly an accounting charge on convertible notes as the company's valuation climbed. It lists nearly eight hundred billion dollars in computing commitments over ten years, and about a fifth of revenue comes from two customers.
[andrew] The governance is unusual. Anthropic stays a public benefit corporation; its co-founders will hold fifty point one percent of the vote through a single special share, and an independent trust elects a majority of the board. Ninety-four pages of risk factors include a warning that a catastrophic A.I. incident could cause harm too large to insure. The Wall Street Journal reports Anthropic is aiming to go public the week of November sixteenth, possibly raising around a hundred billion dollars at a valuation of one and a half to two trillion. Critics point to the losses and the concentrated control; supporters point to growth few companies have ever matched.
> source: Anthropic, "Anthropic files S-1 with the SEC for proposed initial public offering" (Oct 26); Anthropic S-1 on SEC EDGAR (Oct 26); CNBC Anthropic IPO live updates (Oct 26); Financial Times, Bloomberg and Reuters via Techmeme (Oct 26); The Wall Street Journal via Techmeme (Oct 26); Yahoo Finance (Oct 26)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Cathedral breaks into Anthropic systems
[ava] Cyber desk, and Anthropic again. The Washington Post reports that Cathedral, a security startup founded by former DOGE staffers, used A.I. agents to get into some of Anthropic's internal sales, H.R. and I.T. systems in under twelve hours, through flaws in third-party business software. It reached salary and job-offer details, meeting transcripts and Slack messages. The test ran under Anthropic's program for outside security researchers. Anthropic says its monitoring flagged the activity, it cut off access the same day and no customer chats, source code or model weights were reached. It also says Cathedral overstated the national-security angle and didn't disclose all of its activity. Security veteran Alex Stamos said Anthropic "could have done a lot better." The lesson for any business: your vendors' software is part of your attack surface.
> source: The Washington Post, "Researchers at cybersecurity start-up Cathedral got inside Anthropic" (Oct 26); India Today (Oct 26)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 10: Brookline eagle
[ava] Closer to home, a sad find in Brookline. Residents found a bald eagle unable to move and bleeding. New Hampshire Fish and Game responded, but the bird didn't survive. Officials believe it ate a mouse or rat that had swallowed rat poison. N.H. Audubon's Carol Foss says that kind of secondary poisoning also threatens hawks, owls, bobcats and fishers, and a recent study found thirty-four of thirty-five animals tested in the state had been exposed. Audubon urges non-toxic pest control.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, lights out for the birds. On Corvo, a tiny island in Portugal's Azores, residents switched off their lights for five nights so young Cory's shearwaters could find their way to the sea on their first flight, instead of crashing into bright village streets. Volunteers also check nests and rescue grounded chicks, releasing them from the clifftops. Since twenty fifteen, more than thirty thousand fledglings have been saved across the islands, about six thousand on Corvo alone. Marine biologist Tânia Pipa of the Portuguese bird society leads the rescue effort.
> source: The Guardian, "Lights out: the island that goes dark to save young seabirds" (Oct 25); SPEA, "Corvo island turns out the lights to protect seabirds"; Good News of the Day (Oct 26)
