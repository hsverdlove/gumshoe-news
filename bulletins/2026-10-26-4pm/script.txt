## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: $1 million voter-fraud reward
[ava] Breaking this afternoon: President Trump says the U.S. will pay one million dollars to anyone who reports voter fraud that leads to an arrest and conviction, covering past elections and next week's midterms. The White House posted his statement, and Reuters notes it comes barely a week before an election that polls suggest could be tough for Republicans. Details, like where the money would come from and who would run it, weren't immediately available.
[ava] It's separate from, and far bigger than, the fraud whistleblower program the Justice Department launched last week, which pays ten to thirty thousand dollars for tips about fraud in federal benefit programs. Supporters say a large reward will bring cases to light. Skeptics note that most studies have found voter fraud to be rare, and worry a bounty could encourage false accusations against voters and poll workers. The administration points to cases like this morning's charges against forty-eight noncitizens accused of voting illegally.
// Story 2: Maurene Comey ruling
[ava] And in New York, federal Judge Jesse Furman ruled that the Justice Department unlawfully fired prosecutor Maurene Comey, daughter of former F.B.I. Director James Comey, in July of last year. She had worked on the Epstein, Ghislaine Maxwell and Sean Combs cases. The judge found the firing, ordered by then-Attorney General Pam Bondi, violated civil service protections and her due-process rights, and rejected the department's argument that it can fire career prosecutors at will, calling it a sweeping and unprecedented theory of executive power. He ordered her reinstated, with back pay through December, when she started a new job. The Justice Department says it disagrees and will appeal.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: DoorDash and Instacart
[andrew] Tech. DoorDash is in advanced, exclusive talks to buy grocery-delivery rival Instacart, according to the Wall Street Journal and Semafor. The Journal says a deal could come as soon as this week, though both outlets caution it could still fall apart, and no price has been reported. Instacart is valued around twelve billion dollars; Semafor estimates a deal near fifteen billion. Instacart shares rose about seven percent, while DoorDash fell about five. The two talked in twenty twenty-one, at a much higher valuation, but those talks collapsed over price and antitrust worries. Supporters say a combined company could better compete with Amazon and Walmart in grocery delivery; critics see one more merger of direct rivals that would leave shoppers and gig workers with fewer choices.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: GPT-6.1 Terra
[andrew] In A.I., OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra today, a faster, cheaper model aimed at everyday computer work like filling out forms, reading invoices and editing documents. OpenAI says its A.P.I. price is half that of its predecessor and of the bigger GPT-6.1 Sol, at a dollar per million input tokens and five dollars per million output. OpenAI also claims a big drop in factual errors, though those are its own benchmarks and haven't been independently checked. One more notable line: OpenAI rates it at the "critical" level for cybersecurity capability under its own safety framework. It's rolling out now to paid ChatGPT users and developers.
> source: OpenAI, "Introducing GPT-6.1 Terra" (Oct 26); AWS, "OpenAI GPT-6.1 Terra is now generally available on Amazon Bedrock" (Oct 26)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: PHP2Shell confirmed exploited
[ava] Cyber desk, with an update on the PHP2Shell flaws we told you about last week. Until now, attacks on these two critical bugs in PHP, the language behind roughly seventy percent of websites, had only been probes and failed attempts. Now a researcher at Perplexity says his decoy servers caught a successful takeover of an unpatched Apache server, and Expel's Marcus Hutchins calls it the best confirmation yet of real-world exploitation. The patches have been out since last Wednesday, so if you or your web host run PHP, make sure it's updated. And PHP's developers warn of a separate, still-unpatched flaw that only affects unusual setups; their advice is to turn off the session upload-progress feature until a fix arrives.
> source: BleepingComputer, "PHP2Shell security flaws now successfully exploited in the wild" (Sergiu Gatlan, Oct 26); SecurityWeek, "PHP2Shell Exploitation Moves From Probes to Working Shells" (Oct 26)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a sewing machine, a promise to Mom, and twenty-two thousand sweatshirts. When Katelynn Devinney's mother, Patricia, had a mastectomy after her second breast cancer diagnosis, someone gave her a zip-up sweatshirt with pockets inside to hold the surgical drains patients go home with. Her mom said it was a lifesaver and asked for another, so Katelynn dusted off the sewing machine her mother bought her in high school and made one. Then she kept going. Four years later, her Delaware County, Pennsylvania, nonprofit, Pockets of Hope, has donated more than twenty-two thousand sweatshirts across the U.S. and abroad, each with a handwritten note. Her mother is now cancer-free and volunteers too. As Katelynn puts it, "a little goes a long way."
> source: WHYY, "How a Sweatshirt for Her Mom Inspired Katelynn Devinney to Help Thousands" (Cherri Gregg, Oct 26)
