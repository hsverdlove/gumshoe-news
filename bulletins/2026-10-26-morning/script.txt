## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Early vote, one week out
[ava] We start with the midterms, now eight days away. NBC News' tracker counts nearly twenty-nine million early and mail votes already cast nationwide. Registered Democrats make up the larger share so far, but Republicans have tended to vote more heavily on Election Day, so these numbers measure turnout, not who's winning.
// Story 2: Ninth Circuit skeptical (update)
[ava] An update on the California ballot fight. In a rare Sunday hearing, two of three Ninth Circuit judges sharply questioned the Justice Department's bid to make counties set aside ballots from about twenty thousand voters it calls likely noncitizens. Judge Mark Bennett, a Trump appointee, said it clashed with the Constitution's Elections Clause, which lets states run their elections. Judge Gabriel Sanchez, a Biden appointee, asked why the appeals court should step in before tomorrow's lower-court hearing. A ruling is promised quickly. The administration says stricter checks build confidence; California says the federal list relies on suspect data.
// Story 3: New Mexico floods (update)
[ava] In New Mexico, Governor Michelle Lujan Grisham tours flood-hit San Juan County today, around Farmington and Bloomfield, with the National Guard. She's asked President Trump for a federal emergency declaration that would unlock at least thirteen point eight million dollars for cleanup.
// Story 4: Trump vs. Maher

## International | sting=world
// Story 5: Brazil - Lula wins
[ava] Overseas, the big news: Brazil's President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has won a fourth term. With every vote counted, Lula took fifty-one point eight percent to Senator Flávio Bolsonaro's forty-eight point two, a margin of about four point four million votes, after trailing in the first round. Bolsonaro has not conceded. He's asking for the result to be annulled because a Supreme Court justice extended voting by two hours, though both campaigns had requested it. Leaders from Canada, Mexico, the European Union, and right-leaning governments in Chile and Peru congratulated Lula; the White House hadn't formally reacted, and Donald Trump Junior criticized the extension.
// Story 6: Serbia - Vucic concedes
[ava] In Serbia, a political earthquake. Aleksandar Vučić, who has dominated the country for more than a decade, conceded that his party lost Sunday's parliamentary election to the Student List, the movement born of protests over a deadly train-station roof collapse in twenty twenty-four. Preliminary results give the students about fifty-two and a half percent to Vučić's thirty-nine, a clear majority, and crowds celebrated in Belgrade and Novi Sad. The movement still has to choose a prime minister.
// Story 7: Iran - delegations, US readiness
[ava] On Iran, high-level delegations from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey are in Tehran today, trying to head off renewed war. CNN reports, citing two U.S. officials, that American forces could launch major new operations within ninety-six hours of an order, with a third carrier group on the way; officials suggest any action may wait until after the midterms. Iran says messages are still passing through intermediaries. And the Saudi-led coalition says it struck Houthi missile launchers and tunnels around Yemen's capital, Sanaa, including the launcher it says fired at Riyadh.
// Story 8: China plenum, Israel vote
[ava] In China, Communist Party leaders opened a four-day closed-door plenum focused on party discipline, a year before Xi Jinping is expected to seek a fourth term. ## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 12: Gheist
[ava] Cyber desk. If you or your company keeps code on GitHub, check this one. Security firm Wiz is tracking a campaign it calls Gheist that uses stolen GitHub tokens to plant workflows that dump a project's secrets into its logs, then grab the logs and delete the run. It's hit more than eight hundred fifty accounts, including private repositories, and it's still active. Look for branches named "c i dash sync" and a file called sync-check; if you find one, rotate every secret that repository could see.
// Story 13: PHP2Shell correction
[ava] And a correction to a story we aired yesterday: Palo Alto Networks' Unit Forty-Two has withdrawn its claim that PHP2Shell was how attackers breached a European government agency; it can't confirm the entry point. The flaws are still being exploited, so patching remains urgent.
> source: The Hacker News, "Gheist Uses Stolen GitHub Tokens to Steal Private Repository Secrets via Actions Logs" (Oct 26), citing Wiz Research; Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 threat brief (updated Oct 25); BleepingComputer (Oct 25-26)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
[ava] Closer to home, eight days to the election, and one question on your ballot will directly shape Londonderry's schools. Question Two asks each town whether to cap growth in school property taxes at inflation plus new construction, with bonded building costs exempt. It needs sixty percent in each community. Supporters, including Governor Ayotte and Republican lawmakers, say it gives voters control over taxes rising faster than inflation. Londonderry's superintendent warns of about a two-million-dollar shortfall, with possible cuts to busing and courses, though voters could override the cap by sixty percent in March.
[ava] At the top of the ticket, the latest Saint Anselm College poll has Democrat Chris Pappas leading Republican John E. Sununu for Senate, fifty to forty-three, with both candidates' favorability battered by negative ads.
[ava] Halloween week: Manchester's downtown trick-or-treat is Friday from three to six, and Saturday, Leach Library screens a Beetlejuice double feature from one to four-thirty, ages thirteen and up, registration required.
