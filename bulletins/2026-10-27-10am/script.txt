## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Comey third prosecution attempt
[ava] We start with James Comey. Court records unsealed Monday show the Justice Department moved earlier this month to charge the former F.B.I. director a third time, with two counts under the Espionage Act. Prosecutors allege that in twenty sixteen Comey directed staff to share national defense information with a New York Times reporter for a story about Yahoo and email surveillance. Instead of going to a grand jury, the department filed what's called a criminal information, which it says preserves the ten-year statute of limitations, and it calls that a widely recognized practice. Comey's lawyer, Patrick Fitzgerald, says he committed no crime and notes the matter was investigated twice during the first Trump administration with no charges. Some former federal prosecutors called the move highly unusual. Comey has offered to surrender, and the judge has not ruled on the merits.
// Story 2: California ballot hearing today
[ava] In California, Judge David Carter hears arguments at one o'clock Pacific time on the Justice Department's request to set aside mail ballots from about twenty thousand voters it calls likely noncitizens. The department argues every ballot processed without a check can't be undone. The state and Los Angeles County challenge how the federal list was built and say changing procedures mid-election would be disruptive. Carter has promised a prompt ruling.
// Story 3: Tennessee escape
[ava] And in Tennessee, a manhunt is on for Jacob Morley, twenty-eight, who is serving a life sentence for killing a co-worker. Officials say he slipped through the perimeter fence of a prison in Tiptonville late Sunday and stole a white Ford F-three-fifty pickup early Monday. There's a reward of up to twenty-five hundred dollars, and authorities say not to approach him.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: Apple backport for older iPhones
[ava] Cyber desk. If someone in your family uses an older iPhone, like an X S or X R, or a seventh-generation iPad, update it now. Apple released iOS eighteen point seven point eleven to fix a flaw in how the phone processes images that may already have been used in targeted attacks. Newer iPhones got this fix earlier.
// Story 11: Microsoft Authenticator AuthMask
[ava] Researchers at the firm Enclave detailed a flaw they call AuthMask in Microsoft Authenticator for Android. It let any app, even one with no permissions, pose as Teams or Outlook and walk away with sign-in tokens for a work account. Microsoft fixed it in September and says it wasn't exploited, so just make sure Authenticator is up to date.
// Story 12: Tailscale fix pending
[ava] And on the Tailworm flaws in Tailscale: Tailscale has merged the fixes and is targeting a release by the end of today, but the latest version out right now doesn't include them yet.
> source: BleepingComputer, Apple backports CoreGraphics zero-day patch to older iPhones (Oct 27); Enclave, "AuthMask: We Made Microsoft Authenticator Hand Over Tokens to an App With Zero Permissions"; The Hacker News, "Microsoft Authenticator Flaw Let Permissionless Android Apps Steal Work Account Tokens" (Oct 27); Tailscale releases on GitHub (Oct 27)
