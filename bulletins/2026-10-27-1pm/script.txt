## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Judge rebukes prosecutors in ex-FBI officials case
[ava] We start in a Washington courtroom, where a federal magistrate judge sharply criticized prosecutors in the case against two former F.B.I. officials, Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides. They're accused of making false statements to the House Judiciary Committee in twenty twenty-three about their roles in the investigations of Hunter Biden. Early this morning, hours before a scheduled preliminary hearing, the U.S. Attorney's office in Washington filed a new complaint adding an obstruction charge, with the judge's name typed in but not signed. Judge Zia Faruqui said he would not bless it, and asked why prosecutors had changed, quote, the entire history of how you charge cases. Floris's lawyers called the new filing legally deficient and made in bad faith. Prosecutors maintain the two misled Congress; the original charges stand while the court sorts out the new one.
// Story 2: Trump in Texas; Iran war and the midterms
[ava] On the campaign trail, President Trump is in Texas today, with stops in Corpus Christi and Lubbock, backing Ken Paxton in a close Senate race against Democrat James Talarico. Secretary of State Marco Rubio is with him to show off a tanker of Venezuelan crude headed for Texas refineries. The A.P. reports Republican strategists privately worry the eight-month war with Iran, and the gas prices that came with it, are hurting the party a week before the election. Republicans counter that their energy policies would bring prices down faster than Democrats', and the president has called the war a little detour. An A.P.-NORC poll last week found voters in both parties want Congress focused on affordability and ending the war.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Israel exit polls ahead
[ava] Overseas, Israel's polls close at four o'clock our time, with exit polls from the three main TV networks right after. The final pre-election polls showed a deadlock: Gadi Eisenkot's centrist Yashar party narrowly ahead of Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud, and neither bloc near the sixty-one seats needed to govern. Several parties alleged irregularities at polling stations this morning, but the Central Elections Committee says it has seen no evidence of systematic tampering. Soldiers' ballots won't start being counted until Wednesday, which could give right-wing parties a late lift, so treat tonight's exit polls as a first read, not the result.
// Story 4: Qatar presses Iran; China envoy
[ava] On Iran, Qatar, one of the main mediators with Washington, said today that Tehran needs to show good intentions by reopening the Strait of Hormuz and stopping threats against Gulf states. China says it will send an envoy to the region soon to help head off another round of fighting. Iran is still insisting on an end to the U.S. naval blockade, sanctions relief and guarantees against new attacks, while Washington wants nuclear concessions first. A five-day, step-by-step plan from mediators remains stuck on who moves first.
// Story 5: Nairobi building collapse
[ava] And in Kenya, twenty people are still unaccounted for, four days after a multi-story building collapsed in Nairobi's Eastleigh neighborhood. Police are tracing phone signals to help find them, and rescuers are still working through the rubble.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: FCC warns TP-Link
[ava] Cyber desk, and this one's relevant to home networks. The F.C.C. has warned TP-Link that three of its router models exceeded approved power limits in testing, and that the company may have submitted false or incorrect information in getting four models authorized. TP-Link has until Friday to respond. It's the latest step against the company, and it could lay groundwork for further bans. Nothing changes yet for routers already in homes.
// Story 10: Ads used to track Starlink users
[ava] And researchers at Trend Micro say an ad-tech company bid on Starlink users' ad slots far more than usual, in more than a hundred countries, harvesting location and device data that could be used to track people. Reuters sources identify the company as Israel's Discover-Tech. Those are allegations from unnamed sources; the takeaway is that ordinary online ads can leak where you are.
