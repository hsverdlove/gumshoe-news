## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: California ballot hearing; DOJ methodology
[ava] We start in Los Angeles, where Judge David Carter's hearing on California's disputed mail ballots got under way at one o'clock Pacific, four o'clock our time. The Justice Department wants ballots from about twenty thousand voters it says may be noncitizens set aside until their citizenship is checked. In court papers, the department laid out how it built that list: immigration officials compared the state's voter rolls with federal immigration data, then checked naturalization, State Department and Social Security records, and Homeland Security investigators followed up on the strongest matches. California and Los Angeles County oppose the order; the county says once a ballot leaves its envelope it can't be tied back to a voter, and that verifying citizenship isn't something it has the records or authority to do. The judge has said he'll rule promptly. We'll have the outcome when it comes.
// Story 2: FBI search in Swalwell investigation
[ava] Also in California, the F.B.I. confirmed it carried out a court-authorized search early today at the Beverly Hills mansion of developer Stephen Cloobeck, a donor to former Congressman Eric Swalwell. People familiar with the case tell the A.P. it's part of an investigation into Swalwell, who resigned in April amid sexual-misconduct allegations and stayed at the home before he left office. The warrant is sealed. Swalwell has denied any criminal wrongdoing, and Cloobeck has said he cut ties with him as the accusations mounted.
// Story 3: Supreme Court declines immigration-court rule
[ava] The Supreme Court has declined to step into a fight over a Biden-era rule that let immigration judges pause some deportation cases. Baltimore, Columbus, New Haven and a legal group argued the rule protected crime victims and neglected children waiting on other visas. Texas and the Trump administration, which got it blocked, argue it let people stay in the country indefinitely, and the Justice Department said the cities had no standing to sue.

## International | sting=world
// Story 5: Israel polls close
[ava] Overseas, Israel's polls have closed, and the first exit polls are in. The headline: no clear winner. Channels twelve and thirteen both project that Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud, not Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar, comes out as the largest single party, at roughly twenty-four or twenty-five seats to Yashar's twenty-two or twenty-three. That's a reversal of the final pre-election polls. But Eisenkot's camp still leads the bloc count, about fifty-two or fifty-three seats to fifty for Netanyahu's, with neither near the sixty-one needed to govern. Arab-led parties are projected at around seventeen or eighteen seats after a record turnout in Arab communities, and they could end up as kingmakers, though several parties in Eisenkot's camp have ruled out governing with them. Exit polls can miss, and soldiers' ballots are counted starting tomorrow. The A.P. says weeks of coalition bargaining are likely, and if no one can form a government, Israel could face yet another election, with Netanyahu staying on as caretaker.
// Story 6: Houthi claim on Ras Tanura; Iran
[ava] In the Gulf, Yemen's Houthis claim they hit Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery, the kingdom's biggest, with a ballistic missile. Saudi Arabia's energy ministry tells a different story: a limited fire caused by debris from intercepted drones, quickly put out, with no injuries and no effect on supplies. Neither account has been independently verified.
// Story 7: Flávio Bolsonaro reportedly leaves Brazil
[ava] In Brazil, multiple media outlets report that Flávio Bolsonaro, who lost Sunday's presidential runoff and hasn't conceded, has left for the United States just as a court deadline to surrender his passports expired. His whereabouts haven't been confirmed and his spokesperson isn't commenting. He's under investigation for alleged corruption and money laundering, which he denies, and he has claimed fraud in the vote without offering evidence.
// Story 8: US-Cuba at the UN
[ava] And at the United Nations, a rare shouting match: Cuba's foreign minister, Bruno Rodríguez, called U.S. Ambassador Mike Waltz a liar during a debate on the American embargo, and Waltz shot back, this is America, back up. Washington says its pressure aims to force political change in Havana; Havana calls the embargo economic warfare. The General Assembly holds its annual vote on condemning the embargo tomorrow.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 11: Amazon tokenmaxxing memo
[andrew] In A.I., an Amazon engineering memo seen by C.N.B.C. pushes back on a growing habit inside tech companies: measuring A.I. adoption by how many tokens people burn or how much code they generate. Amazon calls those metrics deeply flawed, because they track activity, not results, and can encourage what's being called tokenmaxxing, running up usage just to look busy. It says a team not getting value from today's tools is, quote, a finding, not a failing.
// Story 12: Tokenizer tax
[andrew] And a study reported by Rest of World finds A.I. models can be much more expensive to use in some languages than others: the costliest languages take from one and a half to five times as many tokens as the cheapest for the same content. Researchers call it a tokenizer tax on non-English speakers.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 13: Microsoft drops eMudhra roots
[ava] Cyber desk. Microsoft has pushed out emergency Windows updates that stop trusting six root certificates from the Indian certificate authority eMudhra. It follows the discovery of eight eMudhra signing keys so weak they could be cracked almost instantly, which could have let attackers forge certificates for real websites. Most Windows machines get this kind of change automatically; the updates are for offline or locked-down systems. Edge drops the same roots today.
// Story 14: Warp breach
[ava] And Warp, the A.I. coding tool popular with developers, says an attack on one of its vendors exposed A.I. agent logs and usage data for roughly one percent of users. C.E.O. Zach Lloyd says the attack appeared to be run by an automated agent swarm. CrowdStrike is investigating; affected users have been notified and should watch their accounts.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Microsoft releases Windows OOB updates to distrust eMudhra certificates" (Oct 27); BleepingComputer, "Warp discloses breach exposing AI agent logs, user telemetry data" (Oct 27)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
[ava] Closer to home, Londonderry voters will see a property-tax-cap question on next Tuesday's ballot, required under the state law known as the Taxpayer Protection Act. If it passes, the school district's tax levy could grow each year only by inflation plus new construction, starting in July twenty twenty-seven. School business administrator Amity Small told the School Board that, with costs for contracts, utilities and salaries rising, the cap could mean about two million dollars in cuts just to keep current services. Options floated include ending some in-town bus service, fewer A.P. courses, and trims to sports, music and clubs. Supporters of the law see it as a check on fast-rising property taxes, and voters could later override the cap. District officials stress no cuts have been decided.
> source: Londonderry Times, "District Outlines Potential Effects of Proposed Tax Cap" (Oct 25)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, the wrong road that turned out to be the right one. In northwest Georgia, a driver named Casey followed a G.P.S. detour down an unfamiliar highway Saturday night and saw smoke from the top of a hill. It was the home of James and Larita Burke, foster parents who have cared for about five hundred children over the years. Mr. Burke had dozed off in his recliner; Casey ran inside, woke him, and helped get all eleven children and a grandchild out before the house burned. The family lost nearly everything, but the community has rallied, and the Burkes are looking for a place big enough for all of them while they rebuild.
> source: Good News Network, "Family That's Fostered 500 Children Saved from Housefire Thanks to Stranger's Wrong Turn" (Oct 27), citing Local 3 News
