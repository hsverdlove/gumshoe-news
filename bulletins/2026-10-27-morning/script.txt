## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: California ballots back to Judge Carter; Washington letter
[ava] We start with the fight over California mail ballots. Late Monday, a Ninth Circuit panel voted two to one to dismiss the Justice Department's emergency appeal, without ruling on the merits. The majority said the department hadn't given the trial judge, David Carter, a fair chance to act before appealing. So the case goes back to Judge Carter today, when he hears arguments on the department's request to set aside ballots from about twenty thousand voters it calls likely noncitizens. He's promised to rule promptly.
[ava] Meanwhile, the department sent Washington state a letter asking it to set aside ballots from four hundred five voters it says may be noncitizens. It's a request, not a lawsuit; the state is reviewing it and questions the federal data, while the administration says it's protecting the vote.
// Story 2: FAMU shooting arrest
[ava] In Tallahassee, police have arrested a fifteen-year-old, Anthony Rivers the Third, in the shooting near Florida A and M's stadium after Saturday's homecoming game. He faces eleven counts of attempted second-degree murder, and prosecutors want to charge him as an adult. Police say a fight came first and the victims weren't the intended targets. Eleven people were wounded, including children, and one remains in critical condition.
// Story 3: Walter Koenig
[ava] And Walter Koenig, who played Ensign Pavel Chekov on the original Star Trek and in seven Star Trek films, has died at ninety. His family says he died of natural causes at his North Hollywood home on Sunday.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Israel votes
[ava] Overseas, Israelis are voting today in their first national election since the October seventh attacks. Turnout by ten in the morning, local time, was sixteen point seven percent, the highest at that hour since at least nineteen ninety-six. The race is between Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud and former military chief Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party, with final forecasts giving Yashar a slight edge. Polls close at four this afternoon, our time, with exit polls right after. But no party has ever won a majority on its own, so forming a government could take weeks, and if no one can, Israel votes again.
// Story 5: Oleshky ceasefire
[ava] In Ukraine, a local ceasefire took effect at three this morning, Kyiv time, in the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky, where up to two thousand residents are trapped amid mines and drone strikes. First Lady Melania Trump's office says she brokered the talks between civilian negotiators. The pause is meant to let in food, medicine and water, then evacuate civilians, and both sides say it's holding. Fighting went on elsewhere, though: the AP says attacks on Kyiv and Kharkiv killed three people.
// Story 6: Iran diplomacy
[ava] On Iran, after Monday's four-way talks in Tehran ended with a promise to keep talking, a senior Pakistani official tells Reuters that Pakistan's army chief, with President Trump's blessing, presented Iran with a proposal meant to offer something to both sides, with Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Pakistan and Qatar as possible guarantors. No details were given. Iran's Foreign Ministry, though, says Washington isn't serious about negotiating. And the Saudi-led coalition says it shot down three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province overnight, with no casualties.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Meta's record stock sale, Nvidia
[andrew] Tech. Meta has raised the size of its stock sale to as much as ninety-seven and a half billion dollars, the largest equity fundraising in its history, to pay for A.I. data centers. It includes a twenty-five-billion-dollar share offering and a sixty-billion-dollar program to sell shares gradually over time. ## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Anthropic S-1 details
[andrew] In A.I., we now have a first full look inside Anthropic, from its I.P.O. filing. Revenue in the first half of this year was about sixteen point three billion dollars, roughly thirteen times a year earlier. On paper, Anthropic lost about eighty billion dollars over those six months, but most of that was a non-cash accounting charge tied to its convertible notes; Fortune calculates adjusted operating income of about one point six billion. It holds about a hundred twenty-two billion dollars in cash and short-term investments. The big caution: roughly six hundred thirty billion dollars in computing contracts, about seventy percent of them non-cancellable, so those costs are locked in whether or not sales keep soaring. Shares could trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker A N T as early as mid-November.
// Story 9: OpenAI's unverified ETH claim
[andrew] And OpenAI says its A.I. produced three major scientific results, the most eye-catching a claimed proof that the Exponential Time Hypothesis is false. That's a widely believed conjecture about how hard certain logic puzzles are to solve, and it underpins a lot of computer science. Treat it as unverified: an independent group of computer scientists, including two who first proposed the hypothesis in nineteen ninety-nine, will examine the proof, and OpenAI posted the materials publicly at their request.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: PHP2Shell KEV
[ava] Cyber desk. A quick update on PHP2Shell: the federal cyber agency, CISA, has added both flaws to its list of vulnerabilities being actively exploited and ordered federal agencies to patch by Thursday. If you run PHP, update now.
// Story 11: Tailworm
[ava] If you use Tailscale, the private networking app, take note. A researcher has published eleven issues, dubbed Tailworm, in Tailscale and the open-source headscale server. The main one lets someone who's already allowed to use Tailscale S.S.H. on a device escalate to full root control. That means an attacker needs a foothold first; it's not an open door from the internet. Tailscale calls the issues serious and says a fix is targeted within twenty-four hours, but as of this morning there's no patched release. Until then, review who your Tailscale S.S.H. rules allow in.
// Story 12: Amadey takedown
[ava] And Australia's signals intelligence agency says it got into the back end of an Amadey malware operation and cut the criminal off from about a hundred eighty thousand infected computers worldwide. Amadey steals passwords and personal data, often hidden in pirated software. The malware is still on those machines, but its operator lost control.
> source: BleepingComputer, "CISA confirms PHP2Shell vulnerabilities now exploited in attacks" (Oct 27); The Hacker News (Oct 27); ThreatWire, "Tailworm Tailscale SSH and headscale flaws: what is confirmed, what is not" (Oct 27); The Hacker News on Tailscale's investigation page (Oct 27); Tailscale changelog; The Hacker News, "Australia Gets Into Amadey Malware Backend" (Oct 27); ABC News Australia (Oct 27)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a nine-year-old's moon-landing memory, paid forward. Ken Schreiber watched Neil Armstrong walk on the moon in nineteen sixty-nine and went on to fly fighter jets for the Air Force and then jets for American Airlines. Now retired, he donated four hundred thousand dollars so the Auglaize County Historical Society could buy Armstrong's boyhood home in Wapakoneta, Ohio. The society plans to restore it to how it looked in the nineteen-forties and open it for tours by twenty thirty, Armstrong's hundredth birthday.
> source: People, "Man Who Watched Neil Armstrong Walk on the Moon When He Was 9 Spends $400,000 to Help Buy Astronaut's Childhood Home"; AP, "Historical society to restore Neil Armstrong's boyhood home and provide tours"; Los Angeles Times (Oct 19)
