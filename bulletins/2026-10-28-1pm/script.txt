## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Alaska spoiler report
[ava] Top story. The New York Times reports that people working with Mary Peltola's Democratic Senate campaign in Alaska, along with senior aides at the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, ran what they called a ballot composition project to recruit spoiler candidates, including a second, unrelated Dan Sullivan, to pull votes from Republican Senator Dan Sullivan. The paper cites interviews with twelve people, text messages, and documents prepared for a possible lawsuit. A key source, Peltola's former campaign manager Elisa Rios, who was fired in August, said Peltola wasn't directly involved but was aware; Rios later tried to retract her statements, and the Times says her account matched its other reporting. The Peltola campaign and the D.S.C.C. both deny recruiting any candidates, and the other Mr. Sullivan says he entered the race on his own. The Justice Department has already sued over his candidacy. Election Day is Tuesday.
// Story 2: O'Keefe and Higby charges
[ava] In Minnesota, Hennepin County Attorney Mary Moriarty charged conservative activists James O'Keefe and Cam Higby with felony unlawful voting and identity theft over hidden-camera videos from the August primary. Prosecutors say they gave poll workers the names of real registered voters while testing the state's voter-vouching law. Neither cast a ballot. Moriarty says starting the application under someone else's name is enough to break the law, and that probation is the likely penalty if convicted. Higby has said he was acting as a journalist testing the system, with no intent to commit fraud, and supporters call the case political payback. Secretary of State Steve Simon says the videos actually show the safeguards worked. Both are due in court in November.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Spain crash cause
[ava] Overseas, the toll from this morning's high-speed train crash near Urda, Spain, now stands at sixty-eight, the country's worst rail disaster since twenty thirteen, with fifteen people in critical condition. Transport Minister Óscar Puente says a computer failure knocked out the line's automatic safety system and stopped both trains. The rail network's control center then took manual control and cleared the Renfe train to resume normal speed, even though the Iryo train was stopped ahead. Puente called that decision inexplicable. Police are questioning the controller involved. Puente acknowledged the safety-system failure but rejected calls to resign; critics note it's the second deadly crash on this line this year. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez says the whole country is grieving.
// Story 4: Israel ballots
[ava] In Israel, the election committee now estimates up to four hundred eighty thousand soldiers', police and diplomats' ballots remain to be counted, worth around twelve seats, which could move the current tally of fifty-three seats for Netanyahu's bloc and forty-eight for Gadi Eisenkot's. The full count is due Thursday afternoon, with official results on November fourth.
// Story 5: Mada Masr
[ava] And in Egypt, a Cairo court ordered the closure of Mada Masr, one of the country's last independent news sites, and fined its editor, Lina Attalah, the maximum three million Egyptian pounds, about fifty-three thousand dollars, for running an unlicensed website. She was held for about seven hours until the fine was paid. Egypt's government says it's enforcing licensing law; press-freedom groups call it a crackdown. Mada Masr plans to appeal.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Anthropic drug development
[andrew] In A.I., Anthropic published early results from using Claude in drug development with six partners, including Bristol Myers Squibb. Claude generated about thirty-five thousand antibody designs in a month for Divergence Labs' food-allergy program, and a few early hits beat the standard medicine in lab tests. At Enveda, it caught every problem human reviewers found in manufacturing paperwork, plus seven they missed, and cut review time by almost ninety percent. Anthropic also bought a one-person company working on rare childhood muscular dystrophy. The caveat, in the company's own words: nearly all of this is preclinical, meaning not yet tested in people, and most A.I.-designed drug candidates have yet to prove themselves in trials.
> source: Anthropic, "Early progress on accelerating drug development with Claude" (Oct 27)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Edge logic-bug chain
[ava] Cyber desk. Researcher Orange Tsai has published how he broke out of Microsoft Edge's security sandbox at this year's Pwn2Own Berlin contest using only logic flaws, no memory corruption at all. He chained four bugs so that just visiting a malicious web page could run code on the computer, earning a hundred seventy-five thousand dollars. Microsoft patched the flaws right after the contest, so the takeaway is simple: keep Edge updated.
> source: The Hacker News, "Researcher Reveals How Logic Bugs Alone Cracked Microsoft Edge in $175,000 Pwn2Own Win" (Oct 2026); DARKNAVY blog on the Edge sandbox escape (Oct 2026)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, from Greenville, North Carolina. The JOY Community Center and Soup Kitchen has opened a free dental clinic right where it serves meals. Director Tom Quigley says the idea came years ago, when a guest couldn't chew the pork chops being served and asked for a peanut butter sandwich instead. Working with orthodontist Dr. Dennis Ross, they built two exam rooms with an X-ray machine. On the first surgery day they saw eight patients and pulled eighteen infected teeth, and five of those patients came back just to hug the team. As Quigley puts it, you can't feed people who can't chew.
