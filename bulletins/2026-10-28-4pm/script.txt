## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: FEC filing system
[ava] We start in Washington, where the Federal Election Commission's electronic filing system has been offline since Monday night after what the agency's own staff called a bad actor attempting to breach its systems. That shutdown blocks the twenty-four and forty-eight hour reports campaigns and outside groups must file in the final weeks, so new spending in the most expensive midterm on record is going unreported for now. The F.E.C. says it found the problem and is working on a fix, but hasn't said who was behind it or whether any data was taken. The agency doesn't run elections, so voting itself isn't affected.
// Story 2: Trump on Peltola (development of aired NYT story)
[ava] A follow-up on this morning's New York Times report about a spoiler-candidate effort in Alaska. President Trump posted that it was a fraud on the American public, and said both Democrat Mary Peltola and the second Dan Sullivan should be removed from the ballot and face legal consequences. Peltola's campaign and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee continue to deny recruiting anyone, and the other Mr. Sullivan says he ran on his own. It's not clear what legal path exists to remove a candidate six days before the election; a Justice Department lawsuit over his candidacy is already pending.
// California update
[ava] And in Los Angeles, Judge David Carter's written order in the California ballot case still hadn't been posted as of this hour; he's expected to name a special master rather than rule outright.
> source: NBC News via Reuters, "Hackers target FEC system, disrupting campaign finance disclosures" (Oct 27); CNBC, "FEC campaign finance system offline days before midterms after hackers reportedly target site" (Oct 28); Mediaite on President Trump's Truth Social post (Oct 28); AP, "Report: Peltola's campaign involved in effort to recruit second Dan Sullivan" (Oct 28); AP and ABC News on the Carter case (Oct 27-28)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Cyclone Arnab
[ava] Overseas, Cyclone Arnab came ashore on India's east coast early today, crossing the Andhra Pradesh coast near Yanam as a very severe cyclonic storm before weakening. The Times of India now counts nine dead in Andhra Pradesh, up from earlier official tallies of three to five, mostly from falling trees, collapsing walls and electrocution, and tens of thousands of coconut trees were flattened along with large areas of rice paddy. Neighboring Odisha evacuated about forty-four thousand people and reports no deaths.
// Story 4: Bolsonaro in Miami (development of aired story)
[ava] In the Americas, Flávio Bolsonaro, who lost Sunday's presidential runoff in Brazil, landed in Miami this morning with his family after ignoring a Brazilian Supreme Court order to hand over his passports in a money-laundering investigation he says is persecution. The State Department says it will grant the family visas, and President Trump said he approves. Brazil's President Lula called the departure shameful and said Bolsonaro should have stayed to prove his innocence.
// Story 5: Europe gas
[ava] And Europe's benchmark natural-gas price crossed one hundred euros per megawatt-hour today for the first time since the twenty twenty-two energy crisis, with liquefied gas supplies tight and storage low heading into winter. E.U. Energy Commissioner Dan Jørgensen warned of another energy crisis and said Brussels is weighing relief steps, including joint purchases and targeted support for households.

## Markets | sting=markets
[andrew] Now the closing bell, in depth. The Fed held its key rate at three and three-quarters to four percent, as expected, in a ten-to-two vote. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack wanted another quarter-point hike. The statement upgraded the economy to strong and said inflation remains elevated, and Chair Kevin Warsh said the Fed is ready to act if needed, without promising a December move. He also said one of next year's eight meetings will be devoted to strategy, not rates. Traders now put the odds of a December hike close to ninety percent.
[andrew] Stocks shrugged it off and finished higher. The S and P five hundred rose about three-tenths of a percent to seven thousand seven hundred forty-four. The Nasdaq also gained about three-tenths, to twenty-six thousand five hundred fifty, and the Dow added ninety-eight points, or two-tenths, to fifty-two thousand four hundred seventy-five. The Russell two thousand edged up two-tenths. Energy led the sectors through the afternoon, and real estate lagged.
[andrew] The star was Oracle, up about ten and a half percent after its investor day. It projected more than two hundred twenty-five billion dollars in revenue by fiscal twenty thirty, roughly thirty-five percent growth a year, and said it would cap new borrowing and stock sales at twenty billion dollars through then, easing worries about debt-fueled A.I. spending. Skeptics note it's a four-year forecast that hinges on A.I. demand. Chip stocks rose for a third day after SK Hynix's record profit, and GE Vernova beat estimates, raised its outlook and agreed to buy grid-equipment maker Trench Group for nine billion dollars.
[andrew] In bonds, short-term yields dipped after Warsh spoke while the ten-year Treasury rose about five basis points to just over five percent. Oil jumped: U.S. crude settled up about two point nine percent near a hundred two dollars eighty, on renewed Yemen fighting, attacks on Saudi energy sites, and a surprise drop in U.S. inventories. Gold was little changed near four thousand seventy-eight an ounce, and bitcoin was up about eight-tenths of a percent, around eighty-one thousand four hundred.
[andrew] After the bell: Microsoft, Alphabet, Ford, T-Mobile, eBay, Chipotle and ServiceNow. Tomorrow brings the Fed's preferred inflation gauge and the first read on third-quarter growth.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Apple touchscreen MacBook Pro
[andrew] Tech. As expected, Apple announced its first touchscreen Mac at today's event in Los Angeles: the MacBook Pro with what it calls a PROLED XDR display, a tandem OLED screen with the touch sensor built into the panel. You can scroll, pinch to zoom and draw with a finger, and the iPhone's Dynamic Island moves to the Mac. It runs M5 Pro or M5 Max chips, starts at twenty-eight ninety-nine for the fourteen-inch and thirty-three ninety-nine for the sixteen-inch, and ships November sixth. It reverses years of Apple resisting touch on laptops; the open questions are whether apps get redesigned for fingers, and the notably higher prices.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Gemini 4 Carbon
[andrew] In A.I., Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon, just two weeks after its last model. Google calls it its strongest yet, aimed at long, multi-day coding work and cybersecurity, and says it leads benchmarks for finding, exploiting and patching software flaws. Because those skills cut both ways, Google is releasing it first only to a group of vetted cyber defenders, and says it's taking part in the government's voluntary pre-release testing. Those are Google's own numbers; outside testing will tell.

Nvidia and the Justice Department declined to comment, and an inquiry is not an accusation of wrongdoing.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Volt Typhoon Silent Train
[ava] Cyber desk. Researchers at Lumen's Black Lotus Labs say the Chinese state-backed group Volt Typhoon has spent three years using a hidden relay network they call Silent Train, chains of hacked home and small-office routers it builds for one operation and wipes afterward, to disguise attacks on U.S. telecoms, utilities, defense contractors and government agencies. They say it used at least four previously unknown flaws in network-management products from Cisco, VeloCloud, N-able and Versa. The advice for everyone: keep router firmware updated and don't expose admin pages to the internet.
// Story 10: Sony So-net breach
[ava] And Sony's Japanese internet provider, So-net, says hackers got into its member database and exposed about two point six million customer records, including names, phone numbers and emails, plus email passwords for about fifty-four thousand users. No credit card data was taken.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Volt Typhoon hides zero-day attacks using 'Silent Train' framework" (Oct 28), citing Lumen Black Lotus Labs; BleepingComputer, "Sony ISP subsidiary data breach exposes 2.5 million customer records" (Oct 28); Help Net Security (Oct 28); So-net notice (Oct 27)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, from Ohio, where some of the most dedicated wildlife rescuers are behind bars. Through a partnership with the Ohio Wildlife Center, about two hundred incarcerated people in five prisons raise orphaned animals: opossums at one, songbirds at another, and at the Ohio Reformatory for Women in Marysville, baby rabbits. This year the women there raised more than three hundred, bottle-feeding bunnies as small as a tenth of a pound, with about a ninety percent survival rate. Emily Bush, due for release early next year, says it taught her her worth, and with new state certification and apprenticeships she plans a career in animal rehab, plus a mealworm farm in her grandmother's barn.
> source: WOSU / The Ohio Newsroom via WYSU, "In Ohio prisons, incarcerated people and orphaned animals rehabilitate each other" (Oct 28)
