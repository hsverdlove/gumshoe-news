## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Carter special master
[ava] We start with the California ballot case, where there's a new turn but still no final ruling. After a hearing of more than three hours in Los Angeles, Judge David Carter said he plans to appoint a special master, an independent expert, to examine how the Justice Department built its list of about twenty thousand voters it calls likely noncitizens, and whether counties could even separate those ballots. He called the request a monumental change to an election already under way, and said he'd issue an order today. Federal prosecutors say citizens are being disenfranchised in real time; the state and Los Angeles County say there's no reliable evidence the voters are ineligible and no workable way to comply.
// Story 2: Alexander brothers
[ava] In New York, a federal judge sentenced Tal, Oren and Alon Alexander, the luxury real-estate brothers convicted of drugging and raping dozens of women, to thirty years in prison each. Eleven accusers spoke in court before the sentence.
// Story 3: Ted Bundy cold case
[ava] And investigators in Utah say DNA evidence has tied the serial killer Ted Bundy to the nineteen seventy-four killing of seventeen-year-old Melissa Ann Smith, closing a cold case fifty-two years to the day after her body was found. Bundy was executed in nineteen eighty-nine.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Spain train crash
[ava] Overseas, breaking news from Spain. Just after nine this morning, local time, two high-speed trains collided near Urda, about a hundred miles south of Madrid. Early reports say a Renfe train ran into the back of a stopped train from the private operator Iryo, derailing two cars. Five hundred forty-four people were aboard. Regional officials first confirmed ten deaths and warned that would rise; Spain's Efe news agency now cites regional sources saying at least thirty, and some Spanish outlets report as many as forty-six. Those numbers aren't final. The cause isn't known; early reports point to a possible signalling error. It's the second deadly crash involving the same two operators this year, after forty-six died near Adamuz in January.
// Story 5: Israel count
[ava] In Israel, the count continues after Tuesday's vote. With about eighty-five percent of ballot boxes counted, Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc has fifty-four seats, short of the sixty-one needed to govern, and Gadi Eisenkot's bloc has forty-nine. Arab-led parties have about seventeen. Likud is the largest party. Soldiers' ballots are still to be counted, so those numbers will move. Netanyahu says it's not over; Eisenkot calls him a caretaker without a mandate. Expect weeks of coalition talks.
// Story 6: Iran: WSJ report and Qatar draft
[ava] On Iran, two tracks at once. The Wall Street Journal reports the U.S. has told several Gulf states to prepare for a possible short, intense military campaign against Iran after next week's midterms, with more air defenses and ships headed there. The Journal says President Trump hasn't decided, and the administration hasn't commented publicly. Meanwhile, an official tells Reuters that Qatari mediators gave Iran a new draft deal on Sunday: a seven-day plan of daily confidence-building steps by both sides, backed by guarantor countries, with Tehran's answer expected by week's end.
// Story 7: Bolsonaro exit; Belgrade attack
[ava] In South America, Argentina's migration authority says Flávio Bolsonaro, who lost Brazil's presidential runoff, flew to Argentina and then on to Ecuador with his family, despite a court order to surrender his passports. The State Department says they'll get U.S. visas. President Lula called it shameful; Bolsonaro, under investigation for corruption, denies wrongdoing.
[ava] And in Belgrade, police shot and killed a young man who attacked an officer guarding Israel's embassy with knives overnight. Serbia calls it terrorism and says the attacker was an Islamic State supporter. The officer had minor injuries.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 12: Anthropic exploit chains
[ava] Cyber desk. Anthropic's red team says an unreleased internal A.I. model found flaws in Chrome, Safari, Android, Windows and Linux, and chained them into working exploits. One opened a program on a Mac running Chrome just by loading a web page. Everything was reported to the vendors and is already patched, so update your browser and phone if you haven't. The Hacker News notes it can't verify how much the model did on its own.

And Cloudflare disclosed that one A.I. model on its platform briefly mixed up customers' data, with a hundred fifty-five responses in late summer containing pieces of other users' conversations. It's fixed.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a cavity fix with no drill and no needle. A University of Michigan-led trial of eight hundred thirty children under six, published in JAMA Pediatrics, found that silver diamine fluoride, a liquid painted onto a cavity in seconds, stopped decay in more than half of affected baby teeth when it was reapplied every six months. Dentists have used it off-label for years; this is the U.S. data the maker needs to seek F.D.A. approval to treat cavities. The one drawback: it permanently darkens the decayed spot. For kids who'd otherwise need sedation or surgery, researchers say it could be a game changer.
> source: Good News Network (Oct 27), citing the University of Michigan and a Phase 3 trial in JAMA Pediatrics
