## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Alaska probe (development of aired story)
[ava] In Alaska, Republican Governor Mike Dunleavy has ordered the state's Department of Public Safety to investigate whether Democrat Mary Peltola's Senate campaign broke state law by helping recruit a little-known candidate who shares Senator Dan Sullivan's name, which critics say was meant to confuse voters. The governor says the probe should decide whether criminal charges or civil action are warranted. It follows President Trump's call for a criminal investigation. Peltola's campaign and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee deny any involvement, and it comes five days before the election.
// Story 2: AP on Iran strike warning
[ava] And the Associated Press, citing officials, reports the U.S. has told Middle East allies to prepare for possible renewed strikes on Iran after next Tuesday's midterms, and for retaliation by Iran or its proxies against energy sites and other infrastructure. Officials say the President hasn't decided, and a new operation could last several days. That sits alongside this morning's reports of ongoing talks toward a temporary deal, so both tracks appear to be running at once.

## Markets | sting=markets
[andrew] Markets, in depth. Stocks opened lower: about half an hour in, the S and P five hundred was down six-tenths of a percent, headed for its first loss in four days, the Dow off about two hundred points, and the Nasdaq down nearly two percent.
[andrew] The eight-thirty numbers cut both ways. The economy grew at a three point four percent annual pace last quarter, faster than the three point two expected and well up from two point two in the spring, led by consumer spending and business investment. But the Fed's preferred inflation gauge ran hot as expected: prices up three point six percent from a year ago in September, the core rate three point one, both rising from August, with energy driving the headline number. That's an awkward pair for the Fed, which held rates at three and three-quarters to four percent yesterday, with two officials voting for a hike.
[andrew] The big mover is Alphabet, down roughly eleven to thirteen percent in early trading on its earnings miss and nine billion dollars of negative free cash flow, and it's pulling chip and A.I. stocks with it. Microsoft has given back much of its overnight gain in the tech selloff. Bonds are acting as the safe haven: the ten-year Treasury yield is around five percent, slightly lower, and Brent crude slipped to about a hundred and eleven dollars on the Iran diplomacy reports.
[andrew] In Europe, the E.C.B. held its deposit rate at two and a half percent but warned surging energy prices raise inflation risks, and traders read that as keeping a December hike in play. The euro fell to its lowest since May. After the bell, Amazon reports, along with Coinbase and Intel. The bull case: growth is strong and Microsoft shows A.I. spending can pay. The bear case: inflation is stuck well above target and Alphabet shows how fast the spending can eat cash.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Gemini 4 Carbon
[andrew] In A.I., Google introduced Gemini four Carbon, a model built to help defenders find software flaws, check whether they can be exploited, and write the fixes. Google says it leads key security benchmarks, fixing about seventy-one percent of flaws in one test. For now it's only open to a vetted group of cyber defenders, with governments and critical infrastructure first in line, because the same skills could help attackers.
> source: Google blog, Introducing Gemini 4 Carbon (Oct 29); The Hacker News and Help Net Security (Oct 29); Google DeepMind Fairwind Program page

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: DHS leak
[ava] Cyber desk. A hacktivist group calling itself the Department of Peace has leaked records on more than forty-one thousand Homeland Security employees and contractors, mostly at Customs and Border Protection and I.C.E., including names, work phones and emails, office locations, and which internal systems each person can use. The group says it got the data through the department's Okta sign-on service and framed it as payback for stepped-up immigration enforcement. Okta says it has no evidence its systems were compromised, and D.H.S. says it's responding. WIRED reports the records include some undercover agents, raising safety concerns for officers.
// Story 10: Base freezers
[ava] And Reuters reports the Pentagon is treating a strange string of freezer failures at military base grocery stores as a suspected cyberattack. At least six bases saw freezers switch into defrost mode in late August, spoiling food. Investigators are working with French authorities, who are probing a similar supermarket attack. No attacker has been identified, and the cause isn't confirmed.
> source: SecurityWeek, WIRED, CyberScoop and CNBC on the DHS leak (Oct 28-29); Reuters via Investing.com and The Straits Times on the freezer failures (Oct 29)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 11: Brooklyn crash and Dusk and Darkness
[ava] Here in New York, a Sanitation Department garbage truck struck and killed a thirty-year-old woman, Siobhan Lynch, as she crossed Bay Parkway at Cropsey Avenue in Brooklyn last evening, about forty minutes after sunset. The driver stayed at the scene and hasn't been charged; police are investigating. The Mamdani administration is expected to launch its Dusk and Darkness campaign today, pairing education and traffic enforcement, since the city says traffic deaths peak on fall and winter evenings. With clocks falling back early Sunday, it'll be dark an hour earlier, so drivers, slow down, and walkers, stay visible.
