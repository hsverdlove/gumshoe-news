## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Trump in South Carolina
[ava] Story one. President Trump campaigned in Rock Hill, South Carolina, this afternoon for Senator Darline Graham, who was appointed in July after the death of her brother, Lindsey Graham, and is now in a closer-than-expected race against Democrat Annie Andrews, a pediatrician. Trump told the crowd Graham has "the gene" and urged Republicans to turn out. Andrews is pitching herself as a change candidate, telling voters to "try something new for once." South Carolina hasn't sent a Democrat to the Senate in nearly three decades. Trump also renewed his call for Alaska's Mary Peltola to be removed from the ballot and prosecuted over the second Dan Sullivan; Peltola calls the allegations a distraction, and no charges have been filed.
// Story 2: Boulder plea
[ava] Story two. In Denver, Mohamed Soliman pleaded guilty to federal hate crime charges for the June twenty twenty-five firebombing of a Boulder, Colorado, march supporting hostages held in Gaza. One person was killed and about a dozen hurt. Prosecutors say he targeted the marchers because they were Jewish. He's already serving life on state charges, and federal sentencing is set for January.
// Story 3: Press access filing
[ava] Story three. CNN, MS NOW and Politico told a federal court the White House is defying a judge's order to lift its ban on their reporters, saying they're still kept out of key spaces where the president appears. They call it retaliation for coverage he dislikes and ask the court to make its order permanent. The White House has argued it has wide discretion over who gets access to the president in limited spaces.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Israel interference probe
[ava] Story four. Israel's election committee chair, Justice Noam Sohlberg, has asked the Shin Bet security service to investigate suspected foreign funding and vote-buying, with Israeli media reporting the focus includes possible Qatari and Turkish money to boost Arab turnout. Netanyahu's Likud party has gone further, claiming Arab-led parties "stole" the election. The committee itself says voting was lawful and calls claims of systematic fraud baseless, Qatar denies any meddling, and the opposition accuses Netanyahu of demonizing Arab citizens. No finding has been made.
// Story 5: Bosnia helicopter
[ava] Story five. An Italian Chinook helicopter serving with the European Union's peacekeeping force in Bosnia crashed during routine training near Brčko this afternoon, killing four crew members and injuring eleven, who are reported stable. No civilians were hurt. The cause is under investigation, and Italy's prime minister, Giorgia Meloni, sent condolences.
// Story 6: Iran drone claim
[ava] Story six. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claim they used electronic warfare to seize a U.S. surveillance drone near the Strait of Hormuz and land it intact, and released video of what appears to be an American-made target drone. U.S. Central Command calls the report false, saying the Guards have "repeatedly lied," though it didn't say whether it lost a drone for any reason. Neither side's account has been independently verified.
// Story 7: Ukraine Neptune strike
[ava] And story seven. Ukraine's navy says its Long Neptune missiles hit Marinovka air base in Russia's Volgograd region, destroying two Su-thirty-four fighter-bombers, with open-source monitors reporting two Russian personnel killed. That's Ukraine's claim; Russia hasn't confirmed losses.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: Mistral Large 4
[andrew] Story nine, A.I. France's Mistral has released the full weights of Mistral Large four, a trillion-parameter model nicknamed "Le Chonk," so companies and governments can download it and run it on their own servers. Mistral is pitching it for cyber defense, arguing organizations shouldn't depend on outside labs that could cut off access. It claims performance comparable to China's leading open model at about half the compute per task. Those are Mistral's own benchmarks, and open weights cut both ways: defenders get the tool, but so can attackers, a risk Mistral says is manageable.
> source: TechCrunch, Reuters and Mistral on Mistral Large 4's open weights (Oct 29)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: Freezer hack probe
[ava] Story ten, cyber desk. Reuters reports the Pentagon is treating a string of freezer failures that spoiled food at at least six military base grocery stores in August as a suspected hack. At Fort Huachuca, Arizona, the freezers didn't just stop, they switched into defrost mode and heated the food overnight. Investigators are talking with French police about a similar attack that overrode freezers at more than three hundred fifty French supermarkets this month. No culprit has been named, and the probe hasn't reached public conclusions.
// Story 11: Chrome extensions
[ava] Story eleven. Researchers at Socket found three hundred nineteen Chrome extensions, with about thirteen million installs combined, that quietly hijack your default search engine and route searches through their own servers for ad money. They pose as PDF converters, safe search or A.I. search tools. Most have been pulled from Google's store, but if your searches suddenly look odd, check your extensions.
> source: Reuters via Military Times on the commissary freezer probe (Oct 29); BleepingComputer on Socket's Chrome extension report (Oct 29)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 12: Bus fare enforcement
[ava] Story twelve, here in New York. Enforcement of the three-dollar bus fare is effectively on pause. The M T A has benched its three hundred fifty EAGLE fare agents pending an investigation after videos showed confrontations, including one where agents pinned a Brooklyn school aide who says he had paid. The N Y P D says it has no plans to step in. The M T A says it loses more than three hundred million dollars a year to bus fare beaters, and transit advocates warn that could mean service cuts; Mayor Mamdani's office points to the episode as a case for free buses, though he hasn't said how to replace the lost money.
// Story 13: E-bike fire and Marathon
[ava] In Mott Haven, a fire sparked by an e-bike battery at the Patterson Houses killed a boy and left four relatives in critical condition; the fire commissioner says the bike blocked the only way out. It's the city's sixth lithium-ion battery death this year. And Marathon Sunday is coming: more than sixty thousand runners, with the Verrazzano's upper level closing at eleven Saturday night, and buses unable to cross Fifth Avenue during the race.
> source: Gothamist on the EAGLE team pause (Oct 29); New York Explained daily brief (Oct 29), citing Gothamist, City Limits and NBC New York on the Patterson Houses fire and NYPD and MTA marathon plans
