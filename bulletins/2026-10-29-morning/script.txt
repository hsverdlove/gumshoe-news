## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Carter denies DOJ ballot request (development of aired story)
[ava] We start with a ruling in the California ballot case. Judge David Carter last night denied the Justice Department's request to make California set aside the ballots of up to twenty thousand people the department calls likely noncitizens. In the end he didn't appoint the special master he'd floated. He wrote that the request would wreak havoc on an election already under way, with more than three point seven million Californians having voted, and that a segregated vote is lasting harm. The administration says it's trying to keep noncitizens from voting, as federal law requires; state and county officials say the list is riddled with errors, with an early sample turning up many people who say they were born in the U.S. The department can appeal.
// Story 2: Closing midterm pitches
[ava] With the midterms five days away, both parties sent their biggest names out. President Trump rallied in Chillicothe, Ohio, for Senator Jon Husted, urging supporters to vote and again attacking Alaska Democrat Mary Peltola. Former President Barack Obama campaigned in Georgia for Senator Jon Ossoff, criticizing the President's focus on personal wealth and White House renovations. An Associated Press analysis this morning finds immigration enforcement has become less of a political advantage for Republicans as more voters sour on the tactics; the White House argues the crackdown is delivering what it promised.
> source: AP on Judge Carter's ruling (Oct 28); Politico (Oct 28); Bloomberg Law (Oct 29); ABC News on the Carter hearing (Oct 28); AP news summary on the Trump and Obama rallies (Oct 29); AP analysis on immigration politics (Oct 29)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Spain crash update (development of aired story)
[ava] Overseas, the death toll from Spain's high-speed train collision near Urda has risen to sixty-nine, with fifty-eight people being treated, and Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has declared three days of national mourning. The BBC reports audio showing that after a computer failure knocked out the line's signalling system, the rail operator's control center authorized the Renfe train to resume normal speed, despite a stop signal and a stopped Iryo train ahead. Judicial and technical investigations are under way, and no final cause has been established.
// Story 4: Israel final count
[ava] In Israel, final election results could come as soon as this evening, once about half a million double-envelope ballots, from soldiers, diplomats and others, are counted. Latest projections give Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc fifty-three seats, Gadi Eisenkot's bloc forty-eight, and Arab-led parties nineteen, so neither side has the sixty-one needed. Likud has accused Arab-led parties of fraud; the election committee reports few irregularities, and critics call the claims an attempt to delegitimize Arab voters.
// Story 5: Iran: Semafor
[ava] On Iran, Semafor reports the administration is negotiating toward a temporary peace deal, and the President acknowledged that Vice President Vance wrote to Tehran, though he played it down as humanitarian. The same official told Semafor the military could resume strikes shortly after the midterms if talks fail, with many in the administration skeptical and the President the most open to it. Separately, Al Arabiya reports Iran wants to shorten the proposed seven-day plan to four days, with mediators discussing a meeting in Turkey before November fourth. None of this is officially confirmed.
// Story 6: Dnipro strike
[ava] In Ukraine, a Russian jet-powered drone struck a platform at Dnipro's railway station this morning as a train prepared to leave for Zaporizhzhia, killing at least thirteen people and injuring at least five. Officials say people were still on the platform despite an air-raid alert.
// Story 7: Lagarde
[ava] And in Frankfurt, European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde says she'll step down on February seventh, about nine months before her term ends. The bank gave no reason; the hunt for a successor starts today. The E.C.B. announces its rate decision later this morning and is expected to hold.

## Markets | sting=markets
[andrew] Markets, in depth. Futures are split: Dow futures up about three-tenths of a percent, S and P futures down about four-tenths, and Nasdaq one hundred futures down more than one percent.
[andrew] The reason is Alphabet, down about ten percent before the bell. If that holds, it's the biggest post-earnings drop since it went public, erasing roughly four hundred billion dollars in value. Earnings of two dollars twelve a share missed estimates of about three dollars, search revenue came in light, and free cash flow went deeply negative, minus nine billion dollars, as A.I. spending surged, while Google kept its full-year capital spending plan of a hundred ninety-five to two hundred five billion. To be fair to Google, its cloud business beat expectations handily.
[andrew] Microsoft went the other way, up three and a half to four percent. Revenue of about ninety-two billion dollars and its cloud business beat, its capital spending came in below forecasts, and free cash flow stayed positive. One analyst summed it up: Microsoft is building out in a measured way, while Alphabet had a messy quarter. The selloff spilled into chips overnight, with Nvidia, A.M.D. and Micron lower after hours, and Samsung fell four percent in Seoul despite a fifteen-fold jump in chip profit.
[andrew] At eight-thirty: September's P.C.E. inflation index, the Fed's preferred gauge, expected to show prices up three point six percent from a year ago, and core up three point one. We also get the first estimate of third-quarter growth, expected at about three point two percent annualized, plus weekly jobless claims. Oil dipped on the Iran diplomacy report. After the close, Amazon reports, and Eli Lilly, Mastercard, Caterpillar and Intel are on deck today too. Bulls see Microsoft as proof A.I. can pay; bears see Alphabet as the warning about cash burn.

## Technology | sting=tech

[andrew] Tech. Palmer Luckey's defense company Anduril and Qualcomm have unveiled BlueChip, a rugged laptop for the military, government and first responders that they say will be designed and assembled in the U.S., with circuit boards built at Anduril's Ohio plant and Snapdragon chips partly made in Arizona. ## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: GPT-6.1 Terra
[andrew] In A.I., OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra, a cheaper model for coding, writing, image understanding and operating a computer, at about half the price of its bigger sibling and with a context window over a million tokens. Early benchmarks put it a bit behind OpenAI's top model on vision and noticeably weaker on command-line tasks.

[andrew] Google DeepMind showed off Genie 4, a research preview that generates interactive virtual worlds in real time, now with sound, music and dialogue you can steer by talking to it. And OpenAI says its models produced results on three hard science problems, including the Kakeya conjecture in math. Those claims still need review by outside experts.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 11: Cisco ASA
[ava] Cyber desk. Cisco has patched a firewall flaw that attackers are already exploiting. It lets someone with no login remotely crash Cisco's A.S.A. and Firepower firewalls through their remote-access V.P.N., knocking networks offline. SecurityWeek says it's the second zero-day in Cisco's firewall V.P.N. since August. If you run one, update now; there's no complete workaround.
// Story 12: Alpine and GitHub npm firewall
[ava] The Hacker News reports someone tried a top-severity remote-takeover exploit against the GitLab server of Alpine Linux, a popular base for software containers. Alpine had updated GitLab, but an unpatched Ruby component left it exposed, so it rebuilt the server and reset all passwords. On the defense side, GitHub launched a preview of an npm package firewall for enterprise customers that blocks malicious or risky JavaScript packages before they're installed.
> source: The Hacker News on Cisco's exploited ASA/FTD flaw (Oct 29); Cisco security advisory; SecurityWeek (Oct 2026); The Hacker News on Alpine Linux's GitLab server and GitHub's npm firewall (Oct 29)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 13: Bronx fire
[ava] Here in New York, a sad story from the Bronx. A ten-year-old boy, Zion Maine, died after an e-scooter's lithium-ion battery caught fire near the front door of his family's eleventh-floor apartment at the Patterson Houses in Mott Haven early Wednesday, trapping them inside. Four relatives remain hospitalized in critical condition. Fire officials say these batteries have caused hundreds of fires in the city this year; never charge or store them by an exit.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, from Greenville, North Carolina. The JOY Community Center and Soup Kitchen noticed a regular guest couldn't chew the pork chops at dinner and asked for a peanut butter sandwich instead. Its director, a former paramedic, decided a soup kitchen should fix teeth too. Now, with a veteran orthodontist, it has opened what they say is the state's first permanent free dental clinic, with two exam rooms and an X-ray machine. On its first surgery day it saw eight patients and pulled eighteen infected teeth, and five came back just to hug the staff. As the director put it, you can't feed people who can't chew.
> source: WITN on the JOY Dental Clinic in Greenville, N.C. (Oct 28)
