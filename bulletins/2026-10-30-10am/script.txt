## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Fired general referred for prosecution
[ava] Story one. The Pentagon has referred a fired three-star general to the Justice Department over alleged leaks. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth removed Army Lieutenant General Brett Sylvia this week as the Joint Staff's top planner. Axios, citing an internal investigative report, says Sylvia failed four polygraph tests and then admitted on video to sharing classified information about Ukraine aid and Iran with two reporters, partly over Signal and once at a diner in Arlington, Virginia. No charges have been filed, and Sylvia hasn't commented. The Pentagon says it acted to protect sensitive information; some colleagues and critics question the allegations and see a wider purge of senior officers, coming as the President weighs renewed strikes on Iran.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Novaya Gazeta
[ava] Story four. Novaya Gazeta, Russia's best-known independent newspaper, says authorities are forcing it to close. "We have been killed," its editors wrote; on November ninth they'll turn off the lights in their Moscow office of thirty years and shut down the servers. Founded with part of Mikhail Gorbachev's Nobel Prize money, the paper lost several journalists, including Anna Politkovskaya, to killings. It says its website was blocked, its license revoked, its office is being evicted, and an editor is under house arrest awaiting trial. Moscow labels it a foreign agent, which it says guards against outside interference; critics say the label silences dissent. An exile edition, Novaya Gazeta Europe, will carry on.
// Story 5: Hormuz limpet mines (development)
[ava] Story five, an update on that tanker fire in the Strait of Hormuz we mentioned this morning. Britain's maritime security agency, U K M T O, now says a small boat attached two explosive devices to the ship's hull, which maritime security sources describe as limpet mines, about twenty-five nautical miles northeast of Khasab, Oman. The blasts briefly disabled the tanker; the crew is safe and the ship continued to port. Analysts call it the first known successful attack of its kind since the war began, and no one has been officially blamed. Semafor reports many in the administration are skeptical a deal with Iran can be reached.
// Story 6: Canada-EU alliance

The A P frames it as hedging against U.S. trade pressure; the Trump administration says its tariffs are about fairer deals for American workers.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Wikimedia copyright brief
[andrew] Story eight, A.I. The Wikimedia Foundation, which runs Wikipedia, has filed a friend-of-the-court brief in the New York Times and other publishers' copyright lawsuits against OpenAI and Microsoft, and it's taking neither side. Wikimedia argues that training A.I. models on copyrighted work should count as fair use when the result is something genuinely new. But when a chatbot reproduces articles in place of the original, it says that should require a license, including Wikipedia's own requirement to give credit. The Times wants a narrower reading of fair use, while OpenAI calls regurgitation a rare bug. Wikimedia's worry is that an all-or-nothing ruling either hobbles useful tools or drains the volunteer web.
> source: The Verge, "Wikipedia's operator says a bad AI copyright ruling could threaten the open web" (Oct 30); Wikimedia Foundation statement (Oct 30)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Backdoored Claude Code extension
[ava] Story nine, cyber desk, and this one's for developers. A tampered version of Anthropic's Claude Code extension, version two point one point three one three, was served through Cursor's extension marketplace for about eleven hours on Thursday, from roughly five-thirty in the morning to four-thirty in the afternoon Eastern. Cursor says the malicious build was not made by Anthropic, reached at most five thousand users, and that it will contact them directly. Once loaded, it collects machine details, adds an attacker's S S H key labeled "svc-telemetry," and tries to open a persistent reverse tunnel. Cursor says bugs usually stopped the tunnel without admin rights, and the attacker's server is now offline. Anthropic pulled the version, and how the build was tampered with is still unknown. Important: updating or uninstalling does not remove the payload. If you ran that version, check your S S H authorized keys, use Cursor's cleanup scripts, and rotate credentials from a clean machine.
> source: Cursor security notice on Anthropic.claude-code 2.1.313 (Oct 29-30); Help Net Security (Oct 30); The Verge (Oct 30); Coralogix threat advisory (Oct 30); oss-security mailing list (Oct 30)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a small-world story from China. Years ago, a middle-schooler named He Ziqi took an English reading test about a Beijing strawberry farmer's ad, complete with a phone number. Curious, he looked it up. It was real: the farmer, Mr. Wang, had no idea his ad was an exam question, but for over a decade ten to twenty students a year had been calling him.
