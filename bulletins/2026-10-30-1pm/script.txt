## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Trump rallies back on
[ava] Story one, an update on the campaign trail. President Trump has put his Iowa and Kansas rallies back on the schedule, after the Republican National Committee had swapped them for telerallies. A person familiar with the plans tells the A P he'll now hold back-to-back rallies in both states on Sunday, with the call-in events rearranged around them. Separately, Semafor reports Republicans have asked the Federal Election Commission to investigate Mary Peltola's Alaska campaign over the spoiler-candidate controversy; Peltola has denied knowing about any such effort.
// Story 2: Pope Leo votes
[ava] Story two. Pope Leo the Fourteenth has become the first pope to vote in a U.S. general election. The Vatican confirmed he cast a mail-in ballot in Chicago, as any American citizen living abroad can. Leo, born Robert Prevost, holds U.S. and Peruvian citizenship and has voted whenever eligible; the Vatican framed it as civic participation. The Chicago Sun-Times first reported it. The Vatican didn't say how he voted, and the pope has publicly differed with the administration on immigration and war.
// Story 3: Houston ICE officer charged
[ava] And story three. In Houston, the Harris County district attorney has charged an Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer, Mario Alberto Morales, with aggravated assault with a deadly weapon. Prosecutors say he drove an unmarked car into forty-eight-year-old construction worker Miguel Parra Hernandez, and that video contradicts ICE's account. ICE says the man ran in front of the car and calls the claim that he was hit on purpose false and reckless. The officer is presumed innocent.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Hormuz tankers (development)
[ava] Story four, from the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy says three oil tankers caught fire and exploded today while using what it called unauthorized routes through the strait, and that one, a supertanker carrying about two million barrels of crude, hit a mine. Iran also claims it destroyed an unmanned U.S. vessel in the eastern part of the strait. Reuters couldn't independently verify either claim, and there's no confirmation yet from the U.S. military, so treat these as Iran's account for now. This comes on top of the limpet-mine attack on a tanker we reported this morning. Also today, Saudi Arabia says it intercepted a Houthi drone near Mecca.
// Story 5: Oleshky evacuation done
[ava] Story five. Officials on both sides say residents who wanted to leave the Russian-held Ukrainian town of Oleshky have now been evacuated, on day four of the local truce. A Ukrainian lawmaker says evacuees are going by bus into southern Russia, flying to Belarus, and crossing into northern Ukraine. Russia's representative says residents who chose to live in Russia were evacuated too. The White House credits first lady Melania Trump's involvement in the talks; the wider war goes on, with a deadly Russian missile strike on Kyiv late Thursday.
// Story 6: Salvadoran troops in Haiti
[ava] And story six. El Salvador has rushed about two thousand soldiers to Haiti, days after four Salvadoran members of the U.N.-backed force went missing in a gang ambush. Sources tell Reuters the deployment will be capped for now at twenty-five hundred. Supporters see President Nayib Bukele's tough anti-gang approach as what Haiti needs; critics worry about civilian harm, since it's not yet clear who commands the troops or what their rules of engagement are.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Nvidia and Nscale, OpenAI signal
[andrew] Story eight, A.I. The Information reports, and Reuters relays, that Nvidia is in talks to provide billions of dollars in credit guarantees for Nscale's debt financing of a four-hundred-sixty-megawatt data center in West Virginia that Anthropic plans to use. It plans to implement it in the coming months, and existing crawler controls stay in place.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Trezor implant and Polymarket
[ava] Story nine, cyber desk. Hardware wallet maker Trezor is warning about a suspected spy implant in a Trezor Safe Five sold by a reseller called CryptoBilis in Southeast Asia. An Indonesian buyer reportedly opened the device and found an extra circuit board with a cellular modem and antenna, apparently able to read the recovery phrase off the screen and send it out. Trezor hasn't examined the device yet, but it says don't use any unit bought through that reseller since March, and move funds to a fresh wallet if you did. The lesson: buy hardware wallets directly from the maker.
[ava] And Polymarket briefly halted trading Thursday after a compromised third-party monitoring script pushed wallet-draining code to logged-in users. About two and a half million dollars was traced to the attacker. Trading was back within about an hour, and the company says it will reimburse affected users.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Trezor warns of suspected crypto-theft implant in hardware wallet" (Oct 30); BleepingComputer and CNBC on the Polymarket breach (Oct 29-30)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, from Aubrey, Texas. Marilyn Hampton spent thirty-nine years as the school district's custodian, and by every account far more than that: buying school supplies for kids, helping struggling students study, running the ticket booth at football games. She was the first among her siblings to finish high school, but never went to college, and she says she sometimes felt like a failure. The school board invited her to a meeting, supposedly to talk about challenges opening a new elementary school, then asked her to roll out a red carpet. Stitched into it: Marilyn Hampton Elementary School. Through tears, she said it was an honor she never imagined in her lifetime. At seventy-six, she still helps care for the district's infants and toddlers.
