## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Cook forecast
[ava] Story one, with the midterms four days away. The Cook Political Report, a nonpartisan forecaster, moved twelve House races it had rated as toss-ups to leaning Democratic, and shifted several Republican-leaning seats to toss-up. Cook now projects Democrats winning a House majority, with two hundred twenty-two seats leaning their way and eighteen more that could go either way, for a possible ceiling around two hundred forty. Republicans counter that forecasts aren't votes, and some recent polls, like one in Texas, still show their candidates narrowly ahead. Turnout is the wild card, with early voting running well above twenty twenty-two.
// Story 2: Ninth Circuit and Hubbard
[ava] Story two. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals, on the same two-to-one vote as Monday, has denied the Justice Department's request for an injunction while it appeals in its case against California over roughly twenty thousand voters the department labels likely noncitizens. Legal journalist Chris Geidner first flagged the order this afternoon. The administration says it's trying to keep ineligible ballots from being counted; California and the court majority say setting those ballots aside now risks disenfranchising citizens.
[ava] Also at the White House, President Trump hosted Stephen Hubbard in the Oval Office. Hubbard is the seventy-four-year-old retired teacher freed last week after more than four years in a Russian prison on charges he fought as a mercenary for Ukraine. Mr. Trump says there was no prisoner swap, though he has linked the release to holding off on congressionally passed sanctions on Russia; critics say that's a concession in itself.
// Story 3: Ghost students
[ava] And story three. The Justice Department has unveiled indictments in what it calls Operation Ghostbusters, a federal student-aid fraud scheme it ties to a network in Kenya. Prosecutors say fraudsters used stolen American identities to enroll fake "ghost students," file aid applications, and even used A.I. to complete coursework so the money kept flowing. Five people are charged, officials put losses at more than two hundred million dollars since twenty twenty-one, and they seized over seven hundred fifty thousand dollars in crypto. If you get a financial-aid notice you didn't expect, it's worth checking whether someone used your name.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Poland's top court
[ava] Story four, from Warsaw. Police entered the grounds of Poland's Constitutional Tribunal today after what they described as incidents outside, including objects thrown at officers, and the court's president was suspended. President Karol Nawrocki turned his plane around on the way to Senegal, saying the president must be in Poland at a moment like this. Opposition leader Jaroslaw Kaczynski called it a coup. Prime Minister Donald Tusk's government frames it as restoring the rule of law, after years of fighting over judges appointed under the previous nationalist government. The dispute has left the court essentially paralyzed, with each side refusing to recognize the other's judges.
// Story 5: Iran signals
[ava] Story five. Mixed signals on Iran. President Trump said today the war is "going to end soon," without saying how, as Qatari mediators returned to Tehran to push for a deal. Meanwhile, the head of U.S. Central Command, Admiral Brad Cooper, insisted the main shipping lanes through the Strait of Hormuz are clear of Iranian mines and that Iran's navy has been destroyed. That's a direct challenge to Iran's claim this afternoon that tankers struck mines on what it calls unauthorized routes, claims that still haven't been independently verified. Oil rose again on the uncertainty.
// Story 6: Mayon Volcano
[ava] And story six. In the Philippines, Mayon Volcano had what officials call a moderately explosive eruption, sending ash about three kilometers into the sky. The alert level is back up to three of five, with warnings of possible lava flows and fast-moving pyroclastic flows in the coming days or weeks, and evacuation recommended for the six-kilometer permanent danger zone.

## Markets | sting=markets
[andrew] Markets, right at the close. Final figures were still settling at airtime, but late in the session the Nasdaq was down about three and a half percent, its lowest level in about three months. The S and P five hundred was down a little over two percent and the Dow more than one percent. The Nasdaq one hundred has fallen roughly ten percent from its peak, right at the edge of what traders call a correction.
[andrew] For the month, Investopedia figured the Nasdaq was on pace for a loss around five and a half percent, its worst month since March of last year, and the S and P five hundred was down about one percent for the month. The Dow, with less tech, looked set to end October higher.
[andrew] The day's damage: Intel fell about thirty percent, its worst day in more than four decades, after a weak outlook and a warning about cash burn. A M D and Marvell lost about twelve percent, Nvidia about five, and the chip index nearly nine. Amazon finished down only about two percent after earlier losses. Crypto stocks fell hard after the Binance hack, with Coinbase down about fourteen percent and Bitcoin near seventy-six thousand.
[andrew] Elsewhere: the ten-year Treasury yield eased to about four point nine three percent, as Treasuries head for their best month since early last year. U.S. crude rose about two percent, to around a hundred four dollars, while gold fell more than three percent to roughly thirty-eight hundred eighty, a one-year low. One bright spot: Exxon and Chevron beat profit forecasts on record refining results.
[andrew] The bear case: A.I. spending keeps climbing while the payoff keeps getting pushed out. The bull case, from UBS and others, is that A.I. demand is still strong and this is spending indigestion, not a collapse.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Suncatcher and ChatGPT limits
[andrew] Story eight, A.I. Google says its Project Suncatcher prototype satellite, launched October first, has produced the first A.I. answer from a data-center-class chip in orbit. Engineers sent a prompt to its onboard Gemma model asking for a haiku about being in space, and as the satellite passed over Svalbard, Norway, back came: "Blue marble below, data streams in silent grace, starlight feeds my core." Next come tests of how the chips hold up to heat and radiation, with twin linked satellites planned for twenty twenty-seven. It's a long-term moonshot; nobody's running a data center in space anytime soon.
[ava] Also, OpenAI says ChatGPT will tighten limits on detailed information about past mass attacks for all users, add stricter limits on weapons information for teens, and be able to end chats that show someone fixating on violence. It made more than two hundred law-enforcement referrals last quarter and will publish a safety report early next year. Privacy advocates will watch how its age-prediction system flags who counts as a teen.
> source: Google blog, "We've received our first response from the Project Suncatcher prototype satellite" (Oct 30); OpenAI, "Community safety report and new protections for teens" (Oct 30)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: GitLab and Chrome extensions
[ava] Story nine, cyber desk. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency says attackers are now exploiting a maximum-severity GitLab flaw patched earlier this month. It lets a malicious regular expression in a pipeline configuration run code on the server. If you run your own GitLab, update to the latest patched release now.
[ava] And researchers at Socket found three hundred nineteen Chrome extensions with about thirteen point four million installs that quietly hijacked people's searches, posing as P D F converters, safe-search tools and A.I. helpers, then routing searches through the operators' servers to collect referral fees. Worth a minute this weekend: open Chrome's extensions page, remove anything you don't recognize, and check that your search engine setting is what you chose.
> source: BleepingComputer, "CISA says max severity GitLab flaw now exploited in the wild" (Oct 30); BleepingComputer, Socket and Malwarebytes on the search-hijacking Chrome extensions (Oct 30)
