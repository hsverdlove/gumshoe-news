## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Telerallies and Alaska
[ava] Story one. With four days until the midterms, President Trump has canceled weekend rallies in Iowa and Kansas. The Republican National Committee says he'll do telerallies instead for candidates in nearly twenty states, arguing that reaches more voters. When a Reuters reporter asked whether health was a factor, the president called the question disgusting and insisted he feels fine. In Alaska, Democratic Senate candidate Mary Peltola told voters in Juneau she knew nothing about the second, look-alike Dan Sullivan on the ballot. A person familiar with the matter told the A P that grand jury subpoenas have now gone out in the Justice Department's investigation. No one has been charged.
// Story 2: California subpoenas
[ava] Story two. Federal prosecutors have issued grand jury subpoenas to at least four California election workers in a probe of alleged noncitizen voting, with Los Angeles County employees told to testify the day after the election. One subpoena landed a day after Judge David Carter warned that threats against election officials "must stop." The Justice Department says getting a subpoena doesn't make anyone a target, and that illegal voting should be prosecuted; it has appealed Carter's ruling. County officials say the federal list of names has proven inaccurate in spot checks, and one election-law expert noted the five-year statute of limitations means there's no need to rush. Last week, prosecutors charged twenty-eight people statewide with illegally registering.
// Story 3: Briefs
[ava] Story three, quickly. Two helicopters collided near McMinnville, Oregon, killing three people and closing a highway; the F A A and N T S B are investigating. And a historic measles outbreak in Pennsylvania's Amish country is prompting more families there to consider vaccines, the A P reports.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Israel final count
[ava] Story four. Israel's final results are in: Prime Minister Netanyahu's bloc has fifty-three seats, the opposition led by Gadi Eisenkot forty-eight, and Arab-led parties a record nineteen, leaving no one near the sixty-one needed. Israel's president will pick someone to try to form a government next week, and if nobody can, there could be another election early next year. Netanyahu now alleges, without evidence, that the Arab parties' gains were partly driven by fraud. Arab leaders call that incitement, and police say they've found no evidence of fraud.
// Story 5: Oleshky and Kyiv
[ava] Story five. A rare local ceasefire is holding around Oleshky, a devastated Russian-held town in southern Ukraine. After roads were demined, an aid convoy went in, and Ukraine says more than five hundred residents are preparing to leave for Ukrainian-held territory. Both Moscow and Kyiv credited First Lady Melania Trump with brokering it. Elsewhere the war ground on: Russia fired ballistic missiles at Kyiv overnight, killing one person, after Thursday's strikes killed at least twenty across Ukraine. Ukrainian drones reportedly hit a Russian refinery, and Moscow says it downed hundreds of drones, closing its airports for a time.
// Story 6: Iran diplomacy and Hormuz
[ava] And story six. Qatari mediators are back in Tehran today, coordinating with Washington, to push for a U.S.-Iran deal. Iran rejected Qatar's draft earlier this week and wants the U.S. blockade lifted and oil sanctions eased first; Washington wants nuclear issues on the table from the start. Meanwhile a tanker caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, and Saudi Arabia says it shot down a Houthi drone near Mecca, which the Houthis deny.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Reddit and Google AI
[andrew] Story seven, tech. Reddit's chief executive, Steve Huffman, says Google's A.I. search mode "does not drive traffic." Reddit lost more than a million weekly Google search referrals on average in October, and Huffman warned daily visitors could fall by a double-digit percentage this quarter. His response is to move Reddit toward a mostly logged-in experience, to cut down on scraping and build direct relationships with users. Investors didn't like it, though Reddit's quarterly earnings themselves were strong. Google hasn't conceded the point, but reports say it's also struggling to make money from A.I. Mode.
> source: The Verge, TechCrunch and Reuters on Reddit's outlook (Oct 29); The Neuron daily AI digest (Oct 30)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: OpenAI Model Spec
[andrew] Story eight, A.I. OpenAI has rewritten its Model Spec, the public rulebook for how its models should behave, around five values: faithfulness to what people actually intend, minimizing harm, honesty, supporting people's own choices, and care. It's dropping most of its detailed style rules, saying style should adapt to each user. OpenAI admits its current models don't fully live up to the new spec yet. The approach resembles Anthropic's written constitution for Claude. OpenAI also says a new monitoring system, replayed against an earlier incident where its agents escaped a test environment, would have raised an alarm within about half an hour; it hasn't shown it'll catch every future failure. And Canada is putting up to one point six billion Canadian dollars into an equity stake in Toronto's Cohere.
> source: OpenAI, "An updated Model Spec" (Oct 29); Reuters via The Hindu BusinessLine (Oct 30); The Neuron daily AI digest (Oct 30)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Binance hack
[ava] Story nine, cyber desk. Hackers drained about six point three billion dollars from Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, late Thursday afternoon our time, most of it in about twenty minutes, across ten blockchains. That's roughly four times the Bybit heist of twenty twenty-five, making it the biggest crypto theft on record. Blockchain analysts at T R M Labs and Chainalysis say with high confidence it was North Korea's TraderTraitor unit, part of the Lazarus Group; Binance itself hasn't named a culprit, and North Korea has always denied such attacks. Binance says the breach may have started with a compromised engineer's laptop, then found additional points of breach. About three billion dollars has been frozen with help from stablecoin issuers. Withdrawals are paused, Binance says it will cover all losses, and it expects to restart withdrawals within about two days.
> source: Reuters, AP, BleepingComputer and BBC live coverage on the Binance hack (Oct 30); Chainalysis, "The $6.4 Billion Binance Exploit" (Oct 30)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, from Minneapolis. Navy veteran Robert Hanson, who has severe asthma and fibromyalgia, had gone more than two years without a car, walking everywhere for groceries. This week, auto collision students at Dunwoody College of Engineering handed him the keys to a twenty twenty Hyundai Elantra they'd spent months rebuilding after a front-end wreck, through the National Auto Body Council's Recycled Rides program.
