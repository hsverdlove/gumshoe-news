## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Fairfield shooting
[ava] Story one. A shooting inside a Spirit Halloween store in Fairfield, California, late Friday left one person dead and six wounded. Police say all seven were hit by gunfire, and three of the injured are in critical condition. The store is in the Solano Town Center mall, about thirty-seven miles from Sacramento. Detectives are still interviewing witnesses and have not identified a suspect.
// Story 2: Campaign trail
[ava] Story two, three days to the midterms. After a day of whiplash, President Trump's Sunday rallies are firmly back on: Salina, Kansas, at two in the afternoon local time at the airport, where Senator Roger Marshall is in a tight race with Democrat Adam Hamilton, and then Dubuque, Iowa, where the Senate race between Republican Ashley Hinson and Democrat Josh Turek looks close. His team says the stops come on top of nearly twenty call-in telerallies. Both states are usually Republican strongholds but have turned competitive this year; a Trump adviser says the president simply wanted to close out the campaign in person. And an A P look at older voters finds rising health costs, Medicare changes and Social Security's long-term funding gap among their biggest worries this year.
// Story 3: Swift telescope
[ava] Story three, from space. NASA's Swift observatory, which spent twenty-two years watching some of the universe's biggest explosions, is close to falling back to Earth after a robotic rescue attempt failed. The Space Force projects reentry around Friday. Most of the one-and-a-half-ton telescope should burn up, though NASA says a few pieces could survive.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Poland talks (update)
[ava] Story four, an update from Poland. This afternoon, Warsaw time, President Karol Nawrocki is holding crisis talks over last night's standoff at the Constitutional Tribunal, but the governing coalition is split on whether to show up. The centre-right Polish People's Party leader, Defense Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, is attending, and so is Szymon Holownia of the centrist Poland twenty-fifty party, who said the night's scenes brought shame on Poland and urged the president and Prime Minister Tusk to strike a deal on resetting the court. The Left party is boycotting, saying the president is himself responsible for the crisis. Tusk's justice minister backs the judges who suspended the court's president, Bogdan Swieczkowski, wants his immunity lifted, and called Nawrocki's behavior shameful. Nawrocki's camp calls it a forcible takeover. The U.S. ambassador urged all sides not to take provocative action, and the tribunal's judges are due to meet on Tuesday.

Caribbean nations have long pressed for reparations as well, while some critics in Britain argue today's government shouldn't apologize for centuries-old wrongs.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: Claude Code extension update
[ava] Story ten, cyber desk, with an update on that backdoored Claude Code extension. Anthropic now says its own systems weren't compromised. The tampered version was published through Cursor's extension marketplace by someone with access there, and stayed up for close to twelve hours on Thursday. Microsoft confirms it never appeared on the official VS Code marketplace or npm, and the security firm Socket found no sign of it on Open VSX either. Cursor says it rotated its keys, rebuilt the marketplace and got the attacker's server taken offline, which kills the reverse tunnel. Still unanswered: how the attacker got publishing access. If you're a Cursor user who updated that day, note that removing the extension doesn't remove the planted SSH key or startup entries; those have to be cleaned up separately, and credentials rotated.
// Story 11: Amadeus breach, Cisco SD-WAN
[ava] Story eleven. Japan Airlines says a breach at Amadeus, the reservation system used by more than four hundred airlines, exposed data on about ninety-two thousand customers, mostly booking codes, names and itineraries, and for about two thousand people, passport numbers and contact details too. The access ran from August twenty twenty-three until September. A N A, Asiana and Korean Air report being hit as well. No card numbers or passwords were taken.
[ava] And Cisco's Talos team says a new spying group is exploiting a maximum-severity Cisco SD-WAN flaw, patched back in February, to plant malware called KeyDrift that steals encryption keys from network controllers. Targets include space, defense and policy research organizations, and Talos sees overlap with China's Volt Typhoon network, though it stops short of a firm link. The fix is simple: patch.
> source: The Hacker News, "Official Claude Code Extension on Cursor Marketplace Trojanized" (Oct 30, updated Oct 31); BleepingComputer, "Japan Airlines suffers third-party data breach" (Oct 29); BleepingComputer, "Hackers exploit Cisco SD-WAN flaw to deploy custom KeyDrift malware" (Oct 29)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a man who's almost home. In nineteen ninety-eight, Briton Karl Bushby set out from the southern tip of South America to walk back to his hometown of Hull, England. His rule: no transport to move forward, and he finishes on foot. Twenty-eight years and some thirty-one thousand miles later, across jungle, desert, shifting ice and plenty of red tape, he's nearly there. On Monday morning, the city plans a civic welcome in Queen Victoria Square, and then he'll walk the last stretch to his mother's house in east Hull. Now fifty-seven, he told the A P he's not sure he's ready for it to end, and that the kindness of strangers carried him the whole way.
> source: AP, "In 28 years of walking, Karl Bushby has overcome jungle, desert and red tape" (Oct 31); Hull City Council, "Let's give Karl a Hull welcome home" (Oct 26)
