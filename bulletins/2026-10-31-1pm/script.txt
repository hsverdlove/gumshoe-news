## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Thompson TRO
[ava] Story one. Late Friday night, a federal judge in San Francisco, Trina Thompson, issued an emergency order barring the Justice Department and Homeland Security from threatening, intimidating or coercing state and local election workers over alleged noncitizen voting. The order also blocks federal demands that officials purge voters, refuse ballots, or set ballots aside based on the administration's list of alleged noncitizens, and it runs through November sixteenth. Labor unions brought the suit Friday, and the judge ruled before the department had weighed in, saying Election Day is too close to wait. Separately, a Ninth Circuit panel kept federal investigations into past noncitizen voting on hold in nine states until after Tuesday. The Justice Department says enforcing the ban on noncitizen voting is simply its job, and that every ineligible ballot cancels out a citizen's vote. Critics, including some Republican election officials, call the pressure campaign federal overreach.
// Story 2: AP Iran base analysis
[ava] Story two. An Associated Press analysis of satellite imagery finds that through August, more than a hundred fifteen Iranian or militia drone and missile strikes damaged or destroyed structures at twenty-six U.S. bases and other sites across the Middle East, causing billions of dollars in equipment damage. Iran went after early-warning radars and aircraft hangars first, then warehouses and troop housing. Kuwait took the most hits. The A P notes that major satellite providers with U.S. contracts delayed or blocked public imagery at officials' request, citing national security, which is why the full picture is only now emerging.
// Story 3: Patel threat; Fairfield update
[ava] Story three. A Michigan man, forty-two-year-old Anthony Palazzolo of Chesterfield Township, is charged with making interstate threats after prosecutors say he threatened to kill an associate of F.B.I. Director Kash Patel, and Patel himself, in Facebook messages. He has a detention hearing Monday.
[ava] And one new detail on last night's deadly shooting at a Spirit Halloween store in Fairfield, California: police now say it was a targeted attack by a single suspect, who has not been identified or caught.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Iran Hormuz offer
[ava] Story four. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, told Al Jazeera that talks with Qatari mediators have produced a plan that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days, down from the five days Qatar had proposed. But the conditions are steep: lifting sanctions, fewer U.S. forces in the region, restored oil waivers, release of frozen funds including six billion dollars held in Qatar, and recognition of shipping routes Iran worked out with Oman. He called a letter from Vice President J.D. Vance a positive step, but said Iran sees no need to meet American officials for now. An Iranian delegation is in Doha. Al Jazeera's own Tehran correspondent says there's no remarkable flexibility in Iran's position, and Washington has not publicly responded.
// Story 5: Europe protests
[ava] Story five. Halloween youth protests over living costs and education turned violent in places today. France's government counts three hundred twenty thousand marchers nationwide, fewer than the last two weekends, with about seventy thousand in Paris. That march was mostly calm, but clashes broke out where it ended, near Porte de Charenton; a police officer was hit by a Molotov cocktail and caught fire, and police used tear gas and made arrests. In Germany, police used water cannons in Hamburg and Cologne after protesters under the banner Gen Z regelt set barriers on fire and threw fireworks. Some accounts online cast the unrest as far-left rioting; organizers say the movement is about schools, wages and the cost of living, and most marchers were peaceful.
// Story 6: Poland update
[ava] And story six, an update from Poland. President Karol Nawrocki's crisis talks over the Constitutional Tribunal ended without any agreement or timetable. Prime Minister Tusk's party and the Left stayed away. Krzysztof Bosak of the right-wing Confederation called the government's moves at the court lawless and wants early elections, while Adrian Zandberg of the left-wing Razem party proposed a full reset of the court, with new judges chosen by a supermajority so no camp controls it. Szymon Holownia urged Tusk and Nawrocki to simply sit down over tea. Tusk addresses the nation tonight at eight, Warsaw time, three o'clock our time.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: shared opt-out, ChatGPT accounts
[andrew] Story eight, A.I. OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic say they'll honor one shared signal in a website's robots dot t x t file that tells all of them, at once, not to train A.I. on that site. It was proposed by an Anthropic standards lead through the Internet Engineering Task Force. Anthropic already supports it, OpenAI says in the coming months, and Google and Microsoft next year. Meta voiced support but hasn't said it will comply. Publishers welcome one switch instead of many, but note it's voluntary, and it doesn't stop crawling itself.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Binance update, Northflank
[ava] Story nine, cyber desk. Binance says it has now covered about ninety percent of the roughly six point three billion dollars stolen in Thursday's record hack, using its emergency user fund, part of its futures insurance fund and its own treasury. To be clear, that is Binance backfilling with its own money, not recovering the stolen coins. The ten biggest tokens, including bitcoin, ether and Tether, are fully backed; the rest are less liquid and will take longer to buy. Withdrawals are on track to reopen Sunday, pending security checks. Co-CEO Richard Teng says the attack started with a compromised laptop and malware built to erase its tracks.
[ava] And developers take note: the cloud platform Northflank is telling every customer to assume their stored environment variables were decrypted, and to rotate all secrets, after an incident flagged by the security firm Wiz on Thursday. Some customers reported A.I. A P I keys being used without permission. Northflank hasn't said how the attacker got in or how many customers were affected.
> source: CNBC, "Binance says 90% of $6.3 billion hack losses are now covered" (Oct 31); Reuters via U.S. News, "Binance replenishes 90% of assets stolen" (Oct 31); BBC (Oct 31); Northflank status page, "Security incident" (Oct 30-31); Abijita, "Northflank tells users to rotate secrets" (Oct 31)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 10: Londonderry manager findings; Scanlan; Colebrook ICE
[ava] Story ten, here at home. The Londonderry Town Council has released the findings of two investigations into former town manager Shaun Mulholland, who was fired on a three-to-two vote in September. The investigators found no evidence for the anonymous complaints of a hostile work environment and intimidation, but one of them sustained allegations of bullying, violating town policies and interfering with the investigations into his conduct. A hearing in the matter is set for November twelfth in Rockingham Superior Court.
[ava] With Tuesday's election close, Secretary of State David Scanlan says New Hampshire is ready. Every ballot is paper, so if a counting machine fails, votes get hand-counted. A reminder: student I.D.s are no longer accepted to prove identity at the polls; bring a driver's license, state I.D., passport or military I.D. Polls close by eight p.m., and problems can be reported to the Attorney General's election hotline.
[ava] And up north near Colebrook, ICE detained a twenty-eight-year-old Burundi-born man, the adopted son of a Maine state senator, as he drove to Canada with newly approved Canadian citizenship papers. Homeland Security says he overstayed a visa from twenty fifteen and that a pending asylum application doesn't confer legal status. His family calls the two nights in jail a waste of taxpayer money. He was released at the Canadian border in Vermont on Friday.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, proof it's never too late. At a retirement village in Birmingham, England, Barbara Thompson and Fred Jenkins, both widowed, met in the gym. Barbara had come along to support a friend; Fred, a former Army physical training instructor, started coaching them. The friend dropped out, but Barbara kept showing up every Monday, Wednesday and Friday. Now, at eighty-eight and eighty-nine, they're inseparable: the gym three times a week, darts, dominoes, gardening, and dancing whenever music comes on. They just took their first vacation together. No wedding plans, and they're keeping their own apartments. As Fred put it, after his wife died he thought that was the end of the world, and now it's a whole new ball game.
