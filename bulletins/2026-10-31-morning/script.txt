## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Final weekend
[ava] Story one. It's the final weekend before Tuesday's midterms, and Reuters counts more than forty-nine million Americans who have already voted early. President Trump rallied Republicans in Florida last night, while former President Bill Clinton campaigned for Democrats in Mississippi. Mr. Trump heads to Iowa and Kansas tomorrow, two states with key Senate races. In Michigan, Democratic unity is fraying: Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed and Congresswoman Haley Stevens, his primary rival, traded shots over an outside ad blaming U.S. aid to Israel for grocery prices, and a joint Detroit appearance planned for Sunday is now in doubt. Stevens wants the ad condemned; El-Sayed says she's relitigating the primary.
// Story 2: Alaska
[ava] Story two, Alaska, where there's a new development. Alaska's U.S. attorney, Michael Heyman, publicly confirmed a criminal investigation into allegations that Democrats recruited a candidate named Dan J. Sullivan to confuse voters in the race between Republican Senator Dan Sullivan and Democrat Mary Peltola. He said a New York Times report drove a surge of F.B.I. resources. The Justice Department also asked a judge to order ballots ranking the second Sullivan set aside, and for an injunction that could block certification if the senator narrowly loses. Peltola says she isn't interested in tricking voters, and the Democratic Senate campaign committee says it wasn't involved. The state notes the two men are already listed differently, one as the Republican incumbent and the other with no party, and critics say a ballot can't show whether a voter was confused.
// Story 3: Court roundup
[ava] Story three, two rulings from the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals. It reinstated a jury's four-point-seven-billion-dollar antitrust verdict against the N.F.L. over Sunday Ticket pricing, though the league gets a new trial on damages. And in a two-to-one decision, it paused a judge's ruling that Idaho's near-total abortion ban must allow exceptions for risks to a woman's health. Idaho officials say the law already allows abortions to save a woman's life; the doctor who sued says that's too narrow to protect patients' health.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Poland (new development)
[ava] Story four, Poland, where the fight over the top court escalated overnight. Shortly after midnight, President Karol Nawrocki pushed his way into the Constitutional Tribunal, telling judges recently chosen by Prime Minister Donald Tusk's parliamentary majority, "You are not judges." Each side accuses the other of shoving. Those judges had suspended the court's president and his deputy, both appointed under the previous Law and Justice government. Nawrocki now accuses the government of forcibly seizing a constitutional body, has demanded police leave the grounds, and called emergency talks with party leaders today, citing a threat to internal security. Tusk's camp says it is undoing years of improper appointments; the opposition calls it a coup. Riot police remain outside.
// Story 5: France and Europe protests
[ava] Story five. France braces for a third weekend of youth-led protests over living costs and school conditions, with seventy thousand police deployed. The interior minister warns of a very high risk of disorder after dark on Halloween. Paris has pushed marches to the city's edge, and unions are challenging that in court. Demonstrations are also planned in Germany, Spain and elsewhere.
// Story 6: Patriots for Ukraine
[ava] Story six. The State Department approved selling Ukraine five hundred Patriot interceptor missiles for about two-point-one billion dollars, paid for by Canada, Germany, Norway and a European Union loan, with no U.S. money. President Zelenskyy thanked Washington but warned delivery could take years; Ukrainian media report these are new builds that may not arrive until after twenty thirty.
// Story 7: Ebola
[ava] Story seven. Congo's Ebola outbreak has now killed more than five thousand people out of about ten thousand three hundred infected, a death rate near fifty percent. It's caused by the Bundibugyo strain and is now the second deadliest Ebola outbreak on record. Conflict and distrust in the east are hampering containment.
// Story 8: Gulf calm
[ava] And story eight. At a security forum in Bahrain, Saudi Arabia's deputy foreign minister said the region wants a return to peace and is working to prevent a new round of fighting involving the U.S., Israel and Iran. Gulf officials also pressed Iran to stop the attacks and the shipping disruption around the Strait of Hormuz. Meanwhile in Yemen, Houthi missiles struck near the presidential palace in Aden; casualties weren't immediately known.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 9: French gigabyte tax
[andrew] Story nine, tech. France's National Assembly narrowly voted, eleven to ten, for a tax of five and a half euro cents on every gigabyte that companies like Google, Meta, Amazon and Netflix deliver to French internet users. A two-hundred-gigabyte game download would cost the sender about eleven euros. The Socialist sponsor says it would raise billions and push companies toward better compression. The government opposed it, warning half the bill would hit smaller, often French firms, and critics expect the cost to land on subscribers. It's far from law; the Senate can strip it out.
[andrew] And with airlines using A.I. to fine-tune fares, Reuters reports startups such as JetBack, Junova and Refare now watch your fare after you book, and claim a refund or credit if the price drops.
> source: Numerama, "L'Assemblée vote une taxe de 5,5 centimes par Go" (Oct 30); Europe 1 (Oct 30); Reuters on fare-tracking startups (Oct 30)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 10: OpenAI round, Claude Code games
[andrew] Story ten, A.I. Original and genre-inspired games are still fine. Anthropic cites intellectual property; some developers object that there's no way to switch it off.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 11: Ivanti, ChatGPT link flaw, Cloudflare
[ava] Story eleven, cyber desk. CISA has added two more Ivanti Connect Secure flaws to its exploited list. Chained together with a third, they let attackers run code as root on the V P N appliance without logging in. Patches are out, and researchers warn Ivanti's own integrity checker may miss in-memory activity, so affected organizations should hunt for compromise, not just patch.
[ava] OpenAI also fixed a ChatGPT flaw where a booby-trapped shared chat link could run attacker code in your logged-in session and read your private chats. Researchers say they saw no real-world abuse, and there's nothing you need to do.
[ava] And Cloudflare says it blocked more than eighteen million network attacks in the third quarter, about eight thousand an hour, up thirty-eight percent from the spring, with short hit-and-run bursts surging.
> source: CISA, "CISA Adds Two Known Exploited Vulnerabilities to Catalog" (Oct 30); Volexity on the Ivanti exploit chain; The Hacker News and Parameter on the ChatGPT shared-link flaw (Oct 30-31); Cloudflare DDoS Threat Report 2026 Q3 (Oct 2026)
