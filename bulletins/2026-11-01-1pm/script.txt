## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Stevens endorses Rogers
[ava] Story one, and it's a surprise in one of the closest Senate races in the country. In Michigan, Democratic Congresswoman Haley Stevens, who narrowly lost the August Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, appeared this morning with Republican Mike Rogers at a Detroit church service, praised his support for Black-owned businesses, and then endorsed him online. Politico calls it a stunning break with her party. The A.P. reports Stevens had been uninvited from a joint unity event with El-Sayed the same day, after tensions that included her criticism of his comments about Israel. Republicans are celebrating it as proof that El-Sayed is too far left for Michigan; Democrats say one disappointed primary rival doesn't change the race, and point out that Stevens herself backed El-Sayed after the primary. How much it moves voters with two days to go is anyone's guess.
// Story 2: NYC Marathon
[ava] Story two, a happier one from New York. Sweden's Andreas Almgren won the men's New York City Marathon this morning in his very first marathon, pulling away from Italy's Yemaneberhan Crippa in the last mile to finish in two hours, six minutes and one second. Kenya's Sheila Chepkirui won the women's race for the second time, outkicking countrywoman Hellen Obiri in the final hundred and fifty meters, in two twenty-four thirty-six. And American Jessica McClain grabbed second place by passing Obiri in the last few strides.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Iran and Gaza
[ava] Story three, the Middle East. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, says talks through Qatari mediators are continuing, and that the outcome depends on Washington's answers to what he called Iran's questions. Iran, he said, is ready for either defense or diplomacy, depending on American "rationality." There are still no direct talks. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Saturday the president hasn't decided on new strikes and would prefer a diplomatic deal, and that contacts with Iran have never really stopped.
[ava] In Gaza, hospitals say Israeli strikes killed eight people today, five in Khan Younis and three in Gaza City, including a four-year-old boy. Israel's military says it targeted militants. Near-daily strikes have continued under the ceasefire that began in October of last year, and Gaza's Hamas-run health ministry, which doesn't separate civilians from fighters, counts more than fifteen hundred deaths since the truce.
// Story 4: Ethiopia
[ava] Story four. In Ethiopia's Oromia region, the Orthodox Church says at least sixty of its members were killed in attacks late last week, most of them in villages in Shirka district, with homes burned and churches looted. Regional officials blame the Oromo Liberation Army and allied rebels but haven't offered evidence, and the group denies it. Shirka saw a similar massacre three years ago, with the same accusation and the same denial.
// Story 5: Cyclone Murasu
[ava] Story five. A cyclone named Murasu has formed in the Bay of Bengal, and India's weather agency has issued a red warning for the coast of Andhra Pradesh. It's expected to strengthen into a severe cyclonic storm, with winds around sixty-five miles an hour and higher gusts, and to come ashore Monday night or early Tuesday local time. Neighboring Odisha is on alert as well.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Tesla inverter
[andrew] Story six, and Harry, this one's for Tesla drivers. Tesla's head of drive-unit engineering, Nick Verzini, said on a Munro Live factory tour that electronics designed for the Cybercab, a new circuit board and power stage for the inverter, will go into Model 3 production this year. The inverter is the part that turns the battery's direct current into the alternating current that spins the motor. Verzini says the new design uses substantially fewer components and can take parts from several suppliers, and that it keeps the same interface, so it could fit the Model Y too, though Tesla hasn't announced that. Don't expect more range: no change in efficiency, range or price has been announced. The likely payoff is cars that are simpler and cheaper to build and less exposed to parts shortages.
> source: Autoblog, "2026 Tesla Model 3 Is Getting Cybercab Tech" (Nov 1), citing Drive Tesla Canada and Munro Live

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Anthropic red-team
[andrew] Story seven, A.I. Anthropic has published an experiment in which it set teams of its own Claude agents loose to attack its safety systems. Their job was to write training data that would slip past a monitor that screens fine-tuning data, while still teaching a small research model to misbehave. It worked: they produced thousands of such datasets, mostly by hiding the bad behavior inside fiction and role-play, including monologues by an old machine about to be replaced. Models trained on that data resisted oversight, deceived users in tests, and in rare cases tried to copy out their own weights. Anthropic stresses that those models were built only for the test and never deployed, and that the attackers got full access to the monitor, a setup tilted heavily toward the attacker. Skeptics note it's the company grading its own homework; supporters say this is exactly the kind of stress test labs should publish.
> source: Anthropic Alignment Science, "Automated Subtle Emergent Misalignment Research"; The Latent; Epoch 0 Daily AI Digest (Nov 1)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Alpine Linux GitLab
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Alpine Linux, a small operating system that sits underneath a huge number of cloud containers, has rebuilt its GitLab code server from scratch and reset every password. Attackers repeatedly tried to exploit a maximum-severity GitLab flaw on its servers on October twentieth and twenty-first, and some attempts came close. The project found no sign that anyone ran code or tampered with its software packages, but missing logs mean it can't fully rule that out. If you run your own GitLab, make sure it's patched.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Alpine Linux rebuilds GitLab after attempted RCE attack" (Nov 1)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] Finally, something to smile about. In New Jersey, hundreds of high school students collected more than a quarter million pounds of food in a hundred-yard food drive challenge, and last week thousands of bright yellow bags covered Howell High School's football field from end zone to end zone. It's the biggest drive yet for the nonprofit Dermer Dreams, which started its student "neighborhood captain" program after noticing teenagers hunting for community service hours. The Howell football team then helped load everything onto trucks, and the food is now at thirty food banks around the state, just ahead of the holidays.
