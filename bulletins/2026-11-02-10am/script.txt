## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Palm Beach County crash, pilot identified
[ava] Story one, an update on Sunday's Learjet crash near Greenacres in Palm Beach County, Florida, which killed all nine people aboard. The New York Post, citing her mother and stepfather, has identified one of the two pilots as twenty-year-old Nathalia Gadelha, who grew up in Palm Beach County and earned her private pilot's license at seventeen, before she could drive. Reports differ on whether she was the captain or the co-pilot. Officials still haven't released the names of the other eight people aboard and are expected to brief reporters later today. Some aviation experts are pointing to storms along the route, but the National Transportation Safety Board hasn't named a cause, and early theories are just that.
// Story 2: Michigan fallout
[ava] Story two. The fallout from Congresswoman Haley Stevens' endorsement of Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan's Senate race is spreading inside her own office. Her longtime chief of staff, Justin German, and other staff members have resigned. German said the team was "shocked and heartbroken" and urged voters to back Democrat Abdul El-Sayed. Rogers' supporters say the endorsement shows El-Sayed is too far left for many Michigan Democrats; El-Sayed says he's focused on the issues voters care about.
// Story 3: Colorado floods update
[ava] And story three, a better-than-feared update from southwest Colorado. The San Juan River in Pagosa Springs crested Sunday evening about a foot below its record flood of nineteen eleven, well short of the forecast that had it topping that record by four feet. The nearby Piedra River did set another record, rivers across the Four Corners were still rising this morning, and evacuation orders in low-lying areas of Archuleta County remain in place.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Ukraine truce ends
[ava] Story four. The six-day ceasefire around Oleshky in Ukraine's Kherson region, the longest pause in fighting since Russia's full-scale invasion, ended late Sunday, and Russian attacks resumed this morning. Kherson officials say a Russian drone hit a car, wounding a sixty-two-year-old woman and a seventy-three-year-old man, and a Russian strike killed one person in Izium, in the Kharkiv region. The truce, brokered by First Lady Melania Trump, let aid into occupied Oleshky and let nearly eight hundred residents leave. A senior Ukrainian official told A.F.P. that Russia turned down an offer to extend it. Kyiv says Moscow used the pause to fortify its positions; Moscow says it assumes Kyiv did the same. U.S.-mediated talks are set to resume this month.
// Story 5: Gaza
[ava] Story five. Israeli strikes in Gaza killed at least three people on Monday, including in Khan Younis, according to Palestinian medics cited by Reuters. Israel's military typically says such strikes target militants who threaten its troops or violate the ceasefire; the medics' accounts haven't been independently confirmed.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Tesla FSD 14.3.13
[andrew] Story six, and this one's for Tesla drivers. Tesla's latest Full Self-Driving build, version fourteen point three point thirteen, is now on more than thirty percent of the fleet, according to Tesla Oracle, but a growing number of owner reviews call it a step backward. Owners report late highway exits, phantom braking, a Cybertruck that no longer backs into its garage, and one Model Y that was hit while turning left across two lanes after the car in the near lane stopped to let it through. These are owner videos and posts, not a Tesla statement or regulator finding, and some drivers say the update works fine. Tesla shipped it with the same release notes as the previous version, so owners are judging changes by feel. If you use F.S.D., stay extra alert on unprotected left turns.
> source: Tesla Oracle, "Tesla FSD v14.3.13 gets involved in a crash and shows multiple regressions" (Nov 2); Yahoo Finance (Nov 2); TeslaNorth (Oct 20)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Supermarket freezers hacked
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Dozens of supermarkets in seven European countries woke up to warm freezers this morning in what appears to be a coordinated cyberattack. About forty Delhaize stores in Belgium and Luxembourg were hit, plus shops in Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands, Estonia and Finland. The Belgian refrigeration company Sabcobel says attackers switched the units into defrost mode, and one Swedish store said its freezers had been set to fifty degrees Celsius, about a hundred twenty-two Fahrenheit. One Belgian store estimated a hundred thousand euros in spoiled food. It looks like the same method used last month against hundreds of French supermarkets. Nobody has claimed responsibility. Sweden's cybersecurity chief says the country has many exposed industrial devices that need patching, which is a good reminder for anyone with internet-connected equipment at home or work.
> source: BBC, "Shop freezers in seven European countries hit by suspected cyber-attack" (Nov 2); AP, "Supermarket freezers in European countries face apparent cyberattacks" (Nov 2); Al Jazeera/Reuters (Nov 2); The Guardian (Nov 2)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 9: Londonderry tax cap question
[ava] Story nine, here in Londonderry. Besides the Senate and House races, tomorrow's ballot asks whether to adopt a school property tax cap under the state's new Taxpayer Protection Act. It would limit how much the school tax levy can grow each year to inflation plus new construction, starting July first, twenty twenty-seven, and it would need sixty percent to pass. It's not a cap on total school spending and wouldn't directly cut anyone's tax bill. School officials say that with costs rising, the cap could force about two million dollars in cuts to keep current services, possibly including bus routes, A.P. courses, sports and arts. Supporters across the state say a cap forces districts to live within taxpayers' means, the way families have to. Polls are open tomorrow from six a.m. to eight p.m. at the Londonderry High School gym.
> source: Londonderry Times, "District Outlines Potential Effects of Proposed Tax Cap" (Oct 25); Union Leader, "Londonderry schools could face $2 million in cuts under tax cap" (Nov 2); NHPR, "What will the tax cap on the ballot mean for NH schools? ## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] Finally, a homecoming twenty-eight years in the making. Karl Bushby, a former British paratrooper, set out from Punta Arenas, at the southern tip of Chile, in nineteen ninety-eight, planning a twelve-year walk home to Hull, England. He had two rules: never use transport to move forward, and don't go home until it's done. Thirty-six thousand miles and twenty-nine countries later, after crossing the frozen Bering Strait and the Darién Gap, and facing down a polar bear, a gunpoint threat and a stint in Russian custody, he walked into Hull today to a hero's welcome, the first person to walk continuously across the world. His seventy-five-year-old mother, Angela, waited at the gate of his childhood home, as she'd promised. "You've changed a bit," he told her. "I'm older," she said. Inside, he met his twelve-year-old granddaughter for the first time. His verdict on walking? "Boring as hell."
> source: The Guardian, "'It's been a while': hero's welcome for UK man after 28-year walk across the world" (Nov 2)
