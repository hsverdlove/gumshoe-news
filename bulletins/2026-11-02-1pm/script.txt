## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Trump pardon remarks
[ava] Story one. On the eve of the midterms, President Trump says he'd consider preemptive pardons for Elon Musk, White House aides and other allies if Democrats win control of Congress and open investigations. In a New York Post podcast interview released today, he said, "I would if I needed to," pointing to President Biden's end-of-term pardons for figures like Liz Cheney, Anthony Fauci and members of his own family, and to his own January sixth pardons. Democrats have signaled that a Democratic House could look into Musk's time running DOGE and his companies' federal contracts. Critics say pardoning people who haven't been charged would put allies beyond the reach of oversight; supporters say it's the same shield Biden used, against what they call politically driven probes.
// Story 2: Southwest flooding moves into Arizona
[ava] Story two. The weekend storms that flooded southwest Colorado have also swamped Arizona, which got months' worth of rain in a couple of days. As of this morning, "go now" evacuation orders were still in place in parts of Sedona, Camp Verde, Cottonwood, Cornville, Queen Valley and Winslow, and in several tribal communities along swollen rivers. Sedona firefighters made what their chief called an "excessive amount" of water rescues on Sunday. The rain has ended, and flood warnings are expected to ease through today, though forecasters say some flooding could linger into Tuesday.
// Story 3: Learjet crash new details
[ava] And story three, new details in Sunday's Learjet crash in Palm Beach County, Florida, which killed all nine people aboard. Family members have now identified the twenty-year-old co-pilot as Nathalia Filgueiras, the same young pilot reported earlier as Nathalia Gadelha. The New York Post reports she texted her boyfriend about ten minutes before the crash, "I've got turbulence, I've got ice," and air traffic recordings show the crew reporting ice and turbulence before the jet lost altitude. The National Transportation Safety Board was set to brief reporters at one o'clock, and no cause has been determined. One more thing: viral posts claiming Lenny Kravitz was aboard are false, his representative told Variety.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Iran talks
[ava] Story four. Iran's negotiators have left Qatar after three days of talks aimed at ending the eight-month war with the United States, with revised proposals but no breakthrough. Iran's foreign ministry spokesman, Esmaeil Baghaei, says messages are still being exchanged through mediators, but called the issue "complex." President Masoud Pezeshkian says Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has given the government "certain authorities" in the talks, which some read as more room to deal. Washington hasn't commented on the draft.
// Story 5: UK settlement rules
[ava] Story five. Britain's Home Secretary, Shabana Mahmood, unveiled what she calls "earned settlement." For most migrants, the wait for permanent residence doubles from five years to ten. High earners can qualify in three, and doctors, nurses, teachers and well-paid workers in five. Refugees who arrive by small boat and rely on public money could wait thirty years. After a backlash inside her own Labour Party, she dropped a plan to make care workers wait fifteen years. Mahmood says settlement should be earned, not automatic. Critics on the left call it unfair to people already building lives in Britain, while some on the right say it still doesn't go far enough to cut numbers.
// Story 6: Myanmar strikes
[ava] And story six. In Myanmar, military air strikes over the weekend killed at least fifteen people, according to an ethnic armed group, an aid worker and rights monitors cited by Reuters. In Mrauk-U, in Rakhine State, a strike on a makeshift clinic killed a week-old baby girl, whose twin survived, along with four others. In Loikaw, a school was hit during classes, killing three students. The military-backed government didn't respond to requests for comment. It has said before that it's fighting "terrorists" and takes "maximum restraint" to avoid harming civilians, though U.N. investigators have accused it of deliberately bombing schools and hospitals.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Marvell buys Lumentum
[andrew] Story seven, one of the biggest tech deals of the year. Chipmaker Marvell has agreed to buy optical-components maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal valuing it at a hundred and fifteen billion dollars including debt. Lumentum makes the lasers and optical parts that move data at high speed between A.I. chips in data centers, and Marvell wants to take on Broadcom with a full connectivity lineup. Lumentum holders get four and a half Marvell shares each, about a twenty-four percent premium, and the companies promise more than a billion dollars a year in savings. Investors aren't sold yet. Marvell shares are down more than seven percent, and Lumentum is down about thirteen percent, as the deal's value falls with Marvell's stock. It's a huge bet that A.I. spending keeps growing, just as investors question that. It still needs regulators and is expected to close in the first half of twenty twenty-eight.
> source: Marvell investor press release, "Marvell and Lumentum to Combine" (Nov 2); CNBC, "Marvell to buy Lumentum in $115 billion deal in AI data-center bet" (Nov 2); Investing.com (Nov 2); Reuters via StreetInsider (Nov 2)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: OpenAI Grid Protectors
[andrew] Story eight. With electric bills a campaign issue in plenty of states, including New Hampshire, OpenAI announced what it calls the Grid Protectors Program. It promises that its future data-center campuses will cut their power use when the grid is strained, during a heat wave or a cold snap, and that it will pay the full cost of the grid upgrades they need. The model is a planned campus in Effingham County, Georgia, where its deal with Georgia Power includes up to a thousand megawatts of flexible demand, roughly what seven hundred thousand homes use on average. Supporters say flexible data centers could help spread grid costs out and lower bills. Skeptics note that most of the details still have to be worked out location by location, and OpenAI itself says flexibility can't replace building new power plants and power lines.
> source: OpenAI, "Introducing the Grid Protectors Program" (Nov 2)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: GitLab
[andrew] Story nine, the cyber desk. If you or your company run your own GitLab server, patch it now. Attackers are exploiting a maximum-severity flaw that lets someone with no login run code on the server by slipping a booby-trapped pattern into a pipeline settings file. GitLab fixed it three weeks ago, and C.I.S.A. added it to its list of actively exploited bugs on Friday. Yet the watchdog group Shadowserver says more than a thousand servers on the internet are still unpatched, and over eleven thousand are exposed to a second, similar flaw fixed last month. GitLab's own hosted service is already patched, so only self-hosted servers need action.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Over 1,000 GitLab servers vulnerable to ongoing RCE attacks" (Nov 2); CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] Finally, a dog's happy ending. When a man brought a sweet dog named Hera to the Ulster County S.P.C.A. in New York's Hudson Valley, he said he'd found her as a stray. Staff thought something was off, from the way Hera acted around him. Weeks later, he came back to reclaim her, and the shelter said no. Then the real story came out. Hera was from Oklahoma, and her owner had given up custody in a breakup. When she checked on Hera and learned she'd been dropped at a shelter halfway across the country, she called right away. The S.P.C.A. confirmed her story, and she drove all the way to New York and back to bring Hera home.
