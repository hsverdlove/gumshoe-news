## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: NTSB briefing on Learjet crash
[ava] Story one. Federal investigators gave their first full briefing this afternoon on Sunday's Learjet crash in Palm Beach County, Florida, which killed all nine people aboard. The N.T.S.B. says the jet left Austin a little after ten-thirty in the morning, bound for North Eleuthera in the Bahamas. At thirty-five thousand feet, the crew reported light ice they called manageable and moderate turbulence, and the plane was swinging up and down by about seven hundred feet. They asked to climb to thirty-seven thousand feet and got approval. Less than thirty seconds later the jet was descending. The crew declared a mayday, said they were in an overspeed condition, and reported dropping about twenty thousand feet a minute.
[ava] Investigators say there's some evidence the plane began breaking up before it hit the ground. The debris field is about half a mile long. They believe a cockpit voice recorder was on board but don't yet know if it can be recovered, and they stressed they are not naming a cause. One man on the ground had a leg injury. News outlets, citing family members, report that the passengers included Austin tech entrepreneur Adam Robinson, his wife Helen, their two young children, a nanny and two friends. Officials have not formally released the names.
> source: NTSB media briefing via UPI and CBS12 (Nov 2); BBC live coverage (Nov 2); New York Post and TMZ on passenger identities, citing family (Nov 2)
// Story 2: DOJ asks Supreme Court on California mail ballots
[ava] Story two. The Justice Department has filed an emergency request asking the Supreme Court to order California to set aside about twenty thousand mail ballots, from voters the department says may not be citizens, until their eligibility is checked. The administration argues federal law lets it protect elections from ineligible votes. Lower courts turned the request down, saying the federal lists were unreliable and that holding the ballots could disenfranchise eligible voters on the eve of the election. A U.C.L.A. review found that forty-two percent of the names on the list weren't even on California's current voter rolls. The justices could act within hours or days.
> source: AP, CNBC, NPR, Axios, CBS News on the DOJ emergency application (Nov 2)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Google disrupts XCPE Android TV box botnet
[andrew] Story eight. Google says it worked with Japan's national police, Lumen, HUMAN Security and others to disrupt a botnet of more than one point three million cheap Android TV streaming boxes. The researchers say, with high confidence, that the Chinese manufacturer Videostrong Technology built a backdoor right into the firmware. The infected boxes were used for ad fraud, to sell residential internet proxies, and to install more malware. Taking down the control servers cut off at least ten million more devices, and Google has banned Videostrong from its certified Android program. Videostrong has not publicly responded. The practical advice: the malware reinstalls itself from a protected partition, so a factory reset won't fix it. If you have an off-brand TV box, it needs clean firmware, or it should come off your network.
> source: BleepingComputer on the XCPE botnet disruption (Nov 2)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 10: NH ballot questions, election hotlines; Mulholland response
[ava] Story ten, here at home. Besides the candidates on tomorrow's ballot, New Hampshire Public Radio notes two ballot questions. One would remove the long-defunct office of register of probate from the state constitution, a change that fell short of the two-thirds vote it needed in 2022. The other would tie local spending limits to school administrative costs, capping central office spending at six percent of a district's budget. Supporters say it pushes money into classrooms, and critics question whether it would. If you run into trouble at the polls, the Attorney General's election hotline is one, eight six six, eight six eight, three seven oh three. The U.S. Attorney's office also has staff on call, and the Justice Department says it's sending federal monitors to polling places in Manchester and Nashua.
[ava] And an update on Londonderry's former town manager: according to a resident's summary on TownUnderground, Shaun Mulholland has filed a written response disputing the investigation findings released last week. He objects to how interviews were conducted and says his decisions were legitimate management calls. A court hearing in the case had been scheduled for November twelfth.
> source: NHPR on New Hampshire ballot questions and election hotlines (Nov 2); U.S. Attorney's Office, District of New Hampshire (Nov 2); TownUnderground summary of Mulholland's Oct 31 response (Nov 2)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Kenli Foundation
[ava] Story eleven, something good. Five years ago in Kerrville, Texas, eighteen-year-old Kenli Riney was badly hurt in a car crash. Mike Argote and three other men pulled her from the burning car, and Mike was burned doing it. His fourteen-year-old daughter, Ema, took over C.P.R. Kenli didn't survive, but her family and the people who tried to save her grew close. Her family started the Kenli Foundation to honor everyday heroes, and it has now recognized sixty-seven of them and given out sixty-seven thousand dollars in scholarships. Applications have grown from four the first year to four hundred fifty-one. Ema got one of those scholarships and is now at U.T.S.A. As the foundation puts it, "Strangers ran toward the fire. We exist to find more people like them."
