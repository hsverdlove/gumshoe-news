## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Midterm eve
[ava] Story one. It's the last full day of the midterm campaign. President Trump closes with tele-rallies and a speech at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool today, after Sunday rallies in Iowa and Kansas, two states he carried easily that now hold toss-up Senate races. A new round of New York Times and Siena polls shows Democrats leading in five of seven key Senate battlegrounds. Republicans, led by Speaker Mike Johnson, argue those polls have underestimated their voters before. More than fifty-four million Americans had already voted early as of Sunday, according to a University of Florida tally.
[ava] In Michigan, Democrats are still reeling after Congresswoman Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers over her party's nominee, Abdul El-Sayed. In a CNN interview, Stevens called her support "rock solid" but didn't fully explain the switch; Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer called it a "despicable betrayal." Schumer also held a call with Senate Democrats to prepare for any effort to challenge the results. Republicans call that kind of planning a sign Democrats expect to lose some close races.
// Story 2: Palm Beach County crash update
[ava] Story two, an update on Sunday's business jet crash in Palm Beach County, Florida. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says all nine people aboard the Learjet sixty were killed when it nose-dived and exploded near a busy road, just missing homes. The jet was flying from Austin, Texas, to North Eleuthera in the Bahamas. The National Transportation Safety Board is leading the investigation, with help from the F.A.A. Officials haven't formally released the victims' names.
// Story 3: Colorado floods
[ava] And story three. In southwest Colorado, more than a thousand homes in Archuleta County are under mandatory evacuation orders, as forecasters warned the San Juan River in Pagosa Springs could crest more than four feet above its record flood of nineteen eleven. Every road in La Plata County was closed Sunday afternoon because of mudslides and debris, and some polling places and ballot drop boxes have been moved. Another one to three inches of rain is possible through today, with up to a foot and a half of snow in the higher San Juan Mountains.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Iran
[ava] Story four, Iran. Tehran says it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz and restart nuclear talks within two days, down from the week it proposed in September, if Washington meets its terms. Those include ending the war on all fronts, lifting the U.S. naval blockade and oil sanctions, and unfreezing Iranian assets. Iran's negotiators left Doha on Sunday, but a Reuters source says that wasn't a sign of trouble, and messages are still moving through Qatar. President Trump says he won't strike before the midterms and calls the talks productive, but warned the war will end "one way or the other." The A.P. reports the region is bracing for possible U.S. and Israeli strikes after Tuesday. China is sending a special envoy to Iran, Saudi Arabia and Oman.
// Story 5: Belgium
[ava] Story five. Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever offered his government's resignation to King Philippe this morning after overnight budget talks in his five-party coalition collapsed. The king hasn't accepted it. He'll meet coalition leaders starting tomorrow and see De Wever again Friday, warning that Belgium can't afford a long political crisis. The fight is over how to save about ten billion euros: the center-left parties want more taxes on higher incomes, while De Wever's conservatives and the liberals want deeper spending cuts.
// Story 6: Yemen
[ava] And story six. The U.N. migration agency says more than three hundred thousand people in western Yemen have fled their homes, almost all in the past two months, as fighting between the Houthis and Saudi-backed government forces intensifies. Prices for basic goods in Taiz have risen as much as fivefold, and the agency is asking for forty-five million dollars over six months.

## Markets | sting=markets
[andrew] To the markets, where stocks are set to bounce after a rough October. As of about five-fifteen this morning, Nasdaq one hundred futures were up close to one percent, with S&P five hundred and Dow futures up about half a percent. Friday was ugly: the S&P fell two percent, the Nasdaq one hundred dropped three and a half, and the Nasdaq one hundred lost about six and a half percent for October, its worst month since March of last year.
[andrew] The driver was doubt about A.I. spending. Investors are asking whether Big Tech's huge data center bills, increasingly paid for with borrowed money, will pay off. Intel was the extreme case. It fell nearly twenty-nine percent Friday, its worst day in decades, after forecasting fourth-quarter revenue of fifteen to sixteen billion dollars, below the roughly seventeen billion analysts expected, and saying it doesn't expect positive cash flow this year. It blamed weaker P.C. demand tied to memory shortages. Intel is flat this morning.
[andrew] Oil is giving stocks a lift. Brent crude was down about four percent near a hundred and eight dollars, and U.S. crude slipped just under a hundred, on hopes for the Iran talks. Gold is back near thirty-nine hundred dollars an ounce after hitting a one-year low, and Bitcoin is around seventy-eight thousand eight hundred.
[andrew] Today, the I.S.M. manufacturing survey comes out at ten, with economists expecting a small uptick. A hot reading on prices could push yields higher. Estée Lauder and Marriott report before the bell, Apple and Palantir after the close, and Okta is up nearly four percent premarket on news it will join the S&P five hundred. As for the election, one Janus Henderson strategist said a split Congress "would hardly be unusual," and gridlock may even be good for markets.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Emergency alert apps hacked; Synology ransomware
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. Late Sunday, hackers took over Ukraine's Air Alert app, the one people use to find shelter from Russian missiles, and pushed fake warnings of high radiation, a chemical threat and street unrest in Kyiv. The messages were full of spelling and grammar mistakes. Ukraine suspended the app and restored it overnight. Russia says its Emergency Ministry app was also hacked to send fake alerts, though its wider alert system kept working. A group called Hitcore claimed the Ukraine attack through a Russian Telegram channel linked to the security services; that claim hasn't been verified.
[ava] Closer to home, if you have a Synology network storage box, update it today. Synology is warning of a wave of C3RB3R ransomware attacks against devices running old versions of its D.S.M. software. The attackers are using a flaw that was patched in December twenty twenty-four. Synology says to install the latest D.S.M., turn on automatic updates and keep the admin page off the open internet. It's the company's first warning like this since twenty nineteen.
> source: AP, "Ukraine and Russia report emergency apps hit by cyberattack" (Nov 2); BBC and The Guardian (Nov 2); BleepingComputer, "Synology warns of C3RB3R ransomware attacks targeting NAS devices" (Nov 2)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] Finally, something uplifting. In Charleston, South Carolina, volunteers with the charity Sleep in Heavenly Peace build and deliver beds to kids ages three to seventeen, and for most of them it's the first bed they've ever had. Each one comes with a mattress, new sheets, a pillow and a quilt made by a local group called the Cobblestone Quilters. At one Lowe's-sponsored build, volunteers put together fifty-two beds in three and a half hours. Volunteer Dean Martin remembers a boy who jumped into his arms when his bed showed up, glad he wouldn't have to sleep on a couch anymore. Another bed went to a home where the family stayed warm by the kitchen stove. It was the nicest piece of furniture in the house.
> source: The Post and Courier, "Volunteers build and give beds to Charleston kids who never had one" (Nov 2)
