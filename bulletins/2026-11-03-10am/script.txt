## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Trump on ending Israel aid
[ava] Story one. President Trump says U.S. aid to Israel, about four billion dollars a year, will end after this year. In a phone interview on Fox and Friends this morning, he said he and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu agreed on it, and that Netanyahu brought it up. He said Israel can still buy American weapons, and "we'll help Israel if they need it." He framed the move as taking away a talking point the left uses, naming Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan.
[ava] Some big questions are still open. The current agreement on U.S. military aid to Israel runs through 2028, and Congress, not the president, sets the yearly funding, so it's not clear yet how the change would work. As of the latest reports, Israel's government hadn't confirmed the details. Supporters of the aid say it's a pillar of security in the region. Critics of the aid, on the left and the right, have long argued that a wealthy ally doesn't need it.
> source: Reuters via Investing.com, "Trump says US will stop paying Israel after year-end" (Nov 3); AP, "Trump says US will halt $4 billion in aid to Israel" (Nov 3); NBC News (Nov 3); Jerusalem Post and Israel Hayom (Nov 3)
// Story 2: Election Day update, Westchester scanners
[ava] Story two, an Election Day update. In New York's Westchester County, ballot scanners were down countywide when polls opened at six. The Board of Elections first blamed the thickness of the paper ballots, then corrected itself to say only that there's an issue involving the ballots. Voters are filling out paper ballots that go into sealed orange emergency bags to be counted later. Both candidates in the closely watched seventeenth district race there, Republican Congressman Mike Lawler and Democrat Cait Conley, urged people to stay in line and vote. New York's polls close at nine tonight.
[ava] Nationally, the University of Florida's Election Lab now puts early voting above fifty-nine million.
> source: CBS News live updates, "2026 elections live updates" (Nov 3); NBC New York and FOX 5 New York on Westchester ballot scanners (Nov 3); BBC live updates (Nov 3)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Iran, no rush, Qatar's hope
[ava] Story three, an update on Iran. President Trump told Axios he's in no rush to decide whether to resume strikes, so talks can continue while military options stay open. Axios also reports that C.I.A. Director John Ratcliffe is advising him to stick with what it calls a "no war, no deal" approach while keeping up economic pressure. Qatar, which is mediating, says it still sees hope, even after the strikes on Qeshm Island. Tehran says it will only reopen the Strait of Hormuz if the U.S. makes concessions, including sanctions relief and an end to the naval blockade. Iran's president, Masoud Pezeshkian, has signaled he's open to more diplomacy, though another Iranian official told Al-Monitor a deal is still out of reach.
> source: Axios via Newsquawk (Nov 3); Euronews, "Qatar sees 'hope' for US-Iran deal despite fresh US strikes" (Nov 3); Al-Monitor (Nov 3); Al Jazeera, "Iran war latest" (Nov 3)
// Story 4: UK-EU food checks deal
[ava] Story four. Britain and the European Union have agreed on a draft deal to scrap many of the post-Brexit checks on food and farm goods, the BBC reports. The deal is estimated to be worth about five point one billion pounds a year. Supporters say it cuts costly paperwork and delays for farmers and food exporters. Critics of closer ties have long argued that deals like this tie Britain to E.U. rules it no longer has a say in. The details still have to be finalized.
> source: BBC via Newsquawk, UK-EU agreement on food and agricultural trade (Nov 3)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Shared opt-out, Nvidia China limits, China AI rules
[andrew] Story seven. OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic have agreed to support a shared signal that website owners can put in their robots dot T X T file to say whether A.I. companies may use their content for training. It's a simple, common way to opt out. The catch is that it only works on crawlers that choose to follow it, so publishers who've been fighting scraping will probably keep using other defenses too.
[andrew] And Bloomberg reports that China is planning comprehensive A.I. safety rules that would cover its big model makers, including DeepSeek, Moonshot and Alibaba.
> source: TechRadar, "OpenAI, Google and Microsoft are backing a new way to opt out" (Nov 3); iTechPost (Nov 3); Bloomberg via Newsquawk on China AI rules (Nov 3)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: PHP2Shell + Fragnesia; Node-RED
[andrew] Story eight. Attackers are going after Palo Alto Networks firewalls through a pair of critical flaws called PHP2Shell, which hit firewalls whose management page is exposed to the internet. Singapore's cyber agency says attackers are then using a separate Linux kernel flaw, nicknamed Fragnesia, to get full root control of the device. PHP2Shell also affects some products from Cisco, Citrix, F5 and Siemens. The advice is to patch now and keep management interfaces off the open internet.
[andrew] Also, VulnCheck says hackers are exploiting a maximum-severity flaw in Node-RED, a low-code tool used in some industrial gear, including Siemens devices, to install bots and backdoors.
> source: Cyber Security Agency of Singapore, alert AL-2026-157 (Nov 3); SecurityWeek, "Critical Node-RED Flaw Exploited in Attacks" (Nov 3); The Hacker News on Siemens IoT2050 (Nov 3)
