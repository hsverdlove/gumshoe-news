## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Election Day late afternoon
[ava] Story one, Election Day heading into the evening. In Westchester County, New York, the county executive says scanners couldn't read as many as two hundred fifty thousand ballots at three hundred thirty-two polling places. The state Board of Elections now says the cause was an update to the county's election programming made after the machines were tested, and it's sending replacement software that was used during early voting, aiming to get at least one scanner working at every site. Voters there are still using emergency ballots, which officials say are held under bipartisan oversight and will be counted. In Florida, Broward County says machine problems that turned some early voters away this morning have been fixed, and by three o'clock more than seven point two million Floridians, over half the state's voters, had cast ballots.
[ava] The first actual results came from Guam, where Republicans won the governor's race and Democrats won the territory's nonvoting seat in Congress. Nationwide, more than fifty-nine million ballots were cast before today. The first polls close at six eastern, in parts of Indiana and Kentucky. One thing to keep in mind tonight: some states, like California, take days to count mail ballots. Some commentators on the right point to fast-counting states like Florida and say slow counts invite suspicion, while election officials and analysts say delays reflect state rules, not fraud. Either way, early returns can shift as later ballots come in.

// Story 2: New York measles death
[ava] Story two. New York has its first measles death in more than thirty years. Health officials announced Monday evening that a child in Lewis County, in the state's rural North Country, had died. The New York Times, citing the county coroner, reports it was an unvaccinated seven-month-old boy from an Amish family in the town of Denmark. Lewis County now has sixteen confirmed cases, all in unvaccinated people, and the state counted a hundred seventy-eight cases this year as of late October. Governor Kathy Hochul called the death "a devastating reminder of what's at stake when Washington deliberately erodes decades of progress," an apparent reference to federal health policy under Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Junior, who has questioned vaccines. We haven't seen a federal response to her remarks. Officials say the M.M.R. vaccine is the best protection, and babies as young as six months in outbreak areas can get an early dose.
> source: AP, "New York reports its first measles death in 30 years" (Nov 2); WWNY 7 News citing The New York Times (Nov 3); New York State Department of Health joint statement (Nov 2); Governor Hochul statement (Nov 2)

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Ukraine blackouts
[ava] Story four. Ukraine is living with the fallout from Russia's overnight attack on its power grid, one of the largest this year. Kyiv is under emergency power cuts, with some districts scheduled to be without power around the clock, and widespread water outages because pumps need electricity. Across several regions, some customers face more than twelve hours a day without power. Zelensky is asking allies for more air defense. Separately, the A.P. reports the death toll from Monday's Russian strike in the Zaporizhzhia region has risen to thirty-one. Zelensky says most of the victims were civilians who had come to receive humanitarian aid. Russia says it targets military and energy facilities that support Ukraine's war effort.
> source: Kyiv Independent, "Kyiv faces emergency power cuts, widespread water outages" (Nov 3); bne IntelliNews (Nov 3); AP, "Death toll rises to 31 from Russian strikes on a humanitarian aid site in Ukraine" (Nov 3)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Siri AI for third-party apps
[andrew] Story seven, and this one is for your iPhone. Apple is opening its new Siri A.I. to third-party apps. With iOS twenty-seven point three, in a developer preview starting today, outside developers can let Siri use your personal context, understand natural requests, and see what's on screen to get things done inside their apps. Apple's example is a ride-share app: you could say "Hey Siri, change my ride pickup to a new address" without naming the app. There's also a new "app skills" feature that chains several steps into one action. Apple is bringing the same abilities to its own apps, from Mail and Messages to Maps and Photos, so you could ask something like "find the coffee shop I went to last week." Since it's a developer preview, you won't see it in your favorite apps until developers build it in.
> source: The Verge, "Apple starts giving Siri AI access to third-party apps" (Nov 3); 9to5Mac via The Verge (Nov 3)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: ChatGPT search volume; Anthropic message threads
[andrew] Story eight. OpenAI says ChatGPT now handles about one-fifth as many searches as Google. ChatGPT product lead Adam Fry counts a search as any request that triggers a web search, not simple definitions or how-tos. He didn't say where the Google figure came from, so that comparison is OpenAI's own and unverified. For context, Microsoft said during Google's antitrust trial that Bing had only about three percent of the search market after more than a decade. Fry also said ads in ChatGPT haven't caused a lasting backlash, though some users and critics have objected to them.
[andrew] And for developers, Anthropic opened a public beta of message threads in its A.P.I. Apps can now send just the newest messages plus a pointer to the last reply instead of resending the whole conversation, which Anthropic says cuts the wait for very long requests by about thirty-five percent.
> source: The Verge, "OpenAI says ChatGPT is catching up to Google in search" (Nov 3); Anthropic, Messages API message threads public beta (Nov 3), via DailyCited (Nov 3)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Ivanti Connect Secure zero-days
[andrew] Story nine, a follow-up on the Ivanti attacks we told you about over the weekend. Ivanti has patched two more flaws in its Connect Secure remote-access gateways that attackers chained with an authentication bypass patched in October. One lets an attacker raise their privileges, and the other lets them upload files. Researchers at Volexity saw the chain give attackers root access even on fully patched devices, which they used to steal credentials and map internal networks. They didn't leave a backdoor behind, and they edited logs to hide their tracks. More than five thousand of these gateways are exposed online, and it isn't known how many were hit. The advice is to patch, then check for signs of past compromise, because patching alone won't reveal it.
> source: iSec News, "Ivanti Patches Two More Connect Secure Zero Days Used in Attack Chain" (Nov 3); MSP Business, Patchwork (Nov 3)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 12: Witches Brew, Las Vegas
[ava] Story twelve, something good. In Las Vegas, Lyndsay Erickson's son Christian has inoperable brain cancer, and his treatment has left him too vulnerable to go trick-or-treating. So every Halloween the family brings the party to their front yard with a free dinner they call the Witches Brew: chili, cornbread and gumbo for the neighborhood. The first year drew maybe twenty or thirty people. This year she expected about three hundred. Neighbors bring their candy to Christian, so he still gets his favorite part of the holiday, and at the end of the night, the family boxes up the leftovers and quietly delivers them to neighbors in need.
> source: FOX5 Las Vegas via ParentDiaries, "A Las Vegas Mom Whose Son Can't Trick-or-Treat Because of Brain Cancer Turned Halloween Into a Free Front-Yard Feast" (Nov 3)
