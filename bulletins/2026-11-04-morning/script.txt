## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Democrats win Congress
[ava] Story one. Democrats have won control of both the House and the Senate. The Associated Press called the Senate late Tuesday, after Josh Turek won in Iowa, and the House early this morning. In the Senate, Democrats flipped seven Republican-held seats. Roy Cooper won in North Carolina, Sherrod Brown in Ohio, and James Talarico beat Ken Paxton in Texas. In Maine, Troy Jackson unseated Susan Collins. Adam Hamilton beat Senator Roger Marshall in Kansas, which hadn't elected a Democratic senator since 1932. And in South Carolina, Annie Andrews unseated Senator Graham. Democrats also held Georgia and Michigan.
[ava] As of early this morning, Ballotpedia counts at least fifty-four Senate seats for Democrats, including two independents, to forty-four for Republicans, with two races uncalled. In the House, Democrats are well past the two hundred eighteen seats needed, with many races still being counted. Democrats also flipped four governorships, in Georgia, Iowa, Ohio and Texas, where Gina Hinojosa beat Governor Greg Abbott. In Georgia, Keisha Lance Bottoms becomes the first Black woman elected governor in the U.S.
[ava] Republicans did have bright spots, holding Senate seats in Florida, Louisiana and Nebraska, and re-electing Governor Kelly Ayotte right here.

// Story 3: Trump and Republicans react
[ava] Story three, the reaction. President Trump told The New York Times he is "absolutely willing to accept" the results and expects to "work something out" with Democrats. He told Fox News Democrats "did a good job," but predicted plenty of vetoes before deals. He also posted that Republicans would have won in a landslide if he'd been on the ballot. He also acknowledged that the Iran war and gas prices hurt Republicans, though he told The Wall Street Journal he'd go to war again.
[ava] Democrats call the results a clear rebuke of the president's record. Many Republicans say the second midterm of a two-term presidency has historically been rough for the party in power, and expect the pendulum to swing back by 2028. A White House official blamed candidate quality.
> source: The New York Times, "Trump Says in Interview That He Will Accept Election Results" (Nov 3); BBC, "Trump takes aim at Republicans but says Democrats did a 'good job'" (Nov 4); Axios (Nov 4); RTE (Nov 4); CBS News on Trump's Wall Street Journal interview (Nov 4)
// Story 4: Ballot measures
[ava] Story four, ballot measures. Missouri voters rejected an amendment that would have repealed abortion rights there, and also turned down a new congressional map. Virginia voters added a right to reproductive freedom, including abortion, to the state constitution. Voting rules split: four states added citizenship requirements to vote, North Carolina and Ohio expanded photo I.D., and Arizona, California and Colorado rejected new I.D. measures.
> source: AP, "Abortion rights win and Trump's redistricting loses in ballot measures" (Nov 4); Jefferson City News Tribune, Missouri results (Nov 3); Ballotpedia News, top ballot measure results (Nov 4)

## International | sting=world
// Story 5: Iran reacts; Hormuz tankers
[ava] Story five. Iran's parliament speaker and chief negotiator, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, says Americans voted "no to war," and warned of a "nightmare" if Washington ignores that message. President Trump told the Financial Times he has no immediate plans to strike Iran, after U.S. media had reported plans for strikes as early as today.

That claim hasn't been independently confirmed. Talks continue through Qatar, but Iran called the latest U.S. response "unconvincing."

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: APT29 zero-click watering holes; Android November patches
[andrew] Story ten, and it's about your phone. Researchers at Sekoia say a Russian state hacking group known as A.P.T. twenty-nine, or Midnight Blizzard, hid zero-click exploit chains on hacked websites and lookalike sites, including a fake version of the official America two-fifty site. Just visiting could be enough for the site to identify your phone and push a matching exploit, with no click needed. On older iPhones and Android phones, it stole data like messaging databases, saved passwords and photos. The good news: Apple and Google have already patched every flaw used, and the malware doesn't survive a restart. The attribution is Sekoia's, at moderate-to-high confidence. The fix is simple: update.
[andrew] Speaking of updates, Google's November Android bulletin fixes more than two hundred flaws, including a critical one in Android fourteen through seventeen that could let an attacker run code with no user interaction. Look for the November fifth patch level or later.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Hackers use n-day exploits in zero-click attacks on iOS, Android devices" (Nov 4), citing Sekoia; Android Security Bulletin, November 2026; SecurityWeek, "Android's November 2026 Updates Patch Over 200 Vulnerabilities" (Nov 2026)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 12: NH and Londonderry results
[ava] Story twelve, here at home. Chris Pappas won New Hampshire's open U.S. Senate seat, beating John E. Sununu by about fifty-six to forty-two percent. The A.P. called it at eight twenty, and Sununu conceded. Pappas becomes the first openly gay man elected to the Senate. Governor Kelly Ayotte won a second term over Cinde Warmington, about fifty-one to forty-seven. Stefany Shaheen won the First District seat Pappas is leaving, after Anthony DiLorenzo conceded, and Maggie Goodlander was re-elected in the Second. The Concord Monitor reports Democrats appear to have flipped both chambers of the legislature and all five Executive Council seats. Question one, which would abolish the register of probate, has about sixty-five percent yes, just short of the two-thirds it needs, with votes still being counted.
[ava] In Londonderry, unofficial results show big changes. Democrats appear to have won six of seven State House seats in District sixteen, with Republican David Lundgren the only incumbent to hold on. Former House Speaker Sherman Packard lost his seat. Democrat Michelle Stith beat incumbent Julius Soti in District thirty-five, and Republican State Senator Sharon Carson held her seat by about five hundred votes. And this Sunday, there's a Jingle and Mingle vendor fair from ten to three at the Londonderry Fish and Game Club on Lund Street.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 13: Punjab mosques rebuilt by neighbors
[ava] Story thirteen, something good. In villages in India's Punjab, Sikh and Hindu neighbors are helping rebuild mosques lost after the 1947 Partition. In Jakhiwal, a seventy-seven-year-old Sikh woman, Bibi Rajinder Kaur, urged her grandson to donate land for a new mosque. In Punawal, Jaspal Sharma, from a family of Hindu priests, gave land too, and threw a feast for the whole village when it opened. In Umarpura, Sikh volunteers restored an old Muslim shrine. One of them said, "We live as one."
