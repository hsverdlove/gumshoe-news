## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Trump on the two Dan Sullivans
[ava] Story one, Alaska. President Trump posted this morning that the votes for the, quote, "Fake Dan Sullivan," and for the other trailing candidate, should be given immediately to the real Senator Dan Sullivan, and the election declared over. That's not how Alaska's ranked-choice system works. If no one tops fifty percent, the last-place candidates are eliminated one at a time, and their voters' next choices are counted, a process that starts after the final count on November eighteenth.
[ava] As of this morning, Democrat Mary Peltola led the senator by about six hundred votes, roughly forty-eight point two percent to forty-seven point nine. The other Dan Sullivan, a retired teacher, has about thirty-six hundred votes, and a second Republican on the ballot has about forty-eight hundred, so both could matter. Republicans accuse Peltola's campaign of recruiting the second Sullivan as a spoiler, citing New York Times reporting. Peltola, her campaign and Sullivan himself deny it, and no investigation has found wrongdoing so far. A federal judge threw out the Justice Department's suit over the race on Sunday, and the department is appealing.
> source: Axios (Nov 5); Just the News (Nov 5); Reuters on Judge Gleason's dismissal (Nov 1); View from the Gallery fact check (Nov 5)
// Story 2: Brooklyn fire update
[ava] Story two, an update on the Sunset Park fire in Brooklyn. Six adults died, Mayor Zohran Mamdani confirmed, and none has been identified yet; the medical examiner is handling that. Firefighters rescued two people trapped behind window bars, and the fire spread to a neighboring home. Eight residents are displaced, and the Red Cross is putting them up in hotels. Two lithium-ion batteries were found in the house, but the fire department says it hasn't determined whether they played a role. This comes less than a week after four members of one family died in a Bronx fire blamed on a lithium-ion battery.
> source: ABC7 New York (Nov 5); New York Post (Nov 5); Gothamist (Nov 5); AP via ABC News (Nov 5)

## Markets | sting=markets
// Story 5: Open, BoE, claims
[andrew] Story five, the markets at the open. The Dow rose about three hundred thirty points, or six tenths of a percent, to around fifty-three thousand six hundred eighty-seven. The S and P 500 slipped about two tenths of a percent, and the Nasdaq fell about eight tenths, as chip and data-center stocks kept sliding. Barron's says Nvidia was set to open below two hundred dollars a share for the first time since July, while banks and retailers, like JPMorgan, Goldman, Walmart and Home Depot, are leading the Dow. Qualcomm is down sharply after a weak outlook tied to a memory-chip shortage, and Tesla and SpaceX are lower on worries about more scrutiny from a Democratic Congress.
[andrew] The jobs data were strong. First-time jobless claims fell to one hundred eighty-nine thousand, matching July's reading, which was the lowest since nineteen sixty-nine. Some economists caution that a smaller labor force, after the immigration crackdown, may be part of why claims are so low. Friday's October jobs report is expected to show about sixty-five thousand new jobs.
[andrew] Overseas, the Bank of England raised its key rate a quarter point, to four percent, its first hike in over three years. The vote was eight to one, and the one dissenter wanted a bigger, half-point hike. The Bank blamed energy prices from the Iran war and now expects British inflation near five percent by the middle of next year. Governor Andrew Bailey said this shouldn't be seen as the first of a long series of hikes, but unless the war is resolved, it probably won't be the last. In other markets, U.S. crude is up about one and a half percent, near eighty-nine fifty, gold is up more than one percent, near forty-one thirty, and bitcoin is around seventy-nine thousand six hundred.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: OpenAI community compact
[andrew] Story seven. With local opposition to data centers on the ballot in a lot of places Tuesday, OpenAI published what it calls a National Community Compact for its future "compute campuses," its new name for data centers. The company pledges to pay the full cost of the electricity and grid upgrades it needs, with financial guarantees so other ratepayers aren't stuck if a project is canceled. It also promises to publish water, power, noise and hiring numbers for each site, and to stop using nondisclosure agreements with public officials, except for narrow security and trade-secret protections. It's asking lawmakers to make rules like these mandatory for the whole industry. Critics note these are voluntary promises for now, and they'll judge them by what happens at actual sites.
> source: OpenAI, "Setting a new standard for responsible AI infrastructure" (Nov 5); Bloomberg via Techmeme (Nov 5); Benzinga (Nov 5); TechCrunch (Nov 1)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: NIKKE launcher hack
[andrew] Story eight, for PC gamers. The makers of the popular shooter game NIKKE confirmed that someone tampered with the scripts in the game's Windows launcher, in what's called a supply-chain attack. According to an independent researcher's analysis, which BleepingComputer partly verified, the altered files date back to late September and quietly installed a remote-access backdoor that could steal passwords, browser data and screenshots, and in some cases cryptocurrency wallet details. The publisher pushed a fix on Tuesday and has posted instructions for checking whether a computer was affected. If you or someone in the house plays it on PC, run a full antivirus scan, and if anything turns up, change passwords from a clean device.
> source: BleepingComputer, Bill Toulas (Nov 4, updated Nov 5)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Terns return to Pagham
[ava] Story eleven, something good. In 2022, bird flu wiped out the sandwich tern colony at Pagham Harbour, on England's south coast between Brighton and Southampton, and the birds abandoned it. This year, the count went from zero to seven hundred fifty-six. Britain's Royal Society for the Protection of Birds credits years of work: predator-proof fencing, rat patrols, clearing vegetation so the terns can nest, and volunteers asking dog walkers to give the ground-nesting birds some room. Little terns, ringed plovers and oystercatchers are up too. As the project manager put it, things began to "tern" around last year.
> source: Good News Network, Andy Corbley (Nov 5), citing the RSPB and The Guardian
