## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Massie / Hegseth impeachment
[ava] Story one, Capitol Hill. Kentucky Republican Thomas Massie says he will force a House vote next week on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth unless Hegseth resigns first. Massie filed eight articles in September, accusing Hegseth of waging the Iran war without congressional authorization and of other abuses. He lost his primary in May, so this lame-duck stretch is his last window.
[ava] Multiple Republican sources told CNN they expect the House vote to pass. Impeachment in the House takes a simple majority; removal would still need a two-thirds Senate conviction, which would require a chunk of Republican votes either now or after Democrats take larger majorities in January. The White House says President Trump is backing Hegseth and wants him to fight it. Hegseth's allies call the push scapegoating after midterm losses tied to the war; Massie and other critics say the war itself was unauthorized and badly planned. No vote has been scheduled yet.
> source: CNN via KTIV (Nov 5); Stars and Stripes (Nov 5); Al Jazeera (Nov 5); USA Today (updated Nov 5); Washington Examiner (Nov 5)
// Story 2: National Popular Vote Compact path
[ava] Story two. Democrats' new trifectas in Pennsylvania, Michigan, Arizona, Wisconsin and Nevada have opened a path to activate the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact before 2028. The compact, already law in eighteen states and D.C. for two hundred twenty-two electoral votes, would have member states award their electors to the national popular-vote winner once they collectively hold two hundred seventy. Those five states would push the total to about two hundred eighty-three if they all enact it.
[ava] That's a big if. The states still have to pass the bills, Michigan stalled when it last had a trifecta, and Nevada's then-governor vetoed it in 2019. Legal scholars disagree on whether Congress must approve an interstate compact like this, and court challenges are expected. Supporters say it would end presidents who lose the popular vote; opponents say it sidelines the Electoral College without a constitutional amendment and could dilute smaller states' influence.
[ava] One quick count note: in Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom's lead over Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith has widened to roughly seventeen hundred votes with about ninety-six percent counted. Counties have until November thirteenth to certify. The race is still uncalled.
> source: Axios, Andrew Pantazi (Nov 4); National Popular Vote state status; Ballot Access News (Nov 4); ABC News / CBS News Mississippi tallies (Nov 5, midday)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 6: OpenAI Linux cyber pipeline
[andrew] Story six. OpenAI published results from an autonomous security pipeline that scanned the Linux kernel. In a mid-October run, using an upcoming cyber model, the system reproduced about seventeen hundred vulnerabilities and produced nine hundred forty-eight working local privilege-escalation exploits that could take an unprivileged user to root, plus hundreds of information-leak proofs of concept, at an A P I cost of about one point two million dollars. OpenAI says agents also used at least ten novel exploitation techniques, and that human researchers are reviewing patches with Linux maintainers through its Patch the Planet effort.
[andrew] The company frames this as defense: find and fix faster than attackers can, and it says it is sending critical patches first with human review. Critics and outside researchers will watch how cleanly those patches land, and whether the same capability lowers the bar for offense once similar models spread. OpenAI's own write-up warns that, for a well-resourced actor, an unpatched low-privilege account should increasingly be treated as equivalent to root, which is why sandboxes and isolation matter more, not less.
> source: OpenAI, "A thousand fewer paths to root" (Nov 4); Cyber Security News and Silicon Report summaries (Nov 5)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: CWPdoor
[andrew] Story seven. Researchers at XLab, part of China's QiAnXin group, say a stealthy Linux backdoor called C W P door is spreading through a trojanized copy of C W P serve, the web server behind Control Web Panel, a hosting control panel similar to cPanel. The malware sits quiet until an attacker sends signed, encrypted H T T P headers, then runs commands while trying to keep the trigger out of the access logs. XLab says more than three hundred forty thousand C W P instances have shown up on the public internet in the past month, and a VirusTotal sample with zero detections suggests real victims already exist. Admins are advised to check for odd helper processes and integrity-header strings in the binary, replace a bad binary with a clean build, update, and rotate credentials.
> source: BleepingComputer, Bill Toulas (Nov 5, 11:24 AM ET), citing XLab / QiAnXin

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 10: Janet Chin cat houses
[ava] Story ten, something good. In northern Singapore, Janet Chin, who's in her mid-fifties, has hand-built close to two hundred cardboard houses for community cats. She started in twenty twenty-one after seeing feeders use plain carton boxes as shelters, and after bringing home rescued cats of her own. She uses clean cartons, water-resistant wallpaper and recycled bits, and the fancier designs can take a week or more. Neighbors photograph the cats napping inside castles, photo booths and even M R T-inspired cabins. When someone pays for a custom house, she says the money goes back into her rescue work. It's a small, practical kindness, one box at a time.
> source: Mothership.SG, Chloe Loh (Nov 5); The Straits Times on Chin's cat cottages
