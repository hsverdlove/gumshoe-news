## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Newsom death-row commutations
[ava] Story one, California. Governor Gavin Newsom said today he is commuting the death sentences of all five hundred forty-eight people on the state's death row to life in prison without the possibility of parole. California has not executed anyone since two thousand six, and Newsom imposed a moratorium in twenty nineteen. About three hundred eighty-two of the cases still need California Supreme Court approval because those inmates have multiple felony convictions; the rest can take effect under his executive authority.
[ava] Supporters call it overdue mercy in a system that hasn't carried out a sentence in twenty years. Opponents, including victims' families and some Republican lawmakers, say it overrides jurors' verdicts and weakens deterrence. Newsom framed the move as closing a chapter before he leaves office.
> source: AP News (Nov 5); BBC News (Nov 5); NBC News (Nov 5); California Governor's Office explanatory statement (Nov 5)
// Story 2: Dhillon resigns
[ava] Story two, Washington. Harmeet Dhillon, the assistant attorney general who led the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division, resigned today, two days after Democrats won majorities in both houses of Congress. Attorney General Todd Blanche accepted the resignation and praised her service. Dhillon had overhauled the division's priorities under the Trump administration; civil-rights groups often criticized those shifts, while her allies said she was correcting what they called politicized enforcement. A successor has not been named.
> source: AP News (Nov 5); CBS News (Nov 5); Washington Post (Nov 5); Politico (Nov 5)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Yoani Sanchez detained
[ava] Story three, Cuba. Independent journalist Yoani Sanchez, founder and director of the outlet Fourteen Y Medio, was detained by State Security agents in Havana this morning after she left home to go shopping. She texted her newsroom that she had been stopped; her whereabouts were still unknown hours later. Sanchez's outlet has faced short-term detentions of its staff for years; supporters cast her as a press-freedom case, while the government has long cast independent outlets as enemies of the state.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 6: ChatGPT Lockdown Mode bypass
[andrew] Story six. Check Point researchers say they found a now-patched way around Lockdown Mode in OpenAI's ChatGPT — the optional setting meant to block data from leaking out of a chat after a prompt injection. The flaw was in how HTML previews handled nested sandbox frames and popups, letting content escape the sandbox. In theory that could mean silent chat theft, phishing pages, or malware lures, including zero-click exfiltration on the desktop app. OpenAI fixed it in mid-September after responsible disclosure; Check Point published the details today. The researchers' broader point: A I safety toggles are only as strong as the app shell around them.
> source: The Hacker News, Ravie Lakshmanan (Nov 5), citing Check Point Research / Eli Smadja

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: Truffle Security secrets revocation
[andrew] Story seven. Truffle Security launched a free Secrets Revocation Program aimed at about one point four million publicly exposed A P I keys and other secrets that are still live. The company says eighty-one percent of keys it reported last year still worked six months later, and that A I coding tools have made leaks worse — more than twenty-six hundred working keys turned up in tool config files since early twenty twenty-five. Sixteen issuers have joined so far, including Hugging Face, Notion, Vercel and Twilio, so Truffle can push verified leaks to a revocation endpoint or webhook and kill keys within fourteen days even when owners ignore the email. Google's earlier move to auto-disable leaked cloud keys is the model they're pointing to.
> source: BleepingComputer, Bill Toulas (Nov 5, 3:15 PM ET), citing Truffle Security

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 10: Giant burrowing frogs
[ava] Story ten, something good. In Victoria, Australia, scientists have returned fifty-three southern giant burrowing frogs to the wild in East Gippsland. After the twenty nineteen–twenty black summer fires, researchers found about one hundred sixty tadpoles in puddles, raised them at Melbourne Zoo, and bred the species in captivity for the first time. The males released now are about five years old, roughly the size of a cricket ball, and spend most of their lives buried until the rains come. Each frog was tagged, and song meters will listen for their calls. There are only five known breeding sites left in the state, so it's a small, careful step against extinction.
> source: The Guardian, Donna Lu (Nov 4–5); Zoos Victoria via Guardian reporting
