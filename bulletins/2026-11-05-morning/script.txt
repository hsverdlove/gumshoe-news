## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Iran says "no to war"; Qatar proposal; Hormuz
[ava] Story four. Iran's chief negotiator, parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, posted that "Americans said NO TO WAR, and we endorse this message," then warned that if Washington ignores its voters, Iran's answer would come "with a nightmare." President Masoud Pezeshkian says no one benefits if fighting resumes, and that the Supreme Leader has given the government room to negotiate on issues once off-limits. Iran still wants the U.S. naval blockade lifted, sanctions eased and frozen money released before it reopens the Strait of Hormuz, and CBS reports several ships came under attack in the strait this week, even as the talks go on.
> source: BBC (Nov 5); CBS News Iran war live updates (Nov 4 to 5); Gulf News live blog (Nov 5); Iran International (Nov 5)
// Story 5: Armenia and the CSTO
[ava] Story five, Armenia. Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan said today that his country's interest in the Collective Security Treaty Organization, a Russian-led military alliance, "has run its course." Armenia froze its participation in 2024, after concluding the alliance didn't help it against Azerbaijan, and it has stopped paying dues. The alliance's secretary general says expelling Armenia isn't on the table, but a council meeting in Moscow this month could strip its voting rights until it pays. Moscow sees the alliance as central to regional security; Yerevan has been moving closer to the West.
> source: Armenpress (Nov 5); News.am (Nov 5)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: GhostMap
[andrew] Story ten, for Android users. Researchers at Bitdefender found a flaw they call GhostMap that lets a booby-trapped app, needing no permissions and never even opened, trap a phone in an endless reboot loop, even in safe mode. The fix is either a factory reset, which erases your data, or a technical cleanup that only works if developer debugging was already turned on. Bitdefender says Google closed its report in August as not reproducible and out of scope for a security patch. Google hasn't commented yet. The advice: install apps only from the Play Store and trusted developers.
> source: BleepingComputer, Bill Toulas (Nov 5), research by Bitdefender
// Story 11: APT29 phone exploits
[andrew] Story eleven. The security firm Sekoia says a Russian state hacking group, A.P.T. twenty-nine, booby-trapped real websites, plus fake copies of the America 250 anniversary site and a London hotel, to attack iPhones and Android phones that simply visited. The attacks used already-patched flaws to steal browser cookies, passwords, and even WhatsApp and Telegram data from phones that hadn't been updated. Apple and Google have fixed every flaw involved, and the malware doesn't survive a restart. So the takeaway is simple: keep your phone updated.
> source: BleepingComputer (Nov 4), research by Sekoia

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 13: Wild River Fire; events
[ava] Story thirteen, here in New Hampshire. The Wild River Fire in the White Mountain National Forest, about seven miles southeast of Gorham near the Maine line, has grown to nine hundred eighty-two acres, with containment at eleven percent. A Forest Service spokesperson calls it the forest's largest fire on record. About ninety firefighters, three helicopters and two drones are on it, with crews wading across the Wild River to reach steep, drought-dried slopes. Investigators believe it was human-caused. Wild River Road and several trails are closed through January first, and there's a public meeting tonight from six to seven at Shelburne Town Hall. No homes are threatened.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 14: Roman found safe
[ava] Story fourteen, something good. In the hills of Lawrence County, Kentucky, six-year-old Roman Heilman, who has autism and doesn't speak, went missing on Sunday afternoon. Hundreds of volunteers searched through two cold, rainy nights, with drones, thermal cameras and helicopters. On Tuesday morning, a volunteer on horseback named Jonah Lainhart spotted him about a mile and a half from home. Roman ran to him and clung to his neck. He was scratched, bruised and hungry, but alive, and a small dog was with him, which searchers think may have kept him company the whole time. The riders, who drove two and a half hours to help a child they'd never met, carried him out on a horse named Macee.
> source: The Mountain Citizen, Annie Holler (Nov 4); Kentucky State Police statement as reported
