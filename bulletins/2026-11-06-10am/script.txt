## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: OpenSSH sshole
[andrew] Story eight. Researchers disclosed an OpenSSH flaw nicknamed sshole that can let attackers log into certain Alpine Linux and other musl-based systems even when an account's password is disabled with an asterisk in the shadow file. The trick is an oversized password — longer than two hundred fifty-six characters — that makes musl's crypt function return a star, which then matches the disabled hash. It needs password auth on, PAM off, and at least one real passworded account nearby. Official Alpine cloud images were among the worry cases. OpenSSH ten point six p two patches it; Alpine has backports. Workaround if you can't update yet: turn off password authentication and restart sshd. Systems on glibc with PAM, like most Ubuntu and Debian boxes, aren't hit the same way.
> source: BleepingComputer, Lawrence Abrams (Nov 5); Alpine Linux security advisory (Nov 5); SecurityWeek (Nov 5); oss-security / OpenSSH (Nov 5)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Beavers return to Loch Ness
[ava] Story eleven, something good. A family of six beavers has been released into the Loch Ness catchment in Scotland — the first in that area in about five hundred years. The Ness Beaver Project, led with the Dundreggan rewilding estate, plans more family groups over the next three years. Conservationists say the dams and channels should rebuild wetlands that help frogs, dragonflies, birds and bats. Locals joked that Nessie finally has neighbors; biologists just hope the beavers stick around.
