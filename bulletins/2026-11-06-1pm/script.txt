## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Data centers / midterm backlash
[ava] Story one. Artificial-intelligence data centers took a political beating in Tuesday's midterms, and a tougher local climate for the biggest server farms may be next. The Associated Press reports that opposition to data centers featured in hundreds of millions of dollars of campaign ads, and hard-line stances against the energy-hungry warehouses helped Democrats who flipped longtime Republican governorships in Texas, Ohio and Georgia — all major data-center states.
[ava] In Texas, governor-elect Gina Hinojosa says she wants approvals for new data centers kept on pause until the Legislature writes stronger protections for residents, electricity bills and water. Supporters call that overdue local control after years of projects racing ahead of the grid. Industry backers warn a long pause could push investment and jobs to friendlier states, and note that President Trump remains one of the sector's loudest federal champions, so any national crackdown is far from automatic. Data-center rules themselves were not on most ballots; the fight is shifting to statehouses and permitting boards.

## International | sting=world
// Story 2: Kenya Mackenzie sentencing
[ava] Story two, Kenya. A court in Nairobi has sentenced doomsday cult leader Paul Mackenzie to two hundred years in prison for murdering one hundred ninety-one people — one hundred ninety of them children — in the Shakahola forest starvation case. Justice Diana Mochache said Mackenzie bore primary culpability for ordering followers to fast to death so they could "meet Jesus." Prosecutors had sought the death penalty; the judge declined it, saying capital punishment would erase any chance of rehabilitation. Kenya has not carried out an execution since nineteen eighty-seven.
[ava] More than four hundred bodies were later exhumed from the forest; this murder case covers one hundred ninety-one of those deaths. Twenty-eight co-accused received sentences of up to one hundred sixty years. Mackenzie still faces separate manslaughter and terrorism charges, which he denies. Families who lost relatives said they wanted a sentence that matched what happened in the forest.
> source: BBC News (Nov 6); AP News (Nov 6); The Guardian (Nov 6); Daily Nation (Nov 6)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 6: Claude Dispatch / browser extensions
[andrew] Story six. Security researchers at Forever Security showed that a browser extension able to run scripts on Claude's website could push tasks through Claude Dispatch and make Claude Desktop run code on a user's computer. Dispatch is the feature that sends jobs from a phone — or, in this case, a hijacked web session — to the desktop agent. In a lab test, an extension queued prompts that installed an n p m package; Claude asked for approval, but the extension auto-clicked Allow, and Calculator opened as a harmless proof. The same path could steal files or drop a backdoor if Claude has broad permissions.
[andrew] Anthropic, which makes Claude, told the researchers in mid-October that Dispatch would be retired; the help page still says existing Pro, Max and Ultra users can keep using it for now, closed to new sign-ups. Researchers Gal Weizman and colleagues say related remote-control features may share the design risk. No real-world attacks were reported. Practical steps: turn off cross-device Dispatch if you don't need it, treat extensions like other software you install, and watch for unexpected package installs or setting changes when an agent is running.
> source: The Hacker News / Forever Security (Nov 6); Forever Security technical write-up (Nov 2026)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: Chainalysis breach
[andrew] Story seven. Blockchain intelligence firm Chainalysis says a recent attack exposed credentials and live tokens for all of its customers — including law enforcement, banks and crypto firms that use its tools to trace illicit funds. An earlier note had downplayed the hit as non-sensitive identity metadata; later updates said usernames, password hashes and live tokens for every user were exposed in what the company called an on-path, or man-in-the-middle, attack, after attackers used what it described as offensive agentic A I techniques.
[andrew] Chainalysis says it still has no evidence that investigative graph data was read or attribution repositories altered. It has taken the login portal and several products offline, asked customers for trusted I P addresses for an allowlist, and will require password resets and A P I key rotation. Separately, Q N A P warned of active Plex2Shell compromises on some N A S devices — update Plex, and treat exposed vulnerable boxes as compromised.
> source: BleepingComputer, Sergiu Gatlan (Nov 6); Chainalysis customer advisories (Nov 2026); BleepingComputer on QNAP / Plex2Shell (Nov 6)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 10: Barbados eliminates mother-to-child HIV
[ava] Story ten, something good. The World Health Organization has certified that Barbados has eliminated mother-to-child transmission of H I V as a public health problem. Over three years of assessment, ninety-eight percent of pregnant women were tested and ninety-eight percent of those with H I V received treatment, with no new infant infections attributed to vertical transmission. Testing and treatment are free under Barbados's universal care system. Barbados joins a short list of countries WHO recognizes for wiping out that path of infection. A Unicef official put it simply: an H I V diagnosis in pregnancy there no longer has to mean transmission to a child.
> source: WHO news release (Oct 30, 2026); PAHO / UNAIDS statements (Oct 2026); Positive News week-45 roundup (Nov 6); Barbados Today (Oct 30)
