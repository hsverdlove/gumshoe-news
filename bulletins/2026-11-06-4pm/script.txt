## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Miller replaces Wiles as chief of staff
[ava] Story one, Washington. President Trump said today that Stephen Miller is replacing Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff, effective Monday, days after Republicans lost both chambers of Congress. Wiles resigned Thursday night and is moving to the Trump political operation; Trump called her service outstanding. Miller, a longtime adviser and architect of the administration's hard-line immigration agenda, was deputy chief of staff for policy.
[ava] Allies cast the pick as a signal to the base that Trump wants a loyalist who executes aggressively in the final two years. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, expected to become speaker in January, demanded Miller be fired, calling him a dangerous extremist. Midterm exit polling found most voters saying immigration enforcement had gone too far — so the promotion is also a bet that base intensity matters more than wooing the middle.
> source: AP News (Nov 6); NBC News (Nov 6); Washington Post (Nov 6); Guardian live midterm updates (Nov 6)

[ava] Story two, London. A jury at Southwark Crown Court has found entertainer Russell Brand guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault against women he encountered between two thousand four and two thousand nine. ## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 5: Straight Arrow News closes
[andrew] Story five. Straight Arrow News, the Omaha-based outlet founded by billionaire Joe Ricketts in twenty twenty-one to pitch fact-first journalism without a partisan slant, is shutting down. In a note to readers today, Ricketts said belief in the mission was not enough to make the business sustainable after considerable investment. Staff learned in a Friday meeting that operations would cease; paying subscribers are being told about refunds, and the archive will stay up for a limited wind-down. It is the second newsroom Ricketts has closed after D N A info and Gothamist in twenty seventeen — a reminder that even well-funded digital news startups can fail when advertising and subscriptions do not cover the bill.
> source: Straight Arrow News CEO note, Joe Ricketts (Nov 6); Mediagazer / staff posts (Nov 6); X search_news roundup (Nov 6)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: Barracuda Email Security Gateway chain
[andrew] Story seven. Security firm watchTowr detailed a chain of two flaws in Barracuda's Email Security Gateway that can give an attacker root access with no login. One bug is an authorization bypass that lets someone restore a crafted backup without signing in; the other is a command injection that normally needs an admin account. Chained, they reach the highest privilege on the appliance. Mandiant scored the bypass nine point three out of ten. Barracuda released patches Monday — Email Security Gateway nine point four point zero point zero three zero and later — and watchTowr published a detector, not an exploit. No public attacks are tied to this pair yet; still, if you run Barracuda email filtering, patch and keep the admin interface off the open internet.
> source: The Hacker News, Swati Khandelwal (Nov 6), citing watchTowr and Barracuda advisories

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 10: Appalachian Bear Rescue cubs
[ava] Story ten, something good. Appalachian Bear Rescue in Blount County, Tennessee, has released its first six rehabilitated bear cubs back into the wild since reopening after a year-and-a-half closure. The cubs arrived this summer as light as seven and a half pounds and left around fifty-five pounds, each fitted with a tracker. The rescue had shut down after earlier bears contracted pneumonia; new protocols include a two-week quarantine and earlier releases so cubs do not over-fatten before hibernation. If soil tests clear older pens, capacity could rise from six to eighteen cubs by spring — a small, careful comeback for a place that exists to give orphaned bears a second chance.
> source: WVLT, Jared Austin (Nov 5–6); Appalachian Bear Rescue / TWRA via WVLT
