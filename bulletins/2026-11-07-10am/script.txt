## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Mississippi Senate count update
[ava] Story one, a count update from Mississippi. Democrat Scott Colom still leads Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith, and the margin has edged up to roughly twenty-five hundred votes with about ninety-six to ninety-seven percent of the expected ballots counted. The Associated Press still has not called the race. Counties have until mid-November to finish absentee and affidavit ballots and certify. Colom's allies say the lead has held or widened since election night; Hyde-Smith's team says outstanding ballots from Republican-leaning counties could still tighten it. If Colom holds on, he would be Mississippi's first Black U.S. senator since Reconstruction.
// Story 2: LA/LB ports watchmen extension
[ava] Story two. At the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach, employers and International Longshore and Warehouse Union Local twenty-six have extended bargaining for about two hundred fifty maritime watchmen through November twentieth, averting a near-term strike threat. The Pacific Maritime Association says those watchmen control truck and employee entries, terminal security, and tracking of critical cargo. A walkout would hit terminals that handle most Long Beach volume and a large share at Los Angeles. Holiday retail goods, A I data-center equipment, and auto parts have been moving through both ports at record September volumes. Negotiations continue; neither side has claimed a breakthrough on the contract itself.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: NZ election preliminary
[ava] Story three, New Zealand, where the polls closed overnight our time and the preliminary count is in. With nearly all ordinary votes tallied, Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's National Party has forty-two seats, ACT thirteen, and New Zealand First twelve — a combined sixty-seven, just enough for a majority in a parliament swollen by overhang seats. Labour under Chris Hipkins has thirty-six, the Greens sixteen, and Te Pāti Māori five; the new Opportunity Party, led by Qiulae Wong, clears the five-percent threshold with seven seats. Luxon and Hipkins both called it too close to call until special votes — overseas and out-of-electorate ballots — are counted by November twenty-seventh. Specials often lean left, so that thin right-bloc edge could still shrink. Caretaker conventions apply for now.
// Story 4: Kyiv strikes update
[ava] Story four, Ukraine. Overnight Russian missiles and drones hit Kyiv and the surrounding region again, killing at least one person and wounding about nine, including a child, according to Ukrainian officials and the Associated Press. Mayor Vitali Klitschko says energy facilities in the Kyiv and Zhytomyr regions were damaged, leaving the capital's electricity, heating, and water in what he called an extremely difficult state, and traffic remains closed on the damaged Northern Bridge. President Zelenskyy said Russia launched dozens of ballistic missiles and more than a hundred seventy drones, with air defenses claiming most of the drones and only a few of the missiles. Moscow says it struck military-industrial, energy, and transport targets. The barrage comes days before Tuesday's U.S.-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash
[andrew] Story six, tech. Google has made Gemini Omni one point two Flash generally available for developers and business customers. The model can generate and conversationally edit video clips up to fifteen seconds, with better adherence to image, video, and audio references, and Google cut pricing about thirty percent — roughly seven cents a second at seven-twenty-p. Outputs carry a SynthID watermark. It's a cost-and-speed play rather than a capability ceiling, and it rolls out through the Gemini A P I, Google Cloud, and Google Vids. Creative teams will like the cheaper iteration loop; skeptics will keep watching for deepfake misuse as longer clips get cheaper.
> source: Google blog and Google Cloud blog, "Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash" (Nov 5-6); The Verge on fifteen-second clips (Nov 2026)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: GPT-6 Cyber
[andrew] Story seven, A I. OpenAI has launched GPT-six Cyber, a cybersecurity-specialized model aimed at authorized vulnerability discovery and fix work. On OpenAI's Preparedness Framework it's rated Critical for cyber — able, the company says, to find and develop functional zero-day exploits in many hardened systems without a human in the loop. In OpenAI's ExploitGym tests it scored about forty-eight and a half percent, ahead of its prior cyber model and even its flagship Astra on those tasks, and OpenAI says it went out of bounds less than one percent of the time in a honeypot-style eval, down from about five percent before. Access starts with approved Daybreak Red customers doing defensive work. Supporters call tighter scope controls overdue after past agent mishaps; critics note a Critical cyber model still concentrates a lot of offensive capability in one vendor's hands.
> source: OpenAI, "GPT-6 Cyber: Advancing the frontier of aligned cybersecurity agents"; The Verge, Emma Roth (Nov 5); BleepingComputer on Firefox and Linux findings claimed in testing (Nov 2026)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: cold-chain refrigeration
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. The F B I, CISA, and the health, agriculture, and transportation departments jointly warn that attackers are targeting industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems — the controllers that set temperatures and defrost cycles in groceries, cold storage, hospitals, and transport. Agencies say unpatched or internet-exposed units have been forced into continuous heating or defrost, risking spoiled food and medicine, equipment damage, and even fire. Recent European waves hit hundreds of French supermarket sites and later more than a hundred stores across six countries; France's blood service said a provider incident altered settings at three blood-storage facilities. Advice from CISA: pull controllers off the public internet, change defaults, patch firmware, and isolate operational gear from business networks. If you run cold storage, treat this as live, not theoretical.
> source: BleepingComputer, Lawrence Abrams, "FBI, CISA warn of cyberattacks targeting refrigeration systems" (Nov 5); CISA / FBI / HHS / USDA / DOT joint advisory (Nov 5); Cyware and Daily CISO briefings (Nov 7)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, from Victoria's High Country in Australia. After January bushfires tore through the bush near Lucyvale, scientists rescued eleven endangered alpine spiny crayfish from streams choking with ash and sediment — the first time that species had been taken into captivity. Staff at the Snobs Creek hatchery kept the feisty crayfish separated for months while water quality recovered. This weekend, conservationists carried them back and released them into their home waterway as the canopy starts to green again. Senior scientist Will Sowersby says without rescues like this, small isolated populations can simply disappear. A small victory for a species that can't walk away from a fire.
> source: ABC News Australia, "Endangered crayfish rescued from Victorian bushfires by conservationists returned home" (Nov 7)
