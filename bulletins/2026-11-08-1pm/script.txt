## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Winston-Salem State homecoming shootings
[ava] Story one. Two separate shootings during homecoming weekend at Winston-Salem State University in North Carolina left one person dead and three others wounded. Police said they responded just after nine forty-five Saturday night after hearing gunshots near campus. They identified the person killed as nineteen-year-old Peyton Patterson. A twenty-two-year-old woman was also shot; she was treated at a hospital and released. Winston-Salem police later said they had charged twenty-year-old Ziyaire Spinks with murder, assault with a deadly weapon, and felony possession of a firearm on educational property. He is being held without bond. Officers have not released details of the second shooting or said whether the two are connected. Chancellor Bonita Brown canceled the rest of Sunday's homecoming events.
// Story 2: Georgia-Tennessee flood recovery
[ava] Story two, a recovery update we have not yet covered. Georgia emergency officials said Sunday they are shifting from rescue to recovery after catastrophic flooding earlier this week along the Georgia-Tennessee border — a one-in-a-thousand-year event, according to the Associated Press. The state reports a hundred three water rescues, most of them in Fannin County, after Governor Brian Kemp declared an emergency there and in three neighboring counties. On Friday, Georgia's emergency-management director said one person died when a vehicle was swept away in Murray County. As much as eight to ten inches of rain sent the Toccoa and Ocoee rivers over their banks. In the twin towns of McCaysville, Georgia, and Copperhill, Tennessee, businesses were gutted, and McCaysville's public-safety director said the police department and city buildings took devastating damage. A water-treatment plant is still offline with no reopening date, and residents have been under boil-water notices.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Iran navy / IRGC tanker-mine claims near Hormuz
[ava] Story three, the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's navy said Sunday that an oil tanker caught fire after striking a naval mine east of the strait before noon, while sailing through what Tehran calls a prohibited area with its identification systems switched off. The navy warned that the strait would stay closed to vessels that violate Iranian restrictions until those threats were removed. The Revolutionary Guard had said late Saturday that two tankers hit mines and caught fire after switching off navigation and trying an unauthorized route, and it warned that ships cooperating with U.S. forces would do the same. The Guard did not name the ships or say what happened to the crews. Separately, the British military's maritime-trade center reported that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile while sailing outbound on Saturday; it said the crew was safe and that no environmental damage was reported. It also received a report of another tanker fire near Oman's Musandam Peninsula, with that crew evacuated. The mine claims come from Iranian authorities and have not been independently confirmed; the British report described a projectile, not a mine.
// Story 4: Eisenkot formally receives mandate
[ava] Story four, Israel. President Isaac Herzog formally gave opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form the next government on Sunday in Jerusalem — the ceremony that was still ahead when we reported this morning that he would get the first chance. Eisenkot now has twenty-eight days to assemble a coalition, with up to two more weeks if needed. He said Israel is "in a state of emergency, approaching a point of no return," and that forming a government would take "creative and unconventional" political steps. He renewed his call for a national emergency government and invited Likud to join if it truly believes this is a time for unity. Eisenkot received sixty-seven recommendations from party leaders last week, to Benjamin Netanyahu's forty-six, even though Likud remains the largest single faction. Neither bloc won a clear majority, so if Eisenkot falls short, Netanyahu is likely to get a turn, and another election remains possible.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Apple Ternus presses Federighi for agentic Siri
[andrew] Story six, tech. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports that Apple's new chief executive, John Ternus, has told software chief Craig Federighi the company needs to accelerate its software plans and move quickly beyond basic improvements to Siri. In his Sunday newsletter, Gurman said he was told Apple wants Siri to work more like an agent as early as next year — keeping track of goals and carrying out the next steps without the user prompting every one — more in the vein of OpenAI's Dots, Meta's Muse, or Grok. These accounts rest on unnamed sources inside the company, and Gurman noted that the exact design is still unclear. Supporters see Apple trying to close a gap with rivals whose assistants already act on their own; skeptics will wait to see whether a more ambitious Siri actually ships, or whether Apple's usual caution wins out again.
> source: Bloomberg, Mark Gurman, Power On newsletter, as reported by MacRumors, "Siri AI Rumored to Become More Like an Agent as Early as Next Year" (Nov 8) and 9to5Mac (Nov 8)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Anthropic $3.4B gas-generator deal
[andrew] Story seven, A I. The Financial Times reports that Anthropic has struck a deal worth about three point four billion dollars for gas-fired power generators to support its data centers. According to the paper, the company bought commitments in late September for one point seven gigawatts of heavy-duty generators from OnePWR Solutions, a San Antonio supplier, including the engineering help to install them. Anthropic did not buy the supplier itself. C N B C notes that Reuters could not immediately verify the report, and that neither company had commented. Backers say locking in scarce generators is how you keep building when the grid cannot keep up. Critics hear another large bet on fossil-fuel generation, just as communities push back on the energy and climate cost of data centers.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Botpress internal-tools breach
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. The chatbot platform Botpress has disclosed a breach of its internal tools. In October, an attacker found a flaw in the sign-in proxy in front of those tools and used it to open sessions that looked as if they belonged to staff. The company says it has found no evidence that any employee's main login was taken over. The attacker then obtained an employee's A P I credential and, on October twenty-first, used that person's admin access to read the configuration of bots in sixteen customer workspaces. Those settings can include A I provider keys, messaging tokens, and C R M passwords. Botpress says the goal appeared to be stealing A I usage that could be resold on black markets. It has seen no ransom demand and no sign that customer data was published. It fixed the proxy that same day, revoked the stolen credential, and contacted the affected customers. The lesson is familiar: internal tools and the secrets sitting in a bot's settings need the same protections as the product customers see.
> source: Botpress, Michael Masson, "Security incident disclosure — November 2026" (Nov 6)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, from Northampton, England. An eleven-month-old budgie named Luna vanished when strong winds from Storm Boelo blew open a kitchen window at Cimberley Frost's home. A passing driver spotted her hopping into a roadside storm drain about a mile away and called the R S P C A. Inspector Richard Durant and trainee Rosie Cowie peered in and saw a little beak poking out of a pipe; Cowie gently lifted her out — her first rescue. A few days later Luna was back home. "The house was so quiet without her; she's always squeaking and talking," Ms. Frost said. Luna is back to flying around the flat and taunting the family dog, Ziggy. "It's her first adventure," her owner said, "and hopefully her last.""
> source: Good News Network, "Lucky Bird Found in Storm Drain a Mile From Home After Passing Driver Happens to Spot it Hopping in" (Nov 8), citing RSPCA / SWNS
