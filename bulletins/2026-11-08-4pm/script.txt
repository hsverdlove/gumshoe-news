[ava] Story one. Thomas Markle Senior, the eighty-two-year-old estranged father of Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex, was arrested Friday at a senior care facility in Florida and charged with one count of possessing child sexual abuse material. ## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Egypt train collision
[ava] Story three, Egypt. Two trains collided Sunday in the al-Ayyat district of Giza province, south of Cairo, killing seven people and injuring more than a hundred, according to Egyptian officials. The Associated Press reports that a train bound for Fayoum struck the rear of a stationary train headed to Aswan. Egypt's chief prosecutor's office is investigating, President Abdel-Fattah el-Sissi ordered a prompt inquiry, and crews worked into the evening to clear the wreckage. The A P notes that crashes are not uncommon in Egypt and are often tied to poor maintenance and mismanagement. The cause of this one has not been announced.

The A P calls it the most delicate leg of the trip: the Catholic Church in Argentina has criticized Mr. Milei's austerity measures, while the president's supporters say those cuts are needed to stabilize the economy. ## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Nvidia board investigation
[andrew] Story seven, tech. Nvidia's board says it has opened an independent investigation after reports that chief executive Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. A committee of independent directors, with outside counsel, will oversee it. The board says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised and that it has confidence in Mr. Huang and his team. News reports describe the operation as Chinese-linked, and Bloomberg Law reports Mr. Huang acknowledged an affair. Critics, including members of a House committee, want more answers about what was shared. Nvidia's defenders note the board's statement and that no wrongdoing by Mr. Huang has been established.

[andrew] Story eight. Google has bought Crusoe's unfinished data-center site in southeast Wyoming, Crusoe confirmed Friday. Supporters see needed capacity; opponents, including Senator Bernie Sanders and some newly elected Democrats, renewed calls for a national pause on new data centers.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: OpenAI GPT-6 Cyber
[andrew] Story nine, A I. OpenAI says its new G P T six Cyber model found and validated more than seventeen hundred Linux kernel vulnerabilities, and produced working local privilege-escalation exploits for nine hundred forty-eight distinct flaws. The company also reports eighty-one previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities, with Mozilla already shipping fixes for many. OpenAI says human researchers reviewed the patches, hundreds of reports have gone to maintainers, and it is withholding technical details of the new exploitation techniques. Defenders will welcome the faster bug-finding. Skeptics point out that the same capability, in other hands, could speed up attacks, and the results come from the company's own testing rather than an independent audit.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: SSHole
[ava] Story ten, the cyber desk. A flaw nicknamed SSHole, tracked as C V E twenty twenty-six, one-two-three-one-five-eight, is now being exploited. It affects OpenSSH on systems built with the musl library and without P A M, which includes Alpine Linux and potentially OpenWrt, Buildroot, and Yocto builds. An attacker can log into an account that has no usable password by submitting a password longer than two hundred fifty-six characters. The monitoring firm GreyNoise recorded six successful sessions on its honeypots, where attackers ran reconnaissance commands. Those are decoy systems, and the reports do not identify real victims. The fix is OpenSSH ten point six p-two; otherwise, administrators can turn off password authentication.
> source: Ars Technica, "Meet *sshole, the vulnerability that lets hackers SSH into Linux devices" (Nov 2026); iSec News, "OpenSSH Password Bypass Used Against Alpine Honeypots After Disclosure" (Nov 8), citing GreyNoise; The Hacker News and BleepingComputer on the SSHole flaw (Nov 2026)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, a story about a coat. Thirty years ago in Japan, a high school student named Norihisa Koyama saw a Korean schoolgirl crouched and crying after her traditional hanbok uniform had been slashed in one of the racist attacks of that era. He gave her his brand-new winter coat so she could cover up and get home safely. She kept it for three decades, his full name written inside. Earlier this year she saw a newspaper article about his charity work, recognized his name, and returned the coat. Mr. Koyama, now fifty, works with a Japanese nonprofit supporting single mothers and struggling families. The painter Park Chung-moon, whose work Chogori was inspired by those attacks, invited him to Tokyo and has launched an art project on their story. "He is like a treasure to us Koreans," the artist said.
> source: Good News Network, "She Kept His Coat for 30 Years, Then Finally Found the Boy Who Gave It to Her After Racial Attack" (Nov 8), citing Choson Sinbo
