## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: US disables cargo ship after Iran port call
[ava] Story one. U.S. Central Command said late Saturday that American forces fired on and disabled a Saint Kitts-flagged cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman after it ignored multiple warnings and tried to leave the region following a visit to an Iranian port. The United States has been enforcing a naval blockade on Iran around the Strait of Hormuz for months, and the last confirmed strike on a cargo vessel was nearly a month ago. The disclosure comes as Iran's state media reported explosions Saturday on Qeshm Island and at the port of Jask; the cause wasn't immediately clear, and Central Command didn't immediately comment on those reports. President Trump said Friday the U.S. had carried out limited strikes he described as "minor" against an Iranian air-defense system. Supporters of the blockade say it is needed to cut off revenue for Iran's weapons programs; critics warn that firing on commercial shipping raises the risk of a wider clash and of disrupting a vital oil route.
// Story 2: Five AG flips
[ava] Story two. A Reuters review published this morning underscores a quieter shift from last Tuesday's midterms: Democrats flipped attorney general offices in five Republican-leaning states — Texas, Ohio, Georgia, Iowa, and Kansas. Those offices decide which lawsuits get filed on issues including abortion, immigration, and the administration's agenda, and they often join or lead multi-state cases. Democrats cast the wins as a new check after taking Congress; Republicans see a setback for conservative legal strategy over the next two years. The races were decided on Election Day; today's reporting is about what those new attorneys general will mean in court.
> source: AP via The Independent, "US military says it fired on commercial vessel in Gulf of Oman after it attempted to breach blockade" (Nov 7, late Saturday); Reuters via StreetInsider, Democratic attorney general wins in Texas, Ohio, Georgia, Iowa, and Kansas (Nov 8)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Krakow update
[ava] Story three, new developments from Poland. Prosecutors have opened a terrorism investigation into Saturday's shooting in Krakow's main square, and the country began two days of national mourning today. Police confirmed Sunday that three people found dead at the gunman's home — about a hundred miles northeast of the city — were his father, age fifty-three, his mother, fifty-two, and his sister, twenty-one, and that they had probably died from gunshot wounds. Prosecutors also said they seized seven firearms and a significant amount of ammunition. Six people were killed in the square, including a British citizen, and at least sixteen others were wounded, including two police officers. The twenty-four-year-old suspect remains hospitalized under guard after being wounded by police. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwinski has said the attacker appears to have been influenced by far-right, ultra-nationalist views; Prime Minister Donald Tusk has called it far-right terrorism, while officials still say the full motive is under investigation. Flags are at half-staff, and mourners have left candles in the square.
// Story 4: Saudi missile intercepts / Yemen
[ava] Story four, the Middle East. Saudi Arabia said it intercepted incoming missiles targeting the capital, Riyadh, and other regions on Sunday. Falling debris damaged a building belonging to the state oil company Aramco in Riyadh's financial district. No casualties were reported, and there was no immediate claim of responsibility. Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement has claimed similar attacks in the past. Separately, Houthi officials said airstrikes hit homes in a village near the Yemeni capital, Sanaa, killing at least twenty-seven people, most of them women and children; they blamed Saudi Arabia, which had not commented at airtime. The Houthis said they attacked Saudi Arabia in response. Those casualty figures come from Houthi authorities and have not been independently confirmed.
// Story 5: Eisenkot mandate
[ava] Story five, Israel. President Isaac Herzog is giving opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot, a former military chief, the first chance to form a new government after last month's election. Neither Eisenkot's bloc nor Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's won a clear majority. After consultations this week, most of the main parties recommended Eisenkot, and he now has twenty-eight days to assemble a coalition, with up to two more weeks if needed. If he falls short, Netanyahu is likely to get a turn, and another election remains possible. A formal ceremony was set for today in Jerusalem.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: NASA astronaut class
[andrew] Story seven, tech. NASA is accepting applications for its next class of astronauts through December third. Administrator Jared Isaacman says the people selected could walk on the Moon, live and work at a lunar base, and help prepare for the first crewed missions to Mars. Applicants must be U.S. citizens with a qualifying STEM master's degree — or an equivalent path such as medical school or test-pilot school — plus at least three years of related professional experience, or specified flight hours for pilots. NASA last opened applications in twenty twenty-four, chose ten candidates from more than eight thousand applications, and expects to select the next class in late twenty twenty-seven. The agency currently has thirty-two active astronauts.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Rails ImageMagick upload flaw
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. The Rails web framework has patched a critical flaw that can let an unauthenticated attacker write files on a server by uploading a crafted image, if the app processes uploads with ImageMagick. In some setups the attacker can also read secrets the app can read, which may lead to a fuller takeover. Rails rates it nine point eight out of ten. An app is exposed only if it uses ImageMagick for Active Storage, accepts uploads from untrusted users, and doesn't already block the dangerous image handlers — Debian and Ubuntu's default policies do not. The fix is Rails eight point one point four point one, or eight point zero point five point two, and Rails says those releases also close a related high-severity flaw. The project says one attack chain was reported but did not say whether the flaw has been used in the wild. If you run Rails and take file uploads, update — and if you store files on disk, consider rotating secrets the app process can read.
> source: The Hacker News, "Critical Rails Flaw Lets Attackers Write Server Files via Crafted ImageMagick Uploads" (Nov 4); Rails security advisories GHSA-f3w6-w445-fwh9 and GHSA-v924-7rp8-4mxh

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, from Leicestershire in England. Eleven-year-old Millie, whose parents foster with the county council, buys two matching teddy bears whenever a foster child comes to stay. She and the child each keep one to cuddle, and when the child moves on they swap, so each bear carries the other's hugs. "This means that my teddy has all the baby's cuddles on it, and the teddy they take with them has all my cuddles on it," she says. The family has been approved to foster since twenty twenty-two and has welcomed five children so far. Her parents, Becky and Adam, say the whole family chose to foster together. A small tradition that makes a hard goodbye a little softer.
> source: BBC News, "Teddy bears help ease foster care goodbyes in Leicestershire" (Nov 8)
