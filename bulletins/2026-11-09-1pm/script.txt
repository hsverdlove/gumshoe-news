## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Hegseth impeachment filing
[ava] Story one. Republican Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky is moving today to force a House vote on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. C B S News reports the updated resolution runs thirty-four pages with eight articles. Three accuse Hegseth of waging war on Iran without congressional authorization; others cite civilian casualties, including the strike on an Iranian school earlier this year, the boat strikes in the Caribbean, the capture of Venezuela's Nicolás Maduro, and the Pentagon's scrutiny of Senator Mark Kelly. Once Massie triggers it, House Republican leaders have two legislative days to act, so a vote, or a vote to table or refer it, could come Wednesday or Thursday.
[ava] Democrats are expected to back it, but with a narrow Republican majority, some Republican votes would be needed. Hegseth's side argues he carried out lawful decisions by the president, and some Republicans say he's been effective and they support the war. Massie counters that the question isn't whether you like the war, but whether impeachable acts occurred. Hegseth says he'll hold a Pentagon press conference tomorrow, his first in more than six months, to make his case.
// Story 2: Trump meets Qatar's prime minister
[ava] Story two. President Trump met today with Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, the lead mediator with Iran, who also met Vice President Vance. Al Jazeera quotes the Qatari leader as saying Washington is willing to take steps toward a solution. Both sides are still far apart: Iran insists the U.S. first lift its naval blockade, release frozen assets and ease sanctions, while Secretary Rubio says there's been no progress and calls some of Iran's demands unrealistic. Al-Monitor cites a Middle East Eye report that Turkey's intelligence chief said Qatar would host Vance and a senior Iranian official next week; an Iranian official denied any such meeting is planned, so treat that as unconfirmed.
// Story 3: White House lockdown
[ava] Story three. Two people were arrested in separate incidents near the White House this morning. Just before ten, a car crashed onto the sidewalk on the north side of Lafayette Park, and the Secret Service says the driver was combative but was quickly arrested; a police bomb squad swept the vehicle as a precaution. Around the same time, a second person jumped a barricade at the park's northwest corner and was also arrested. The area was locked down until about eleven. No motive has been released, and both incidents are under investigation.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: G7 in Paris
[ava] Story four. In Paris, G7 foreign ministers pressed Secretary Rubio today to make sure any settlement of the war in Ukraine is fair to Kyiv. Some European capitals are uneasy about American talk of a new security framework for Europe. Germany's foreign minister, Johann Wadephul, said a fundamental change to Europe's security setup is neither necessary nor appropriate, adding, "Europe belongs at this table." Rubio says Europe's postwar security comes up in every negotiation, but the fighting has to end first. Canada's foreign minister, Anita Anand, struck a more hopeful note, calling it positive that both sides are willing to talk. Ukraine's foreign minister joins the meeting tomorrow.

[ava] Story five. The Kremlin insists the talks themselves stay three-way, Russia and Ukraine with the U.S. mediating, and says Europe can talk through separate channels.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Qubole, Swarm, MEXC
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. Researchers at Wiz, working with Sentra and Mandiant, say an attacker used credentials from an Amazon Web Services account tied to the data platform Qubole to get into customer cloud accounts between October fourteenth and twenty-sixth. Because customers had set up roles trusting Qubole's account, often roles no longer in use, the attacker could log in, harvest stored secrets and download data from storage buckets. Wiz's advice: delete vendor access you no longer use, and lock what remains to the vendor's known networks.
[ava] Also today, attackers hijacked the release system of Ethersphere, the project behind the Swarm decentralized storage network, and published poisoned versions of its bee J S software library and Swarm Desktop app that steal wallet keys and install a backdoor. Both were pulled within hours, and download counts appear small, but anyone who ran them should treat that computer as compromised.
[ava] And Treasury has ordered U.S. banks to stop processing transfers with the crypto exchange M E X C Global, saying it moved at least sixteen billion dollars tied to Russian, Iranian and North Korean sanctions evasion and cybercrime. The order takes effect thirty days after it's published.
> source: Wiz, "Trusted by Default: Qubole Cross-Account Access Abused in the Wild" (Nov 9); The Hacker News, "Hackers Compromise Ethersphere's bee-js and Swarm Desktop to Deliver Wallet-Stealing Malware" (Nov 9); StepSecurity; U.S. Treasury, "Operation Economic Outcast Severs MEXC Global's Access to the U.S. Financial System" (Nov 6); BleepingComputer (Nov 9)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, the lights are coming back on in Yorkshire. Glowworms, once common across Britain, haven't been seen at the Nosterfield Nature Reserve in North Yorkshire for about a hundred years. Now a volunteer named Peter Cooper is raising them in his garden shed, about nineteen hundred larvae this year, and releasing them on the reserve. The manager credits his "raw passion and enthusiasm." It'll take until about twenty thirty to know whether wild-born glowworms are shining on their own, but the volunteers, nicknamed the glow-getters, are thrilled.
> source: Good News Network, "Glowworms Shine Again in the UK from Larvae Reared Pro Bono in Garden Shed" (Nov 9); The Guardian
