## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Trump 60 Minutes on immigration; Miller starts today
[ava] Story one. In his first sit-down interview since last week's midterm losses, President Trump told C B S's 60 Minutes that his deportation policy "might have been too tough." He said removing people with criminal records helped him, but deporting longtime workers whose only violation was entering illegally hurt Republicans, and he said he'd "love to do something on immigration" with the incoming Democratic majority. On Iran, he said voters didn't like the war or the gas prices, but he'd make the same call again and isn't looking to escalate. The interview was taped Friday, hours before he named Stephen Miller, the architect of the deportation program, as chief of staff; Miller starts today. Supporters hear openness to a deal; skeptics question whether a softer tone fits with Miller running the White House.
// Story 2: DOJ vs Democrats over oversight
[ava] Story two. A clash is building over congressional oversight. On Saturday, President Trump called Democrats' threats of investigations and impeachment "extortion" and said he was ordering the Justice Department to look into it. On Sunday, Attorney General Todd Blanche acknowledged Congress's oversight power but would not rule out prosecuting Democratic lawmakers if their statements crossed a line; he named no crime and confirmed no investigation. Representative Robert Garcia, expected to chair House Oversight next year, said Democrats won't be intimidated. Administration allies say some Democratic rhetoric amounts to threats against officials and donors; critics warn that prosecuting lawmakers over oversight would test the separation of powers.
// Story 3: Lame duck opens: Hegseth resolution, GOP leadership vote
[ava] Story three. The lame-duck Congress opens this week. Roll Call and Punchbowl News report that Representative Thomas Massie is expected to bring up his privileged resolution to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth as soon as today, which could force a House vote within two legislative days. Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries says he's a "hard yes"; President Trump calls the effort "very unfair" and says he has confidence in Hegseth. In the Senate, Republican leadership elections are still set for tomorrow, but Senators Dave McCormick and Ron Johnson are urging a delay to first debate what went wrong. No one has formally challenged John Thune.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Qatar PM at White House on Iran
[ava] Story four, the Iran war. Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, meets President Trump at the White House today. Axios describes a two-step proposal from mediators: first, the U.S. lifts its naval blockade and releases some frozen Iranian funds while Iran invites U.N. nuclear inspectors back; then the Strait of Hormuz reopens and nuclear talks resume. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, at G7 talks in Paris, called Iran's conditions "outrageous"; Iran says it's the most reasonable offer on the table. Some senior U.S. officials reportedly oppose a deal now, arguing it could rescue Iran's government as its economy weakens. Separately, Houthi authorities now say Saturday's airstrikes near Yemen's capital killed forty-four people, a figure not independently confirmed.
// Story 5: Russia-Ukraine strikes before Abu Dhabi talks
[ava] Story five. A day before U.S.-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi, Russia and Ukraine traded heavy strikes. A Russian attack on Odesa killed three people, and Russia hit power substations in several regions. Ukrainian drones set fire to the Kirishi refinery near Saint Petersburg, Russia's second-largest, where officials say one woman was killed by debris. President Trump says the two sides are "very close" to a deal; the Kremlin insists the talks stay three-way, even as France says Europe will be represented.
// Story 6: Krakow gunman dies
[ava] Story six. Polish police say the gunman who killed six people in Krakow's main square on Saturday died in hospital this morning, after being critically wounded by police. The terrorism investigation continues.
// Story 7: Turkey steel plant explosion
[ava] Story seven. An explosion and fire hit a steel plant in the Aliaga district of Izmir, in western Turkey, this morning. The A P, citing officials, reports at least three dead and about ten workers missing; some Turkish outlets report a higher toll. Early accounts of the cause differ.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 9: Tesla ends Cybertruck
[andrew] Story nine, tech. Tesla is winding down the Cybertruck. Elon Musk said Friday that Texas production ends around the middle of next year to make room for the Cybercab, and posted on X this morning that orders exceed production, so Tesla will build as many as it can before the line shuts in about eight months but will have to return many deposits. ## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 11: Web3Auth / Keplr; LNbits
[ava] Story eleven, the cyber desk. Some Keplr crypto wallet users who signed up with Google or Apple have had money stolen through a flaw at Web3Auth, the company that runs that sign-in. In an unusual move, Web3Auth says it then used the same route to empty other at-risk wallets first, moving about six million dollars from more than forty-four thousand wallets into recovery accounts, so an empty balance may mean a rescue, not a theft. Wallets made with a recovery phrase or hardware wallet aren't affected. Neither company has said how much was actually stolen. Separately, the Bitcoin Lightning platform L N bits has told server operators to shut down immediately after attacks drained funds; it says fixes are coming within a day or two.
> source: The Hacker News, "Web3Auth Flaw Exploited to Steal Keplr Crypto, Provider Empties Other Wallets for Safety" (Nov 9); The Block (Nov 8); Bitquery analysis; The Hacker News on the LNbits shutdown alert (Nov 9)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, some very good dogs. Scientists in Leipzig, Germany, trained volunteer dogs and their owners to sniff out invasive plants like Himalayan balsam, ragweed, and knotweed, which crowd out native species. Of the twelve teams that finished the field test, eleven passed, finding their targets in the wild about seventy-five percent of the time. The study, in the journal P L O S One, suggests everyday dog walks could become an early-warning system for conservation, turning something people already love into a gift to biodiversity.
> source: Good News Network, "Dogs Can Sniff-Out Invasive Plant Species With High Accuracy to Curb Their Destruction" (Nov 8), citing PLOS One and UFZ
