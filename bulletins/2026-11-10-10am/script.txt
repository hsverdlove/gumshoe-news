## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Hegseth presser, House vote tonight
[ava] Story one. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth came out swinging at his first Pentagon news conference since May. He called Representative Thomas Massie's impeachment effort "a political spectacle, a personal manifesto driven by ego and resentment," and said, quote, "This is not patriotism. This is sabotage." He argued no defense secretary has ever been impeached for carrying out lawful military operations, and compared the effort to President Trump's two impeachments. The briefing ran under half an hour, and the president posted that Hegseth earned an "A plus, plus, plus."
[ava] Now the timing: CBS News reports the House is set to vote at five-thirty this evening on whether to move forward. Massie's resolution centers on the Iran war, which he says Congress never authorized, and also cites operations in Yemen and Venezuela. Supporters call it overdue oversight; Hegseth and the White House call it a policy disagreement dressed up as high crimes. What counts as an impeachable offense is left to Congress, so tonight's vote is the real test.
// Story 2: GOP leadership votes
[ava] Story two. Republicans in both chambers are choosing their leaders this morning, a week after losing both majorities. In the Senate, John Thune is unopposed, but a group led by Wisconsin's Ron Johnson wants the vote delayed so the party can talk through what went wrong first. Missouri's Josh Hawley called it "the worst set of losses since Watergate" and said he hadn't heard from the leader. On the other side, South Dakota's Mike Rounds says most senators are "probably ready to vote." In the House, Speaker Mike Johnson is stepping aside and says the vote can't be delayed, leaving Steve Scalise and Jim Jordan in a secret-ballot race for minority leader. Results are expected later today.

## Markets | sting=markets
// Story 4: CPI and the open
[andrew] Story four, and the big one for your wallet. October consumer prices rose four tenths of a percent for the month, more than the three tenths economists expected. Core prices, without food and energy, rose three tenths, also above forecasts. There's no year-over-year number this time, because last fall's shutdown wiped out the October twenty twenty-five data.
[andrew] Inside the report: food prices jumped half a percent, with beef up nearly a full percent. Shelter rose three tenths and made up more than thirty percent of the increase. Energy rose seven tenths, far less than September's spike, and used cars fell more than one percent. To be fair to both readings, headline inflation did slow from September's six tenths. But core has now run at three tenths for three straight months, which one strategist told CBS shows price pressures "have yet to meaningfully ease."
[andrew] That matters because Fed Governor Christopher Waller said this morning a December rate hike is likely, and the Fed should consider a half-point hike if inflation worsens. Traders now see a December hike as a near certainty. The ten-year Treasury yield jumped about eight basis points, to around four point nine five percent.
[andrew] Stocks opened lower. Around nine-fifty, the S&P five hundred was down about seven tenths of a percent, the Dow down roughly two hundred fifty points, and the Nasdaq off about nine tenths, led by chip stocks. Micron and Marvell fell about three percent, and Nvidia was down more than three percent as its board launches an independent investigation, led by outside directors with outside lawyers, into Jensen Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese intelligence operative. The board says it has no reason to believe company or national security secrets were compromised.
[andrew] One bit of relief: oil is down around three percent again, and AAA says the national average for gas slipped below four dollars a gallon today for the first time in nearly four months.
