## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Hegseth impeachment, the vote math
[ava] Story one. Since Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's news conference, the House vote math has sharpened ahead of tonight's procedural vote, expected around five-thirty.
[ava] Speaker Mike Johnson told reporters the motion to set aside the impeachment resolution is, quote, "not going to fail. We have the votes." Nebraska Republican Don Bacon, undecided yesterday, now says he'll oppose impeachment, though he says party leaders "can't just be a rubber stamp for the president."
[ava] Representative Thomas Massie says some colleagues back some of his eight articles but not all, so if the motion to kill or refer his resolution fails, he'll ask for a separate vote on each one.
[ava] Vice President J D Vance called the impeachment question "absurd" and "a ridiculous show that stands in for actual policymaking." But Senator Ted Cruz predicted on his podcast that the House will likely impeach, and that Hegseth would then step down or be fired. Only tonight's roll call will settle it, and removal would still take sixty-seven Senate votes.
// Story 2: GOP leadership, where things stand
[ava] Story two. As of airtime, neither chamber has announced results in the Republican leadership races. House Republicans return at one-thirty to vote by secret ballot, Steve Scalise versus Jim Jordan for minority leader. Scalise says "I've got a proven record" and can win back swing districts; Jordan says he's best positioned to counter Democratic investigations.
[ava] In the Senate, the morning meeting turned into what some called an "open-mic session" on the midterm losses. Senators return at two to decide whether to vote today or postpone. No one has challenged John Thune, and Kevin Cramer said the criticism "hasn't been about John Thune." Rand Paul's diagnosis: "The cost of living defeated us."

## Markets | sting=markets
// Story 5: Midday markets
[andrew] Story five, markets at midday. The selloff has deepened. Around noon, the Dow was down about three hundred forty points, or two-thirds of a percent, the S&P five hundred down about eight-tenths, and the Nasdaq off about one and a quarter percent. Decliners outnumbered gainers more than three to one.
[andrew] The real story is bonds. After the hot inflation report, the two-year Treasury yield jumped about twenty-one basis points, its biggest one-day jump since April of last year, and the ten-year is near five percent. Traders put December rate-hike odds at about ninety-nine percent, and Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said the Fed may need at least two more quarter-point hikes. One economist told Reuters it's less a panic than markets pricing back tightening they'd priced out. Gold is down nearly three percent.
[andrew] Tech is the weakest sector. Nvidia is down about five to six percent after The Information reported, citing two people who know her, that Jensen Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, whom U.S. officials believe worked for Chinese intelligence, helped route at least seven and a half billion dollars of Nvidia chips to Chinese companies through firms in China and Singapore. Reuters couldn't independently verify it, and Nvidia hasn't commented; it has previously said its own review found no sign of diversion.
[andrew] A M D bucked the trend, up about three percent, while Skydance fell almost nine percent after warning weak Warner Brothers films could cut fourth-quarter profit by up to two hundred million dollars.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Ring Drone Cam and Rover Cam
[andrew] Story six, tech. Amazon's Ring has finally launched the flying indoor camera it first teased in twenty twenty. The two-hundred-fifty-dollar Ring Drone Cam, about the size of a textbook, flies a preset route through the house, upstairs included, when a door, window or alarm sensor trips, and flies about eight minutes per charge. It's indoors only and ships January twenty-seventh, pending F C C approval.
[andrew] For businesses, the one-thousand-dollar Ring Rover Cam, a rebuilt Amazon Astro robot, patrols offices and warehouses starting December sixteenth.
[andrew] The other side: Ring has faced years of privacy criticism, largely over its partnerships with police, and a camera that roams your whole house raises the stakes. Ring says both devices flash a light whenever they record.
> source: CNBC, "Amazon's Ring unveils flying drone and robot to patrol homes and businesses" (Nov 10); TechCrunch (Nov 10); Amazon, "Ring announces Ring Robotics" (Nov 10)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Gemini 4 Carbon public rollout
[andrew] Story seven, A I. After first giving it to security defenders, Google is rolling out Gemini four Carbon, its strongest model yet, to the public, plus a preview "swarm" feature that turns a single prompt into a coordinated team of A I agents.
[andrew] It's available to paying developers and businesses, and to A I Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app and Search; no word on cheaper tiers.
[andrew] The eye-catcher is the price: an introductory two dollars per million tokens in, and ten dollars out. The Verge figures that's about a fifth the cost of OpenAI's top model and a quarter of Anthropic's Fable five point two. Those prices double at the end of November, and cheaper per token isn't always cheaper per task. Google's own benchmarks put Carbon just behind Fable, and companies pick benchmarks that flatter them.
> source: The Verge, "Google releases Gemini 4 Carbon with agent 'swarm' feature" (Nov 10); Google, "Gemini 4 Carbon is now available" (Nov 10)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally. In Shepton Mallet, in the English county of Somerset, Kim Reynolds noticed her Doberman, Saraya, acting strangely around her six-month-old son, Alexander. The dog wouldn't leave his side, and cried every time he was carried away.
[ava] It nagged at her, so she took him to the emergency room, half worried she was wasting doctors' time. She wasn't. His heart was racing at about two hundred eighty beats a minute. Doctors brought it down with medication and diagnosed Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome, an extra electrical pathway in the heart. Alexander now takes daily medicine and is doing well.
[ava] Saraya isn't a trained medical dog, but Kim trusts her completely, and she and her sister are now walking a hundred miles to raise money for Tiny Tickers, a charity that helps catch heart conditions in babies early.
> source: The Washington Post, "Her dog sensed something was wrong with her baby. It may have saved his life." (Nov 10); Somerset County Gazette, "Somerset dog alerts mum to baby's dangerously fast heart"
