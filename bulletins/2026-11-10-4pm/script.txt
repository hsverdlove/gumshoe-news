## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: GOP leadership results; Hegseth vote ahead
[ava] Story one. The Republican leadership races are settled. In a secret ballot, House Republicans chose Judiciary Committee Chairman Jim Jordan of Ohio as their leader for the next Congress, when they'll be in the minority. He beat Majority Leader Steve Scalise, a decade-long member of leadership, one hundred six to seventy-three. Tom Emmer kept his job as whip, beating August Pfluger one hundred three to seventy-six.
[ava] Supporters see Jordan, a founder of the Freedom Caucus, as the fighter the party needs against a Democratic majority. Skeptics, including some Republicans, worry he's better at confrontation than at winning back swing districts.
[ava] In the Senate, John Thune was re-elected Republican leader without a challenger, after a group led by Ron Johnson tried to delay the vote at least a week. Axios reports that push failed thirty-one to fifteen. The rest of the Senate GOP team stays in place, except that Bernie Moreno of Ohio will take over the party's Senate campaign arm.
[ava] For scale, Democrats have now won at least two hundred fifty-four House seats, with about a dozen races still uncalled.
[ava] Still ahead tonight: the House procedural vote on the Hegseth impeachment resolution, expected around five-thirty. If a motion to table or refer it passes, there's no impeachment vote. If it fails, a vote on the resolution itself could come Wednesday.
// Story 2: Epstein report
[ava] Story two. House Oversight Republicans released their final report on the Jeffrey Epstein investigation, after fifteen months and nineteen interviews and depositions. It concludes Epstein's crimes after two thousand five could have been prevented if not for failures by federal prosecutors and investigators, starting with the two thousand seven non-prosecution deal in Florida.
[ava] It urges the Justice Department to re-interview key witnesses, give survivors unredacted access to their own records, and investigate allegations against some Epstein associates, and asks Congress to limit nondisclosure agreements that silence abuse survivors. It also says nothing the committee found implicates President Trump in Epstein's crimes.
[ava] The committee's top Democrat, Robert Garcia, called the report incomplete, misleading and partisan, noting there were no public hearings, and says Democrats will keep investigating when they take control in January. The Justice Department says the Attorney General has repeatedly said the case isn't closed.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Iran-backed operatives indicted; Rubio at G7
[ava] Story three. The Justice Department unsealed terrorism charges against three men it says worked for Iran's Revolutionary Guard and the Iraqi militia Kata'ib Hizballah. Prosecutors allege they were involved in nearly twenty attacks and attempted attacks in Europe and the U.S., including arsons and explosives this spring, many aimed at Jewish communities in and around London.
[ava] They're also accused in the September plot at R A F Fairford in England, where U.S. aircraft were based. Prosecutors say vans disguised as fuel-delivery trucks and loaded with barrels of fuel were turned back at a security roadblock, and British police arrested the occupants. One defendant was already in a New York jail and allegedly passed coded messages through family phone calls. Two are believed to have been in Iran at the time. These are allegations, and the men are presumed innocent.
[ava] Meanwhile in Paris, Secretary of State Marco Rubio told G7 foreign ministers that America will keep imposing what he called "devastating" economic costs on Iran, and said the pressure is working so well that the president doesn't need major combat operations. Diplomats tell Reuters Vice President Vance would prefer a deal, while Rubio and others favor continued pressure.
// Story 4: Abu Dhabi day one wrap
[ava] Story four. The first day of U.S.-brokered talks between Russia and Ukraine in Abu Dhabi ended after about four hours. Rubio called them "very productive," and U.S. envoys said there are "some good ideas" for bridging the gap. But the core dispute is unchanged: Moscow wants Ukraine to give up the rest of the Donetsk region, and Kyiv wants to freeze the front line. Talks resume Wednesday morning.

## Markets | sting=markets
// Story 5: Closing markets
[andrew] Story five, the close. Stocks finished lower but well off the morning lows. Reuters has the Dow down about three hundred points, or six-tenths of a percent, at fifty-two thousand eighty-seven. The S&P five hundred lost seven-tenths of a percent, and the Nasdaq about nine-tenths.
[andrew] The driver was this morning's inflation report. October consumer prices rose four-tenths of a percent, a bit hotter than expected, and core inflation was hotter too. The two-year Treasury yield jumped about nineteen basis points, its biggest one-day jump since April of last year, and the ten-year ended near five percent. The dollar rose, gold fell more than two percent, and oil hit two-month lows, with U.S. crude near eighty-five dollars, after officials played down more military action against Iran.
[andrew] Nvidia fell almost six percent, its second big drop in two days. New this afternoon: House Foreign Affairs Chairman Brian Mast told ABC News he's prepared to subpoena Jensen Huang if he doesn't answer a request for a closed-door briefing by Wednesday about his reported relationship with an alleged Chinese spy. Nvidia hasn't commented on the latest reports.
[andrew] Bright spots: Broadcom gained almost four percent, and Take-Two jumped about eight percent on huge preorders for Grand Theft Auto six, even though its quarterly results missed. After the bell, CoreWeave reports, a key test of how investors feel about the cost of the A I buildout.

// Story 6: Starlink Mini 2
[andrew] Story six, tech, and one for the road. SpaceX has announced the Starlink Mini two, its laptop-sized satellite internet dish, and the big change is a built-in battery. SpaceX says it runs up to four and a half hours on a charge, and you can hot-swap in spare batteries, sold separately, or power it from a sixty-watt U S B C source.

Lower prices, two hundred forty-nine for the kit and fifty for a battery, have turned up in the website's code, but SpaceX hasn't confirmed them, so treat those as unconfirmed.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Patch Tuesday
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. It's Patch Tuesday. Microsoft fixed well over a thousand flaws this month, its second giant release in a row, but only one is known to be under attack: an Outlook bug where opening a booby-trapped message file makes Outlook quietly connect to an attacker's server and leak your Windows login hash, which can then be used to impersonate you. Just previewing the message isn't enough to trigger it.
[ava] For businesses, the Zero Day Initiative flags critical fixes in Windows Search through Exchange, LDAP and Schannel. For your own P C, the advice is simple: run Windows Update and Office updates tonight, and don't open unexpected attachments. Adobe also patched a hundred ten flaws, none known to be exploited.
> source: Zero Day Initiative, "The November 2026 Security Update Review" (Nov 10); BleepingComputer, "Microsoft November 2026 Patch Tuesday fixes 1,235 flaws, Outlook zero-day" (Nov 10); SecurityWeek (Nov 10)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally. In September, a college student named Violet Anniskett was surveying a remote beach in northwestern Alaska with a U.S. Geological Survey biologist, Tony Fischbach, when she spotted something alive between two dead adult walruses: an orphaned calf, about five months old.
[ava] With gulls circling and polar bears nearby, they got permission from wildlife officials and the local Inupiaq community, and sheltered him in an abandoned building for nearly four days while storms grounded flights. With no formula, they fed him honey and water. He slept curled against Fischbach in his sleeping bag, and if Violet moved away, he'd wake up and call for her.
[ava] The calf, named Annautaq after Violet's father, recovered at the Alaska SeaLife Center. He can't go back to the wild, so he now lives at SeaWorld Orlando, up to about two hundred fifty pounds, and just met another rescued walrus, Uki. The two bopped noses through a barrier, the start of his new herd.
> source: Smithsonian Magazine, "This Adorable, Orphaned Baby Walrus Was Rescued by Two Researchers in Alaska" (Nov 10); The New York Times and Alaska Public Media, as cited by Smithsonian
