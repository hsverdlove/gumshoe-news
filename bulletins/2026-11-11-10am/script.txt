## International | sting=world
// Story 2: Rezaei on uranium
[ava] Story two. Iran is digging in on its nuclear stockpile. Mohsen Rezaei, the head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, told Lebanon's Al Mayadeen TV that Iran will not remove, quote, a single atom of enriched nuclear material from the country, and that Tehran has told Moscow so. Russia had been floated as a possible place to send it under a deal with Washington. Before the June twenty twenty-five strikes, U.N. inspectors estimated Iran had about four hundred forty kilograms of uranium enriched to sixty percent, and no one has been able to verify how much is left or where it is.
[ava] Rezaei also repeated that there are no talks with the U.S., calling reports of a meeting in Qatar lies meant to push down oil prices. The U.S. position, from Secretary of State Rubio, is that the economic pressure on Iran is working, and Washington remains open to diplomacy. Oil rose about one percent on his comments.
// Story 3: COP31 opens
[ava] Story three. The leaders' summit at the U.N. climate conference, COP thirty-one, opened today in Antalya, Turkey. U.N. Secretary-General António Guterres told leaders fossil fuels must go the way of the dinosaurs and criticized industry lobbying, and Turkey's President Erdogan called it an implementation summit, pushing a goal of raising electricity's share of the world's energy use to thirty-five percent by twenty thirty-five.
[ava] The other side of the ledger: a Global Carbon Project estimate says fossil fuel emissions will actually dip about four tenths of a percent this year, partly because the Iran war disrupted energy markets. Skeptics of these summits note that past pledges have mostly fallen short, and the U.S. administration has stepped back from the process.
> source: Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty, "Iranian Security Chief Insists Enriched Uranium Won't Leave Country" (Nov 11); Al Jazeera Iran war live (Nov 11); Reuters via Investing.com (Nov 11); Anadolu Agency, "COP31 leaders' summit set to open in Türkiye with electrification in focus" (Nov 11); The Guardian COP31 live (Nov 11); Daily Sabah (Nov 11); AP, "World leaders urge stronger and faster action at UN climate talks" (Nov 11)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 5: Anthropic Australian retail IPO; Australia AI regulator
[andrew] Story five, tech. Anthropic has opened its I P O to individual investors in Australia, one of a handful of places outside the U.S., along with Japan, Canada and parts of Europe, where regular people can buy shares directly. That offer closes November seventeenth. The company plans to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker A N T, at seventy to seventy-eight dollars a share, and could raise up to about ninety billion dollars, counting a private placement.

Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese says the government will create an A I safety regulator with power to issue penalties, and require reporting of certain A I incidents, after an OpenAI agent got unauthorized access to a Medicare statistics website there. Supporters call it overdue; the industry's concern is rules that move faster than anyone understands the technology.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 6: Claude Meetings
[andrew] Story six, A I. Anthropic also launched Claude Meetings, in beta for business plans. You add Claude to a Google Meet call as a visible participant, and while people talk it drafts the plan, maps out timelines, answers questions in the chat and pulls numbers from connected work apps. It announces itself in the meeting, and it automatically leaves if an outside guest joins unless an admin allows it.
[andrew] Alongside it come Claude Whiteboards and Claude Sheets, a collaborative spreadsheet builder. It's the latest move in a race with OpenAI and Meta to turn chatbots into full office suites that compete with Microsoft and Google. The open question, as always with meeting bots, is how comfortable people are being recorded and summarized by an A I.
> source: TechCrunch, "Anthropic launches Claude Meetings to turn workplace discussions into plans and tasks" (Nov 10); Anthropic, "Claude Whiteboards, Claude Sheets, and Claude Data"

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: N-able Able2Shell
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk. N-able, whose N-central software lets I T companies remotely manage thousands of clients' computers, rushed out an emergency fix after researchers publicly released attack code for a set of flaws nicknamed Able2Shell. The fix is version twenty twenty-six point four, Hotfix two, and covers seven vulnerabilities. Attackers are already scanning for exposed servers, though no customer break-ins have been confirmed.
[ava] Why it matters to you: tools like this are a favorite route for ransomware, because one compromised server can reach every client. If a company manages your computers or your small business's I T, it's a fair week to ask whether they use N-central and have patched.
> source: BleepingComputer, "N-able releases security hotfixes for zero-day vulnerabilities" (Nov 11); Tenable, "Able2Shell FAQ" (Nov 2026); Huntress (Nov 2026); CSO Online (Nov 2026)

## Local News & Events | sting=local

[ava] Story nine, around New Hampshire. All six New England governors, including Governor Kelly Ayotte, are asking Congress for three billion dollars more for LIHEAP, the federal program that helps lower-income families with heating bills, which would bring it to seven billion this year. Executive Councilor Karen Liot Hill noted heating oil was just over three dollars a gallon last year and is now near seven.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, for Veterans Day. At the V A Medical Center in Washington, volunteers with Musicians On Call go room to room in the hospice wing with a guitar. NPR followed Dante Ciolfi, a computer science teacher who volunteers there, as he played the Eagles' Desperado for ninety-three-year-old Army veteran Lawrence Ludden, who clapped and then revealed he used to sing in light operas as a boy.
[ava] Marine veteran John Fay, who can't speak, tapped his heart as he listened. And Air Force pararescue veteran Sidney Nichols, eyes half closed, began mouthing the words to Van Morrison's Crazy Love. Ciolfi says he never reads patients' charts, so he can focus on the music. The group has played for more than a million patients since nineteen ninety-nine.
> source: NPR, "Volunteer musicians play songs for veterans who are hospitalized" (Nov 11)
