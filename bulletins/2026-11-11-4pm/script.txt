## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: White House counsel warns Democrats; shutdown deadline
[ava] Story one. The White House fired back today at House Democrats' plans to investigate the administration. In a letter to California Congressman Robert Garcia, who's set to chair the House Oversight Committee in January, White House Counsel Will Scharf said the administration is monitoring what he called abusive and capricious threats, and suggested some of the planned probes could amount to, quote, blatant criminality.
[ava] Garcia's response: it appears Donald Trump has something to hide. The Washington Post reports the counsel's office has already hired fifteen more lawyers to prepare for the fight, and a former Democratic Oversight lawyer predicts subpoenas in January.
[ava] The two sides here: Congressional oversight is a standard power, and courts have long upheld it, so legal experts are skeptical that investigating could itself be a crime. The White House and its allies argue that Democrats have openly promised retribution rather than legitimate fact-finding, and that the administration is entitled to defend executive privilege.
[ava] Meanwhile, the government runs out of money on December eleventh, one month from today, and top appropriators left a meeting this week with no deal. Democrats want a short-term bill at current spending until they take control in January. Republicans want to use their last weeks in charge to lock in full-year funding.

## International | sting=world
// Story 2: Darchiev dies; envoys to Moscow and Dublin
[ava] Story two. Russia's ambassador to the United States, Alexander Darchiev, has died suddenly at sixty-six. Russia's Foreign Ministry announced it today, and spokeswoman Maria Zakharova later said he died in Moscow of a chronic illness she didn't name. Darchiev, a longtime America specialist who previously served as ambassador to Canada, took the Washington post early last year as relations thawed after President Trump returned to office.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 5: AMD driver SSD writes
[andrew] Story five, tech, and a practical one. AMD has confirmed that its Radeon graphics drivers on Windows can keep writing to your solid-state drive nonstop, even when the computer is sitting idle. The culprit is a background helper called the A T I external events utility, which keeps copying itself. Some users say it chewed through more than one percent of their drive's lifespan a month.
[andrew] AMD says a fix is coming in its next driver, Adrenalin twenty-six point eleven point one, later this month. A hobbyist has released an unofficial tool that blocks the behavior now, but it needs administrator rights, so most people are better off waiting for AMD's update. If you have a Radeon card, you can open Resource Monitor and check the Disk tab to see if it's happening to you.
> source: VideoCardz, "AMD confirms Radeon driver bug causing constant SSD writes, fix planned for Adrenalin 26.11.1"; TechPowerUp (Nov 2026)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 6: Gemini Robotics 2.5, AlphaGenome 2
[andrew] Story six, A I. Google DeepMind rolled out Gemini Robotics two point five, a new A I brain for robots. In real-world tests at Mercedes-Benz, Apptronik's Apollo humanoid robots running it packed car parts with a ninety-six percent success rate. DeepMind says it learns new skills faster and works twenty to thirty percent quicker than the last version. It's going out through partners including Boston Dynamics. Success rates still range widely by task, from thirty percent to near perfect, so robots aren't ready to take over the factory floor.
[andrew] DeepMind also announced AlphaGenome two, which predicts how changes in D N A affect specific types of cells. The goal is to help researchers find drug targets faster. DeepMind stresses it guides experiments rather than replacing them, and it's not approved for medical diagnosis.
> source: SiliconANGLE, "Google DeepMind releases new advanced robotics AI model and next-generation gene prediction model" (Nov 11)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: Xen Orchestra
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk. Hackers are exploiting a critical flaw in Xen Orchestra, a popular tool for managing virtual servers and backups. The maker, Vates, says the bug lets an attacker skip the login entirely and take administrator control, and then fully take over the servers it manages. The same attacker is also using a second, unpatched zero-day.
[ava] There's a fix for the main flaw in the newest releases, and a fix for the second one is still in the works. Vates warns that break-ins may not show up in the logs, and if you're compromised, cleaning up isn't enough; you need a full reinstall. This one's for businesses and home-lab tinkerers who run their own servers. Your everyday devices aren't affected.
> source: SecurityWeek, "Critical Xen Orchestra Flaws Exploited in Attacks" (Nov 11)
