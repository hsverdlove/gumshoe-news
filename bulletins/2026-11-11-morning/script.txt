## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Hegseth impeachment shelved
[ava] Story one. The impeachment push against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth is effectively over. Last evening the House voted two hundred eleven to two hundred four to send Representative Thomas Massie's resolution to committee, which kills it for this Congress. Massie, the Kentucky Republican who lost his primary this year, was the only Republican to vote to keep it alive.
[ava] His eight articles accused Hegseth of waging the Iran war without authorization from Congress, ignoring war-powers limits, the boat strikes in the Caribbean and Pacific, the Venezuela operation, and retaliating against Senator Mark Kelly. Massie said someone has to ask whether the country will follow the Constitution.
[ava] Hegseth called it a farce and a stunt, and said every operation was lawful. Even some Republican critics, including Nebraska's Don Bacon, said the charges didn't rise to high crimes and misdemeanors, and California's Darrell Issa noted there were never sixty-seven Senate votes to convict. President Trump called the outcome an exoneration. On the other side, Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, likely the next speaker, has promised Democrats will run Hegseth out of town when they take over in January.
// Story 2: Veterans Day at Arlington; arch fight
[ava] Story two. President Trump and the First Lady lay a wreath at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at Arlington National Cemetery at eleven this morning, and the President speaks at eleven-thirty.
[ava] Hanging over the visit is his proposed two-hundred-fifty-foot arch just outside the cemetery gates. A federal court has paused construction, and nine members of the Kennedy family have sued to block it, arguing it would block the view of President Kennedy's grave and the graves of thousands of veterans. Administration officials tell The Washington Post the president still wants it built as soon as the court lifts the pause. Opponents, including D.C. leaders and the incoming Democratic majority in Congress, say they'll keep fighting it.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Abu Dhabi ends
[ava] Story three. The two days of U.S.-brokered talks between Russia and Ukraine in Abu Dhabi wrapped up this morning, after a session of under two hours. The concrete result is a prisoner exchange. White House envoy Jared Kushner called it the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners and said the numbers will be finalized tomorrow. President Zelensky said earlier the sides were looking at one thousand, or possibly fifteen hundred, prisoners each.
[ava] Beyond that, no breakthrough. Russia's lead negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, said there was mutual understanding on some issues and more talks will follow, but he was cool on bringing Europe into the process. Territory is still the core dispute: Moscow wants Ukraine out of the rest of the Donbas, and Kyiv says it never discussed giving land away. Zelensky also said NATO's top commander, General Alexus Grynkewich, backed Ukraine's battlefield maps over Russian claims of big gains.
// Story 4: Hormuz
[ava] Story four. In the Gulf, Iran says the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until the U.S. meets its conditions. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei called reports of talks with Washington a lie, and said Tehran is still waiting for a clear answer on its seven demands, which include lifting sanctions and the naval blockade.
[ava] Iran's Revolutionary Guard says three oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines on what it called an unauthorized route. The British maritime agency U K M T O reported incidents with two tankers, one hit by an unknown projectile, and says crew conditions and damage aren't yet known. Monitoring firms count at least thirty-seven attacks on ships in and near the strait this month, the most of the war. The U.S. side says it has escorted about one and a half billion barrels through since summer, and Secretary of State Rubio says Washington is open to diplomacy while economic pressure does the work.
> source: AP, "Russia and Ukraine agree to a large exchange of prisoners of war, officials at Abu Dhabi talks say" (Nov 11); The Guardian Europe live, via Reuters (Nov 11); Euronews (Nov 11); TASS (Nov 11); Al Jazeera with Reuters, "Iran denies talks with US, says Hormuz will stay shut until demands are met" (Nov 11); Arab News, "Iran says oil tanker hit mine and caught fire in Strait of Hormuz" (Nov 11)

The caution: CoreWeave still carries roughly fifty billion dollars of debt.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Businesses in ChatGPT
[andrew] Story six, tech. OpenAI has launched Businesses in ChatGPT, in beta. Much like claiming your listing on Google Maps, a company can now claim and verify its profile inside ChatGPT, update its details and product feeds, and see how often people engage with it. Eligible U.S. online stores and service businesses can also publish a business agent, an A I rep that answers customer questions using that company's own information and policies.
[andrew] For shoppers, that means answers in ChatGPT may increasingly come straight from the seller. Worth keeping in mind that the seller now has a hand in what the chatbot tells you. Separately, Google is bringing Gmail's Help Me Write tool to work accounts.
> source: OpenAI, "Introducing Businesses in ChatGPT" (Nov 10); Reuters, "OpenAI lets businesses claim ChatGPT profiles, adds AI agents for US e-commerce firms" (Nov 10); The Verge, "Google is bringing Gmail's Help me write AI tool to work accounts" (Nov 11)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Gemini 4 Carbon; Maya site
[andrew] Story seven, A I. Google has released Gemini 4 Carbon, which it calls its strongest model, built for long, multi-step work like complex coding and scientific research. It's especially pitched at cybersecurity: Google says it led benchmarks for finding, exploiting and patching software flaws, and it first went to a small group of security defenders two weeks ago. Google also claims Carbon blocked ninety-nine percent of simulated attack attempts by its own red-team agent. Those are Google's numbers, not independent ones yet.
[andrew] It's available now to paying developers and Google A I Ultra subscribers, at an introductory price that doubles after November thirtieth.
[andrew] And a fun one: a Google researcher, Pietro Miotti, says a swarm of forty-eight A I agents combing NASA lidar data spotted signs of a previously unrecorded ancient Maya site in Mexico. He's not an archaeologist, and it needs fieldwork to confirm, so treat it as a promising lead.
> source: Google, "Gemini 4 Carbon is now available" (Nov 10); The Verge, "An AI agent swarm may have unearthed an unmapped Maya site" (Nov 11)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: CISA KEV adds
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. The federal Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency added six flaws to its list of bugs being actively exploited. The one that matters for your own computer is a Google Chrome memory bug, so make sure Chrome, and Edge or Brave too, are updated and restarted. The list also includes the Outlook flaw we covered yesterday, so if you haven't run Office updates, do that today.
[ava] The rest are business gear: two Barracuda email security flaws, including a login bypass, an Openwave messaging product, and a Cisco BroadWorks flaw, used by phone carriers, that lets an attacker read sensitive configuration files without logging in. Cisco's fix is release R I twenty twenty-six point oh seven.
> source: CISA, "CISA Adds Six Known Exploited Vulnerabilities to Catalog" (Nov 10); BleepingComputer, "CISA flags Cisco BroadWorks flaw as actively exploited in attacks" (Nov 11); Cisco Security Advisory cisco-sa-bworks-xxe (Nov 2026)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally. Millions of people followed Jackie and Shadow, the bald eagle pair livestreamed from a pine tree above Big Bear Lake in California. In July, Jackie was found weak and unable to fly after a scuffle with two younger eagles, and she was cared for at the Ojai Raptor Center until she died in August.
[ava] What came next surprised the staff. Cards, letters and kids' drawings poured in from every U.S. state and from places like Australia, France and Germany, so many that the local post office set aside a special cart just for the center. Volunteers covered the care team's break room, walls, door and table, with them. Director Chris Eksteen calls it a room filled with kindness, and says some of the notes made the staff laugh, and some made them cry.
[ava] His request, if you were moved by Jackie: support your local wildlife rehab center.
> source: Los Angeles Times, "'Thank you': Ojai Raptor Center inundated with expressions of gratitude over treatment of sick bald eagle" (Nov 10)
