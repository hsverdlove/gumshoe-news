## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 6: AlphaGenome 2; AWS Emulator
[andrew] Story six, A I. Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome 2, a model that predicts how small changes in your DNA affect genes in specific types of cells. It was trained on more than a hundred million cell profiles from sixty-eight tissues, and DeepMind says it finds nearly twice as many disease-relevant variants as the first version. It's for research only, not for clinical diagnosis.
[andrew] And a sign of the times: Amazon released AWS Emulator, a free tool that lets developers test against copies of more than two hundred Amazon cloud services on their own computers. Amazon says A I agents wrote and maintain all of the code, with no manual review by its engineers. Supporters call it a milestone; skeptics will want to see how it holds up, and Amazon admits it can't yet fully mimic outages or service limits.
> source: Google DeepMind, "AlphaGenome 2: Deciphering how cells read DNA to better understand disease" (Nov 11); The Verge (Nov 2026); The Neuron, "Everything That Happened in AI Today" (Nov 12)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: ScreenConnect RMM+ extension
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk. Attackers used a flawed third-party add-on for ScreenConnect, the remote-support tool many I T shops use to manage customers' computers, to plant malware on at least two hundred eighty-eight machines at forty-eight organizations. Security firm Huntress says all of them were hit within ninety minutes. The add-on, called R M M Plus, would accept a blank access key, letting attackers run commands without ever logging in, so multifactor sign-in didn't help.
[ava] ConnectWise, which makes ScreenConnect, says it found no flaw in ScreenConnect itself, and has disabled five of these extensions in about four hundred installations. The nasty twist: the malware tries to infect the technician who connects in to investigate. The lesson for the rest of us: if a support company has remote access to your computer, ask how they lock it down.
> source: The Register, "Hackers exploiting ScreenConnect plugin flaw to spread malware across hundreds of PCs" (Nov 12); Huntress (Nov 11); ConnectWise security advisory (Nov 2026)
