## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Logan Circle resolution
[ava] Story one: the standoff in Washington is over. Police say the man behind this morning's shooting in the Logan Circle neighborhood is dead from a self-inflicted gunshot wound. Interim police chief Jeffery Carroll says the gunman killed a man inside the Vida Fitness gym, then shot four more people nearby, one of whom is in critical condition. The other three are expected to recover.
[ava] Officials told NBC News he appeared to target one specific person in the gym before firing outside. He then barricaded himself in what police believe was his own seventh-floor apartment, fired through the door at officers at least twice, and shot himself while on the phone with negotiators. Police have not released his name. The chief said he'd been in a dispute with his building's homeowners association, but the motive is still under investigation. The shelter-in-place order was lifted around eleven thirty-five. Mayor Muriel Bowser called it a senseless act of gun violence.

// Story 2: Brooklyn collapse update
[ava] Story two. In Brooklyn, Mayor Zohran Mamdani says two children, reported to be a nine-year-old girl and a ten-year-old boy, are among the eight people killed in the Flatbush building collapse. Two of the adults who died were construction workers. Eighteen people were hurt, including four firefighters; one person remains in critical condition.
[ava] The fire commissioner stressed this is still a rescue operation, not a recovery, and crews are now using grapplers to lift big slabs so they can search underneath. Rain and thunderstorms moving into New York this afternoon could make that harder. The city has halted work at the excavation site next door, and the cause is under investigation.
> source: AP, "Brooklyn building collapse killed 2 children and 6 adults, mayor says" (Nov 12); NBC New York live blog (Nov 12); New York Post live updates (Nov 12, 12:07 PM ET); AccuWeather (Nov 12)

// Story 3: RFK Jr. Samoa probe
[ava] Story three. Senate Democrats have opened an investigation into whether Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Junior misled Congress about a two thousand nineteen trip to Samoa, months before a measles outbreak there killed eighty-three people, mostly young children. At his confirmation hearings, Kennedy testified the trip had nothing to do with vaccines. But letters obtained by the Associated Press and the Guardian show he wrote Samoa's prime minister beforehand saying he wanted to study the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine there.
[ava] Senators Ron Wyden and Angela Alsobrooks are demanding records from Children's Health Defense, the group Kennedy used to lead, by December fourth. The letter isn't a subpoena, but Wyden becomes Finance Committee chair when Democrats take the Senate in January. The other side: Kennedy says he never discouraged anyone in Samoa from getting vaccinated, and his department has called any claim that he lied under oath false and defamatory, though it hasn't explained the gap between the letter and his testimony.
> source: The Guardian, "Senator opens investigation into evidence RFK Jr 'unlawfully lied' to Congress" (Nov 12); AP, "Senators probing RFK Jr.'s testimony on Samoa trip after AP and Guardian showed documents refute it" (Nov 12)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Gemini Decisions API; OpenAI monitorability research
[andrew] Story seven, A I. Google launched what it calls the Gemini Decisions A P I, powered by a new model, Gemini Decision one. Instead of writing out answers, it handles quick multiple-choice and yes-or-no judgments, up to a thousand questions in one pass, in a fifth of a second, with a confidence score on each. Think of the small calls A I agents make constantly: which tool to use, or whether a message breaks a rule. Google says it's about five times cheaper than its lightest chat model for that work.
[andrew] And a sobering note from OpenAI. Its safety researchers published a study finding that its newest model, G P T six Astra, is harder to monitor by reading its step-by-step reasoning, and better at reasoning without showing its work at all. Their words: "These results are sobering." Reading a model's reasoning has been one of the main ways to catch an A I going off the rails, so OpenAI says monitoring will need new approaches.
> source: Google, "Gemini Decisions API" announcement (Nov 11); OpenAI, "Chain-of-thought monitorability across the model lifecycle" (Nov 10); SEN-X Daily Briefing (Nov 12); The Neuron (Nov 12)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: SignDrop
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. A researcher known for publicly dumping Windows exploits has released one called SignDrop. It claims to let someone with an ordinary user account on a Windows PC take full system-level control, by abusing a protection Microsoft added last year to the Windows log-file system. There's no patch, no official C V E number yet, and Microsoft hasn't confirmed it. The researcher's claim that about eighty percent of Windows machines are exposed is an estimate, not a measured count.
[ava] The important limit: this isn't a break-in from the internet. An attacker already needs to be running code on your machine. Experts say don't switch off the protection to dodge it, since that reopens older attacks. Keep Windows updated and watch for a fix, likely on a future Patch Tuesday.
> source: BleepingComputer, "New Windows SignDrop zero-day gives hackers SYSTEM privileges" (Nov 12); Cyber Security News, "Windows SignDrop 0-Day Exploit Lets Attackers Gain SYSTEM Privileges" (Nov 11); Microsoft Learn, CLFS logfile authentication

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
[ava] And finally, revenge, served warm and fuzzy. A cat rescue in Saskatoon, Canada, got an offer from a supporter who had come into some money in her divorce. She'd match up to ten thousand dollars in donations, because, as she put it, spending it on rescue cats would irritate her husband. The rescue called the campaign Spite for Strays.
[ava] Its tongue-in-cheek Facebook post went around the world, with donations from Sweden, Britain, Croatia and nearly every U.S. state. They passed the goal in seventy-two hours and have raised nearly three times the match. The rescue, which spent more than half a million dollars on vet bills last year, says it's a big relief, and it's promising an interview with the anonymous donor soon.
> source: 980 CJME, "Pet-ty philanthropy: Spite-fuelled fundraiser draws global donations" (Nov 12)
