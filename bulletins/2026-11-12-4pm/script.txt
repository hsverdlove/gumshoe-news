## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Huang fallout + Wyden/Neal probe
[ava] Story one: the fallout from Jensen Huang's exit at Nvidia keeps widening. Reuters reported this afternoon that Huang also had an intimate relationship with a second Nvidia employee, which would make three alleged affairs in all. Reuters isn't naming her, and Nvidia declined to comment. CNBC reports Huang has agreed to testify before Congress before Thanksgiving about the national security questions around his relationship with Alice Huang, the businesswoman U.S. intelligence suspects of working for China. Beijing denies she's a spy. Interim chief executive Colette Kress has called an all-hands meeting for next Wednesday, and Nvidia still plans to report earnings next Tuesday as scheduled.
[ava] In Washington, the top Democrats on the Senate Finance and House Ways and Means committees, Ron Wyden and Richard Neal, opened an investigation into the administration's approval of advanced Nvidia chip sales to Chinese companies. Under that arrangement, chips made in Taiwan were routed through the U.S. and charged a twenty-five percent tariff before export licenses were granted. The two lawmakers call it an unconstitutional export tax dressed up as national security, and cite press reports that the president and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick were briefed on Huang's relationship before approving the sales. For now they're asking Commerce to preserve records. Those briefing reports come from press accounts and haven't been confirmed, and the administration hasn't publicly responded to the letter.

// Story 2: Brooklyn inspections
[ava] Story two. In Brooklyn, the toll from the Flatbush building collapse stands at eight dead, including two children, and eighteen hurt. At an afternoon briefing, Mayor Zohran Mamdani said the city has identified five companies tied to the excavation next door, and those companies are connected to about a hundred other construction sites across the city. Building inspectors began sweeping those sites today. Fire Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore says crews are still in search-and-rescue mode, and the rain will slow them down but won't stop them until they're sure no one else is trapped. The cause hasn't been determined; the developers had active permits for the excavation work.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: Gootloader takedown; Chrome reminder
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk. The Justice Department and F B I announced they've knocked out the Gootloader malware network, scrubbing it from more than a hundred servers, many of them hijacked WordPress websites, and seizing the domains used to run it. Gootloader typically tricks people into downloading a file from a booby-trapped site, then sells access to their computers to ransomware gangs. Since twenty eighteen it's infected at least twenty-five thousand machines and been linked to about twenty-nine million dollars in U.S. losses. It was part of Europe's Operation Endgame, alongside takedowns of two other malware families.
[ava] And a quick reminder: the federal cyber agency CISA says a Chrome flaw patched on Monday is being actively exploited. A booby-trapped web page alone can trigger it. Open Chrome's settings, go to About Chrome, and restart once it updates; Edge and other Chromium browsers should update too.
> source: U.S. Department of Justice, "Gootloader Malware Disrupted in International Cyber Operation" (Nov 12); BleepingComputer, "Police dismantle Gootloader, CastleRAT, StealC malware infrastructure" (Nov 12); Cyber Security News, "CISA Warns of Google Chromium 0-Day Vulnerability Actively Exploited in Attacks" (Nov 12)
