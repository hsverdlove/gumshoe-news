## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 5: AlphaProof Code verifies Signal
[andrew] Story five, tech. Google DeepMind says a new A I tool called AlphaProof Code has mathematically proven that key parts of Signal's encryption software do what they promise. Instead of just testing the code, the A I wrote millions of lines of formal proofs, checked by an independent proof checker, covering things like forward secrecy, meaning old messages stay safe even if a key is stolen later.
[andrew] What makes it notable is that the proofs reach all the way down to the compiled machine code that actually runs on phones, a level that's usually too laborious for human experts. The caveat: a proof only covers the properties it was asked to check, so it's not a blanket guarantee.
> source: Google DeepMind, "Introducing AlphaProof Code" (Nov 2026); WIRED, "A New AI Tool From Google DeepMind Proved the Signal Protocol Is Secure" (Nov 2026); The Register (Nov 10)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: Google Calendar invite session hijacking
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk, and this one is aimed at Google accounts. Researchers at Material Security are tracking a campaign that sends Google Calendar invites, often from a real contact whose account has been hijacked, with dull themes like a voicemail notice, a missed payment or a court summons. The links run through genuine Google addresses, so filters see nothing wrong.
[ava] They lead to a live copy of the Google sign-in page that passes your password and your multifactor approval straight through to Google, and the attacker walks away with your logged-in session. Text codes, authenticator apps and tap-yes prompts don't stop it. Material says it has taken down eight hundred ninety-seven attacker domains. The best defenses: be suspicious of unexpected invites that ask you to sign in, and consider a passkey or hardware security key, which this kind of relay can't fake.
> source: Material Security, "Reputation Says It's Fine: How Calendar Invites and a Live MFA Proxy Steal Google Sessions" (updated Nov 2026); BleepingComputer, "Google Calendar phishing campaign hijacks sessions, bypasses MFA" (Nov 2026)

The developers will chip in about one hundred thirty thousand dollars toward roughly four hundred seventy-six thousand in road work, including a right-turn lane at Pillsbury and Gilcreast, new stop signs at Michels Way, and a left-turn lane into one of the new neighborhoods.
