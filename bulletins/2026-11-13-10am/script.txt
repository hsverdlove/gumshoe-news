## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Bessent on Megaspeed and threats
[ava] Story one. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent put the spotlight on Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick this morning. On CNBC, Bessent said Treasury, the War Department, the Trade Representative and State had all favored putting Megaspeed on the government's entity list for months. That's the Singapore company accused of illegally funneling U.S. chips to China, whose executive was linked to Jensen Huang's resignation at Nvidia. But, Bessent said, "Commerce has the pen," and you'd have to ask them about their internal processes.
[ava] Commerce hadn't responded as of airtime. Lutnick's defenders would point out that export blacklists are a Commerce call by law and that the department weighs the trade impact on U.S. companies. Lutnick is already being asked to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on December tenth.
[ava] In the same interview, Bessent confirmed he moved from his Washington-area home to a military base on Secret Service advice, after death threats he links to Iran's leadership. He said he will not be intimidated, and he faulted the Wall Street Journal for publishing a photo he says revealed where his house is.

## Markets | sting=markets
// Story 4: PPI and the open
[andrew] Story four, the markets. Wholesale prices rose three tenths of a percent in October, a little under the four tenths economists expected, and core prices also rose three tenths, right on forecast. The catch: over the past year producer prices are up six and a half percent, the fastest pace since November twenty twenty-two. Truck freight costs drove most of the rise in services, and food prices jumped one point three percent.
[andrew] Futures got a lift from the report, and stocks opened higher, but the gains faded fast. As of about nine forty-five, the Dow was up roughly two to three tenths of a percent, the S&P five hundred about flat to slightly lower, and the Nasdaq down three to four tenths. Nvidia turned lower again, and chip stocks are slipping ahead of Nvidia's earnings next Tuesday.
[andrew] The pressure point is bonds. The ten-year Treasury yield touched five point one six percent, its highest since October, and markets still fully expect a Fed rate hike when it meets December eighth and ninth. Bitcoin fell below seventy thousand dollars for the first time in nearly three months. Even so, the S&P five hundred is on pace for its first winning week since early October.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: Check Point zero-day; FaceStealer
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk. Check Point has issued emergency fixes for a flaw in its firewall software that attackers have used since late October to take full root control of devices, against a couple of dozen customers. In the attacks Check Point described, intruders broke in through the Mobile Access portal, escalated to root, planted backdoors, then moved on to VMware servers and deployed destructive wiper malware. If you or a business you help runs Check Point gear, patch now.
[ava] And the FBI and Justice Department say they've disrupted a North Korean scheme the FBI calls FaceStealer, tied to more than four hundred million dollars in stolen crypto over about eighteen months. The hackers posed as people victims knew on fake video calls, using secretly recorded webcam footage and deepfakes, then showed a phony technical error that tricked victims into installing malware. Agents seized the cloud servers and more than nine hundred recorded videos. The lesson: if a video call suddenly asks you to install a fix, hang up and verify another way.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Check Point warns of zero-day flaw exploited for root access" (Nov 13); FBI, "FaceStealer Cryptocurrency Theft Scheme"; Justice Department press release; BBC, "North Korean hackers used fake video calls in $400m crypto hack, says FBI" (Nov 2026); Nextgov/FCW (Nov 2026)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 10: 3D-printed hand
[ava] And finally, story ten. In Baton Rouge, eleven-year-old Jak'kyra Brown was born without a right hand below the wrist. Her classmates on the robotics team at Kenilworth Science and Technology Charter School decided to make her one. They 3D-printed the pieces, strung them together with fishing line and rubber bands, and finished it in her favorite colors, red and gray, with sparkly stickers and her name engraved. It cost them fifty-six dollars, and they gave it to her free.
[ava] Her review: "Now I can have two hands." She says she can play basketball and do cartwheels better, and someday she wants to be an engineer and make hands for kids like her.
> source: WBRZ, "Students make prosthetic hand for classmate using 3D printer"
