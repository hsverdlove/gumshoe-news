// Story 2: Wichita soccer field, no charges
[ava] Story two. In Kansas, the woman whose pickup drove onto a youth soccer field in Wichita last month, killing a six-year-old boy and a thirty-five-year-old man and injuring ten others, will not be criminally charged. Sedgwick County's district attorney said she had reclined her seat to nap after a two-hundred-mile drive, and the most likely explanation is that the truck was never put in park and her foot hit the accelerator in her sleep. Her blood tests were clean, and a General Motors team found no mechanical problem. The prosecutor said Kansas law requires more than a single negligent act for vehicular manslaughter. Police acknowledged some questions may never be answered with certainty, and the decision is a painful one for the victims' families, who were told before it was announced.
> source: NBC News, "Woman who drove onto Kansas soccer field, killing 2, won't be charged, officials say" (Nov 12); AP, "Napping driver whose truck accelerated on Kansas soccer field, killing 2, won't be charged" (Nov 12)

// Story 3: Trump on Cuba
[ava] Story three. President Trump told radio host Hugh Hewitt he wants to "get Cuba done" before he leaves office, and that he would "certainly consider" a short military operation, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio playing a key role. Supporters say decades of communist rule and Cuba's collapsing economy make change overdue. No decision has been announced, and analysts quoted by CNBC see more economic pressure as likelier than force.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Dublin decision; Nairobi collapse
[ava] Story four, the world. In Dublin, European leaders didn't pick a single envoy for the Ukraine peace talks after all. Five others were rescued and taken to the hospital, and authorities say all the workers at the site are now accounted for. Construction has been halted while investigators look at the owner and the contractor.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Microsoft apology and cause
[andrew] Story six, tech. Microsoft has apologized for its nearly day-long outage, saying it is, quote, deeply sorry. The company says the cause was a problem introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced after a recent network change, and it says again that it wasn't a cyberattack and had nothing to do with A I or agents. Microsoft says it fixed the underlying issue and restored service slowly on purpose, checking stability at each step, which it admits took longer. A full post-incident report is promised once its review is done.
> source: Windows Central, "'We are deeply sorry' Microsoft apologizes for major outage" (Nov 13); PCWorld, "Microsoft: AI, hackers not to blame for Outlook services outage" (Nov 13)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: ChatGPT Wallet; Meta Muse calls
[andrew] Story seven, A I. OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, which lets its personal agents, called dots, actually buy things for you. It's built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. You add a card once, then approve every purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint, and the agent gets a one-time payment token instead of your real card number, so you keep your usual rewards and dispute protections. It starts with U.S. Pro subscribers only, and OpenAI says it isn't taking a cut for now. The hurdle is the stores themselves: United Airlines says it will block agents from booking, and Amazon doesn't let them buy. Meanwhile, Meta is rolling out a feature that lets its Muse agent place phone calls to businesses for paying U.S. subscribers.
> source: OpenAI, "Introducing ChatGPT Wallet" (Nov 13); CNBC, "OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet, allowing its 'dots' AI agents to make purchases for users" (Nov 13); TechCrunch via Techmeme, Meta Muse outbound calling (Nov 13)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Project Zero; PostgreSQL; Luna Moth
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Google's Project Zero, the team that hunts security holes in other companies' software, is shortening the clock. Starting today, it will publish bug details exactly ninety days after reporting them, fixed or not, dropping the old extra thirty-day grace period. That shrinks to sixty days in February and thirty days in January twenty twenty-eight. Google's reasoning: A I helps attackers find and weaponize bugs so fast that long embargoes no longer protect anyone. Some vendors warned that rushing could expose unfixed flaws, which is why Google is phasing it in.
[ava] Also, PostgreSQL, the popular open-source database, patched fifty-two security flaws, including a twenty-two-year-old bug that lets some logged-in users run code on the server. Update the server and its extensions together.
[ava] And the extortion gang known as Luna Moth is going back to law firms that refused to pay the first time. Researchers at ReliaQuest say the crooks pose as I T support on a video call and talk victims into copying a Microsoft access token out of their browser's developer tools. The rule of thumb: real I T will never ask you to open developer tools or hand over a token. Hang up and call back.
> source: Google Project Zero, "Policy and Disclosure: 2026 Edition" (Nov 13); The Hacker News, "PostgreSQL Patches 52 Flaws, Including 22-Year-Old Bug Enabling Authenticated RCE" (Nov 2026); PostgreSQL.org security advisory CVE-2026-6460; Dark Reading, "Extortion Group Luna Moth Flits Into Law Firms a Second Time" (Nov 12)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 10: Menhaden quota; Seasmoke Trio
[ava] Story ten, local news from where you are. Interstate fishery regulators voted to cut the Atlantic menhaden catch by about nine percent next year and another nine percent in twenty twenty-eight. Menhaden, or pogies, are the main bait for Maine's lobster boats. Maine expects a quota of roughly seventeen point eight million pounds in twenty twenty-seven. Maine's marine resources commissioner, Carl Wilson, said Maine wanted no change, but the outcome is far better than proposals for cuts of up to thirty-nine percent. Supporters of deeper cuts point to menhaden's role as food for striped bass, birds and whales.
[ava] And for Saturday night, the Seasmoke Trio plays jazz, swing, blues and American songbook standards at the Kittery Art Association gallery on Walker Street, seven-thirty to nine. Tickets are fifteen dollars, with a limited number at the door.
> source: Maine Department of Marine Resources, "Statement from DMR Commissioner on Menhaden Quota for 2027 and 2028" (Nov 12); The Maine Monitor, "Fisheries regulator cuts menhaden catch limit"; PortsmouthNH.com, "The Seasmoke Trio at the Kittery Art Association" (Nov 14 listing)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Paddleboard rescue
[ava] And finally, story eleven. During record flooding in Brevard County, Florida, Brittany Grant and her son were stranded on their kitchen counter as the water rose, and she says 911 told her crews couldn't reach them.
