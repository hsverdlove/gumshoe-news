## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: New Orleans tanker hits river cruise boat (BREAKING)
[ava] Story one, and it's developing. In New Orleans, a tanker called the Atlantic Asphalt slammed into a docked river cruise boat, the American Heritage, at about twelve-thirty local time this afternoon. The paddlewheeler, run by American Cruise Lines, had about one hundred twenty-nine passengers aboard, plus crew, and was getting ready to let them off near the Convention Center, just below the Crescent City Connection bridge. Video verified by NBC News shows the boat rolled onto its side, with people in life jackets in the water.
[ava] The Coast Guard, police, fire crews and helicopters spent the afternoon pulling people from the river, and survivors are being triaged at the Port of New Orleans administration building, some on stretchers. As of our latest check, officials had not released any count of injuries, deaths or missing, and the Port says the cause is not yet known. American Cruise Lines says guests and crew were evacuated. The Coast Guard closed a three-mile stretch of the river, Governor Jeff Landry is heading to the city, and President Trump has been briefed. Treat any casualty numbers you see online with caution until officials confirm them.

// Story 2: SCOTUS asylum; Hassett denial
[ava] Story two. The Supreme Court agreed today to decide whether President Trump can suspend access to asylum at the southern border. A lower court and the D.C. Circuit ruled the policy goes beyond what Congress allows, though the ban stays in effect while the government appeals. The administration argues the president has broad power to keep out people who cross illegally, and that the border was overwhelmed. The challengers, thirteen asylum seekers and three nonprofits, say only Congress can rewrite the asylum law, and that crossings had already fallen sharply before the order. Arguments are expected in the spring, with a ruling likely by late June.
[ava] Also, Semafor reported this afternoon that National Economic Council director Kevin Hassett has been asking about jobs outside the administration. Hassett told CNBC that's "not true," and a White House official said there's "no factual basis" for the report.
> source: SCOTUSblog, "Supreme Court agrees to hear case on Trump administration's suspension of asylum" (Nov 13, 3:44 PM); AP, "Supreme Court will consider Trump's executive order suspending asylum access at the southern border" (Nov 13); CNBC, "Hassett denies report he's leaving White House: 'Not true'" (Nov 13)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Prisoner swap; Pope in Chiclayo
[ava] Story three, the world. After this week's U.S.-brokered talks, White House envoy Jared Kushner says Washington is working toward what would be the largest prisoner exchange of the war in Ukraine, potentially reuniting thousands of people with their families. Neither Kyiv nor Moscow has given the final numbers or a date. Earlier this week, Russian state media claimed more than a thousand people on each side. Kushner also said significant differences remain, especially over territory, and that apart from humanitarian steps like this one, nothing will be final without a full agreement.

The Vatican estimates about half a million people came to his Mass near the city, with a million more lining the motorcade route. It's exactly a month since his lung cancer surgery, and he thanked them for their prayers during his recovery.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 5: Starship flight 15; BMW recall
[andrew] Story five, tech. SpaceX got its F A A approval this afternoon, so Starship Flight Fifteen is set to lift off from South Texas at five forty-five our time, in a seventy-five-minute window. The goal is a first: catching both stages back at the launch tower. The booster comes back about six and a half minutes after launch. The ship deploys twenty-six Starlink satellites, circles the Earth for about twenty-two hours, and, if everything checks out, comes home for its own catch tomorrow afternoon. If anything looks off, SpaceX will ditch it in the Pacific instead.
[andrew] And a recall worth checking if there's a BMW in your family. BMW is recalling about seven hundred twenty thousand U.S. vehicles because a water-pump connector can short out and catch fire, even while the car is parked and off. Until it's fixed, owners are told to park outside, away from buildings. The fix is still being developed, and owner letters start around Christmas.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: Trezor implants; Uber/Fireworks AI breach
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk. Hardware wallet maker Trezor confirms that three of its Safe 5 crypto wallets sold by an Indonesian reseller, CryptoBilis, had a hidden extra circuit board that captured recovery words off the screen and sent them out over a cell network. A firmware update released Wednesday blocks it, but it can't protect a phrase that was already stolen, so anyone who bought from that reseller should move funds to a new wallet. No thefts have been reported. Trezor has cut about eighty percent of its resellers. The lesson: buy hardware wallets direct.
[ava] And Uber confirms hackers stole driver's license details for thousands of its U.S. drivers, at least seventy-eight hundred in Massachusetts, Rhode Island and South Carolina alone, by breaking into an A I vendor, Fireworks A I, using stolen credentials. Uber says it has seen no misuse and is offering credit monitoring.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Trezor confirms crypto-stealing implants in three wallets, releases fix" (Nov 13); TechCrunch, "Uber confirms hackers stole thousands of drivers' license information in data breach" (Nov 13); PCMag (Nov 13)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 10: Jacey Abbott hijab thread
[ava] And finally, story ten. Jacey Abbott is a forty-one-year-old mom of three in southern Alabama with stage four breast cancer, and she hates wigs. Too hot, too itchy. So she asked Muslim users on the social app Threads: can I wear a hijab, or is that disrespectful? Within about eleven hours, she had roughly nineteen hundred replies, and nearly all of them said, yes, of course you can.
[ava] People sent tips on soft fabrics and satin-lined caps, offers of free scarves, including one small shop in Europe that offered express shipping to America, and a lot of prayers. One woman asked, "Is it ok with you if I pray for you to have ease and comfort?" Another simply wrote: wearing anything comfortable is the right of every human being. Jacey says the hijab she bought is super soft, and she plans to pass extras on to other cancer patients.
> source: The Washington Post, "A cancer patient asked if it was okay to wear a hijab. Thousands rallied for her." (Nov 13); The Global Filipino Magazine (Nov 6)
