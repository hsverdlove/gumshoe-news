## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Nvidia fallout in Washington
[ava] Story one. The fallout from Jensen Huang's exit at Nvidia has reached the Senate Banking Committee. Chairman Tim Scott, a South Carolina Republican, sent letters asking Huang to testify publicly on December third and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on December tenth, citing national security concerns around Huang's relationship with a woman U.S. intelligence suspects of working for China. Scott wrote that if they don't appear voluntarily, the committee is prepared to use subpoenas.
[ava] Inside Nvidia, the mood is different. WIRED reports an internal suggestion-box post urging the board to weigh employee sentiment has drawn more than sixteen hundred comments, most of them asking for Huang to be brought back under the banner "bring back THE boss." The other side: the board, now chaired by former Synopsys chief Harvey Jones, moved after three reported affairs, including one with a direct report,. Investing dot com reports President Trump floated former Apple chief Tim Cook as a possible successor; Nvidia hasn't commented on candidates.

Next, investigators want the readings from the vibration monitors that were part of the excavation's safety plan.

Qatar and Pakistan are still trying to mediate.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: SilentAir
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. A telecom security firm called Enea has uncovered a spying group it calls SilentAir, which hacked equipment inside mobile carriers in at least six Asian countries and used it to reroute selected people's calls and texts, mostly targeting users in Taiwan. For some targets, the attackers grabbed two-factor login codes sent by text right after rerouting their messages, a sign they were trying to break into other accounts. Enea calls SilentAir a state-level actor and links it to China only with low confidence. It says the group has been disrupted for now but could return.
[ava] The takeaway for the rest of us: text-message codes travel over a phone network that runs on trust between carriers. Where you can, use an authenticator app or, better, a passkey or security key.
> source: The Hacker News, "SilentAir Hijacks Asian Telecom Networks to Intercept SMS 2FA Codes and Spy on Taiwanese Users" (Nov 13); Enea, "The Enemy Within: Tracking LIMINAL PANDA and SilentAir through Mobile Networks"; Infosecurity Magazine (Nov 2026)
