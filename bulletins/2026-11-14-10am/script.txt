## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Ground stop day two, cause reporting
[ava] Story one. Day two of the air travel mess. As of about nine forty this morning, FlightAware counted more than thirty-seven hundred U.S. flights canceled today and close to two thousand delayed, on top of roughly nine thousand cancellations yesterday. Atlanta, Chicago O'Hare and New York's J F K are among the hardest hit. Secretary Sean Duffy says operations are slowly returning to normal and it's safe to fly, but warns of more cancellations as airlines move planes and crews back into place. He scheduled a briefing for ten thirty this morning.
[ava] On the cause, there's new reporting. C B S News, citing multiple sources, says the trouble followed an update to the private communications network that links the air traffic control system, run by contractor L three Harris, which rebooted and then crashed. Bloomberg reports the F B I is looking at a software update as a possible cause and doesn't believe it was a cyberattack or a crime. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin says investigators have seen no footprints of hacking, but aren't ruling it out yet. No official root cause has been announced, and L three Harris hasn't commented publicly.
> source: CNBC, "FAA ground stop lifted but U.S. flight disruptions continue" (Nov 14); Washington Post, "Travel chaos, flight cancellations continue following nationwide ground stop" (Nov 14); SecurityWeek, "No Signs of Hacking in FAA Ground Stop as FBI Reportedly Probes Software Update" (Nov 14), citing CBS News and Bloomberg

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Hormuz tankers, Predator claim
[ava] Story four, the world. Iran says three more oil tankers hit naval mines and caught fire overnight in and near the Strait of Hormuz, one of them a supertanker. Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two of them had switched off their tracking systems and were using an unauthorized route. Iran hasn't named the ships, nobody has independently confirmed the incidents, and the British maritime monitor hadn't reported them as of this morning. The Guards also claim they shot down an American M Q one Predator drone over Qeshm Island. The U.S. military says it has cleared the main shipping lanes of mines, and it continues to enforce its blockade of Iranian ports.
> source: CNA/AFP, "Iran reports three incidents of oil tankers hit by mines in Hormuz" (Nov 14); Rudaw, "IRGC says two oil tankers explode after striking mines in Strait of Hormuz" (Nov 14); Tampa Free Press (Nov 14)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Amodei compute dilemma, IPO next week
[andrew] Story eight, A I. In a final pitch before Anthropic's stock market debut, C E O Dario Amodei told J P Morgan's Jamie Dimon that buying computing power is the company's deepest business dilemma. Buy too little, and you can't serve customers or build new models. Buy too much, and you can financially overextend yourself. He says Anthropic tends to buy on the low side and that a big cash cushion is one reason it's going public now. Its filings show nearly nine hundred one billion dollars in commitments, mostly for chips and computing.
[andrew] Anthropic expects to price the offering Wednesday and start trading Thursday, seeking up to about ninety billion dollars at roughly a one point four trillion dollar valuation, below the two trillion some investors had expected a few weeks ago. Bill Ackman, who was in the room, called Amodei thoughtful and measured. Skeptics point to rising borrowing costs and the Nvidia turmoil as reasons to be cautious about A I valuations.
> source: Bloomberg via Business Standard, "Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei calls computing power deepest business dilemma" (Nov 14); Yahoo Finance, "Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei aims to 'underpromise and overdeliver'" (Nov 13)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: wfx-loot GitHub campaign
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. Researchers at Aikido Security say an attacker used stolen logins for nearly forty-eight hundred GitHub accounts to probe more than sixty-one thousand code repositories, looking for ones they could write to. Where they could, they slipped a script into the projects' automated build pipelines that grabbed passwords, access tokens and cloud keys for Amazon, Microsoft and Google, then erased their tracks in under a minute. At least eighteen hundred repositories had secrets stolen, and some of the hijacked accounts belonged to big companies and government agencies.
[ava] The giveaway is an innocent-looking pull request titled "docs: dependency audit notes," and ninety-two percent of them are still sitting open, unnoticed. If your projects ran the payload, the advice is to treat every secret as compromised and change it.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Hackers compromise 4,800 GitHub accounts to steal cloud credentials" (Nov 12); Aikido Security, "wfx-loot campaign pushes CI secret stealer to tens of thousands of repositories"

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 11: Brewer data centers; tonight's music
[ava] Story eleven, local news. Remember that statewide data center debate? Brewer, near Bangor, is moving the other way. Its planning board has recommended zoning changes that would allow data centers of up to fifty megawatts, about the power use of a small city, in certain industrial zones in south Brewer. Nothing has been formally proposed. The city manager says one such project could nearly double Brewer's tax base and cut property taxes by a quarter. The one board member who voted no says the downsides got far less discussion than the upside. Neighboring Bangor and Old Town have banned data centers this year. There's a public information session on November twenty-fourth.
[ava] For tonight on the Seacoast: Springtide plays The Dance Hall in Kittery at seven-thirty, singer-songwriter Antje Duvekot is at The Music Hall Lounge in Portsmouth at eight, and York's own Lexi James and Wildheart play The Stone Church in Newmarket at eight. Tomorrow, the Kittery Community Market is open, and Terri Lyne Carrington brings her band to Jimmy's in Portsmouth at seven-thirty.
> source: Bangor Daily News, "Brewer poised to allow data centers that use as much power as a small city" (Nov 13); Seacoast Local Loop, "This Weekend on the NH Seacoast (Nov 13-15)"

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 12: Gomez the vulture
[ava] And finally, story twelve. At the Pocono Wildlife Rehabilitation and Education Center in Pennsylvania, a turkey vulture named Gomez came in with a broken wing and lead poisoning. He got treatment and surgery, but there was a problem. Vultures are social birds, and Gomez was the only one there. So the staff gave him a stuffed vulture. Director Janine Tancredi says he ran right up and snuggled in.
[ava] Soon he had a whole flock of plush friends, inside and out. Then a real one arrived: another injured turkey vulture named Gizmo. Staff say Gomez was thrilled. It's not yet clear whether either bird will fly free again, but for now, neither one is healing alone.
> source: The Dodo, "Vulture With Broken Wing Falls In Love With Stuffed Birds While Healing At Sanctuary" (Nov 13)
