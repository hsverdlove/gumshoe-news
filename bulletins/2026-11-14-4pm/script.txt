## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Ground stop recovery day 2
[ava] Story one. Day two of the flight mess is winding down. FlightAware counted more than fifty-one hundred flights within, into or out of the U.S. canceled today, as airlines worked to get planes and crews back where they belong after yesterday's nationwide ground stop. The F A A says the system is back at full operations, and it expects airline schedules to be back to normal by the end of Sunday, well ahead of the Thanksgiving rush. Secretary Duffy repeated today that no one was ever in danger; more than three thousand flights that were in the air when the order came down all landed safely. If you're flying tomorrow, check your flight before you leave for the airport.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Turkey-Qatar defense pact
[ava] Story four, the world. Turkey and Qatar have signed a mutual defense agreement. After meeting in Istanbul Friday, President Erdogan and Qatar's emir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, committed to a common position against any outside armed attack on either country, along with more joint exercises, defense-industry work and training exchanges. The deal comes amid the fallout of the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran and the disruption in the Strait of Hormuz. Analysts quoted by Al Jazeera say it's meant to deter, not to build an offensive alliance, and the published text doesn't spell out what would trigger it or what a joint response would look like. Turkey already keeps troops in Qatar.
> source: Al Jazeera, "Qatar and Turkiye sign a mutual defence pact: What do we know?" (Nov 14); France 24, "Turkey and Qatar pledge unified stance against attacks in mutual defence pact" (Nov 13)

Big sticking points: money for poorer countries to adapt, how hard to push on moving away from fossil fuels, and a proposed target to raise electricity's share of energy use to thirty-five percent by twenty thirty-five, which some large developing countries oppose. A first draft of the final deal is expected early next week.
> source: Climate Home News, "COP31 Bulletin Day 6: Climate marchers call for justice as talks make limited progress" (Nov 14); DW, "COP31: Protesters, UN urge swift climate deal" (Nov 14)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Starship ship catch
[andrew] Story seven, tech, and it just happened. SpaceX has caught a Starship upper stage with its launch tower for the first time. At about four twenty this afternoon Eastern, about twenty-two and a half hours after launch, the ship made its banking turn and final approach, and the tower's mechanical arms, nicknamed the chopsticks, grabbed it at Starbase in South Texas. SpaceX posted, quote, "Mechazilla catches Starship!"
[andrew] It's a big step toward a fully reusable rocket, though it wasn't a clean sweep. Yesterday the Super Heavy booster skipped its own catch: SpaceX says it cut its boostback burn short on a low-fuel alarm, then guidance saw it was out of position, so it diverted to the Gulf of Mexico and was destroyed on impact. The root cause is still under investigation. The ship also deployed twenty-six Starlink satellites. We'll have more on what the catch means for the next flights as SpaceX shares details.
[andrew] One footnote: Microsoft says it finished restoring Outlook, Teams and Copilot for all customers around one o'clock this morning, closing out an outage that ran close to two days.
> source: SpaceX on X, "Mechazilla catches Starship!" (Nov 14, 4:21 PM ET); Space.com, "SpaceX Starship Flight 15 live updates" (Nov 14); PBS News, "SpaceX's Starship soars into orbit after booster malfunction thwarts double catch attempt" (Nov 13); The Verge, "Microsoft has finished restoring service after its massive Outlook outage" (Nov 14)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Amodei age of miracles vs pharma
[andrew] Story eight, A I. Ahead of Anthropic's stock market debut, Dario Amodei's roadshow video predicts that cancer, Alzheimer's, heart disease and mental illness will be, in his words, relics of the past in less than a decade, an "age of miracles." Anthropic has backed that up with money: it runs a wet biology lab in the Bay Area, has bought small biotech startups, and says it has begun its own drug discovery.
[andrew] The drug industry's reply has been polite but skeptical. Eli Lilly C E O Dave Ricks posted that it would be amazing, but not likely, because proving a drug works in human trials is the long pole in the tent. Vertex's executive chairman, Jeff Leiden, was blunter, calling the timelines complete B S, and said that in tests of about fifty A I companies, none produced a working drug on its own. Supporters counter that A I is already speeding up early discovery, and that's where the bottleneck has been. Even Amodei wrote in August that "A I will cure cancer" is more cliche than inspiring.
> source: Yahoo Finance/Benzinga, "Anthropic's Dario Amodei Promises 'Age of Miracles' ― Medicine Isn't Buying It" (Nov 13); Domina IA (Nov 13); Reuters, "Anthropic quietly sets up biology lab as it ramps AI drug program" (Sept 18)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: FaceStealer details, Binance seizure
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. New details on North Korea's FaceStealer scheme, which the U.S. says it has now disrupted by seizing its cloud servers. Hackers hijacked real accounts on Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack and Discord, invited victims to fake video meetings, and played secretly recorded webcam clips of people the victim knew. Then they faked an audio glitch and offered a "fix" that was actually malware. Investigators found more than nine hundred recorded webcam videos, and researchers at Arctic Wolf count six hundred seventy-seven targets in fifty-eight countries, mostly founders and executives. More than four hundred million dollars in crypto was stolen over eighteen months, and the F B I says the same crew went after open-source software maintainers.
[ava] Separately, the Justice Department has court approval to seize more than one point two billion dollars stolen in last month's roughly six billion dollar Binance hack, part of more than two point seven billion frozen so far. The takeaway: if a call invite asks you to install anything, stop and verify through another channel.
> source: BleepingComputer, "US disrupts North Korean campaign behind $400 million crypto theft" (Nov 14); FBI, "FaceStealer Cryptocurrency Theft Scheme"; U.S. Justice Department, "Justice Department Announces Actions to Disrupt North Korea's Massive Theft of Cryptocurrency" (Nov 12); CyberScoop (Nov 12)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 12: Vilitha Clay and Michael Cloyd
[ava] And finally, story twelve. Michael Cloyd, a Vietnam veteran and retired carpenter now in his seventies, had spent four months in a Wisconsin V A hospital after a mild stroke, with no family nearby and nowhere to go. Before that he'd lived in a run-down trailer park and then a motel room. Vilitha Clay, a V A contractor, looked at him and asked, "How would you like to come home with me?"
[ava] He said yes, and became the first resident of the adult family home she opened in Madison in memory of her late mother, who had hoped to live with her one day. It has three private rooms, home-cooked meals and nurses who visit. On his first day, Michael looked at his dinner plate and asked, "What's this?" Today he's part of her family, and her home has since taken in other veterans too.
> source: Woman's World, "'How Would You Like to Come Home With Me?': How One Woman Saved a Lonely Vietnam Veteran" (Nov 14)
