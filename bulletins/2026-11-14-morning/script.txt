## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: FAA nationwide ground stop
[ava] Story one. If you're flying this weekend, check your flight. Yesterday afternoon, the F A A grounded every departure in the country after air traffic control centers started seeing unstable radar and radio links at about three thirty-six Eastern. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says new technology being added to the system overloaded older switches and routers, which began feeding controllers inconsistent data, so they shut it down. He says radar and communications were never completely lost, and more than three thousand planes in the air landed safely.
[ava] Airspace reopened at eight fifteen last night, after roughly four hours, the longest nationwide ground stop since the September eleventh attacks. By late evening, Flightradar twenty-four counted nearly ten thousand cancellations. Homeland Security says there's no evidence of a cyberattack. Analysts expect disruptions for days, though officials say the system should be back to normal before Thanksgiving. Duffy says he can't guarantee it won't happen again, and he's asking Congress for fifty billion dollars to replace the system.
> source: Reuters, "US to resume flights after air traffic network failure sparks chaos" (Nov 13); AP, "Trump administration blames 'overloaded' systems for ground stop after FAA lets flights resume" (Nov 14); FAA, "Statement on Nationwide Ground Stop" (Nov 13)

// Story 2: New Orleans riverboat, no deaths
[ava] Story two, an update from New Orleans, and it's good news. After the tanker Atlantic Asphalt slammed into the docked riverboat American Heritage yesterday, everyone aboard, about one hundred twenty-nine passengers plus crew, has been accounted for, and there were no deaths. Thirty-two people went to the hospital, thirty passengers and two harbor police officers, none with critical injuries. Mayor Helena Moreno called it a miracle on the Mississippi. Some early reports point to a steering problem on the tanker, but investigators have not confirmed a cause.
> source: AP, "All passengers and crew on New Orleans cruise ship survive crash with tanker on Mississippi River" (Nov 13); NOLA.com live updates (Nov 13); Fox 8 WVUE (Nov 13)

// Story 3: Alaska governor
[ava] Story three. Alaska has elected a Democratic governor for the first time since nineteen ninety-eight. Former state lawmaker Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins beat three Republicans, including one endorsed by President Trump, after campaigning on affordability, full school funding, a corporate income tax on all oil and gas producers, and a pause on new data centers until the state writes rules for them. Democrats will now hold thirty governorships. Alaska voters also rejected, for the second time in two years, a measure to repeal the state's open primaries and ranked-choice voting.
> source: AP, "Alaska voters elect a Democrat for governor for the first time since 1998" (Nov 14)

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Zelensky says US raised NATO-for-Donbas
[ava] Story four, the world. President Zelensky says American negotiators asked this week, for the first time, whether Ukraine would pull its forces out of the Donbas in exchange for joining NATO. He stressed there's no concrete offer and that Ukraine won't withdraw, but said he'd put a deal to a referendum if it came with firm security guarantees, no limits on Ukraine's military, and money to rebuild. "NATO is back on the agenda," he said. Some caution: U.S. officials had earlier said NATO membership was off the table, and Moscow has long called Ukraine in NATO unacceptable while demanding the rest of the Donbas.
[ava] The fighting goes on. Russian officials say a Ukrainian drone killed a couple and their two-year-old daughter in the Belgorod region overnight, and Ukraine's air force says Russia launched ninety-six drones, eighty-one of them brought down.
> source: BBC, "US has floated Ukraine ceding Donbas for Nato membership, Zelensky says" (Nov 14); Al Jazeera/Reuters, "Three killed in Russia as Ukraine claims US floated NATO membership option" (Nov 14)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Starship flight 15; Friday close
[andrew] Story seven, tech. Starship Flight Fifteen lifted off at five forty-seven last night. The upper stage reached orbit and released twenty-six Starlink satellites, but the double catch didn't happen. On its way back, the Super Heavy booster detected a guidance problem, called off the tower catch on its own, and diverted to a fiery splashdown in the Gulf. This newer version of the booster has yet to make a successful return. The ship itself looks healthy, and SpaceX is aiming for its catch at Starbase at about four twenty this afternoon, our time.
[andrew] And a business footnote, since markets are closed today. Friday's final numbers: the Dow edged up a tenth of a percent to about fifty-two thousand three hundred forty-eight, the S and P fell four tenths to about seventy-five sixty-two, and the Nasdaq dropped about nine tenths, with chip stocks again leading the way down. Airline shares fell late in the day as the ground stop hit.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Pope Leo on transhumanism
[ava] Story eight, A I, with a theologian's view. Speaking at a Catholic university in Chiclayo, Peru, Pope Leo warned about what he called the mirage of transhumanism, the idea that technology will sooner or later eliminate disease, pain, aging and even death. He praised scientific progress as a supreme achievement of human intelligence, but said it must not become a privilege for millionaires seeking immortality while whole peoples live in poverty. Human limits, he argued, aren't a manufacturing defect but part of human dignity, because they call us to look after each other.
[ava] Some tech enthusiasts on X shared that quote as if the pope were cheering the idea on; in context, he was warning against it. Today he visits Zaña, the heart of Afro-Peruvian culture.
> source: America Magazine, "Pope Leo at Catholic university in Peru: 'Science without a conscience ruins everyone's life'" (Nov 13); Vatican News, "Pope in Peru: Search for truth in universities becomes service to humanity" (Nov 13); AP, Zaña preview (Nov 14)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Quantum Systems update hack; Microsoft patch issues
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. German drone maker Quantum Systems, whose drones fly for Germany's military and which is helping build interceptor drones for Ukraine, confirms it was hit by what it calls hostile actors in late October. Der Spiegel and the Financial Times report the attackers pushed a malicious update through the company's mission-planning software, and the German military reportedly grounded the company's drones as a precaution. The company hasn't named a culprit, but believes its support for Ukraine made it a target. It's another reminder that a trusted software update is one of the most dangerous ways in.
[ava] And if you manage Windows machines, Microsoft confirms this month's Patch Tuesday updates are causing activation failures for organizations that script their Windows licensing, plus some printing problems. Home users with already-activated P Cs shouldn't be affected, and fixes are coming.
> source: BleepingComputer, "German military drone operations disrupted by cyberattack" (Nov 13); BleepingComputer, "Microsoft confirms Windows activation failures after November updates" (Nov 13); BleepingComputer, "Microsoft says November security updates cause printing issues" (Nov 13)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 12: Parker of Charleston, Arkansas
[ava] And finally, story twelve. In Charleston, Arkansas, a town of fewer than three thousand people, a man named Parker picks up litter every day. He isn't a city worker. It started on impulse last August, on a walk with his daughter Gabrielle, when they filled one plastic bag with wrappers and cans. It felt good, so he went back the next day, and the next.
[ava] A year later, he puts in about two hours a day, seven days a week, dodging snakes, fire ants, wasps and traffic, and he's collected around nine hundred bags. Parker lives with several mental health conditions and says working in front of people is hard for him, so this is how he serves his family and his town. People honk, wave, and sometimes stop to hug him. "It's never a job," he says. "I enjoy it tremendously."
> source: WDIY, "A Community United | When Good Things Happen" (Nov 13)
