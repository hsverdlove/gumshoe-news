## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Flights recovering; Trump weighs in on ATC money
[ava] Story one. Air travel is closing in on normal. FlightAware showed about fourteen hundred U.S. cancellations as of seven this morning, far below the roughly sixty-seven hundred on Saturday and more than nine thousand on Friday. American Airlines says its planned cancellations are done, and it's waiving change fees for travel through Wednesday.
[ava] President Trump commented on the ground stop for the first time this morning. On social media, he blamed the Biden administration for making an old system worse and urged Congress to approve air traffic control money "FAST," after Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy asked for fifty billion dollars on Friday. Democrats point out that the failure happened during this administration's own upgrade work, and that most of the twelve and a half billion dollars Congress approved last year hasn't been spent. Supporters say equipment that's decades old is exactly why the work should be done all at once.

// Story 3: Richard Sterban dies
[ava] Story three. Richard Sterban, the bass voice of the Oak Ridge Boys, has died at eighty-three. He died Saturday in Hendersonville, Tennessee, of complications from bronchitis and pneumonia after a long fight with cancer. Sterban joined the group in nineteen seventy-two, and his "oom poppa mow mow" on "Elvira" became one of the best-known bass lines in American music. The Oak Ridge Boys had seventeen number-one country singles and are in the Country Music Hall of Fame.
> source: WKRN, "Richard Sterban, bass voice of The Oak Ridge Boys, dies at 83" (Nov 15); AP (Nov 14); Variety (Nov 14)

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Iran targets Bahrain again
[ava] Story four, the world. Sirens sounded in Bahrain before dawn as Iran targeted the kingdom for the second time in three days. Iran's Revolutionary Guard says it sent six drones at a U.S. air defense radar at the Riffa helicopter base, in what it calls retaliation for hostile U.S. actions. Bahrain, home of the Navy's Fifth Fleet, says its air defenses shot down the incoming attacks before they reached their targets, and there's no U.S. or Bahraini confirmation that anything was hit. After Friday's attempt, U.S. Central Command said two Iranian drones were shot down with no damage, contradicting Iran's claim of a hit.
[ava] Separately, Saudi Arabia says it intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh, plus drones over its oil-rich Eastern Province.
> source: BBC, "Iran targets US military base in Bahrain with drone attack" (Nov 15); CNBC/Reuters, "Bahrain intercepts Iranian aerial attacks as Tehran says it attacked US base" (Nov 15); France 24 (Nov 15); The National (Nov 15)

// Story 5: Yemen government retreats from Bab el-Mandeb
[ava] Story five. In Yemen, Saudi-backed government forces say they're pulling back from Dhubab airport and the area overlooking the Bab el-Mandeb Strait to defend their base in Aden. That appears to end a six-week counteroffensive and leaves the Houthis in control of the coast at the mouth of the Red Sea. Reuters notes that, with Iran also choking the Strait of Hormuz, Gulf oil exporters now face pressure on both of their main sea routes. Yemen's presidency calls it a calculated military move. Reuters reports that the besieged city of Taiz is cut off from aid.
> source: Reuters via NBC News, "Yemen government forces withdraw from area near Bab el Mandeb Strait, Houthis in control" (Nov 15); Asharq Al-Awsat, "Yemeni Presidency: Redeployment of Forces Near Bab Al-Mandab a 'Calculated Military Move'" (Nov 15)

// Story 6: Black Sea drones near Romania; Prague rally
[ava] Story six, Europe. Drones hit three commercial ships in the Black Sea near Romania's Sulina canal Saturday night, two of them inside Romanian waters. Fires broke out but the crews put them out, and no one was hurt. Romania scrambled F-sixteens twice. Officials didn't say who launched the drones, but Prime Minister Luca Niculescu blamed the deteriorating security caused by Russia's war. Russia denies targeting Ukraine's neighbors. Romania's defense minister plans to ask NATO allies for more patrols and counter-drone gear this week.
[ava] And in Prague, tens of thousands of people rallied in Letna Park against a government plan to scrap the public-broadcasting fee and fund Czech TV and radio from the state budget instead. Critics compare it to Viktor Orban's Hungary. Prime Minister Andrej Babis says he's simply easing a cost on households.
> source: Reuters via NBC News, "Three ships struck by drones in Black Sea near Romanian shipping canal, PM says" (Nov 15); Politico Europe (Nov 15); Reuters via Internazionale, "Tens of thousands of Czechs rally against government media funding plan" (Nov 15)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Tech Against Terrorism on stripped open-weight models
[andrew] Story eight, A I. A U N-backed group, Tech Against Terrorism, is warning that governments have just months to act on A I models stripped of their safety guardrails. The group tested a hundred sixty-two models. It found the major closed models refused to help plan attacks, but versions of open-weight models with their safeguards removed answered every time.
[andrew] It says those guardrails can now be removed within about three days of a model's release, down from more than three weeks in twenty twenty-four, and it counted nearly thirty thousand stripped copies on the A I hub Hugging Face. Executive director Adam Hadley calls it a national security crisis. Hugging Face pushes back: its Yacine Jernite says open models have many legitimate research uses, and that labs should do more to keep terrorist material out of training data. The group wants an independent international testing system that publishes which models pass or fail.
> source: ABC News (Australia), "UN-backed anti-terrorism group calls for urgent policy action to halt spread of 'rogue' AI" (Nov 15)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Microsoft 365 outage phishing
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. Microsoft is warning about phishing emails that use this week's outage as bait. They look like Microsoft 365 service-health notices, with subject lines that start "Outage" followed by a short code, and they even cite the real incident number. The message says your held emails will be deleted within twenty-four hours unless you click to restore them, and the link leads to a page that steals passwords. Email security firm Mimecast reports similar "held messages" lures.
[ava] The fix is simple. Check service status in the Microsoft 365 admin center or with your I T team, and don't click links in unexpected outage emails.
> source: Cyber Security News, "Microsoft Warns of Active Password Stealing Attack Exploiting the Microsoft 365 Service Disruption" (Nov 14); India Today (Nov 14); Mimecast, "Attackers Exploit the Microsoft 365 Outage with Fake 'Held Messages' Lures" (Nov 14); iTnews (Nov 14)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 12: The Filthy Signs couple
[ava] And finally, story twelve. Paul and Ruth Richmond-Watson of Brighton, England, noticed that a lot of road signs near them were too grimy to read at night. So in September, they started scrubbing them clean, at least one a day, with a backpack water pump, eco-friendly foam and a long brush. Each sign takes about twenty minutes.
[ava] They're newlyweds, both autistic with A D H D, and Paul, a former window washer, says they wanted something to do together that helped the neighborhood and their own mental health. They pay for it themselves, with help from a couple of supply-company sponsors. Their before-and-after videos have caught on, and they dream of a road trip to clean signs all over Britain. As Paul says, driving past a sign weeks later and seeing it still sparkle makes you proud.
> source: Good News Network / SWNS, "Couple with Autism and ADHD Find Passion Cleaning Filthy Street Signs" (Nov 14)
