## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Duffy on the Sunday shows: El Dorado router
[ava] Story one. On the Sunday shows, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy laid out the most detailed account yet of Friday's nationwide ground stop. He says contractor L3Harris was replacing an old copper phone line with fiber at a remote F A A site in El Dorado, Arkansas, because the phone company was shutting down the copper. When the new router was switched on, it started flooding other routers in the air traffic control network with data. A system in Amarillo, Texas, that was upgraded about three weeks ago then started doing the same thing.
[ava] Duffy says the F A A has now turned off that broadcasting feature on all sixty-nine routers of that type, which fixed the problem. The two routers are being examined at an L3Harris lab in Florida, with the F B I helping. Duffy says investigators still need to learn whether it was installer error or faulty equipment. He noted that this kind of swap has been done thousands of times without trouble, and that more than seventy percent of the copper lines have already moved to fiber.
[ava] The open question, which ABC's Martha Raddatz pressed him on, is how one remote site could disrupt the whole national network. Duffy says that's exactly what they're investigating, and he promised to share the findings. L3Harris says it's supporting the investigation. Meanwhile, Cirium counted just over thirteen hundred cancellations today, about five percent of flights, and officials expect schedules to be back to normal by tonight.
> source: ABC News, "'This Week' Transcript 11-15-26: Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy" (Nov 15); CBS News, "Ground stop shows 'we have to get off old network,' Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says" (Nov 15); BBC, "Copper wire upgrade behind US flight suspension, transport secretary says" (Nov 15); AP, "What to know about flights after the US nationwide ground stop" (Nov 15)

// Story 2: East Potomac golf course order
[ava] Story two. In Washington, a federal judge has kept East Potomac Park and its public golf course open, at least for now. Judge Ana Reyes issued a temporary order late Saturday, a day before the National Park Service planned to close the area south of Buckeye Drive, including Hains Point and the waterfront paths. The order runs through November twenty-eighth.
[ava] The administration wants to turn the historic public course into a championship venue. The Park Service says it can't safely manage the park after the course operator leaves, pointing to staffing and a damaged seawall. The judge found those reasons were likely a pretext for advancing the redesign without the required environmental review. A preservation group and two local golfers brought the suit, arguing the project could make the course unaffordable. President Trump called it a highly destructive and political decision and says he'll appeal on an expedited basis, though temporary orders like this generally can't be appealed right away.
> source: AP, "Trump blasts judge's 2-week halt to his Washington golf project and says administration will appeal" (Nov 15); WJLA, "Court order blocks East Potomac Park closure as golf course lawsuit continues" (Nov 14); Washington Post, "Trump vows appeal of East Potomac ruling as golf operations remain unclear" (Nov 15)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Gemini + Google Wallet, read-only
[andrew] Story seven, A I. Google now lets U.S. users connect Gemini to Google Wallet. If you link your bank and card accounts through Plaid, you can tag Google Wallet in a chat and ask things like which subscriptions went up in price, or what a holiday budget should look like based on last year's spending.
[andrew] The key difference from OpenAI's new ChatGPT Wallet is that Gemini's access is read-only. It can't move, manage or invest money. It's opt-in, and you can turn it off under Connected Apps. Google also added a Memory Summary page that shows what Gemini remembers about you and lets you edit or delete it. The privacy trade-off is real: your transaction history becomes something the assistant can read. Google is betting people will be more comfortable letting it look than letting it pay.
> source: Google, "Gemini finance insights with Google Wallet integration"; 9to5Google, "You can now set up Gemini's Google Wallet integration" (Nov 13); Plaid; The Next Web

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Italy fines Meta over gallery suggestions
[ava] Story eight, the cyber and privacy desk. Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, has fined Meta eight hundred five million euros, about eight hundred eighty-seven million dollars. The problem is a Facebook for Android feature, around since twenty twenty-three, that suggests photos and videos from your phone's gallery to post.
[ava] Regulators say it was on by default, and it read photos, videos and their metadata from about twenty million Italian users under one broad app permission, without clear, specific consent. Meta has sixty days to switch it off by default and ask separately. Meta says it strongly disagrees, calls the fine disproportionate, and plans to appeal. A practical tip wherever you live: on your phone, check which apps have full access to your photos, and switch them to selected photos only where you can.
> source: Garante per la protezione dei dati personali (Nov 14); The Guardian, "Italy fines Meta $887m for accessing Android users' photos and videos" (Nov 15); The Hacker News (Nov 15); AP (Nov 14); BBC (Nov 15)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 10: Maine school withdrawals; Hampton DoorDash
[ava] Story ten, local news. The Press Herald and Maine Morning Star report that Maine's two-decade-old push to consolidate school districts keeps unraveling. On November third, more than a dozen towns voted on leaving or reorganizing their districts, and only Kingfield succeeded, with over seventy percent voting to leave M S A D 58 and run its own elementary school for about eighty kids. Supporters want local control and to keep their neighborhood schools. Superintendents warn that smaller districts cost more per student and will compete for the same scarce special-education teachers and bus drivers.
[ava] And a Seacoast story with a twist. Hampton police stopped a driver on Route 101 doing more than ninety in a fifty-five zone, and found he had a suspended license and was in the middle of a DoorDash run. So another officer finished the job and delivered the order to the Hampton CVS. The driver got summonses for speeding, improper passing and driving on a suspended license.
> source: Portland Press Herald / Maine Morning Star, "How Maine's two-decade old school consolidation effort continues to unravel" (Nov 15); WMUR, "Hampton police officer completes food delivery after driver pulled over" (Nov 14); Boston Globe (Nov 13)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Brady Brandon's Bow High sendoff
[ava] And finally, story eleven. In Bow, New Hampshire, twenty-two-year-old Brady Brandon walked the halls of Bow High School one last time on Friday, with classmates and teachers lining the way and cheering him on. Brady has Down syndrome. He graduated in twenty twenty-three and was able to keep attending school until he turned twenty-two.
