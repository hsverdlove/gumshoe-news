## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Ebola, Africa CDC warning
[ava] Story three, the world. Six months after it was declared, the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo is still not under control. That's the word today from Jean Kaseya, head of the Africa Centres for Disease Control, who told Reuters that if it isn't stopped now, it could become the worst Ebola outbreak the world has seen.
[ava] Government figures show more than eleven thousand two hundred cases and about fifty-five hundred deaths across eight provinces. Kaseya says more than eighty percent of new cases are turning up among people who weren't on any contact list, and most deaths happen at home rather than in treatment centers. This is the Bundibugyo strain, with no approved vaccine or treatment of its own, and it kills about half the people it infects. Only one point three billion of the two point nine billion dollars pledged by donors has actually been released, and some health workers haven't been paid in months.
[ava] On the hopeful side, the World Health Organization says transmission has slowed in several early hot spots, and trials of two new vaccines should report early next year.
> source: Al Jazeera/Reuters, "Africa CDC says Ebola outbreak in DR Congo 'still not under control'" (Nov 15); AP, "Ebola outbreak remains out of control in Congo 6 months into response"; WHO Regional Office for Africa; ECDC (Nov 13)

Today he prayed the Angelus in the cathedral square in Cusco, high in the Andes, then flew down to Pucallpa in the Amazon, where the heat index was over a hundred degrees. There he met missionaries and about a hundred forty-five Indigenous representatives, some of whom arrived by boat on the Ucayali River.
[ava] His focus is protecting the rainforest from illegal mining and deforestation, and the rights of Indigenous communities, echoing Pope Francis. He heads back to Lima tonight.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Luxor Commander miner hijack
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Attackers took over roughly sixty-four thousand Bitcoin mining machines on Friday night and pointed their computing power, about thirteen exahashes per second, to a mining pool called OCEAN, with any rewards meant for a wallet they controlled.
[ava] Luxor, which makes fleet-management software called Commander, has confirmed it was breached and that mining power from just over a dozen customer accounts was redirected. It says it will pay customers as if their machines had mined normally. The attackers mined no blocks and collected nothing, and Bitcoin itself wasn't compromised. The lesson for anyone running big fleets of machines: the management software is the soft spot, so lock down remote-admin tools and check that critical settings haven't quietly changed.
> source: Crypto Briefing, "Attackers hijack about 64,000 Bitcoin miners and point 13 EH/s at OCEAN" (Nov 15); The Defiant, "Luxor Pledges Full Compensation for Commander Hijack" (Nov 15); TokenPost (Nov 15)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Heart dolls in Porto Alegre
[ava] And finally, story eleven. At a hospital in Porto Alegre, Brazil, children recovering from heart surgery are getting a special gift: a handmade doll with the very same scar they have. Each doll has a little zipper on its chest that opens to show a cloth heart.
[ava] Staff say it helps kids understand what happened to their bodies, and that seeing themselves in the doll makes recovery easier. Seven-year-old Joaquim got a doll he named Estevão, and it helped him get over his fear of his own scar. Hospital volunteers and staff have sewn more than a hundred of them in recent months, an idea that started at the institution's headquarters in Brasília. Sometimes the best medicine is just knowing you're not the only one.
> source: g1 Globo, via News Minimalist, "Hospital in Porto Alegre gives children dolls with matching heart surgery scars" (Nov 15)
