## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Starship ship catch
[andrew] Story seven, tech. For the first time, SpaceX has caught a Starship spacecraft returning from orbit. Saturday at about four twenty Eastern, roughly twenty-two and a half hours after liftoff, the ship flew back to Starbase in South Texas and lowered itself upright between the launch tower's mechanical arms, nicknamed the chopsticks.
[andrew] It wasn't the double catch SpaceX wanted. Friday's Super Heavy booster aborted its own catch, diverted to the Gulf of Mexico and was destroyed, and that cause is still under investigation. But catching the upper stage is the key step toward a fully reusable rocket, which SpaceX needs for cheaper launches and its moon and Mars plans.
> source: AP, "SpaceX's Starship returns from orbit and is captured by launch pad arms in a dramatic first" (Nov 14); Xinhua (Nov 15); SpaceX on X, "Mechazilla catches Starship!" (Nov 14); Space.com (Nov 14)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Cursor breach traced to Fireworks AI key
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. The coding-tool company Cursor has explained how attackers got in. It says an access key it had given A I vendor Fireworks A I was exposed in a breach at Fireworks, the same vendor tied to last week's Uber driver data theft. The attacker used it to copy coding-session records Cursor had shared with Fireworks, some containing passwords and keys that weren't scrubbed. Those led into Cursor's other business tools, and on October twenty-ninth, to a poisoned copy of Anthropic's Claude Code extension slipped into Cursor's extension store for about eleven hours. Up to five thousand users may have received it.
[ava] The malware tried to plant a hidden remote-access backdoor, though coding errors kept that from working on most machines. Anthropic's own systems weren't compromised. Cursor says the incident is contained, with no attacker activity since November first. If you installed Claude Code versions two point one point three one two or three one three through Cursor, run its cleanup script; uninstalling alone may not be enough.
> source: Cursor, "An update on our recent security incident" (Nov 13); The Hacker News, "Cursor Says Stolen Fireworks AI Key Led to Trojanized Claude Code Extension Incident" (Nov 14); BleepingComputer, "Cursor confirms security breach led to malicious Claude Code extension" (Nov 14); Microsoft Security Blog (Oct 30)
