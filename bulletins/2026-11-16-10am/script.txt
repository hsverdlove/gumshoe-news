// Story 2: NYC disbands SRG
[ava] Story two. In New York City, Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch have announced they are disbanding the NYPD's Strategic Response Group, the unit that handled both counterterrorism and protests. Starting in January, counterterrorism moves to the department's intelligence and counterterrorism bureau. Protests will be handled by a thirty-officer dialogue team, a new patrol unit trained in crowd control that can still make arrests, and a civilian de-escalation office with no weapons or arrest powers. Kettling and tear gas will be banned for crowd control.
[ava] Supporters say the unit was too heavy-handed at protests, and the mayor says protesters shouldn't be treated like a terror threat. Critics, including some police supporters and conservative commentators, say the change will leave the city less prepared if protests turn violent.
> source: NYC Mayor's Office, "Mayor Mamdani and Police Commissioner Tisch Disband NYPD's Strategic Response Group" (Nov 16); CBS New York (Nov 16); NBC New York (Nov 16); New York Post (Nov 16)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Microsoft outage root cause
[andrew] Story six, tech. Microsoft has explained why Outlook and Exchange Online went dark for much of last Thursday. In a preliminary report, it says a software change from July sat quietly on its network gateway servers for months. It had passed load testing, but Microsoft admits that testing focused on older machines with fewer cores, and the bug was worse on newer hardware.
[andrew] Last week a routine failover of Teams traffic hit at the same moment about fourteen percent of gateway capacity was offline. That set off the bug, and as traffic shifted from one failing server to the next, more than eighty percent of the servers stopped responding. Because the change was months old, a simple rollback wasn't possible, so engineers had to write a fix for the fix. A final report is due Friday.
> source: The Register, "Microsoft blames monster email outage on software defect introduced in July fix" (Nov 16); Microsoft preliminary post-incident review (Nov 15)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Gödel notebooks
[andrew] Story seven, AI. Kurt Gödel, the logician whose incompleteness theorems changed mathematics, left behind more than a thousand pages of notebooks from the early nineteen-forties, much of it in an old German shorthand. Helsinki logician Jan von Plato had been working through it for years, at about a page a week. In September he gave his transcriptions to Anthropic's Claude.
[andrew] Within days, Claude produced a six-hundred-fifty-page explanation and listed six hundred sixty-three theorems Gödel had proved or sketched. A proof-checking program confirmed about four hundred as written, about two hundred needed fixes, and about fifty were wrong. Von Plato says Gödel came within one step of a famous result that won someone else the top prize in math in the nineteen-sixties. Notre Dame logician Joel David Hamkins adds a caution: being on the path isn't the same as having the proof, and historians will have to work out how much is Claude and how much is Gödel.
> source: The Guardian, "'Ahead of his time': AI helps unlock discoveries hidden in notebooks of man who transformed maths" (Nov 13)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Google GTIG AI threat report
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Google's threat intelligence team says that for the first time it has caught attackers using AI to find and exploit software flaws with no human involved. On an exposed server, Google found a set of AI agents that reads new software updates, works out what flaw each one fixed, writes an exploit and fires it at websites that haven't patched yet. It was used against media and education sites in France. Google also says suspected North Korean hackers tampered with Anthropic's coding tool, Claude Code, on victims' machines, so it would install a backdoor at cryptocurrency firms.
[ava] Google expects simple, mostly automated attacks within three months and sophisticated fully autonomous ones within six. The lesson for anyone running a website: the gap between a patch coming out and an attack has shrunk to hours, so patch fast.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Google warns that fully-automated AI attacks are coming soon" (Nov 16); The Hacker News (Nov 16); The Register (Nov 16)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Cats meow at us on purpose
[ava] And finally, story eleven, for every cat owner who suspects they're being bossed around. Researchers at the University of São Paulo put thirty-eight cats' favorite treats in a clear box the cats couldn't open, then watched. With the owner in the room, cats were about sixteen times more likely to meow. More than half of those meows came while the cat was looking right at its human, and many cats glanced back and forth between the person and the box.
[ava] Left alone, the cats mostly stood still and stared at the door. The scientists say it suggests meows really are aimed at us, though the study was small and only used chatty cats. So when your cat meows at you, it probably wants you to do something about it.
> source: Phys.org, "Talking or just meowing? Food test suggests cats call for human attention" (Nov 12); Fukimoto et al., Animal Behaviour (2026)
