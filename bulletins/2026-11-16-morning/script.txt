## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Duffy, no apology; FAA funding fight
[ava] Story one. Air travel should be close to normal this morning after Friday's nationwide ground stop, and the argument has shifted to who's responsible. On NBC's Meet the Press, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy declined to apologize. He said it happened on his watch, but blamed thirty years of underinvestment by administrations of both parties, and said he can't apologize for a system that was handed to him.
[ava] Duffy says investigators still don't know whether the root cause was a faulty installation by contractor L3Harris or a fault in the router itself, and he wouldn't guarantee it can't happen again. He's asking Congress for the rest of the money to modernize air traffic control, including a new telecom backbone. The twelve and a half billion dollars Congress approved last year, he says, didn't cover telecom or software.
[ava] Democrats on the House Appropriations Committee push back. They say the White House budget office pushed about five point three billion dollars in already approved FAA modernization money out to October twenty twenty-seven. The administration strongly disputes that this is a freeze.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Hormuz claims; third US carrier
[ava] Story three, the world. Iran's Revolutionary Guard says two more tankers struck naval mines in the southern corridor of the Strait of Hormuz, and claims one of them, a supertanker carrying two million barrels of crude, sank. Iran hasn't named the ships, and nobody has independently verified it. Seatrade Maritime notes that Iran's video of the supposed sinking shows a large fire on the water, but no ship you can actually make out.

In both cases the crews are safe. Iran says only its own northern route is allowed, and it's going after ships that take the southern corridor, where US forces have been guiding vessels through.
[ava] Meanwhile the carrier Theodore Roosevelt has arrived in the region, giving the US three carriers there. Iran's parliament speaker, Mohammad Ghalibaf, says Tehran won't live in a state of neither war nor peace, and is ready for either talks or war.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Vulcan failure, Amazon Leo
[andrew] Story six, tech. United Launch Alliance, the Boeing and Lockheed Martin joint venture, lost a Vulcan rocket Sunday along with forty Amazon Leo internet satellites. ULA says the rocket suffered an anomaly about ten minutes into flight. It's the company's first outright mission failure since it was formed in two thousand six.
[andrew] The cause isn't known yet. ULA's own update pointed to a problem during the upper stage burn, but sources told Ars Technica the trouble started in the first stage, where the two Blue Origin engines appeared to fire at least thirty seconds longer than planned before the rocket began to spin.
[andrew] The bigger hit is to the schedule. This was the first of thirty-eight Vulcan launches Amazon bought for its Starlink competitor, and Vulcan will likely be grounded during the investigation. Amazon's other big ride, Blue Origin's New Glenn, hasn't flown since a launch pad explosion in May.
> source: Spaceflight Now, "ULA suffers first outright failure with loss of Vulcan rocket carrying 40 Amazon Leo satellites" (Nov 15); Ars Technica, "Vulcan rocket fails during launch, dealing major blow to SpaceX competitors" (Nov 15); Reuters (Nov 15); SpaceNews (Nov 15)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: ChatGPT Wallet
[andrew] Story seven, AI. OpenAI has launched ChatGPT Wallet, which lets its agent shop for you and actually pay. You add a Visa, Mastercard or American Express card once. When the agent finds something, you review the items and the total and approve it with Face ID or a fingerprint. The merchant gets a one-time token, so neither the agent nor the store sees your real card number. It's starting with US subscribers to the top-tier Pro plan, and every purchase still needs your approval. OpenAI says preset spending limits will come later.
[andrew] Not everyone is ready for shopping bots. Some airlines, United among them, have been blocking AI agents they haven't authorized.
> source: OpenAI, "Introducing ChatGPT Wallet"; The Verge, "ChatGPT Wallet lets OpenAI's Dots agents make purchases for you"; The Neuron, "Everything That Happened in AI Today" (Nov 16)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Maileva breach; SANSAR
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. In France, La Poste says its subsidiary Maileva, which prints and mails letters for businesses, was hacked in mid-September. The names and mailing addresses of twenty-two million people were taken. These are people who got pay slips, bills or hospital payment reminders through the service, and most have never heard of Maileva. Bank details weren't exposed, but some letter contents were read, including invoice amounts and treatment dates from one hospital's billing contractor. French experts warn about convincing fake letters, and the advice works anywhere: if a bill looks odd, contact the sender through a number you already trust.
[ava] And Elastic Security Labs describes Linux malware called SANSAR that secretly mines cryptocurrency on Nvidia graphics cards while doctoring Nvidia's monitoring tool so the card reports zero percent use. A hidden kernel module covers its tracks. If you run GPU servers, don't trust the usage meter alone. Watch your power draw and temperatures too.
> source: Security Affairs, "Data Breach at La Poste Subsidiary Maileva Affects 22 Million People in France"; franceinfo (Nov 14-15); France 24/AFP (Nov 14); Elastic Security Labs, "0% GPU utilization: How SANSAR hides Linux cryptomining"; The Hacker News (Nov 2026)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 10: Stokes Road bridge; events
[ava] Story ten, local news. In Londonderry, the Town Council has put off a decision on two bridges on the state's Red List. In July it voted to shift about a million dollars meant for the Stokes Road Bridge to the busier Hall Road Bridge, which carries roughly two thousand vehicles a day. Public Works has since suggested removing the Stokes Road Bridge entirely and building a cul-de-sac. Residents objected, and fire officials say emergency access would only work if the road were widened to twenty-four feet, which neighbors don't want. The council will wait for a state study due in December, invite the transportation commissioner in January, and hold a public meeting in February or March.
[ava] Around Manchester this week: tonight at six-thirty, Stage of Fools does a free, ninety-minute, beer-in-hand cut of Shakespeare's "Henry the Fourth" at Stark Brewing on North Commercial Street. And Friday through Sunday, the Manchester Holiday Food and Artisan Market runs from ten to five.
> source: Derry News, "Bridge repairs spark debate" (Nov 12); Visit Manchester NH, "Henry IV, One Night Only"; Eventbrite, "2026 Manchester Holiday Food & Artisan Market"
