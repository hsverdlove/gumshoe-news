## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Hung Cao Navy hearing
[ava] Story one. Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao is before the Senate Armed Services Committee this morning, seeking the job permanently. He's a combat veteran and a close Trump ally. He needs a waiver from Congress because he left active duty less than seven years ago.
[ava] Expect questions about the Iran war and about transparency. The Associated Press reports the Navy has recently removed several senior officers without the usual public announcement, including the entire leadership team of an attack submarine after an underwater collision. Democrats say Congress wasn't told. Supporters of Cao argue a wartime Navy needs continuity at the top and that some personnel matters are sensitive. The hearing comes during the lame-duck session, while Republicans still control the Senate.
> source: AP via PBS News, "Senate Armed Services Committee confirmation hearing for Hung Cao as Navy secretary" (Nov 17); AP, "Trump's pick for Navy secretary likely to face Iran war questions" (Nov 17); InsideDefense (Nov 17)

// Story 2: Mullin says outages not an attack
[ava] Story two. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin went on CNBC this morning to address the string of outages over the past week. That includes Microsoft's email trouble, Friday's FAA ground stop and Monday's bank delays. His message was blunt. We are not under attack, he said, and no cyber threat has taken place.
[ava] The official explanations so far are aging FAA telecom equipment and a software bug inside JPMorgan Chase. Some security experts agree the incidents look unrelated. Others say the cluster still shows how fragile critical systems are, whatever the cause.
> source: CNBC Squawk Box, "We are not under attack: DHS Secretary Mullin on recent outages" (Nov 17); Rapid Response 47 on X (Nov 17); Fox News, Morgan Wright interview (Nov 17)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Iran drones at Bahrain; dirty bomb denial
[ava] Story three. Iran's Revolutionary Guard says it launched drones at a US radar system at Isa Air Base in Bahrain this morning. Residents of Manama got a phone alert telling them to take shelter, and explosions were heard. Bahrain says its air defenses intercepted the drones. US Central Command says there was no damage to US equipment and no one was hurt.
[ava] It's Iran's third such attempt on US targets in Bahrain since Friday, after a two-month lull. Iran says it is responding to US violations of its airspace. Separately, Iran's Foreign Ministry called last night's CNN report about a possible dirty bomb plot in Washington baseless.
> source: Al Jazeera / AFP, "Bahrain thwarts drone attack; Iran claims targeting US radar" (Nov 17); AP, "A 2-month lull ends with Iran's third strike on US targets in Bahrain" (Nov 17); Anadolu (Nov 17)

// Story 4: Crimean bridge update
[ava] Story four. An update on the Crimean bridge. Russia's transport ministry now says both road and rail traffic were suspended after a second attack overnight, though Russian officials later said trains were running again on one track. Ukrainian monitors and Russian military bloggers both describe a collapsed road arch. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov, who called Monday's damage minor, now says the bridge will be rebuilt even better than before. Deputy Prime Minister Marat Khusnullin is leading the repair effort and arranging other supply routes into Crimea.
> source: Kyiv Post, "Kremlin Says Crimean Bridge Will Be 'Better Than Before'" (Nov 17); Kremlin.ru, "The President received an update on the Crimean Bridge situation" (Nov 17); RIA Novosti Crimea (Nov 17); AP (Nov 17)

// Story 5: Turkey seizes Sozcu TV
[ava] Story five. In Turkey, police raided the Istanbul headquarters of the opposition-leaning channel Sozcu TV before dawn. They detained executives, including the board chair, and the government's deposit insurance fund was named trustee of the channel and its website. The channel went off the air briefly and is now running under the trustee.
[ava] Prosecutors cite alleged links between the owner and the Gulen movement, which Turkey calls a terrorist group, plus terror-financing violations. The channel's lawyer says the case is based on a file where Turkey's top court found no crime. Opposition leader Ozgur Ozel called it an effort to silence criticism. It comes a month after the same thing happened to Halk TV.
> source: Euronews / AFP, "Police raid Turkish pro-opposition broadcaster Sozcu TV, carry out arrests" (Nov 17); Reuters via StreetInsider (Nov 17); Hurriyet Daily News (Nov 17); Medyascope (Nov 17)

## Markets | sting=markets
// Story 6: Markets at the open
[andrew] Story six, markets. Stocks opened mixed. About twenty minutes in, the Dow was down about half a percent, near fifty-one thousand three hundred fifty. The S&P five hundred was off a couple tenths and the Nasdaq was roughly flat.
[andrew] The October retail sales report was soft but in line. Sales rose two tenths of a percent, down from eight tenths in September, and the Census Bureau says that gain is within its margin of error. Car dealers fell more than one and a half percent. But the so-called core measure, which feeds into GDP, rose six tenths, better than expected. With prices rising because of the Iran war, markets still see a December Fed rate hike as nearly certain.
[andrew] Some movers. Intel is sliding after Nvidia disclosed it sold about a quarter of its Intel stake last quarter, roughly five and a half billion dollars of stock. Optical networking names like Coherent jumped early after Reuters reported the administration will push ahead with blocking imports of Chinese data center equipment. And Deutsche Bank fell more than two percent after whistleblowers leaked about fifteen million internal files. Reports based on the leak say the bank flagged about five hundred eighty million dollars in suspicious Jeffrey Epstein-linked payments, far more than was known. The bank says it cut ties with Epstein in late twenty eighteen and has acknowledged it was wrong to take him on.
[andrew] Anthropic kept its IPO range at seventy to seventy-eight dollars in an updated filing this morning. It prices tomorrow and trades Thursday.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Fugaku breach; Check Point gateways
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. Japan's Fugaku supercomputer, once the fastest in the world, has been shut down since Saturday after a breach. Its operator, RIKEN, says an intruder got into the internal network from November tenth to the thirteenth through a public web page that showed the machine's status. The attacker tampered with servers and installed malicious programs. Names and emails of up to about forty-six hundred users may have been taken, along with SSH keys and other credentials users had stored for outside systems. RIKEN says the attacker has already tried some of those credentials elsewhere, but it has no evidence yet that research data left the building.
[ava] And if you run Check Point firewalls, patch now. Check Point says attackers chained a PHP flaw with a Gaia operating system bug to plant backdoors on unpatched gateways, and in some Israeli companies they deployed data-wiping malware. Researchers see overlap with a China-linked group but say the evidence is mixed.
> source: RIKEN, "Notice regarding a possible personal information leak affecting Fugaku and RIKYU users" (Nov 16); The Japan Times (Nov 17); Asahi Shimbun (Nov 16); The Register (Nov 17); Cybersecurity Dive, "PHP2Shell flaw exploited in chained attacks targeting unpatched Check Point gateways" (Nov 16); CISA KEV catalog (Nov 13)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 12: The Bench People
[ava] And finally, story twelve. Maren and David Douglass are teachers in Florida. While David was being treated for cancer, their doorbell camera kept showing a middle schooler waiting for the bus at the end of their driveway. There's no sidewalk on that busy road, and she was sitting on the ground close to traffic.
[ava] So they put out a bench. The camera caught her little jumps of joy the first morning. Then a handwritten thank you note showed up, and they wrote back. When they learned she loved to read, they added a small free library, and other neighborhood kids started borrowing books too. The couple has now turned the story into a children's book, signed simply, The Bench People.
> source: Upworthy (Nov 16) via Good News of the Day, "Teachers build safe bus stop for waiting student" (Nov 17)
