## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: FAA router pause; Apple outage; no evidence of attack
[ava] Story one. Two new developments in the week of outages. First, the FAA has paused installing new Juniper routers on its telecommunications network while it works out what caused Friday's nationwide ground stop. The agency says a new router at a radio site in El Dorado Arkansas and another in Amarillo Texas flooded the network with about a thousand connection attempts every four to five seconds. It has switched off that function on more than sixty similar routers. Thirteen Democratic senators had asked for exactly this pause. Hewlett Packard Enterprise, which owns Juniper, says its device worked as the installer configured it and did not fail.
[ava] Second, Apple. For much of the day iCloud sign-in, the App Store, Apple Music and Apple Pay had trouble. Apple blamed an internal update, ruled out a cyberattack and says most services are back. Gmail users also reported problems, but Google says it sees no sign of a widespread outage.
[ava] Some voices online are calling all this a coordinated attack. No evidence has been made public to support that. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin has floated the idea that AI-written code could be causing a ripple effect, while Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and the FAA say AI played no role in Friday's failure.
> source: AP, "FAA temporarily stops router installations connected to air travel stoppage" (Nov 17); FAA, "Nationwide Ground Stop: Questions and Answers"; Newsweek and Axios (Nov 17); Yahoo Finance, "Apple outage today" (Nov 17); AP, "Apple joins a parade of technology hiccups" (Nov 17); Anadolu, "Google says no evidence of widespread Gmail outage" (Nov 17); Fox News live updates (Nov 17)

// Story 2: Pentagon fails ninth audit
[ava] Story two. The Pentagon has failed its financial audit for the ninth year in a row. Auditors looked at about four point nine trillion dollars in assets and five point two trillion in liabilities. The department did win one more clean opinion, for a total of twelve, and says that by early next year more than half its assets will have clean books. Comptroller Jules Hurst says a new strategy will get there, cutting twenty-five separate financial statements to eight and bringing in outside auditors, with a goal of a clean department-wide opinion in twenty twenty-eight. Officials say systems built over decades take years to fix. Critics note this is the ninth straight failure since the first full audit in twenty eighteen.
> source: Breaking Defense, "Pentagon fails 9th audit, asserts path towards clean books in 2028" (Nov 16); The Guardian (Nov 17); The Hill (Nov 17); US Department of War release (Nov 16)

// Story 4: Board of Peace Gaza report
[ava] Story four. The body overseeing the US-brokered ceasefire in Gaza gave the UN Security Council a bleak first annual report. The Board of Peace warns the truce could collapse without immediate action. It points to continued Israeli strikes, blocked aid and Israel not letting Gaza's interim administration operate, and also to Hamas military activity. It says if the ceasefire fails there is no road map to return to, and no basis for rebuilding or an international stabilization force. Hamas rejected the criticism and accused the board of failing to stop Israeli violations. Israel has said Hamas must disarm before reconstruction moves ahead.
> source: AP, "Board of Peace delivers bleak report on Gaza, warning of ceasefire collapse without immediate action" (Nov 17); Ilke News Agency, Hamas statement (Nov 17); The New Humanitarian (Nov 17)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Equinor; ARTEX; Java4Shell details
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Norway's Equinor, Europe's largest natural gas supplier, says it is fighting an ongoing cyberattack on some of its IT systems. It says there's no sign data was stolen, oil and gas production is not affected, and authorities have been told. No group has claimed it.
[ava] The FBI is warning about ARTEX, an open-source AI hacking framework that plugs into commercial AI models and automates break-ins, sometimes going from first probe to admin access in under three hours. An FBI official told ABC News ARTEX has no known connection to this week's US outages.
[ava] And details of the Java4Shell flaw are out. It's a memory bug in Java's Base64 decoding on newer Intel and AMD server chips, affecting Java seventeen and later. Patch, or ask your vendor about the workaround that turns that feature off.
> source: Equinor, "Equinor detected cyber-attack" (Nov 17); Reuters via TradingView (Nov 17); FBI IC3 FLASH, "Threat Overview of ARTEX Autonomous Penetration Testing Framework"; BleepingComputer, "FBI warns ARTEX AI hacking tool is powering global cyberattacks" (Nov 17); ABC News (Nov 17); Upwind, "Java4Shell: What We Know Now" (Nov 17); The Hacker News (Nov 17)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Baby Ellis stem cell appeal
[ava] And finally, story eleven. In Devon England, eleven-month-old Ellis has a rare and life-threatening blood disorder called Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome. His only cure is a stem cell transplant, and there was no match on the donor register.
[ava] So the charity DKMS put out an appeal, and the response was the biggest it has seen in years. Nearly ten thousand people asked for a cheek swab kit in a single day. A match for Ellis isn't guaranteed, but his dad says if all those new donors end up helping somebody else, that's just as important. Signing up takes about six minutes.
