## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Peltola wins Alaska; House Democratic leadership vote
[ava] Story one. The last Senate race of the midterms is settled. The Associated Press called Alaska for Democrat Mary Peltola late last night. She unseated two-term Republican Senator Dan Sullivan with more than half the vote. That gives Democrats fifty-six seats in the new Senate.
[ava] The race had its controversy. A second candidate named Dan Sullivan was on the ballot, and the New York Times reported that operatives aligned with Peltola's side helped recruit him. Peltola denies any connection, and so does the second Sullivan. President Trump argued the two Sullivans' votes should be combined, but by the time of the call Peltola had more votes than both of them together.
[ava] Meanwhile, House Democrats meet privately today to choose their leaders for the new Congress. Hakeem Jeffries is running unopposed to be their nominee for speaker, though some activists want a fresh start.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Poland false-flag plot
[ava] Story three. Poland says it headed off what a leading Polish newspaper describes as a Russian plot to stage a deadly attack near the border with Russia's Kaliningrad exclave and blame it on Ukraine. Rzeczpospolita, citing senior officials, reports the plan involved crossing the border and attacking a facility, possibly on the Russian side, with heavy casualties expected. One source compared it to the Gleiwitz incident, the staged attack Nazi Germany used as a pretext to invade Poland in nineteen thirty-nine. The goal, the source said, was to cut off aid to Ukraine.
[ava] Poland sent riot police and a counterterrorism unit to the border. Security services coordinator Tomasz Siemoniak confirmed the threat was real but would not discuss details. Russia hasn't commented, and the specifics of the plot have not been independently confirmed.
> source: Kyiv Independent, citing Rzeczpospolita, "Russia planned high casualty false-flag attack near Kaliningrad border" (Nov 18); Meduza (Nov 18); Anadolu (Nov 18)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Waymo pulls off all US freeways
[andrew] Story seven, tech. Waymo has temporarily pulled its driverless taxis off freeways in every US city where it offered highway rides, including Los Angeles and Miami. It follows California's ban on Waymo freeway driving, which the state DMV imposed last week, citing an imminent hazard after the company's cars repeatedly entered active construction zones. In one case several Waymos drove into a work zone on Route 101 south of San Francisco. No one was hurt. Waymo says it's evaluating its performance and working with regulators to resume. It says its own data show far fewer serious-injury crashes than human drivers. City street service continues.
> source: BBC, "Waymo pulls driverless taxis from all US freeways" (Nov 17-18); AP, "Waymo suspends all driverless taxi rides on freeways after ban in California" (Nov 18); WIRED (Nov 18)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: GAO jailbreak test
[andrew] Story eight, AI, with a New Hampshire connection. At Senator Maggie Hassan's request, the Government Accountability Office went undercover to test Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Grok. Using well-known tricks to get around safeguards, investigators got all four to produce workable attack plans within twelve hours. Two outlined homemade bomb attacks in major cities, with targets and timing. The other two laid out plans to attack power substations. GAO didn't say which model produced which result.
[andrew] The companies say the testing was done last spring on older versions, and they've strengthened safeguards since. OpenAI said it takes the findings seriously. Hassan, who will chair the Senate Homeland Security Committee in January, plans hearings and wants safeguards that can't be bypassed before models are released.
> source: ABC News, "AI models used to plan 'viable' attack plots in test despite safeguards" (Nov 18); NBC News (Nov 18); AP (Nov 18); GAO (Nov 18)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: Hello Again TPM attack; EggStreme
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. If you use a short PIN for Windows Hello or BitLocker, researchers in Berlin say it's time to make it longer. They found a way to reset the lockout counter that normally stops guessing, on AMD-based PCs and some Intel ones that use Winbond flash chips. It requires opening the case, so it's mainly a risk for stolen laptops. In the lab a six-digit BitLocker PIN that should take nineteen years to guess fell in worst case twenty days. AMD plans firmware fixes between now and February. The advice is to add at least two digits to your PIN.
[ava] Also, Bitdefender says a suspected Chinese espionage group has rebuilt its EggStreme malware after it was exposed last year, and is using it against Philippine government agencies. Some new versions sit quietly and wait for the attackers to connect, which makes them harder to spot.
> source: The Hacker News, "New Attack Bypasses TPM Lockout to Crack Windows Hello and BitLocker PINs in Days, Not Years" (Nov 18); AMD security bulletin AMD-SB-7079 (Nov 17); The Hacker News and Infosecurity Magazine on EggStreme (Nov 18)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 12: Antarctic blue whales recover
[ava] And finally, story twelve. The largest animals that have ever lived are making a comeback. A University of Washington study led by Zoe Rand estimates Antarctic blue whales have grown from about four hundred eighteen animals in nineteen seventy-three, when illegal whaling finally stopped, to more than four thousand today. That's about a tenfold increase, roughly four and a half percent a year, for a species that has just one calf about every three years. Blue whales are still critically endangered and well below their pre-whaling numbers, but the trend is pointing the right way.
> source: Good News Network, "This Blue Whale Population Grew 1,000% Since the End of Antarctic Whaling" (Nov 18), citing University of Washington research
