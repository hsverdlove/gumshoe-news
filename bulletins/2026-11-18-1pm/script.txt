## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Worldwide threats hearing outcome
[ava] Story one. The country's top security officials told Congress this morning that the string of recent technology outages was not a cyberattack. At the House Homeland Security Committee's worldwide threats hearing, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said the problems at the FAA, airlines, banks, Microsoft and the NSA were separate outages tied to maintenance and technical changes, not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack. FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia said there is no indication whatsoever of malicious cyber activity, though the FBI will keep working with private companies out of caution.
[ava] Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin called the government's response to last week's nationwide ground stop a success, saying more than four thousand planes landed without a single incident. He also defended flying to the LSU and Texas football game afterward. He said the trip with his nieces was planned weeks in advance, that canceling once the airspace reopened could have signaled a lack of confidence, and that he paid for the flight himself. Democrats were not satisfied, and Ranking Member Bennie Thompson pressed him to answer document requests ahead of investigations planned for January.
[ava] Clayton also said Iranian plotting inside and outside the US has dramatically increased, while Raia cautioned that much of that reporting is aspirational.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Allies rebuke Colby on deep strikes; Xi-Putin talks
[ava] Story three, the world. A rare public split has opened between Washington and its European allies over Ukraine's long-range strikes inside Russia. At NATO headquarters, Pentagon policy chief Elbridge Colby said those deep strikes have not helped end the war or slow Russia's advance, and may have hardened Russian resolve. He did praise Ukraine's shorter-range drone campaign. German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius called the criticism, in his words, quite a stupid thing, and said the focus should be on Russia's nightly attacks on Ukraine's power, water and heating. Britain's defense secretary Wes Streeting said it is not for allies to tell Ukraine how to fight.
[ava] In Shenzhen, Xi Jinping met Vladimir Putin and, according to Chinese state media, called for closer ties between their governments, legislatures and militaries. No new military agreement was announced. The three-way meeting with President Trump is set for tomorrow and is expected to last about an hour.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: OpenAI chip design with AMD
[andrew] Story seven, AI. OpenAI says its models are now helping design the chips that run AI. In a report today, the company said it worked with AMD on AMD's flagship graphics processor design. Its model rewrote the circuit-level code and found up to fourteen percent gains in power, performance and area over designs that were already heavily optimized. OpenAI calls it roughly a year of progress in under five months. AMD has put some of the changes into a design it recently sent for manufacturing. It's also teaming up with chip software maker Synopsys on a specialized model. These are OpenAI's own figures, and independent experts have not yet reviewed them.
> source: OpenAI, "Advancing chip design with OpenAI models" (Nov 18); Unite.AI (Nov 18)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Apache Roller; TheMailer
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Apache is warning that attackers are actively exploiting a critical flaw in Apache Roller, an open-source Java blogging server. The bug lets an attacker run code on the server with no login at all, through its XML-RPC interface. It affects versions four point zero through six point one point four. The fix is to upgrade to six point one point six. Apache warns that just turning off XML-RPC in the admin screen does not stop the attack.
[ava] Also, researchers at Varonis have detailed a phishing kit called TheMailer. Criminals run it on their own servers and connect it to commercial AI models, including Claude, ChatGPT, Grok and Gemini. It writes personalized lures, strips out telltale AI phrasing, and tests delivery. If a message lands in spam, it keeps rewriting it until it reaches the inbox. The takeaway: polished grammar is no longer a sign an email is safe.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Apache warns of Roller RCE vulnerability exploited in attacks" (Nov 18); The Hacker News (Nov 18); oss-security, CVE-2026-129448 advisory (Nov 17); Varonis, "Meet TheMailer: An AI Phishing Kit" (Nov 2026); Dark Reading (Nov 18)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Sky ladder
[ava] And finally, story eleven. In China's Zhejiang province, an eighty-six-year-old mother named Chen never liked city life after her children moved her off the mountain where she was born. The old path home had become overgrown and full of snakes. So her children decided it was their turn. Led by her daughter Huang Zhulian, the family hauled stones and mixed cement from August to October, even using a heavy-lift drone, and built a four-hundred-thirty-step staircase up the mountain, parts of it fastened to a sheer cliff. Hikers and herb gatherers use it now too. As Huang put it, when they were young their mother held their hands going down the mountain, and now it's their turn to hold hers and take her home.
> source: Good News Network, "Siblings Build 430-Step 'Sky Ladder' for Their Mother Who Refuses to Live in the City" (Nov 18), citing Chao News and the South China Morning Post
