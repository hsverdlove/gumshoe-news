## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: BAAJA permitting bill final cloture vote
[ava] Story one. The biggest rewrite of federal permitting rules in a generation faces its last major hurdle in the Senate today. The Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act would speed approvals for power lines and pipelines and make data centers pay for the grid upgrades they need. Monday the Senate voted seventy to twenty-seven to cut off debate on the revised bill. A final sixty-vote test is expected starting around midday, and Senator Jeff Merkley of Oregon is expected to raise an objection first.
[ava] The fight is over one section that makes a finished permit very hard to revoke. Supporters, including Democratic coauthors Martin Heinrich and Sheldon Whitehouse, say that protects projects like offshore wind from being cancelled midstream, and that Democrats should take the win now. Critics like Earthjustice warn it would lock in pipeline and export terminal approvals that no future president could undo. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries wants changes. Sponsors are racing to finish before Democrats take control of Congress in January.
> source: Hill Heat, "Final Cloture Vote on BAAJA" (Nov 18); Inside Climate News, "The New Rules of Permitting" (Nov 17); The Hill, "Senate clears hurdle for bipartisan permitting reform package" (Nov 16); Bloomberg Government (Nov 16)

// Story 2: Rubio dismisses dirty bomb report
[ava] Story two. Yesterday morning we told you about a CNN report, citing seven US officials, that Iran had been exploring plans for a dirty bomb or chemical attack on senior officials in Washington. Now Secretary of State Marco Rubio says he does not believe there is any imminent threat. He called some of the sources behind such reports liars, and said others may be breaking the law by leaking. The White House says it is monitoring all potential threats but sees no credible threat to the homeland. Iran's foreign minister called the reports rubbish and war propaganda. CNN's report itself said Iran appeared to be exploring options, not preparing an imminent attack.

[ava] Story five. The European Commission has proposed a new forty-five billion euro loan to keep Ukraine running through twenty twenty-seven. ## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 7: Google Quaise superhot geothermal
[andrew] Story seven, tech. Google has signed what it calls the world's first commercial deal for superhot rock geothermal power. It will buy fifty megawatts from Quaise Energy's Project Obsidian on the flank of Newberry Volcano in central Oregon. That's enough for about fifty thousand homes, and it will feed Google's data centers in The Dalles starting as early as twenty thirty. The idea is to drill miles down to rock near eight hundred degrees Fahrenheit and use it to make steam around the clock. The price wasn't disclosed, and Google admits it costs more for now. Quaise still has to build a ten-mile power line, in a region that's struggled to build transmission.
> source: OPB, "Google announces world's first deal to buy commercial superhot rock geothermal energy from Central Oregon" (Nov 17); Quaise Energy announcement (Nov 17); Canary Media (Nov 17)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 12: Voyager 1 one light-day
[ava] And finally, story twelve. At sixteen minutes after five this morning our time, Voyager 1 became the first human-made object to reach one light-day from Earth. That's about sixteen billion miles. A radio signal from the spacecraft now takes a full twenty-four hours to reach us. Voyager launched in nineteen seventy-seven. It took forty-nine years to get this far, and it's still talking to us.
[ava] Last night NASA invited people around the world to flash V G R, for Voyager, in Morse code at the sky. And tonight at eight the Planetary Society hosts a free livestream with the mission team and Bill Nye. As Nye put it, most people alive today weren't born when Voyager launched, but it changed how all of us see our pale blue dot.
> source: EarthSky, "Voyager 1 reaches 1 light-day from Earth!" (Nov 18); NASA Science, "Voyager 1: What Is a Light-Day" (Nov 2026)
