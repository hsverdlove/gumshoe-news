## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Trump heads home with Asia deals
[ava] Story one. President Trump is flying home from Asia, and the White House is touting more than seventeen billion dollars in deals for American companies. The biggest is a twelve billion dollar sale of twenty Boeing 787 jets with GE engines to Philippine Airlines. Thailand may buy American HIMARS rocket launchers, and Malaysia gets US help building a government AI cloud. A proposed trade deal with Thailand would end tariffs on almost all American goods once it's signed. The White House calls the trip historic. Critics say this morning's three-way summit with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin delivered more symbolism than substance, and some Republican senators are wary of it too.

// Story 2: Permitting bill stalls over data center amendment
[ava] Story two. In the Senate, the big bipartisan permitting bill is stuck on its last lap. It cleared its final procedural hurdle yesterday, sixty-five to thirty-one, and supporters hoped to pass it today. But Oregon Democrats Ron Wyden and Jeff Merkley are holding up the deal on amendment votes. Wyden says Republicans are blocking his amendment to ban data centers on public lands, after reports the administration was considering building them there. Republican aides say Wyden opposes the bill and is just trying to slow it down. The bill would speed approvals for power lines, pipelines and other projects by limiting environmental reviews. Backers say it lowers energy costs and helps clean power get built. Opponents warn of more fossil fuel projects and weaker protections, including for tribal input on historic sites.
> source: The Hill, "Data center fight snarls amendment votes on energy bill" (Nov 19); Politico Live, "Senate standoff threatens permitting deal's bipartisan support" (Nov 19); US Senate Daily Press (Nov 18-19)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Daytime drone attack on Kyiv; Dutch embassy damaged
[ava] Story three, the world. Hours after Vladimir Putin promised a painful response to Ukraine's strikes on the Crimean Bridge, Russian drones hit Kyiv in broad daylight, with nearly a dozen air raid alerts since morning. Mayor Vitali Klitschko says a twenty-six-year-old man was killed and five people were hurt. A drone struck right behind the Dutch embassy in the Podil district. Dutch Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen says the building has minor damage and the staff are safe. It isn't known whether the embassy was a target. Debris also shattered windows at Saint Volodymyr's Cathedral, a landmark more than a century old, and fell on the grounds of Ukraine's Foreign Ministry. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha urged the world to condemn attacks on diplomats, and he said the drone fragments are going into the ministry's museum. One more detail: the cruise missiles Ukraine used against the bridge are made by a Dutch company.
> source: Kyiv Independent, "Dutch Embassy damaged in massive, ongoing Russian drone strike on Kyiv" (Nov 19); Hromadske (Nov 19); Kyiv Post (Nov 19); Meduza (Nov 19); BBC News Ukrainian (Nov 19)

// Story 4: Hormuz mine claims; White House strike assessments
[ava] Story four. Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy says two supertankers carrying more than four million barrels of crude hit naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz today and were burning near the coast of Oman. Iran says they were using a route it hadn't authorized. A day earlier Iran's regular navy said three other tankers hit mines, and the UAE's national oil company says one of its ships was attacked Tuesday evening, injuring eleven crew members, with one still missing. None of the new Iranian claims has been independently confirmed, and no ships have been named. Meanwhile Axios reports the White House has asked the Pentagon and intelligence agencies how Iran would retaliate against each US strike option, and whether any of them would avoid an Iranian attack on Gulf oil and gas sites. A US official says economic pressure on Iran is working so well that major combat isn't needed, but that all options remain open.
> source: Anadolu Agency, "Iran's IRGC Navy says 2 oil supertankers strike naval mines in Strait of Hormuz" (Nov 19); Al-Monitor (Nov 19); IRNA (Nov 18-19); AP Mideast live (Nov 19); Axios, "Trump weighs strikes on Iran as Israel warns of chemical weapons" (Nov 19); Reuters via Investing.com (Nov 19)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Musk timelines for Optimus and Terafab
[andrew] Story six, tech. Elon Musk has put new dates on some big promises. Speaking by video from Shenzhen to the TomorrowX conference in Austin, he said Tesla's Optimus robot should be able to do household chores within twelve to eighteen months, like loading dishes, washing and drying them and putting them away. He admitted no robot can do that today, and he said the hand and forearm are harder to engineer than the rest of the robot combined. He promised a public Optimus demo in the next month or two. And he said Terafab, the giant chip plant Tesla and SpaceX are building, should turn out its first test chip next June, with mass production by the end of twenty twenty-eight. Musk often hits big goals, but usually well after his first deadlines. He once promised a self-driving trip across the country by the end of twenty seventeen.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: CISA nominee hearing; Synology ransomware; PHP fix
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. President Trump's pick to run CISA, the nation's cyber defense agency, faced the Senate today. Mike LoSapio is Palantir's security chief. He called CISA the cybersecurity version of nine-one-one and promised to report back on gaps left by staff and budget cuts. CISA has lost about a third of its workers. Pressed by Senator Elissa Slotkin, he said more money doesn't necessarily mean more security. The sharpest exchange came with Senator Richard Blumenthal. LoSapio declined to say who won the twenty twenty election, and said he doesn't believe the Capitol was attacked on January sixth. Blumenthal called him disqualified. The agency has gone almost two years without a confirmed director, and a committee vote likely waits until after Thanksgiving.
[ava] Two quick alerts. If you have a Synology network storage drive at home, update it. CISA says a flaw is being used in ransomware attacks on those devices. And PHP, the language behind a huge share of websites, is shipping fixes today for a critical flaw. It only affects unusual setups, but website owners should patch.
> source: The Record, "CISA nominee LoSapio vows to bolster cyber defenses while denying Jan. 6 Capitol attack" (Nov 19); CyberScoop (Nov 19); The Hacker News, "CISA Warns of 4 Exploited Flaws" (Nov 19); CISA KEV alert (Nov 18); The Hacker News, "PHP Warns of Critical Security Flaws" (Nov 19)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Loud upstairs neighbor
[ava] And finally, story eleven. When Heidi Widmer moved from Buffalo to Culver City California, she was losing sleep over her upstairs neighbor. Weeks of thumping, like someone bowling. Then one day the neighbor, an eighty-seven-year-old woman named Carole, spilled her recycling outside Heidi's door. Carole has Parkinson's disease, and it had taken her twenty minutes to get down the stairs with her walker. Her first words were, are you my new neighbor? Am I too loud for you? The thumping was just Carole trying to get around on her own. Heidi now takes out her trash, cooks for her every week and got the landlord to replace a fridge that had been broken for years. Her posts about Carole drew almost thirty million views, and strangers have given more than a hundred nine thousand dollars, enough for a stair lift so Carole can stay in her home. Carole says Heidi has become her family. As Heidi puts it, it costs nothing to be kind.
> source: Good News Network, "She Checked on Her 'Loud' Upstairs Neighbor, Inspiring Strangers to Raise More Than $100,000" (Nov 19)
