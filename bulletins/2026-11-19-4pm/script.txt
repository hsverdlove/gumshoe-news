## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Senate rejects Iran war powers resolution
[ava] Story one. The Senate has rejected another attempt to end the war with Iran. The war powers resolution failed forty-eight to fifty-one, the first such vote in either chamber since the midterm elections. Republicans Susan Collins, Lisa Murkowski and Rand Paul joined Democrats in voting for it, while Democrat John Fetterman voted no. Republican Thom Tillis, who backed a similar measure in September, voted against this one. He said this version wouldn't even go to the president, so it was only a statement. Democrats argue voters sent a clear message against the war, and they wanted every senator on the record. Supporters of the president say Congress shouldn't tie his hands while Iran is still attacking shipping. At a hearing today, a Pentagon budget official put the cost of the war at forty-eight point three billion dollars so far. Meanwhile the big Senate permitting bill was still waiting on a final vote this afternoon.
> source: The Hill, "Senate Republicans reject war powers resolution to end Iran conflict" (Nov 19); AP, "Senate Republicans block latest Democratic effort to end the war in Iran" (Nov 19); Politico Live, "Despite midterm shellacking, Senate GOP declines to rein in Iran war" (Nov 19); Bloomberg Government (Nov 19)

// Story 2: Florida flooding shifts north; St. Johns River record
[ava] Story two. The storms that flooded South Florida earlier this week have moved north to the Space Coast. The St. Johns River near Cocoa hit about eighteen point two feet today, breaking a record that stood since nineteen fifty-three. The St. Johns is one of the slowest rivers in the world. It drops only about an inch per mile, so AccuWeather says some flooding near Cocoa could last into January. More rain is expected through Friday. Volunteers answered a plea on social media and used trucks and trailers to move dozens of horses from a flooded rescue ranch in Cocoa to higher ground. The National Weather Service says the Fort Lauderdale area got nearly seventeen inches of rain in forty-eight hours. And on the other coast, heavy rain in California is flooding streets and has forced hundreds of flight cancellations.
> source: AccuWeather, "St. Johns River smashes 73-year record in Florida, with flooding that could last into 2027" (Nov 19); Florida Today, "Horses saved from rising waters at Cocoa ranch as community pitches in" (Nov 19); AP News Summary (Nov 19, 3:44 p.m. ET)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Houthi strike hits PIF Tower in Riyadh
[ava] Story three, the world. A Houthi missile hit the tallest skyscraper in Riyadh today, the eighty-story headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund, the Public Investment Fund. Saudi civil defense says several floors and nearby buildings were damaged, with no injuries. Reuters reports windows were blown out across seven or eight floors and part of the metal facade was bent. Residents described a huge blast before dawn. The Saudi-led coalition first said it had dealt with the missile and that falling debris caused the damage. Three sources told Reuters the tower was hit directly. The Houthis claim they also targeted Riyadh's main airport and other sites, which Saudi Arabia hasn't confirmed. It's the second strike on a landmark in Riyadh's financial district this month, after the Aramco building was hit on November eighth. Saudi officials call it a terrorist attack on civilian infrastructure, and the coalition says it destroyed a Houthi launch site in Yemen.
> source: Reuters via Al-Monitor, "Riyadh's tallest skyscraper hit in Houthi strike on Saudi wealth fund HQ" (Nov 19); Gulf News (Nov 19); The National (Nov 19); Al Jazeera (Nov 19); Middle East Eye (Nov 19)

// Story 4: Kushner on Article 5-style guarantees; Europe rejects "Yalta"
[ava] Story four. After meeting Ukraine's European allies in Berlin, US envoy Jared Kushner says Russia is now comfortable with the idea that any security guarantee for postwar Ukraine would be modeled on NATO's Article Five, the promise that an attack on one is an attack on all. Kushner called that a really big deal for America. He said President Trump is open to it, but only if it's in US interests and part of a broader package. Moscow hasn't publicly confirmed that view, and Kushner himself acknowledged skepticism. European leaders were cooler on the week's summitry. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said a Yalta-style deal, with big powers dividing Europe into spheres of influence, will not bring peace, and that Ukraine and Europe must be at the table. President Trump says he'll hold a news conference at the White House tomorrow.
> source: AP, "Kushner says he believes Russia is 'comfortable' with NATO-like security guarantees for Ukraine" (Nov 19); Ukrainska Pravda citing European Pravda (Nov 19); Euronews, "European leaders reject 'Yalta 2.0' for Ukraine" (Nov 19); The Guardian, Europe live (Nov 19)

[andrew] Story five, the closing bell. Anthropic's first day as a public company ended well, just not as well as it started. [andrew] Everything else had a rough day. The Dow fell about eleven hundred points, or two point two percent, to about forty-nine thousand one hundred. The S&P 500 lost two percent to about seventy-one seventy-six. That's its sixth straight loss, the longest losing streak in two and a half years, according to AP. The Nasdaq dropped two and a quarter percent. More than nine out of ten stocks in the S&P fell.
[andrew] The drivers. Investors sold the rest of the AI trade even as they bought Anthropic, a sign of nerves about whether the AI boom can last. AMD lost almost eight percent after short seller Hunterbrook questioned whether its chips are still reaching a blacklisted Chinese company. [andrew] Elsewhere, the ten-year Treasury yield is just above five percent. US crude rose about one percent to around eighty-three dollars on the Hormuz tanker reports. Gold fell two percent to about thirty-six sixty-eight, and Bitcoin is near sixty-two thousand.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Tesla Cybercab in Dallas
[andrew] Story six, tech. Tesla's Cybercab, the two-seat robotaxi with no steering wheel or pedals, is now giving public rides in Dallas. That's its second city after Austin, where rides began in September. Riders book through the Tesla Robotaxi app. Tesla has run driverless Model Y robotaxis in Dallas since April, so this adds the purpose-built car to that network. Tesla's chief engineer for the Cybercab calls the Dallas cars version one point five, and riders have already spotted a Starlink dish built into the roof. Tesla says it had more than three hundred Cybercabs in Austin last month. Outside Texas and Florida, rules are tighter. In California, Tesla's robotaxis still carry safety drivers.
> source: Teslarati, "Tesla Cybercab without steering wheel and pedals expands public rides to Dallas" (Nov 19); InsideEVs, "The Tesla Cybercab Is Now Available In Dallas" (Nov 19); Not a Tesla App (Nov 19); Stocktwits (Nov 19); Tesla on X (Nov 19)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Gemini computer use on Mac
[andrew] Story seven, AI. Google's Gemini can now operate your Mac for you. A new feature in the Gemini app lets you describe a chore and have Gemini click through menus, type and fill out forms across your files, desktop apps and the web. Google's examples include pulling numbers from PDFs into shipping forms, building slide decks from dashboards and listing clothes for resale. It works in a separate background window, so you can keep using your computer while you watch a live preview. You choose which apps it can touch, and it asks before key actions. It's rolling out in the US to paying Google AI subscribers with Apple Silicon Macs. Agents like this save time, but they can also be fooled by booby-trapped web pages, so that approval step matters.
> source: Google, "Let Gemini tackle repetitive chores on your Mac" (Nov 19)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: TfL Oyster breach; Drupal critical; NGINX update
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. London's transit agency says about two hundred sixty-five thousand Oyster photocard customers may have had their details exposed. Those are the cards that give discounted travel. Transport for London found the breach on Sunday, after customers got emails from TfL's own systems asking them to create accounts. Names, addresses, birth dates and security questions and answers may be exposed. TfL says passwords and payment cards were not. That security question detail matters. If you've ever reused those answers elsewhere, change them.
[ava] Second, Drupal, the website system used by many governments and universities, has fixed a critical flaw that could let anyone take over a site through its installer. It was found by Anthropic's code scanner. Drupal warns that attacks could appear within hours or days. And a follow-up to yesterday's NGINX alert. Researchers have now shown two critical flaws they're calling nginx2root can take over web servers. A well-known researcher says most servers aren't set up in the vulnerable way, but admins should still patch now.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Transport for London breach exposes 265,000 Oyster card users' data" (Nov 19); BleepingComputer, "Drupal patches critical RCE flaw, warns exploits may surface quickly" (Nov 19); BleepingComputer, "Critical NGINX 'NGINX2Root' bugs let attackers hijack servers" (Nov 19); Drupal Security Team advisory (Nov 18)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Caroline Sullivan, 42 years at Londonderry Burger King
[ava] And finally, story eleven, from right here in Londonderry. Caroline Sullivan is eighty-five, and she has just marked forty-two years at the drive-thru window of the same Burger King. She started in nineteen eighty-four, looking for something to do between dropping her son at school and picking him up. She stayed as her four kids grew up and married, and she's now a great-grandmother of four. She still works at least three days a week, and her manager says people come in just to see her. She wears pins for each milestone on her visor, which her daughter calls the Burger Queen crown. Caroline says she's been there longer than anywhere else in the world, and she has no plans to retire. On her days off, she's tempted to call in and ask if they need her. Her health secret? She tries hard to stay healthy, but admits she eats a lot of chicken nuggets.
