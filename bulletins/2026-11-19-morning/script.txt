## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: FAA ground stop; L3Harris
[ava] Story one. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says last Friday's nationwide ground stop could have ended sooner if the contractor that runs the FAA's telecom network had found the problem faster. A newly installed router at a site in El Dorado Arkansas began flooding the network within eight minutes of being switched on, scrambling radar feeds and halting departures for more than four hours. Duffy said contractor L3Harris did not find and isolate the problem quickly enough. Engineers have recreated the failure in a Florida lab with the FBI on site, but the root cause is still unknown, and router installs are paused. Officials see no sign of a cyberattack. L3Harris says it takes the disruption seriously and is supporting the investigation.
> source: AP, "DOT's Duffy said ground stop might have been shorter if L3Harris had spotted the problem sooner" (Nov 18); CBS News, "During nationwide ground stop, L3Harris struggled to find location of system outage" (Nov 18); CNBC, "FAA ground stop investigation: Engineers replicate network overload" (Nov 18); Reuters via Quartz (Nov 18); US Department of Transportation (Nov 18)

// Story 2: South Florida floods; Temple Sinai; Iowa detonation
[ava] Story two. Rain supercharged by El Niño has flooded much of South Florida. Parts of Broward County saw more than fifteen inches. In Hollywood Florida, the roof of Temple Sinai, a synagogue that has survived hurricanes since the nineteen sixties, partly collapsed under the weight of the water. No one was hurt. The rabbi went in and rescued the congregation's most treasured possession, a Torah that survived the Holocaust. The building has been declared unsafe, and with Shabbat starting tomorrow evening the congregation is looking for a place to pray.
[ava] And in southeast Iowa, residents near the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant are bracing for tomorrow, when the Army plans to detonate about five tons of unstable explosive chemicals that it says are too dangerous to move. People in part of nearby Middletown are urged to leave or stay inside with windows shut, because of a possible plume of lead dust.
> source: AP, "Rainfall supercharged by El Niño floods roads and collapses a south Florida synagogue's roof" (Nov 18-19); CBS Miami, "Historic Hollywood synagogue's roof collapses in floods, rabbi saves Holocaust-era Torah" (Nov 18); NBC 6 South Florida (Nov 18); AP, "Iowa residents brace for a lead dust cloud as Army plans to detonate 5 tons of bomb-making chemicals" (Nov 18)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Trilateral summit; Europe reaction; Berlin
[ava] Story three, the world. President Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin held the first-ever three-way meeting of their countries' leaders this morning in Shenzhen. Trump called it making history and pressed for nuclear arms cuts. After about an hour behind closed doors they issued a short declaration pledging United Nations reform, more refined fuel supplies and a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks. The statement did not mention Ukraine or Iran, the two wars Trump had said he hoped to start resolving. Trump is now headed home, according to the White House.
[ava] In Europe the mood was wary. Poland's Prime Minister Donald Tusk, reacting to Trump greeting Putin as his friend, posted, God protect us from such friends. At the same time, Ukraine's allies are meeting in Berlin with President Zelensky and White House envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. They're discussing air defense and security guarantees for after any ceasefire. Supporters of Trump's approach argue that talking directly with rivals is the only way to end the wars, while critics fear deals being shaped over Europe's head.
> source: AP, "Trump, Xi and Putin hold first-ever three-way talks" and live updates (Nov 19); Kremlin, "Russia-China-U.S. Heads of State Declaration toward Peace" (Nov 19); Al Jazeera (Nov 19); South China Morning Post (Nov 19); White House statement on departure (Nov 19); The Guardian, Europe live (Nov 19); NPR on Witkoff and Kushner in Berlin (Nov 19)

// Story 4: Iran strikes weighed; chemical weapons intel

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Google vs. big-city schools on Search and Gemini
[andrew] Story six, tech. The nation's biggest school districts are threatening to drop Google Search. The New York Times reports Google told schools that starting next year, if they switch off its Gemini AI features, they lose Search too. A Google education executive said Search and AI are deeply integrated. New York and Chicago schools say they'll look at other search options if Google doesn't reconsider, and critics called it extortion. Google says it hears the concerns, that it's piloting a step-by-step Guided Learning mode in Search, and that next year Gemini and Search will be off by default until schools choose what to turn on.
> source: The Verge, "The nation's largest school districts consider ditching Google Search" (Nov 18), citing The New York Times; Google statement to The Verge (Nov 18)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: EU Commission; Nvidia Gentlemen claim; N-able; Java4Shell exploitation
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. The European Commission confirms a major cyberattack on its internal HR systems. Staff lost access to payslips and leave requests for days. A spokesman says the attacker used a publicly available AI tool, there was no ransom demand, and so far there's no evidence of a breach affecting staff as a whole. But investigators are still checking whether names, pay grades and home addresses were taken.
[ava] The Gentlemen ransomware gang has listed Nvidia on its leak site and threatens to publish data on November twenty-eighth, though it has shown no proof. Nvidia says it has found no sign its own systems were hit, but is looking into an incident that may have affected a third-party partner.
[ava] N-able says hackers got into a cloud server behind its Mail Assure and SpamExperts email filtering services. Management tools remain offline, though mail is flowing and there's no evidence mailboxes were accessed.
[ava] And an update on Java4Shell, the serious Java flaw we told you about Tuesday. Security firms now see attackers probing for it and trying exploits, though no confirmed break-ins yet. If you run Java servers, patch as soon as updates land.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Algeria repays Lawrence, Kansas
[ava] And finally, story eleven. During the World Cup this summer, Algeria's team chose Lawrence Kansas as its base. Hundreds of Algerian fans from across North America poured in, and the town caught World Cup fever. One local man went viral thanking Algeria for choosing Lawrence. As one resident put it, we didn't adopt the team, the Algerians adopted us. This month Algeria returned the favor. At the invitation of Algeria's president, Lawrence's mayor, local police officers who guarded the team's practices, and fans from Kansas flew to Algiers. They landed at four thirty in the morning to find a crowd waiting with flowers. They toured Roman ruins and cheered Algeria to a win over Togo. A small welcome, it turns out, can travel a very long way.
