// Story 2: Judge lets Trump's NYT defamation suit proceed
[ava] Story two. A federal judge in Tampa says key parts of President Trump's fifteen billion dollar defamation suit against The New York Times and Penguin Random House can go forward. The suit targets a Times article by Peter Baker and the book Lucky Loser by Times reporters Susanne Craig and Russ Buettner. Judge Steven Merryday said the president plausibly alleged that statements accusing him of planning complex crimes, like tax fraud, rested on incomplete information. He let four counts proceed and threw out two others, including claims over coverage of The Apprentice. That's not a ruling that anything was false, only that the case survives for now. The Times calls the suit a baseless attack on a free press, and Penguin says it stands by the book. The president's lawyers say the defendants will now have to answer for it.
> source: Reuters via USA Today, "Trump's $15 billion defamation case against New York Times can proceed, judge rules" (Nov 19-20); Politico (Nov 19); Bloomberg Law (Nov 19); Reason/Volokh Conspiracy (Nov 20)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Signal blocks AI agents from reading the screen
[andrew] Story six, tech. Signal is fighting back against AI agents that read your screen. The messaging app will now block screenshots on Android and iPhone. That stops built-in assistants like Google's Gemini and Apple's Siri from scanning your chats, and iPhone users get a toggle for it. Signal president Meredith Whittaker says AI agents built into operating systems are hostile to privacy and undermine Signal's ability to guarantee it. She says Google, Apple and Microsoft don't give app makers an easy way to wall off their data, which is why there's no Mac protection yet. The tradeoff is losing easy screenshots of your own chats.
> source: The Verge, "Signal is blocking Gemini and Siri AI from reading your screen" (Nov 20); Signal blog post by Meredith Whittaker (Nov 20)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Alpoge claims Claude-assisted Riemann hypothesis proof
[andrew] Story seven, AI, and it's a big claim. Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, says he and Claude have found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis. It's an eighteen fifty-nine problem about how prime numbers are spread out, and one of the million-dollar Millennium Prize problems. He says the method also settles the broader generalized version.
[andrew] In a four-page note on his website, he writes that Claude far outpaced him near the finish line. He released no human-readable proof. He says drafts ran past two hundred fifty thousand pages. Instead he posted the proof in Lean, a language that lets a computer check every logical step.
[andrew] That's strong evidence if it holds up, but it isn't confirmed. In the latest reports, independent checkers had confirmed the files are intact but were still running the full check. Experts also have to confirm the final theorem really states the hypothesis, and that it rests on no hidden assumptions. As mathematician Kevin Buzzard put it, let's see if it compiles first. Anthropic hasn't commented. Even if it's right, the Clay Institute requires publication and two years of acceptance before awarding the prize.
> source: New Scientist, "Anthropic mathematician claims AI-assisted proof of the Riemann hypothesis" (Nov 20); The Verge, "Anthropic mathematician claims to have solved prestigious Millennium Prize problem with Claude" (Nov 20); Simon Willison, quoting alpo.ge/remark.pdf (Nov 20); Kingy AI on independent verification status (Nov 20); Hugging Face mirror kzoacn/riemann-lean-archive (Nov 20)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: SonicWall Gen 6 end of patches; Bazuka Android malware
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. SonicWall is telling anyone still running its older Generation Six firewalls to replace them right away. Those boxes won't get security fixes anymore, even for critical flaws. SonicWall says attackers now use automation and AI to find unsupported devices faster than ever, and it expects internet-facing Gen Six units to be targeted. That includes popular small-office models in the TZ and SOHO lines.
[ava] And researchers at the Dutch firm ThreatFabric have uncovered new Android banking malware called Bazuka. It can show you a screenshot of your own home screen as a fake cover while it steals passwords and texted login codes in the background. Its operators even use AI to rank which victims are worth targeting. The usual advice holds. Stick to official app stores, and be wary of any app that asks for accessibility permissions.
> source: The Hacker News, "SonicWall Warns Gen 6 Firewalls Won't Get Security Patches, Even for Critical Flaws" (Nov 20); SonicWall advisory (Nov 2026); The Hacker News, "Bazuka Android Malware Uses Fake Screen Overlays to Hide Credential Theft and Device Control" (Nov 20); ThreatFabric report (Nov 2026)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: England's first lifelong guardianship ceremonies
[ava] And finally, story eleven. Allan was fourteen and had already been through eight foster families and a children's home when he moved in with a foster carer named Russ, in Kettering England. Russ fought for the school placements he needed and took him to kickboxing every week. Allan is grown now and has his own flat. But this week the two stood up together at one of England's first lifelong guardianship ceremonies. They're a new way for young people leaving care to publicly honor the person who stuck by them. Russ told Allan, you've got me for life now, I'm coming to your wedding. Allan still drops by every six weeks to have his braids taken out, and to raid the cupboards. In Allan's words, moving out meant leaving the house, but not the family.
