## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Shutdown fight over Iran war funding
[ava] Story one. Congress leaves for its Thanksgiving break with the government's money set to run out on December eleventh, and the two parties are far apart. The sticking point is the war with Iran. Many Democrats don't want to fund the war, but they're split on whether that's worth a shutdown right before Christmas. As Connecticut's Rosa DeLauro, the top Democrat on House Appropriations, put it, shutting the government down doesn't stop the war.
[ava] Republicans are divided too. The president wants three hundred fifty billion dollars in new Pentagon spending. Senate Republicans have chosen bipartisan talks on a defense bill with only sixty-nine billion of that. Majority Leader John Thune calls another shutdown a colossal mistake. One likely outcome is a short-term bill that pushes the fight into next year, when Democrats take control of both chambers.
> source: Reuters, "Congress struggles to avert US shutdown as Iran war divides lawmakers" (Nov 20); NPR, "Fight over funding for Iran war could put the government at risk of a shutdown" (Nov 20); Axios (Nov 19); The Hill (Nov 20)

// Story 2: Second judge blocks Trump's arch
[ava] Story two. A second federal judge has blocked the president's planned two hundred fifty foot arch at Memorial Circle, across the Potomac from the Lincoln Memorial. Judge Leonie Brinkema in Alexandria Virginia ruled for Arlington County. She called the project a rushed job with no approval from Congress. She also faulted the Park Service for not studying risks like tall cranes near Reagan National Airport. Her order keeps work on hold while the case goes on. The White House blamed what it called another activist district court, and the administration says a nineteen twenty-five law authorizes the project. Opponents note that law called for columns, not an arch.
> source: AP, "A 2nd federal judge blocks construction of Trump's memorial arch in DC" (Nov 20); Politico (Nov 20); Courthouse News Service (Nov 20); The Washington Post (Nov 20)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Russia rejects NATO-style guarantees; Trump says Putin wants a deal
[ava] Story three, the world. Russia is pushing back on what the US envoy said just yesterday. Jared Kushner had said Moscow was comfortable with security guarantees for Ukraine modeled on NATO's Article Five, where an attack on one is an attack on all. Today Russia's deputy foreign minister, Sergei Ryabkov, said any kind of quasi-membership in NATO for Ukraine is unacceptable. He said Ukraine must first accept it will never join the alliance.
[ava] At his news conference, President Trump said he's having very good talks with Vladimir Putin and that Putin wants the war to end. Asked about the contradiction, he called it a public relations war, where each side says things. He said he won't discuss guarantees until there's a deal. And Poland's prime minister, Donald Tusk, says the Russian missile that crashed in his country this morning looks like a deliberate provocation, timed to allied military drills nearby. Russia hasn't commented.

// Story 4: COP31 overtime
[ava] Story four. The UN climate summit in Antalya Turkey has gone into overtime with no deal. Friday was supposed to be the last day. The latest draft drops every mention of fossil fuels. The European Union warned that risks turning it into a fossil summit, and a dozen countries from France to Panama to the Solomon Islands demanded clear language on moving away from them. Big oil producers have long resisted that, and nations are also split on money for poorer countries. The summit's president says they're close but not there yet, and a new draft is expected Saturday.
> source: AFP, "Nations row over fossil fuels as COP31 goes into overtime" (Nov 20); The Guardian, "Cop31 talks go into overtime as debate rages over lack of fossil fuels in draft deal" (Nov 20); UN News (Nov 20); Carbon Brief (Nov 20); Euronews (Nov 20)

## Markets | sting=markets
// Story 5: Markets at the close
[andrew] Story five, markets at the close, and it was ugly. The S&P five hundred fell three point seven percent to about six thousand nine hundred ten. That's its seventh straight loss, and it closed in a correction, more than ten percent below its October record. The Nasdaq dropped about five and a quarter percent to roughly twenty-two thousand six hundred and is now down for the year. The Dow lost about nine hundred fifty points, almost two percent, to around forty-eight thousand one hundred fifty. The VIX fear gauge jumped to about thirty-eight.
[andrew] For the week, the S&P lost about eight and a half percent and the Dow about eight. The Nasdaq fell about ten percent. Technology was by far the worst sector. Defensive names like Walmart, Costco, Johnson and Johnson and Procter and Gamble actually rose.
[andrew] Chipmakers led the way down. Nvidia fell fourteen percent to about one twenty-five after Reuters reported that the SEC is looking at possible accounting issues there. Nvidia hasn't commented, and no wrongdoing has been found. AMD and Micron each lost about thirteen percent. Anthropic fell eight and a half percent to just under eighty-nine dollars, still above its eighty-three dollar IPO price.
[andrew] Elsewhere, investors ran to bonds. The ten-year Treasury yield fell to about four point nine one percent. Gold dropped four and a half percent to about thirty-five eighteen, its lowest in more than a year. Bitcoin fell about six percent to around fifty-eight thousand two hundred. Oil was the exception, with US crude up almost one percent near eighty-four dollars.
[andrew] The drivers are the same ones we've talked about all week. Investors doubt the huge AI spending will pay off, and the Fed may raise rates in December. Some strategists call it a healthy reset after a huge run. Others warn the risk is still to the downside.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: OpenAI models improve AMD chip design
[andrew] Story seven, AI. OpenAI says its models are now designing better computer chips. Working with AMD, the models rewrote parts of the low-level code that describes a flagship graphics chip. OpenAI says that produced gains of up to fourteen percent in power, performance and chip area, compared with designs human engineers had already tuned heavily. Some of those changes are in a design already sent for manufacturing. On its own in-house inference chip, model-proposed changes shrank key parts by ten to thirteen percent.
[andrew] Those are the company's own numbers and haven't been independently tested. But if they hold, AI helping to build the chips that run AI is a big deal for an industry where shaving a few percent is hard.
> source: OpenAI, "Advancing chip design with OpenAI models" (Nov 20); Tom's Hardware (Nov 20); The Next Web (Nov 20); Wccftech (Nov 20)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: AI agent hacking platform Cairn finds zero-day; Qubole link to bank vendor breach
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Amazon's threat researchers say hackers turned an AI agent platform loose with one instruction: get shell access. The tool, called Cairn, was built for legitimate security testing. Amazon says suspected China-linked attackers used it in September against universities, mostly in East Asia. The agents found a brand-new flaw in MONAI Label, open-source software for labeling medical images. In eleven minutes they went from mapping the app to exploiting it, and later took full control of a server. The attackers left their own control panel open to the internet, which is how Amazon found it. A fix came out Wednesday.
[ava] And on the vendor breach that hit Chase customers, Visa and Mastercard now confirm incidents at outside providers too. Both say no card numbers or payment systems were involved.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Hackers use autonomous AI tool Cairn to find zero-day, take over server" (Nov 20); Amazon Threat Intelligence report (Nov 2026); BleepingComputer, "Vendor data breach exposes Chase, Visa, Mastercard customer info" (Nov 20)

## Local News & Events | sting=local
// Story 10: Londonderry tax rate; Special Olympics; weekend events
[ava] Story ten, local news. Londonderry's Town Council voted three to two not to dip into the town's reserves to lower December tax bills. ## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Atlanta seniors weave plastic-bag sleeping mats
[ava] And finally, story eleven. At Clairmont Place, a retirement community in Atlanta, residents have bathtubs full of plastic grocery bags, on purpose. They loop them into chains and weave them into waterproof sleeping mats for people living on the street. Each mat takes five hundred to seven hundred bags. The group is called Mats Matter, and a local nonprofit hands the mats out.
[ava] It started when resident Peter Paulsen saw a man sleeping on church steps downtown and thought about how hot, and then how cold, those steps must get. A few months ago, on his way to worship, he passed a man carrying one of their mats on his back. Sadly, the station reports Paulsen has just passed away. He was eighty-two. His neighbors plan to keep on weaving.
> source: 11Alive (WXIA Atlanta), "Atlanta seniors turn plastic bags into sleeping mats for people experiencing homelessness" (Nov 20)
