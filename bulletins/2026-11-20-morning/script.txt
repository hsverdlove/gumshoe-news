## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Senate passes permitting overhaul
[ava] Story one. The Senate has passed the biggest overhaul of federal permitting in decades. The vote late last night was sixty-two to thirty-three. The bill aims to speed approvals for power lines, wind and solar farms, mines, and oil and gas projects by changing how environmental reviews work. It's also meant to make sure data centers, not ordinary ratepayers, cover the cost of the grid upgrades they need. Fifteen Democrats and independents joined nearly all Republicans in support. Republicans Josh Hawley and Mike Rounds voted no, as did thirty-one Democrats. Supporters, led by Republican Shelley Moore Capito and Democrat Sheldon Whitehouse, say it will lower energy costs and get the country building again. Critics, including many environmental groups, say it weakens the laws communities use to challenge harmful projects. The bill now goes to the House after Thanksgiving. Republican leaders want it done before Democrats take control of both chambers in January.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Russian missiles land in Poland; massive attack on Ukraine
[ava] Story three, the world. Poland says two Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone came down on its territory this morning, during one of the largest Russian air attacks of the war on Ukraine. Poland's defense minister, Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, says one was almost certainly a Kh-101 cruise missile, which came down near the village of Nowy Lipowiec, about thirty miles from the Ukrainian border. Debris was found at two other sites in the southeast. No one was hurt and no critical infrastructure was damaged. Prime Minister Donald Tusk says it doesn't look like an accident, and that NATO partners share that view, calling it an intentional provocation. Poland will ask NATO for more forces and air defenses. Moscow had not commented as of this morning. A Russian missile also strayed into Poland in July.
[ava] In Ukraine, President Zelenskyy says Russia fired more than four hundred drones and about a hundred ten missiles, hitting power facilities, rail lines and bridges. At least eight people were killed, six in Kyiv and two at a train station in Lviv. The attack came hours after President Putin promised a painful response to Ukraine's strikes this week on the Crimean Bridge.
> source: France 24, "Tusk denounces Russian 'provocation' after Warsaw says missile hit territory" (Nov 20); The New Voice of Ukraine, "Kh-101 missiles and jet drone fall in Poland, Defense Ministry says" (Nov 20); BBC News Polska (Nov 20); PAP Biznes (Nov 20); DW, Ukraine updates (Nov 20); AP, "Russian attacks on Kyiv kill 3, cripple bridges and cut power" (Nov 20)

// Story 4: UK-EU reset deal
[ava] Story four. Britain and the European Union have sealed what both sides call a reset, ten years after the Brexit vote. After a summit in Brussels today, Prime Minister Andy Burnham's office said negotiations had concluded on three things: a youth mobility program that lets young people live and work in each other's countries, easier rules for food and farm exports, and linking their carbon trading systems. Brussels signals that putting it into practice may depend on a separate fight over tuition fees for European students in Britain. Burnham has said rejoining the EU shouldn't be ruled out someday, but he calls it far from imminent. Brexit supporters see it as a step back toward the bloc.
> source: Al Jazeera, "EU and UK seal reset deal" (Nov 20); BBC News, UK-EU summit live (Nov 20); Politico Europe, "Brussels to link UK-EU summit package to tuition fee demands" (Nov 20); GOV.UK (Nov 20)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Intuit sells Mailchimp to Bending Spoons
[andrew] Story six, tech. Intuit, the maker of TurboTax and QuickBooks, is selling Mailchimp to the Italian company Bending Spoons for three point six eight billion dollars in cash and stock. Intuit paid twelve billion for the email marketing service in twenty twenty-one, so it's getting back less than a third. Intuit also trimmed its full-year forecast, and the stock is down about eleven percent before the bell. Bending Spoons, which is backed by Berkshire Hathaway, specializes in buying faded tech names, including Evernote, Vimeo, WeTransfer, Eventbrite and AOL. It typically cuts costs and raises prices. So Mailchimp customers may want to watch their bills. The deal should close early next year.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: vCenter zero-day; F5; GitLab
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Broadcom has patched a critical flaw in VMware vCenter, the software that runs virtual machines in many corporate data centers. Google's Mandiant says a suspected Chinese espionage group exploited it for more than two months before the fix. The hackers used it to create their own administrator accounts, steal encryption keys and plant backdoors. Older version seven systems, which are out of support, get no patch. Mandiant says companies should patch and also hunt for signs they've already been hit.
[ava] Also this morning, F5 issued emergency fixes for critical flaws in its BIG-IP network gear that can knock systems offline and may allow a takeover. And GitLab urged anyone running their own server to update now, to fix a critical bug in the Ruby language called Ruby2Shell. No attacks on that one have been seen yet.
> source: SecurityWeek, "Newly Patched VMware vCenter Flaw Exploited as Zero-Day by Chinese APT" (Nov 19); Google Cloud threat intelligence blog (Nov 19); BleepingComputer, "F5 releases emergency patches for critical BIG-IP RCE flaws" (Nov 20); GitLab patch release 19.6.1 (Nov 20); SecurityOnline (Nov 20)

[ava] Story nine, from your X feed. Gastroenterologist Keith Siau flagged what experts are calling the biggest advance in pancreatic cancer treatment in decades. In an early trial of sixty-one patients, a daily pill called daraxonrasib, given with chemotherapy, stopped tumors from growing in nine out of ten. Tumors shrank in about sixty percent, and in one in twelve they disappeared from scans. Doctors caution it's not a cure, and a larger trial is now under way to test whether it extends lives.
[andrew] The Hacker News warned about an unpatched Windows trick called FastUnlock. Someone holding your locked laptop can run programs on it without signing in, by abusing the Narrator screen reader. The simple defense is to shut down or hibernate your laptop when it leaves your hands, and to use a startup PIN with BitLocker.
