## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Schumer vs Van Hollen leadership race
[ava] Story one. Democrats won't take control of the Senate until January, but their first big internal fight is already here. On December first, in a secret ballot, they'll choose their next majority leader. Chuck Schumer of New York, who has led Senate Democrats since twenty seventeen, faces a challenge from Chris Van Hollen of Maryland. It's the first contested race for the job in more than three decades.
[ava] Van Hollen says voters demanded change and is promising a more aggressive approach toward President Trump. He's picked up public support from Bernie Sanders, Jeff Merkley and, this week, Michigan's Elissa Slotkin, along with two incoming senators. Schumer says he already has the votes, and his allies predict more than forty. His backers credit him with winning back the majority. The Associated Press notes that many of the thirteen newly elected Democrats, several from Republican-leaning states, haven't picked a side. They campaigned as independent voices, and this vote is an early test of that promise. Schumer remains the favorite.

// Story 2: Thanksgiving travel forecast; ORL toothpaste recall
[ava] Story two, two things to know before the holiday week. AAA expects about eighty-one and a half million Americans to travel at least fifty miles between Tuesday and next Monday. That's a hair below last year's record, ending a three-year streak, and AAA partly blames higher costs. Gas prices set monthly records in August, September and October, and domestic airfares booked for the holiday average about eight hundred dollars round trip, up seventeen percent. Nearly nine in ten travelers will drive. AAA's advice is to check tires, batteries and fluids before you go.
[ava] And a recall worth checking in your bathroom cabinet. ORL Labs is recalling about fifty-two thousand units of six ORL Organic Oral Care toothpastes and mouthwashes, for both kids and adults, sold nationwide. Federal testing found the hormones progesterone, estradiol or DHEA in samples, which the FDA attributes to cross-contamination. It's a Class Two recall, meaning any health effects are expected to be temporary or reversible. The affected products have expiration dates from September twenty twenty-six through August twenty twenty-eight.
> source: AAA Newsroom, "Thanksgiving Travel Expected to Dip Slightly Compared to Last Year" (Nov 16); USA Today, "Over 51K toothpaste, mouthwash products recalled" (Nov 20); FDA Enforcement Report; Fox Business (Nov 20)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 5: Tesla FSD v15 begins rolling out
[andrew] Story five, tech, and this one's for the Tesla in your driveway. This afternoon Tesla started rolling out Full Self-Driving version fifteen, first to early-access owners, with a wider release expected in December. Tesla says a new vision system, shared with its robotaxis and Optimus robots, handles safety-critical situations twenty-five times better. The flashiest piece is Reality View, a detailed live rendering of the road that lets you see and control what the car plans to do.

[andrew] The skeptic's view, from Electrek: almost every fix targets a known weakness, which is good, but Tesla gives the twenty-five times figure without the underlying number. Past major versions have gone two steps forward, one step back. It also loosens driver monitoring a bit at higher speeds. FSD is still supervised, so your hands and eyes stay on duty.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 6: Why the AI sell-off worries Wall Street; Meta net-zero rethink
[andrew] Story six, AI. With markets closed, the weekend question is whether last week's AI sell-off was just a correction or something bigger. NPR lays out why some on Wall Street are worried. The big one is how tangled the industry has become. Nvidia owns stakes in AI companies like Anthropic, lends them money, and they in turn buy Nvidia's chips. Debt-heavy data center builders like CoreWeave sit in the same web. If one or two stumble, the fear is a chain reaction. And since AI stocks are now a big slice of the S&P five hundred, that reaches ordinary retirement accounts.
[andrew] The other view, from Haverford Trust's Hank Smith: this isn't the dot-com bust. The AI trade rests on profitable giants, and Nvidia still posted strong results. But he expects the healing to take a while.
[andrew] And one cost of the boom: Meta says it's re-evaluating its goal of net-zero emissions by twenty thirty. Its net emissions rose about forty-five percent last year as it raced to build AI data centers, and it says clean energy and grid hookups can't keep pace. Meta hasn't set a new date.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: Microsoft Entra Connect Health breach; AI prompt leak study
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk. Microsoft is notifying customers that attackers exploited a flaw in Entra Connect Health, a cloud tool companies use to monitor their sign-in systems. The bug sat there from July until Microsoft found it November ninth. Attackers got read-only access to administrator email addresses, tenant names and configuration details. Microsoft says no passwords, tokens or valid keys were exposed, and it fixed the problem Thursday. It hasn't said how many organizations were hit or who did it. The practical risk is targeted phishing aimed at those admins, so Microsoft recommends phishing-resistant multifactor sign-in for privileged accounts.
[ava] And a cautionary study for anyone pasting sensitive data into chatbots. HD Moore of the security firm Dragos sent nearly two million AI prompts seeded with unique fake secrets through AI providers and resellers, then watched where they surfaced. He found three hosting providers had been breached, some cheap resellers logged prompts, and some keys were later used, even on requests marked for zero data retention. The lesson: never put real passwords or keys into an AI prompt.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Microsoft Entra Connect Health flaw exploited to steal tenant data" (Nov 21); Microsoft customer notification (Nov 20); Ars Technica, "Researcher plants 2m canaries into the AI supply chain. A privacy disaster surfaced." (Nov 2026); Dragos, "AI Supply Chain Security: What Canary Tokens Found" (Nov 19)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 10: Knaresborough pub holiday lights
[ava] And finally, story ten. In the old English market town of Knaresborough, in North Yorkshire, pub owner Christian Ogley decided his neighbors needed cheering up after a tough year of soaring living costs. So he spent about six thousand dollars turning his two-story pub, Blind Jack's, into a holiday showpiece.
[ava] He started sketching the design in January on the backs of beer mats. With help from seven staff members and regulars, he built a sixteen-foot tree out of wood and tarp, surrounded by more than ten thousand lights that look like falling snow, and giant red ribbons. It costs only about three dollars a day to run. He says people keep stopping to talk about it, and it's given the town something positive to share. Next up: two seven-foot nutcrackers on the sidewalk. He hopes it becomes a tradition.
