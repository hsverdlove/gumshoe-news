[ava] Story one. Three people are dead after gunfire broke out at a baby shower in Parma, Ohio, a suburb just south of Cleveland. A seventeen-year-old girl died, along with a forty-five-year-old man who was the father of the expectant mother, and a nineteen-year-old man who was the father of the unborn baby. // Story 2: ICE arrests on Sea-Tac jet bridge
[ava] Story two. A confrontation at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport has become the latest flashpoint over immigration enforcement. On Saturday morning, masked Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers detained two people on a jet bridge as passengers boarded an Alaska Airlines flight to Los Angeles. Video shows officers struggling with a person on the floor while a woman shouts that her daughter is being taken and has a work permit.
[ava] ICE says it was targeting a Mexican man who had overstayed his visa, and that a woman who intervened threw hot coffee at officers. ICE says she also overstayed a visa and will be charged with assaulting a federal officer. Washington Governor Bob Ferguson, a Democrat, called the scene chilling and said ICE is completely out of control. He's asking airlines to tell federal immigration agents to stay out of the state's airports. Alaska Airlines says it got no advance notice. Supporters of the administration's approach argue airports are legitimate places to arrest people who are in the country illegally, and that interfering with officers is a crime. The flight left about three hours late.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Merkury blast in Benghazi; Dymer toll rises
[ava] Story three, the world. A Russian warship appears to have been destroyed in a huge explosion at the port of Benghazi in eastern Libya. Naval analyst H I Sutton, writing for Naval News, provisionally identifies it as the Merkury, a modern missile corvette commissioned in twenty twenty-three. He says satellite images show it engulfed in flames and calls it almost certainly destroyed. It had been escorting Russian arms shipments.
[ava] Libyan authorities first called it an ammunition depot explosion. Later Sunday, Libya's eastern-based government told Reuters a preliminary investigation found the blast happened aboard a Russian ship and that a drone attack is suspected. Medical sources report injuries but no deaths. Nobody has claimed responsibility, and neither Moscow nor Kyiv has commented. Pro-Ukrainian accounts are crediting Ukraine, which has been linked to past attacks on Russian ships in the Mediterranean, but that is not confirmed.
[ava] And an update on the apartment building hit near Kyiv this morning. In Dymer, Ukrainian authorities now say at least two people were killed and four wounded, including a child. Rescuers have pulled nine people out, and about eleven are still believed trapped.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 5: FT and NYT trace Alice Huang chip network
[andrew] Story five, tech. Two major newspapers have new reporting on the scandal that pushed Jensen Huang out as Nvidia's chief executive. It centers on Alice Huang, no relation, the woman he admitted having an affair with. US officials suspect her of working for Chinese intelligence, which Beijing calls a smear.
[andrew] The Financial Times says she was the public face of Megaspeed, a cloud company that it describes as part of a network of gaming investors and shell companies that moved restricted Nvidia chips into China. Its Malaysian unit declared about four point six billion dollars in imports from a US server maker that used to be part of the Chinese company Inspur. The paper says the final smuggling step ran partly through a man US authorities call the second-in-command of Cambodia's Prince Group, which Treasury has labeled a criminal organization. The New York Times separately reports about three point six billion dollars in Chinese state money flowed through companies tied to her family and associates, some linked to military suppliers.
[andrew] Now the other side. Megaspeed says it runs a lawful business and that Alice Huang left in August twenty twenty-five. Nvidia says it blocked Megaspeed last year, restricted sales to the server maker, and has stopped shipping its H200 chips to China. Prince Group denies the man worked for it. And the Times itself notes most links are indirect, with no proof the Chinese military used the computing power.
> source: The Next Web, "FT, NYT tie Alice Huang's chip network to gaming tycoons and PLA suppliers" (Nov 21); Financial Times (Nov 21-22); The New York Times (Nov 20); Australian Financial Review (Nov 22); O Globo (Nov 22)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 6: Governors' AI principles
[andrew] Story six, AI. The National Governors Association released a set of principles today on who should write the rules for artificial intelligence. A bipartisan group led by Maryland Governor Wes Moore, with Republicans like Utah's Spencer Cox and Indiana's Mike Braun, wants Washington to oversee the most powerful AI models, with independent safety reviews and mandatory reporting of serious incidents. But they want states to keep handling consumer protection, discrimination and AI's effect on jobs.
[andrew] That puts them at odds with the White House, which has asked Congress to override state AI laws it considers burdensome, arguing that a patchwork of fifty state rulebooks raises interstate commerce and national security concerns. The governors' statement never uses the word preemption. Both sides agree some state laws should survive, so the real fight is where to draw the line, and it's expected to land in the new Congress in January. No bill has been introduced yet.
> source: National Governors Association, "Governors Call for AI Action" and Statement of Principles (Nov 22); Bloomberg via Startup Fortune, "Governors ask Congress for one AI rulebook that leaves state laws standing" (Nov 22); Executive Order 14365 (Dec 2025)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: Chainflip $12M theft, bounty offer
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk. Chainflip, a service that swaps cryptocurrencies across different blockchains, says an attacker stole about twelve million dollars from its vaults on Saturday. That's well over double the first estimate. The haul included about a hundred twenty-three bitcoin, five hundred twenty ether, and four million dollars in stablecoins. Outside analysts say the withdrawal was signed with the platform's real master key, which points to stolen key material rather than a coding bug.
[ava] Chainflip paused all deposits, swaps and withdrawals, says it has found and closed the hole, and has traced the attacker's preparation back to November eleventh. It posted a public message offering a deal: return ninety percent by Monday afternoon and keep ten percent as a bounty, with no further questions. It also warned the attacker that it has identifying details to share with law enforcement. If you use cross-chain crypto services, this is a reminder that big pools of funds guarded by shared keys remain prime targets.
> source: Cointelegraph, "Chainflip Confirms $12M Hack, Offers Hacker 10% Bounty" (Nov 22); Chainflip Labs, "Message from Chainflip Labs" (Nov 21); crypto.news (Nov 22); Defimon and Bitquery analyses via PandaSecurity (Nov 21)
