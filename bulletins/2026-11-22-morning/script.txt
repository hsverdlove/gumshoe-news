// Story 4: COP31 ends without a deal
[ava] Story four. The UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey has collapsed without an agreement. After two days of overtime, making it the longest COP on record, summit president Murat Kurum told the closing session this morning that there was no consensus, and the decisions were not adopted.
[ava] The sticking point was fossil fuels. The final draft did not mention coal, oil or gas by name after Saudi Arabia and other producers objected. It only pointed back to the pledge countries made three years ago in Dubai to transition away from them. The European Union formally rejected it, saying it was weaker than the previous version, and Panama joined in. The hosts, Turkey and Australia, argued the text kept the Dubai commitment alive and would have acknowledged for the first time that the world will overshoot the one point five degree warming goal. Reuters says the last comparable breakdown was in Copenhagen in two thousand nine. Earlier climate agreements and their rules still stand.
> source: BBC News live, "COP31 climate talks in jeopardy as EU formally rejects latest draft" (Nov 22); Reuters via Devdiscourse, "COP31 climate talks break down as EU, others reject deal" (Nov 22); Al Jazeera/AP/Reuters (Nov 22); AP (Nov 22)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 6: AI-assisted Navier-Stokes blowup claim
[andrew] Story six, AI, and another big claim from mathematics. Tristan Buckmaster of NYU and Levent Alpöge of Anthropic say they've proved that the Navier-Stokes equations, which describe how water and air flow, can break down in finite time with no outside force pushing the fluid. That question is one of the Clay Institute's million-dollar Millennium Prize problems. In September OpenAI published a proof that required an outside force. This claim removes it.
[andrew] The authors say Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's Codex helped throughout, and the proof comes with a huge computer-checked version in the Lean language, about six hundred thousand lines. Now the caution. Buckmaster admits the write-up was rushed and heavily AI-assisted, because they'd been scooped once before. The posted files are missing a piece of numerical data the proof depends on, and that check runs outside the formal verification. UCLA's Terence Tao calls the strategy clear but says a properly written proof still needs work. A companion paper on the related Euler equations, which Tao calls human-readable, came out the same day. Expect months of expert review before anyone declares the problem solved.
> source: The Next Web, "Navier-Stokes can blow up with no outside force, Buckmaster and Alpöge say" (Nov 21); The New York Times (Nov 2026); NYU Courant; GitHub, EulerBlowupPublic repository; Terence Tao's blog (Nov 2026)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: OpenRouter drops providers; Ghostscript Upload2Shell
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk, with a follow-up to yesterday's story about AI prompts leaking across the supply chain. OpenRouter, a popular service that routes AI requests to many hosting companies, has removed three providers from its platform and stripped one hundred twenty-five endpoints of their "zero data retention" label, the promise that your prompts aren't stored. It's notifying affected customers and says its own systems weren't breached. OpenRouter hasn't named the three. Customer notices posted online point to resellers that forwarded requests for the Chinese lab MiniMax's models back to MiniMax itself, and one, Novita, has confirmed a routing error did exactly that. Researchers say attackers sat inside three providers for months, copying traffic and testing stolen cloud keys within hours.
[ava] And a patch reminder for anyone running websites or servers. Researchers at pwn.ai showed that a flaw in Ghostscript, the behind-the-scenes program that renders PDFs and images, lets a booby-trapped file disguised as an ordinary picture run commands on the server. They triggered it through about ten common apps, including Roundcube webmail just by opening an email, shared print queues and WordPress upload forms. It was fixed in Ghostscript version ten point oh eight in September and no attacks have been reported, but several Linux distributions still ship the vulnerable version.
> source: The Hacker News, "OpenRouter Drops 3 Providers After Researchers Catch Months of Prompt Theft at AI Hosting Firms" (Nov 22); OpenRouter, "Data Processing Incidents on Inference Providers" (Nov 2026); Privacy Affairs (Nov 2026); The Hacker News, "Researchers Demonstrate Ghostscript Sandbox-Escape RCE via Crafted File Uploads in Popular Apps" (Nov 22); pwn.ai Upload2Shell research; oss-security (Nov 21)
