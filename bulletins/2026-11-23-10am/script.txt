## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: DNC 2028 debate rules, dark money pledge, NH debate
[ava] Story one. The Democratic National Committee has set the rules for its twenty twenty-eight presidential primary debates, and New Hampshire is on the list. There will be twelve debates: five next year, starting in June, and seven in twenty twenty-eight. Six of those will be held in early primary states, including New Hampshire, South Carolina, Nevada, New Mexico, Michigan and Virginia.
[ava] The headline rule: to make the stage, candidates must publicly denounce super PAC and dark money spending that promotes them or attacks their Democratic rivals. Party chair Ken Martin says Democrats are on offense after the midterms. Skeptics point out the rule can't stop outside groups from spending. Candidates only have to disavow it, and the rules don't cover the general election.
> source: ABC News, "Democrats holding 12 debates in 2028 cycle, requiring candidates denounce 'dark money'" (Nov 23); NBC News (Nov 23); Reuters (Nov 23); DNC, 2028 Democratic Primary Process

// Story 2: DOJ concedes deported Austin man is a US citizen
[ava] Story two. The Justice Department has acknowledged in federal court that a Texas man it deported to Mexico in April is a US citizen. Brian Morales García, a twenty-five-year-old air conditioning technician from Austin, was born in Denver and raised in Mexico. He was stopped as a passenger without his ID, and federal agents relied on Mexican records listing a Mexican birthplace.
[ava] His lawsuit says agents pressured him into signing papers for a fast deportation and turned away relatives who tried to bring proof of citizenship. The government says he left voluntarily, so he has no grounds to sue, and it let him back into the country in August. A judge has not ruled on the government's request to dismiss the case.
> source: The Guardian, "Trump administration deported US citizen to Mexico, DoJ admits in court" (Nov 23); Texas Tribune, "Feds admit in court filing that deported Austin man is citizen" (Nov 20); FOX 7 Austin

// Story 3: Amtrak Sunset Limited derailment near Lafayette
[ava] Story three, briefly. An Amtrak Sunset Limited train hit an eighteen-wheeler stuck on a crossing near Lafayette, Louisiana on Sunday evening. The truck was hauling a bulldozer. The locomotive and two baggage cars derailed, but the passenger cars stayed upright. Five passengers had injuries that aren't life threatening, and the other travelers were bused to New Orleans.
> source: ABC News, "Amtrak train derails after striking truck in Louisiana, injuring 5, officials say" (Nov 23); USA Today (Nov 23); KLFY (Nov 22)

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Daytime drone strike in central Kyiv; NATO council meets on Poland
[ava] Story four, the world. Russia kept up a rare daytime drone attack on Kyiv today. This afternoon Kyiv time, debris from a downed drone set fire to cars and a five-story apartment building near Khreshchatyk, the main street, not far from the government quarter and the president's office. Mayor Vitali Klitschko says three people were hurt, including an American citizen, and two residents were rescued. Ukraine's air force says Russia launched more than a hundred fifty drones overnight.
[ava] In Brussels, NATO ambassadors are meeting today at Poland's request over Friday's three Russian cruise missiles. Warsaw wants another early-warning plane and more fighter jets on the eastern flank, and a Polish deputy defense minister says NATO's top commander responded positively. Poland is not invoking Article Four, which calls for formal consultations. Moscow denies any provocation.
> source: The Kyiv Independent, "Falling drone debris sparks fire in central Kyiv, 2 rescued, 3 injured, including foreign national" (Nov 23); Interfax-Ukraine (Nov 23); BBC News Ukrainian (Nov 23); The New Voice of Ukraine (Nov 23); PAP, "Poland to seek stronger NATO air defences after Russian missile incursions" (Nov 23)

// Story 5: Hoax bomb threats to schools in Germany and Denmark
[ava] Story five. About four hundred fifty schools in Germany got identical emailed bomb threats this morning with the subject line Death awaits you. Schools across Denmark got them too. Police in both countries say the threats look unfounded and advised against evacuations, though some German schools sent students home. It's the latest wave in a string of email hoaxes that have hit schools, airlines and other targets across Europe in recent weeks. No one has claimed responsibility.
> source: The National with Reuters, "Police investigate 'death awaits you' bomb threats in Denmark and Germany" (Nov 23); Anadolu Agency (Nov 23); Bild via Freiburg police (Nov 23)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang
[andrew] Story six, tech, and it's the big one this morning. Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president and CEO, effective immediately. Less than a week ago the same board said it would not take him back. Huang resigned after admitting affairs, including one with a woman US intelligence suspects of ties to China's spy agency.
[andrew] The board says it got input from senior levels of the US government and is confident Huang's return poses no national security risk. President Trump said the Attorney General confirmed the government has no evidence Huang broke the law. Huang says he can't wait to get back to work. Interim CEO Colette Kress returns to her job as finance chief.
[andrew] Not everyone is cheering. The board's own investigation will continue, and so will government probes into whether Nvidia chips were smuggled to China. Some investors are asking how a company this big can swing so much on one person.
> source: NVIDIA Newsroom, "NVIDIA Board Reinstates Jensen Huang as President, CEO and Board Director" (Nov 23); CNBC, "Nvidia skyrockets 30% on Huang's return as CEO" (Nov 23); AP (Nov 23); Ars Technica (Nov 23); The Washington Post (Nov 23)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX over Grok deepfakes
[andrew] Story seven, AI. Virginia and Oregon are the first states to sue SpaceX, which now owns X and the Grok chatbot. The attorneys general say Grok let users make sexual deepfakes from real photos of women and children without consent, while the company marketed its apps as safe. They're suing under consumer protection laws and want penalties and stricter guardrails.
[andrew] SpaceX didn't comment right away. The company has said it has strict controls, and in other cases it has argued Grok is a neutral tool, that users are responsible for what they prompt, and that federal law shields platforms from liability for user content. Regulators in Europe and California are also investigating.
> source: CNBC, "SpaceX sued by Oregon and Virginia over AI deepfake porn, Grok safety claims" (Nov 23); AP (Nov 23); Virginia Office of the Attorney General news release (Nov 23); Wired (Nov 23)
