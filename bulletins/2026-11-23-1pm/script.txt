// Story 1: Trump DOJ event on Newsom death row commutations
[ava] Story one. President Trump is at the Justice Department today for an event condemning California Governor Gavin Newsom's plan to commute every death sentence in the state to life without parole. That's five hundred forty-eight people. The White House says Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel are taking part, and families of murder victims are attending to tell their stories. The commutations aren't in effect yet. For three hundred eighty-two of the inmates, the California Supreme Court has to sign off first because they have more than one felony conviction.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: NATO council on Poland: update
[ava] Story three, the world, with an update from NATO. Alliance ambassadors met in Brussels today on the three Russian cruise missiles that crashed in Poland on Friday. NATO described it as regular consultations among allies, not an emergency session under Article Four. Poland's defense minister says allied early-warning planes are already flying to help protect Polish airspace, and Warsaw is asking for more fighter jets and another surveillance plane. No decision on extra reinforcements has been announced.
[ava] Secretary General Mark Rutte called it a dangerous incident but said it's not yet clear whether the missiles crossed in on purpose, and he won't speculate. Poland plans to summon Russia's ambassador once its investigation is done. Germany's foreign minister put Europe's mood this way: we are not at war, but we are certainly not at peace anymore either. Moscow denies any provocation.

// Story 4: Armenia draft constitution, Azerbaijan peace
[ava] Story four. Armenia has published a draft constitution that drops any reference to its nineteen ninety declaration of independence. Azerbaijan says that document lays claim to Nagorno-Karabakh, and removing it has been Baku's main condition for signing a final peace deal. Azerbaijan's foreign minister Jeyhun Bayramov called the change a positive development and said a peace agreement can be signed once the issue is settled.
[ava] It's not done yet. The draft needs a two-thirds vote in Armenia's parliament to go to a referendum, and Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan's party is six votes short. If it passes there, voters would have the final say.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: DeepMind agents speed up quantum algorithms; Google Search Personal Intelligence
[andrew] Story seven, AI. Google DeepMind says teams of its Gemini 4 AI agents found big improvements to quantum computing algorithms, and checked every result with a formal proof system called Lean, so the math is verified rather than taken on faith. For one fusion energy calculation, the agents cut the computing cost almost twelve-fold. For an enzyme that breaks down most medicines, they made the simulation two and a half times cheaper. Google says these programs still need more power than the first error-corrected quantum computers are likely to have, and it's open-sourcing the tools.
[andrew] And Google Search is getting more personal. Starting today you can opt in to connect Google Wallet to Search's AI Mode, so it can look at your spending and card perks, with Drive and the Gemini app coming in the next few weeks. Google says AI Mode can only read that information and can't move your money. Privacy advocates will want to see how that data is handled. You can turn these connections off any time.
> source: Google DeepMind, "Finding faster quantum algorithms with agents and formal verification" (Nov 23); Google, "A more tailored Google Search that's uniquely yours" (Nov 23); The Neuron, "Everything That Happened in AI Today" (Nov 23)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: ScreenConnect flaw exploited against MSPs; GlobeTopper loss
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Attackers are exploiting a critical flaw in ConnectWise ScreenConnect, the remote support tool many IT providers use. Researchers at Huntress say a malicious guest machine can push a file to the technician's computer and get it to run, with no prompt, just by having the technician join a session. In one case it took about nine seconds to plant a rogue remote access tool. That's dangerous because a technician's PC often holds the keys to every customer. The fix is ScreenConnect version twenty-six point six point five or later.
[ava] And a cautionary tale. GlobeTopper, which distributes digital gift cards for brands like Target and Spotify, says an October break-in cost it about two and a half million dollars in lost revenue, even though the hackers only redeemed about seventy thousand dollars in gift cards. It had to take its platform offline and rebuild it from scratch.
> source: The Hacker News, "Hackers Exploit Critical ScreenConnect Flaw to Hijack MSP Technician PCs Without Warning" (Nov 23); Huntress research; BleepingComputer, "GlobeTopper loses $2.5 million after hackers steal $70k in gift cards" (Nov 23); iQSTEL SEC filing
