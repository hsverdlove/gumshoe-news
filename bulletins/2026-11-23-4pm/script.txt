## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Trump pauses Iowa lead azide detonation (new development)
[ava] Story one, a new development in Iowa. President Trump says he has ordered the Army not to go ahead with its planned detonation of nearly five tons of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown. The blast had been reset for Wednesday. In a Truth Social post, Trump said he agrees with military experts that a controlled detonation is the far safer option. But he says it won't happen until a federal appeals court signs off and Iowa's incoming governor, Democrat Rob Sand, agrees. If they don't, Trump says he'll hold Sand responsible for whatever happens next.
[ava] The appeals court has paused the operation's environmental permit while environmental groups challenge it. The Army says cold weather raises the risk of an uncontrolled blast, and Middletown's mayor wants it to go ahead.
> source: AP, "Trump orders the Army not to detonate bomb-making chemicals in Iowa without court approval" (Nov 23); Iowa Public Radio, "Trump tells Army to wait for courts, Sand to weigh in on lead azide detonation" (Nov 23); Politico (Nov 23); The Hill (Nov 23)

[ava] Story two, a follow-up on the fight over California's death row. Fourteen Republican state attorneys general, led by Florida's James Uthmeier, sent Governor Gavin Newsom a letter today accusing him of choosing murderers over victims by planning to commute all five hundred forty-eight death sentences. They pledged to back every lawful effort to restore the sentences. Herr has called Newsom's move a cowardly act.

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: EU 22nd sanctions package
[ava] Story four, the world. The European Commission has proposed its twenty-second package of sanctions on Russia. It would cut about sixty more Russian banks off the SWIFT payment network and target crypto schemes and banks in other countries that help Moscow get around sanctions. Seventy more shadow fleet tankers would be hit. It also proposes a visa ban on Russian combat veterans and a ban on Russian athletes competing under their flag at events in the EU.
[ava] All twenty-seven members have to agree, and some question whether the veterans' visa ban can be enforced. Brussels wants a deal before the holiday break.
> source: Euronews, "EU goes after Russia's banks, soldiers and athletes in new sanctions package" (Nov 23); Agence Europe (Nov 23); European Commission press release IP/26/2521 (Nov 23); The Straits Times (Nov 23)

// Story 5: Germany: reports of Russian nuclear satellite
[ava] Story five. Germany's government says it's taking very seriously German media reports that Russia may be getting ready to launch a satellite armed with a nuclear weapon. Citing Western intelligence, the reports say the satellite is called Sputnik S and could go up within weeks. That would break the nineteen sixty-seven Outer Space Treaty. NATO's Mark Rutte, alongside Chancellor Friedrich Merz, said the alliance will defend every inch of its territory, including in space.
[ava] Berlin says there's no immediate threat. The Kremlin dismissed the reports as full of alleged and possible claims from unnamed sources and called them propaganda.
> source: The Guardian, "Germany taking seriously reports Russia may be planning to launch nuclear weapons into space" (Nov 23); tagesschau.de, NDR/WDR and Sueddeutsche Zeitung (Nov 23); Yahoo News, "Merz: Report that Russia may put a nuclear weapon in space 'shocking'" (Nov 23); NATO transcript (Nov 23)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: Azure API Management Classic flaw exploited; OpenRouter providers named
[ava] Story ten, the cyber desk. Microsoft says attackers exploited a flaw in Azure API Management Classic, a cloud service companies use to run their APIs, to steal stored credentials. Norway's National Security Authority links it to attacks on its energy sector, including Equinor. Microsoft closed the hole on November nineteenth. But it warns that the fix doesn't cancel keys that were already stolen, so customers need to rotate those secrets now. The newer version two tiers aren't affected.
[ava] And a follow-up to yesterday's story on leaked AI prompts. The three providers OpenRouter dropped are now named: Together AI, Phala and Novita. Each blamed a different slip, including a password an AI coding agent exposed on GitHub. Users should rotate any secrets they sent through them.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Microsoft Azure flaw exploited to steal customers' API credentials" (Nov 23); Norwegian National Security Authority (NSM) advisory; BleepingComputer, "OpenRouter drops AI providers over leaked prompts and secrets" (Nov 23)
