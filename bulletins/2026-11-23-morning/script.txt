// Story 2: Army invokes emergency exclusion to detonate lead azide in Iowa
[ava] Story two. The Army plans to go ahead Wednesday with detonating nearly five tons of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant, even though an appeals court put the permit on hold. Justice Department lawyers say an emergency exclusion in hazardous waste rules lets the Army act without a permit. They argue the explosive gets more likely to go off on its own as temperatures drop, so a controlled blast is far safer. The Army says it will still follow the permit's safety measures.
[ava] Environmental groups call it an end run around the courts. They note the Army's own modeling shows lead dust could settle over an area about twenty-five miles long, including homes and waterways.
> source: AP, "US Army plans to invoke emergency provisions to detonate bomb-making chemicals in Iowa" (Nov 22-23); ABC News, "Army moves detonation of 10,000 pounds of lead azide to Wednesday" (Nov 22); New York Post (Nov 23); CBS2 Iowa, court filing (Nov 22)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: CISA KEV Cisco Expressway; ports under attack
[ava] Story seven, the cyber desk. CISA warns that attackers are exploiting a critical flaw in Cisco Expressway, the gateways that connect a company's voice and video systems to remote users. The bug lets an attacker with no login take full control of clustered devices. Cisco patched it in October and says it hasn't seen attacks, but CISA gave federal agencies three days to fix it. The security firm F6 says attackers used an AI assistant to break in and move deeper into a network. If your company runs Expressway, patch now.
> source: BleepingComputer, "CISA flags critical Cisco RCE flaw as actively exploited in attacks" (Nov 23); CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Molly Falco, one year with a pig kidney
[ava] And finally, story eleven, from right here in New Hampshire. A year ago Molly Falco of Lyndeborough became the fourth person to get a genetically modified pig kidney at Mass General in Boston. Now the fifty-eight-year-old mother of two has set the record for living longest with an organ from another species.
[ava] Falco spent nearly a year on dialysis, and with her rare blood type she could wait five to seven years for a human kidney. She says she said yes almost before the doctor finished asking. She still tires on the stairs, but she feels less sick, and she hopes the kidney lasts until a human donor comes through. Her goal this winter: go skiing with her kids.
> source: WMUR, "'I was willing to try anything': NH mother sets record for living year with pig kidney transplant" (Nov 23)
