// Story 2: Judge permanently blocks ICC speech penalties
[ava] Story two. A federal judge in New York has permanently blocked the administration from punishing Americans for speech that helps the International Criminal Court. The challenge was to part of a twenty twenty-five executive order that sanctioned the court and barred providing it services, with penalties of up to twenty years in prison. Judge Jesse Furman ruled that applying that ban to advocacy and advice is an unconstitutional restriction on speech, and his order covers people nationwide.
[ava] The ruling doesn't touch the sanctions themselves, asset freezes, or anything involving money or goods. The administration has argued the court is targeting Americans and Israelis without legitimate authority, and it can appeal.
> source: Bloomberg Law, "Trump's ICC Order Violates First Amendment, New York Judge Says" (Nov 24); Anadolu Agency (Nov 24); DAWN, court opinion and order (Nov 24)

// Story 3: 22 states sue HUD over mixed-status families rule
[ava] Story three. Twenty-two states and the District of Columbia are suing the Department of Housing and Urban Development to stop a rule that takes effect December fourteenth. Right now, families where some members aren't eligible because of immigration status can still get reduced aid for the eligible members, often US citizen children. Under the new rule, one ineligible person means the whole household loses its subsidy. The states say that could affect about twenty thousand families, around seventy-eight thousand people, and that it breaks federal housing law. HUD Secretary Scott Turner says the rule ends the misuse of taxpayer dollars and frees up to two hundred eighteen million dollars for eligible Americans on waiting lists.
> source: AP, "States sue HUD over a rule that could cut off housing aid for mixed-status families" (Nov 24); States Newsroom (Nov 24); Illinois Attorney General release (Nov 23)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Tidal bans fully AI-generated music; Nvidia modules in Russian weapons
[andrew] Story eight, AI. Tidal says it will become the first major music streaming service to ban songs made entirely by AI. Starting December ninth, it will reject new fully AI-generated tracks and remove existing ones, about twenty-nine million songs. Tidal says forty-three percent of tracks delivered to it in the last ninety days were flagged as AI-generated. Music made with AI help but not entirely by AI is still allowed.
[andrew] And Ukrainian investigators say they've found Nvidia Jetson computing modules inside several Russian attack drones and a cruise missile. These small boards help weapons see and pick targets on their own. Nvidia says it doesn't sell the modules in Russia. Civilian parts are often resold through third countries, which makes them hard to trace.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 12: Thanksgiving Heroes
[ava] And finally, story twelve. When Rob Adams was a boy in Texas, his family of eight lived for a while out of a pickup truck. One Christmas, neighbors lent them their home while they were away, with a full fridge, a turkey, pumpkin pie, and presents under the tree. Eleven-year-old Rob told his mom that when he was rich, which then just meant not sleeping in the car, he'd pay it forward.
[ava] Years later, as his mother faced brain cancer, she reminded him of that promise. He started Thanksgiving Heroes in Utah hoping to feed ten families. The first year it fed about seven hundred fifty-five. This year the goal in Utah is thirty-six hundred, and chapters from Cleveland to New Haven have now served more than twenty-nine thousand families. Neighbors nominate the families, and no one has to prove need. As Adams puts it, the chance to feel love and fulfillment is right there.
> source: Good News Network, "A Childhood Promise Mobilizes 'Thanksgiving Heroes,' Serving Thousands Nationwide This Holiday" (Nov 24); Thanksgiving Heroes
