## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Iowa lead azide: evacuations recommended (new development)
[ava] Story one, a new development in Iowa. Federal officials now recommend that people living in a five-mile cone southwest of the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown leave home before tomorrow's planned detonation of nearly five tons of degraded lead azide, set for nine o'clock local time. Pregnant women, children and anyone with a chronic illness are urged to go and stay away until at least Friday, with FEMA offering rides, lodging and meals.
[ava] Army leaders say the barrels are too degraded to move or treat, so "inaction is no longer an option." Water walls and foam barriers are meant to contain the blast, and the EPA and NASA aircraft will track lead in the air. Environmental groups still fear lead dust spreading but say they won't sue again.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: OpenAI posts its own VP != VNP proof
[andrew] Story eight, AI. Hours after Anthropic released Claude's claimed proof that VP is not equal to VNP, the algebraic cousin of P versus NP, OpenAI posted its own thirty-page proof of the same result. OpenAI says its model got there on November sixth, a quiet claim to priority. The two proofs follow strikingly similar strategies, and OpenAI's actually cites a technique from an earlier Claude paper.
[andrew] There are differences. Anthropic's bound is somewhat stronger, while OpenAI spells out more consequences. No human mathematician has checked either one yet, and OpenAI withdrew three manuscripts last month after a sign error. Still, two independent proofs make the result more convincing. It's not P versus NP itself, and no encryption depends on it.
> source: OfficeChai, "Hours After Claude, OpenAI Posts Its Own Proof That VP ≠ VNP" (Nov 24); openai/math GitHub repository, history (Nov 23-24); sysnote (Nov 24)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: CIA/ODNI breach; Inception extortion gang
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk. The New York Times reports that the CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence recently found a breach in an unclassified system that analyzes public information, like social media, commercial data sets and satellite images. Information from other agencies was also likely exposed. The intelligence director's office says there was no disruption to critical capabilities or classified systems, and it's not known who did it. It follows recent incidents at the FBI and the Agriculture Department. Officials, including National Intelligence Director Jay Clayton, say such breaches are common and not cause for alarm, though some former intelligence officials are more worried.
[ava] And a new extortion gang called Inception is phoning employees at big companies to steal their single sign-on logins. It claims victims including AMD, PepsiCo and Cigna, though none have confirmed a breach. Okta links it to the ShinyHunters crew, which Inception denies. The fix: passkeys or security keys.
