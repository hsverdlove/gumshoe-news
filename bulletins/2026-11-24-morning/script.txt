## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Iowa lead azide detonation cleared for Wednesday (new development)
[ava] Story one, a new development in Iowa. The Army now plans to detonate about five tons of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown on Wednesday morning. A federal appeals court declined to block it Monday, finding the EPA's emergency permit has adequate safeguards. Governor Kim Reynolds supports going ahead.
[ava] President Trump had said earlier that it shouldn't happen without the court and Governor-elect Rob Sand. Sand says the decision belongs to the experts on the ground. Environmental groups still warn of lead dust spreading into Iowa and Illinois. The court said the bigger risk is an uncontrolled blast with no safety measures at all.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: Claude VP vs VNP claim; Axios scoop on insider fears
[andrew] Story nine, AI. Anthropic has posted a paper claiming that an internal research version of Claude proved V P is not equal to V N P. That's the algebra version of P versus NP, a question open since Leslie Valiant posed it in nineteen seventy-nine. It does not settle P versus NP itself. The proof comes with a computer-checked Lean formalization, but Anthropic is upfront that no human mathematician has checked it yet.
[andrew] And Axios reports that more than two dozen sources say many employees at Anthropic and OpenAI believe some unreleased models are too powerful to release safely. OpenAI says it has adopted safety requirements that could slow releases. Anthropic says it may hold models back even when it costs the company.
> source: Anthropic, github.com/anthropics/formal-math, vp-vnp README and paper (merged Nov 23); Axios, "AI employees sound alarm: models too powerful to ever release" (Nov 24)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: Shirudo; Chrome critical update; Iran suspected in tanker cyberattacks
[ava] Story ten, the cyber desk. OpenAI and Cisco have released Shirudo, an open-source Linux tool that lets AI agents write small targeted shields to block an exploit on a running server before the real patch is installed, without a restart. A person approves each one before it goes live. In a test, it stopped a Java4Shell attack on a live Elasticsearch service.
[ava] Google has an urgent Chrome update for a critical flaw that could let a malicious web page run code outside the browser's sandbox. There's no sign it's being exploited yet, but update to version one fifty-eight and restart.
[ava] And The Wall Street Journal reports US officials see Iran as the main suspect in cyberattacks on as many as fifty tankers, some with bomb threats and ransom demands. Russia is also being examined.
> source: OpenAI, "OpenAI and Cisco introduce Shirudo" (Nov 23); Cisco Newsroom (Nov 23); Google Chrome Releases blog, CVE-2026-134401 (Nov 23); CNBC, citing The Wall Street Journal (Nov 24)
