## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Iowa detonation (update)
[ava] Story one, an update from Iowa. The Army's controlled detonation of about ninety-seven hundred pounds of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown was set for ten o'clock our time. As we put this update together, there was no official word yet on how it went.
[ava] An hour before, FEMA's final phone alert said it was no longer safe to evacuate, and told everyone within twenty miles, including Middletown, Augusta and Burlington, to shelter in place with windows closed. NASA research planes are helping track the dust. The EPA says it has taken more than a thousand air, soil and water samples beforehand and will keep testing along the plume. The Army says any home with lead above two hundred parts per million will be cleaned up at government expense. Independent scientists at the University of Iowa are running their own monitors.

// Story 5: Leaked Russian death records
[ava] Story five. A BBC Russian analysis with the independent outlet Mediazona finds at least four hundred ninety thousand Russian soldiers have died in the war in Ukraine. It's based on more than eleven million leaked records from Russia's state statistics agency, taken in a hack claimed by Belarusian and Ukrainian groups. The BBC says names on its own casualty list matched those records about ninety percent of the time, which supports their authenticity. The Kremlin doesn't publish its losses.

## Markets | sting=markets
// Story 7: Market open
[andrew] Story seven, markets. Stocks opened sharply lower. About fifteen minutes into trading, the Dow was down around eight hundred thirty points, or one point seven percent. The S&P five hundred was down about two and a half percent, and the Nasdaq nearly four percent. That's a normal full session today, with markets closed tomorrow and an early one o'clock close on Friday.
[andrew] Three drivers. First, inflation. October's PCE index was up three point eight percent from a year ago, as expected, but core PCE came in at three point three, a tenth higher than forecast and the highest since late twenty twenty-three. Third-quarter growth was revised up to a strong four point three percent, and jobless claims were just a hundred eighty-nine thousand. Traders now see about a ninety-eight percent chance of a quarter-point Fed hike in December.
[andrew] Second, tariffs. The trade representative's office proposed new tariffs on fourteen economies it says flood the world with subsidized factory output, including China, the European Union, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and India. Rates run from fifteen to twenty-five percent, with China facing up to twenty percent on top of existing duties. They're proposals for now, with comments and hearings first, so January at the earliest. The administration says it's fighting unfair overcapacity; critics warn it will add to prices.
[andrew] Third, the chip rout. Nvidia was down about eight percent early, and Micron and Dell around nine to ten. Anthropic shares are near seventy-six dollars, well under their IPO price. The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, is above thirty.
[andrew] Elsewhere, the ten-year Treasury yield fell to about four point eight four percent as bonds rallied. US crude is near eighty-two and a half dollars. Gold is lower, and Bitcoin is near fifty-eight thousand four hundred.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 8: Amazon in talks over Meta Muse
[andrew] Story eight, tech. Fortune reports Amazon has held talks about letting Meta's Muse AI agent shop on its marketplace, which it currently blocks. The sticking points, according to the report: Amazon makes a lot of money from ads that humans see, it worries about the quality of what Muse recommends, and it doesn't want an agent standing between it and its customers. Walmart, Best Buy and others already let shopping agents in. Amazon has also blocked Perplexity's agent while pushing its own Alexa shopping assistant. Neither company commented.
> source: The Verge, "Amazon is reportedly in talks to end Muse AI shopping block" (Nov 25); Fortune (Nov 25)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 9: Google Cogentic math papers
[andrew] Story nine, AI. The race to have AI do original math now has three big players. A day after OpenAI's nine papers, Google Research quietly posted six papers produced by Cogentic, a team of AI agents running on Gemini four. Google says each paper and a computer-checked proof were produced with no human help beyond the first prompt.
[andrew] The headline claim is a proven method for factoring any whole number that matches the speed class of the best practical method, which until now rested on unproven assumptions. Others claim progress on game theory and a decade-old barrier in computer science.
[andrew] The caveats: none of it is peer reviewed yet, and the computer-checked proofs haven't been released. And no, this doesn't break real-world encryption. It's a theory result, and the new method is far slower in practice.
> source: OfficeChai, "Google's Cogentic AI Releases Six Math Papers In One Day" (Nov 25); FourWeekMBA (Nov 25); Agentic Feed (Nov 25); Google Research GitHub repository (Nov 25)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: Microsoft NTLM auth issues; GeoServer exploited
[ava] Story ten, the cyber desk. Microsoft confirms that this month's Windows security updates, which fix a serious remote code flaw, are breaking logins for some third-party products that use the older NTLM sign-in method, including some single sign-on tools and proxy servers. There's no fix yet, just a temporary registry workaround. The same updates are also causing Windows activation failures and SQL Server slowdowns. If you manage Windows machines, check before you roll back a security patch.
[ava] And attackers are exploiting two flaws in GeoServer, an open-source server for map data used by governments and utilities. Chained with older bugs, they can let an outsider take over a server with no login. Updates are out now.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Microsoft confirms authentication issues after November updates" (Nov 25); The Hacker News, "Hackers Exploit Two GeoServer Flaws as Researchers Warn of Unauthenticated RCE Chains" (Nov 25); GeoServer security advisory GHSA-rv5g-g2r9-58rj
