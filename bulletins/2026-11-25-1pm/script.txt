## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Iowa detonation result
[ava] Story one. The Army's controlled detonation of about five tons of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant went off just after ten our time, and officials are calling it a success. Major General Eric Shirley of Army Sustainment Command says every barrel was destroyed, with no secondary explosion and no damage to infrastructure, and that the risk to Middletown was reduced as close to zero as humanly possible.
[ava] More than two hours later, the EPA's air monitors were still showing no detectable lead at any station. FEMA says more soil and water testing will continue for days to weeks. Iowa's incoming governor Rob Sand says early results look good, but he wants a full accounting of why the lead-up was so chaotic and unclear.
> source: AP, "Army calls detonation of bomb chemicals in Iowa a success after earlier fears of a toxic plume" (Nov 25); BBC News live, "US Army says controlled blast of explosives in Iowa was 'huge success'" (Nov 25, 17:38 GMT); CBS2 Iowa, "Iowa leaders react to lead detonation" (Nov 25); US EPA (Nov 25)

// Story 2: Huawei convicted
[ava] Story two. A federal jury in Brooklyn has convicted Huawei and three of its subsidiaries on ten of twelve counts, including bank and wire fraud, violating US sanctions on Iran, money laundering and conspiracy to steal trade secrets. Prosecutors say Huawei misled banks about its business in Iran and copied source code from Cisco routers. The jury acquitted the company of racketeering conspiracy and obstruction.
[ava] Huawei says the remaining charges are without legal or factual merit, says the trade-secret disputes were resolved long ago as civil matters, and it's weighing its legal options. The penalties would be financial. Sentencing is set for September of next year.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 10: COLDCARD postmortem; Project Gutenberg
[ava] Story ten, the cyber desk, and one for bitcoin holders. Coinkite has published its full postmortem on the COLDCARD hardware wallet thefts. From March twenty twenty-one until July of this year, a firmware setting quietly switched off the chip's hardware random number generator, so new seeds were far weaker than they should have been. No device was hacked. Thieves rebuilt keys offline and took about seventeen hundred eighty bitcoin, worth over a hundred million dollars, from more than eighty-six hundred addresses. At least fifteen outside reviews missed the bug.
[ava] The key point: updating the firmware doesn't fix an old seed. If you made a COLDCARD seed in that window without your own dice rolls, Coinkite says to update, create a new seed and move your coins.
[ava] And Project Gutenberg, the free library of classic books, has been offline after attackers got root access and planted spam. Volunteers say the books are safe, and they expected the site back later today.
> source: Bitcoin Magazine, "Coldcard Postmortem Says Weak Seeds, Not Hacked Devices, Drove Bitcoin Thefts" (Nov 24); Coinkite, "COLDCARD Seed-Generation Incident: Postmortem" (Nov 23); File 770 (Nov 25); Project Gutenberg on Mastodon (Nov 25)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 13: Crookston Thanksgiving tradition
[ava] And finally, story thirteen. For about thirty years, Lester and Sue Wilkens ran the free community Thanksgiving dinner at the cathedral in Crookston, Minnesota. Twenty-plus turkeys and mountains of mashed potatoes, and Lester out in the parking lot radioing in to-go orders.
