// Story 2: SNAP rule debate
[ava] Story two. On the eve of Thanksgiving, a fight over food aid. The Agriculture Department has proposed ending what's called broad-based categorical eligibility for SNAP. Right now many states let families qualify for food stamps because they receive a TANF-funded service, sometimes as little as a brochure. The rule would limit that to families getting actual TANF cash assistance.
[ava] USDA's own estimate is that about six point three million people would lose benefits by twenty twenty-nine, saving about forty-five billion dollars over five years. Supporters say it closes a loophole and refocuses the program on the people it was meant for. Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro, packing food at a food bank today, called it wrong and cruel, and his state estimates about three hundred ten thousand Pennsylvanians would be cut off. Public comments are open until December tenth.
> source: USDA Food and Nutrition Service, "Proposed Rule: SNAP - Reforming Categorical Eligibility" (Federal Register, Nov 10); Bloomberg Law, "Trump Plan Would Strip Food Aid Benefits From 6.3 Million People" (Nov 2026); Philadelphia Inquirer (Nov 25); Focal News Philadelphia (Nov 2026)

## International | sting=world
// Story 4: Netanyahu trial
[ava] Story four, the world. In Jerusalem, the judges in Benjamin Netanyahu's corruption trial took an unusual step today. With the attorney general in the courtroom at their request, they recommended prosecutors drop the bribery charge in the case involving the Bezeq telecom company and a news site owned by Shaul Elovitch, saying the chances of conviction are low. They also questioned whether the newspaper case involves a crime at all, and suggested a plea deal in the gifts case.
[ava] The court stressed this is a recommendation, not a ruling. Netanyahu called it a ray of light for truth and justice. Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara said she'd review it, but noted the case can end only in a verdict or a fitting plea deal, and that her office's earlier offer went unanswered. Netanyahu denies all the charges.
> source: Reuters via Investing.com, "Israeli judges recommend prosecutors drop one bribery case against Netanyahu" (Nov 25); Globes (Nov 25); Ynet (Nov 25); Anadolu Agency (Nov 25); BBC (Nov 25)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 8: Amodei responds; Democrats call for limits
[andrew] Story eight, AI. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei is pushing back hard on an Axios report about views inside his company that humans will inevitably cede some decisions to superintelligent AI. In a long post on X, he said it's absurd to suggest he wants to surrender human free will, and that turning governance over to AI would be a bad idea. ## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 9: 1Password breach; Ledger letters
[ava] Story nine, the cyber desk, and if you use 1Password, here's what you need to know. The company says an attacker got into an employee's corporate GitHub account on November nineteenth, pushed malicious code to backend systems, and copied names, sign-in email addresses and account details for a subset of customers. The attacker also took source code. 1Password says vaults, passwords and secret keys were never exposed, and you don't need to change anything.
[ava] The real risk is phishing. Watch for unexpected emails about 1Password invitations or account recovery that you didn't start. The company has lengthened its emailed recovery codes from six digits to ten characters as a precaution.
[ava] And for hardware wallet owners, scammers are mailing paper letters that look like they're from Ledger, warning of a tampered device and telling you to scan a QR code. Ledger says it never sends those and never asks for your recovery phrase. Throw it out.
> source: 1Password, "Update on a security incident involving 1Password's backend systems" (Nov 25); TechCrunch (Nov 25); The Verge (Nov 25); Ledger support, "Physical Mail Phishing Scam" (Nov 2026)
