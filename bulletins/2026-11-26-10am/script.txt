## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Hormuz tanker claims
[ava] Story three, the world. Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two more oil tankers exploded and caught fire after hitting sea mines north of Oman's Khasab port, at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz. They say one was a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude, and that the ships had switched off their tracking and were relying on US fighter jets for cover.
[ava] Those claims haven't been independently confirmed. Maritime security agencies often describe incidents in the area as strikes by unknown projectiles, and their counts don't always line up with Iran's. Separately, the British agency UKMTO reported an explosion near a tanker about fifty nautical miles west of Dubai today. A Guards spokesman said Iran is using heavy firepower to keep the strait closed to pressure the US economy.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Claude rewrites API in Rust; OpenAI limits; NY AI toy ban
[andrew] Story seven, AI. Anthropic says it used Claude agents to rewrite the Messages API, the service that delivers Claude to customers, moving it from Python to Rust. The agents compared old and new versions against live traffic and fixed mismatches, with humans reviewing the changes. Anthropic reports about seventy percent less processor use per request, a twenty percent faster first response and no downtime. As The Next Web points out, verifying the rewrite was harder than writing it.
[andrew] OpenAI says it had been undercounting usage of its new GPT-6.2 models. After the fix, paying subscribers will hit limits forty percent sooner on Astra and twenty-five percent sooner on Sol. Subscribers get one banked reset to make up for it.
[andrew] And New York has banned the manufacture and sale of toys with AI chatbots for five years, starting in February twenty twenty-seven. Supporters cite risks to kids, and social psychologist Jonathan Haidt urged parents this morning not to buy such toys. The Toy Association opposed a moratorium and favors studying the risks first, alongside its own safety guidelines.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: SYS010 malware; Italy and Switzerland AI-agent attacks
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Bitdefender is warning about malware it calls SYS010, spread through Facebook ads for free animated wallpapers. The wallpaper works, but the installer quietly enrolls your browser in an attacker's management console, forcing in an extension that steals session cookies, reads recent Gmail and Outlook messages and can delete security alerts. The red flag is a browser that suddenly says it's managed by your organization on a personal computer.
[ava] And cyber agencies in Italy and Switzerland warn that attackers are now using autonomous AI agents to break into critical infrastructure. Italy saw multiple agents working in parallel to compromise many systems within hours. Switzerland says every AI-assisted attack it knows of succeeded, mostly through weak passwords and unpatched systems. Their advice is to patch critical flaws within a day, use multi-factor authentication or passkeys, and cut what's exposed to the internet.
> source: BleepingComputer, "SYS010 malware abuses browser management to spy on Gmail, Outlook" (Nov 26); Bitdefender Labs (Nov 26); BleepingComputer, "Italy and Switzerland warn of autonomous AI-driven cyberattacks" (Nov 25)
