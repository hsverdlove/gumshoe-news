// Story 2: Medicare notices to legal immigrants
[ava] Story two. About three hundred thousand legal immigrants are getting letters from Social Security warning that their Medicare coverage may end on February first. The Washington Post reports that many of them paid into the system for years. The change comes from last year's big tax and spending law, which limits Medicare to citizens, green card holders and a few other groups. Refugees, people granted asylum and trafficking survivors who don't have green cards are among those affected.
[ava] The letters are preliminary. People can keep coverage by proving citizenship or an eligible status by the deadline, and anyone cut off can ask for reconsideration. The Center for Medicare Advocacy points out that the government's estimate is three times what the Congressional Budget Office projected last year. Backers of the law have argued that federal benefits like Medicare should be reserved for citizens and permanent residents.
> source: The Washington Post, "300,000 legal immigrants told they're losing Medicare coverage" (Nov 24); Center for Medicare Advocacy, "CMS Releases Final Rule: Some Immigrants will Lose Medicare in 2027" (Nov 12); Medicare Rights Center (Nov 12)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Italians killed in Kuwait; Israel on Iran
[ava] Story three, the world. Two Italian civilians were killed today in a drone attack on what Kuwait's army called a vital site. Italy's foreign ministry says Antonio Saccoccio, sixty-five, and Emanuele Lo Russo, thirty-six, worked for Rheinmetall Italia and were maintaining radar antennas for Kuwait's Skyguard air-defense system. A Kuwaiti and a Pakistani were also hurt. Italian officials say the drones were of Iranian origin, and Bloomberg notes it's the second Iranian drone attack on Kuwait this month. Iran's armed forces flatly deny it, saying they carried out no operation in the region and suggesting the accusation is a pretext.
[ava] Tensions are high elsewhere too. Israel's military spokesman Effie Defrin told public broadcaster Kan that Israel is ready to strike Iran at any moment, even without the Americans. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu then canceled tonight's full security cabinet meeting in favor of a smaller consultation, Ynet reported, without giving a reason. Not everyone sees urgency. Gadi Eisenkot, who is trying to form Israel's next government, said this week he sees no immediate threat from Iran that requires action before then.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: CBS Nvidia partners probe; SemiAnalysis chip benchmarks
[andrew] Story six, tech. A CBS News investigation found that Nvidia is selling its most advanced chips to at least two California companies that are actually controlled by Chinese parents. One, RiCloud AI in San Jose, is an Nvidia preferred partner owned by a Shenzhen-based firm whose CEO studied at a Chinese military university. Another, Aivres, was renamed after its Chinese parent was blacklisted by Washington. CBS says some of those chips were then leased or sold to companies in Southeast Asia. Neither company has been accused of wrongdoing, and the deals alone don't show any export rules were broken. Nvidia didn't answer CBS's questions about how it vetted its partners.
[andrew] And in the chip race, the research firm SemiAnalysis added Google's and Amazon's custom AI chips to its independent benchmarks. In a preview result, Amazon's Trainium3 delivered about two point three times the performance per dollar of Nvidia's GB300 system on an AI-agent workload. Google's Ironwood TPU beat it by about one point eight times in one test. The caveats are big. Most of the edge comes from estimated rental costs rather than raw speed, and Nvidia still came out ahead in other setups.
> source: CBS News, "2 California companies are buying Nvidia's most advanced chips. Those companies are actually Chinese-owned" (Nov 25); The Statesman (Nov 26); SemiAnalysis InferenceX, "TPU v7 (Ironwood) vs GB300 NVL72: AgentX Results" (Nov 2026); Supercomputing News, "Amazon's First Trainium3 Results Reach SemiAnalysis's AgentX Benchmark as an Official Preview" (Nov 2026)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Reuters on Chinese AI lab delays
[andrew] Story seven, AI. Reuters reports that two of China's leading AI labs are slipping behind schedule. Zhipu AI had planned to release its giant next model, GLM five point five, this month. Sources say it's been held up partly by tougher defenses at OpenAI and Anthropic against distillation, where a developer trains its model on answers from a stronger rival's model. A new US rule requiring licenses for large blocks of overseas computing power took effect last week, and it could push the launch into next year.
[andrew] Moonshot AI, maker of the Kimi chatbot, rents much of its computing power abroad. One source says the gap between its major releases could double to a year or more, and that it has turned to distilling from Google and Meta models instead. Moonshot has previously denied relying on distillation, saying its gains came from its own design changes. Supporters of the export rules see this as proof they're working. Critics argue the pressure will only push China to build up its own chips faster.
> source: Reuters via Investing.com, "Exclusive: US chip curbs, distillation crackdown threaten Chinese AI model development, sources say" (Nov 26); The Next Web (Nov 26)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: GoodGuys phishing; Remus fake Chrome extension
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. Push Security is tracking a phishing campaign it calls GoodGuys that offers up to six hundred dollars for a short paid expert interview or webinar. It impersonates brands like Google Cloud, Nvidia, SpaceX, Accenture and Spotify, and the emails arrive through legitimate services like HireVue and SendGrid. The pages ask you to sign in with your work Google or Okta account, and the attackers relay your multi-factor prompts in real time. Researchers think the kit was built with help from an AI coding assistant, based on hidden labels calling it a sign-in simulation.
[ava] And researchers at Gen Digital say the Remus infostealer's advertised Gmail zero-day isn't a Gmail flaw at all. The malware quietly installs a fake Chrome extension called Google Sheets that grabs unread emails every minute. It survives password changes because it lives in the browser. The fix is to check your Chrome extensions page for anything you don't recognize, and make sure Developer Mode hasn't been switched on.
> source: BleepingComputer, "Fake paid expert interviews impersonate brands to steal Google logins" (Nov 26); Push Security (Nov 2026); BleepingComputer, "Remus malware steals Gmail messages via fake Google Sheets extension" (Nov 26); Gen Digital (Nov 25)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Gloria Kennard
[ava] And finally, story eleven. For thirty-seven years, Gloria Kennard has cooked Thanksgiving dinner for her Texas community. It started in nineteen eighty-eight, when she was working at Texas A and M and felt called to help someone. Now she feeds more than two thousand people every year, plus a New Year's Day meal and weekly shifts at a food pantry.
[ava] This summer another driver totaled her truck, and the insurance money wasn't enough to replace it. So the pantry, The Bridge Ministries, started a fundraiser with a fifteen thousand dollar goal. A hundred seventy-four donors and a local cabinet maker pushed it past twenty-seven thousand. Friends surprised Miss Gloria with a brand-new SUV in her driveway, just in time for this year's feast. As one organizer put it, every dollar represents a life she has touched.
