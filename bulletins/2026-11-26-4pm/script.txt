## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Storm update
[ava] Story one, the Thanksgiving nor'easter. The Weather Channel says roughly eight hundred thousand homes and businesses were without power by midafternoon, up from about six hundred thousand at midday. Virginia has more than two hundred thousand of those, Maryland about a hundred seventy-seven thousand by Reuters' count, and Missouri around a hundred fifty thousand after heavy wet snow. Gusts have topped seventy miles an hour in spots including seventy-three in western Virginia.
[ava] The damage is piling up. In Wentzville Missouri, the city's ice arena collapsed under the snow and is considered a total loss, though the mayor says they'll rebuild. Maryland halted traffic on the Chesapeake Bay Bridge before noon when sustained winds topped fifty-five. The Commodore Barry Bridge between Pennsylvania and New Jersey is expected to stay closed until Saturday after the wind tore up work platforms. And ocean water poured over the dunes onto North Carolina's Highway twelve on the Outer Banks. FlightAware counted more than four hundred cancellations and about sixteen hundred delays. The Weather Service says the storm should pull away overnight into Friday.

// Story 2: Trump GDP revision probe
[ava] Story two. President Trump is calling for an immediate investigation of the government's own growth figures. On Wednesday the Bureau of Economic Analysis revised third-quarter growth up to a four point three percent annual rate, from the three point four percent it reported just before the midterms. On Truth Social this morning, the President called the earlier number fake and a massive and purposeful error, and asked why the correct figure wasn't out before the election.
[ava] Here's the context. Revisions are routine, because the first estimate relies on incomplete data. The bureau says this one came mainly from stronger software investment and consumer spending on goods, and it was released on its normal schedule. CNBC notes the change was about double the average revision, the largest since twenty twenty, but far from a record. Axios points out the President offered no evidence the error was deliberate. His supporters say the bigger story is that the economy is stronger than first reported. The Commerce Department hadn't commented.
> source: AP, "Trump calls the pre-election GDP figures 'fake' and demands an investigation of Commerce Department" (Nov 26); Axios, "Trump demands investigation into GDP data revisions" (Nov 26); CNBC, "Trump demands probe after Q3 GDP revised to 4.3%" (Nov 26); BEA, "GDP (Second Estimate), 3rd Quarter 2026" (Nov 25)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Meloni on Ukraine's Libya strike
[ava] Story three, the world. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, one of Kyiv's steadiest backers, delivered a public rebuke today. Speaking at the EU summit in Brussels, she said the war in Ukraine must not and cannot spread to the Mediterranean. She was reacting to Sunday's Ukrainian drone strike on a Russian warship and a cargo vessel in the Libyan port of Benghazi. Meloni said Italy will keep helping Ukraine defend itself, but must also protect its own borders and citizens, and she intends to stay very clear about that with President Zelensky.
[ava] Libya matters a lot to Rome. It's a former Italian colony tied to Italy by gas pipelines and migration routes, and the energy company Eni runs major fields there. Ukraine sees Russian vessels as fair targets and has struck them near Libya before, badly damaging a Russian gas tanker near Malta in March. Bloomberg describes Meloni's comments as a balancing act ahead of Italy's election next year.
> source: AP via Africanews, "Italy's Meloni concerned after Ukraine attack on Russian ship in Libya" (Nov 26); ANSA, "Ukraine war must not spread to the Mediterranean says Meloni" (Nov 26); Italian Government press point (Nov 26); Bloomberg (Nov 26)

// Story 4: Armenia to take over its borders
[ava] Story four. Armenia's Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan says it's time for his country to fully guard its own borders. Since nineteen ninety-two, Russian border troops have helped patrol Armenia's frontiers with Turkey and Iran. Pashinyan thanked them today but said Armenia should take full responsibility in the coming years. He gave no timetable. Armenian officers already run the main crossings, including Yerevan's airport, and the government links the change to its push for visa-free travel to the European Union. A Russian military base in Gyumri stays under a separate treaty, and one Armenian military analyst estimates a full handover could take two to four years.
> source: Anadolu Agency, "Pashinyan announces full withdrawal of Russian border guards from Armenia" (Nov 26); News.am (Nov 26); Kursiv Media, "Armenia wants Russian patrols to pull out from border with Türkiye and Iran" (Nov 26); Vectors.am (Nov 4)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Rockstar union vote; CoreWeave bonds
[andrew] Story six, tech. One week after Grand Theft Auto Six launched, Rockstar Games staff in the UK voted against recognizing a union. With turnout above ninety-three percent, about three in four voted no, eleven hundred sixty-three to three hundred eighty-one. Recognition would have meant formal bargaining on pay, overtime and how the studio uses generative AI. Rockstar says the result backs its direct dialogue with staff. The union says it isn't retreating, and a tribunal still has to rule on Rockstar's firing of more than thirty union members last year, which the company says was over leaked confidential information.
[andrew] And a warning sign in AI financing. Bonds of the AI cloud provider CoreWeave fell to record lows this week. Its twenty thirty-one notes trade around seventy-four cents on the dollar, a yield near eighteen percent, as investors question the debt behind the data center boom. Bulls counter that much of CoreWeave's spending is backed by long-term contracts with big tech customers.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Putin at AI Journey; SAP job cuts
[andrew] Story seven, AI. Vladimir Putin told Moscow's AI Journey conference today that Russia should extend its national AI strategy through twenty thirty-six. He pushed for homegrown foundation models trained on Russian data, warning that a model trained on foreign values shapes the thinking of millions of users. He also said banning the technology is impossible and counterproductive. Western analysts have long noted that sanctions limit Russia's access to the advanced chips such models need.
[andrew] In Germany, Bloomberg reports software giant SAP is weighing at least ten thousand job cuts, about nine percent of its workforce, as it reorganizes around AI. Product, engineering and sales teams are said to be in focus. The final number depends on talks with labor representatives, and SAP hasn't confirmed any cuts.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Bank of England quantum; Rackspace Spot shutdown
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. The Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee now calls the move to quantum-resistant encryption an immediate priority. The worry is that a future quantum computer could break the codes behind payments, identity and digital signatures, and that attackers may already be stealing data to decrypt later. The UK's roadmap asks firms to finish migration planning by twenty twenty-eight and fully switch over by twenty thirty-five, with the biggest firms urged to move faster. Officials stress that such a machine is still years away.
[ava] And Rackspace is shutting down its low-cost Spot cloud service after a breach. Attackers used a leaked credential to roam the platform for almost twenty-two hours on November seventeenth, and could read secrets stored in customer Kubernetes clusters. Customers have until December eighth to move their workloads, and they need to rotate every password and API key they kept there.
> source: Bank of England, "Financial Policy Committee Record – November 2026" (Nov 26); The Register, "Rackspace pulls plug on Spot cloud service after security breach" (Nov 26); BleepingComputer (Nov 26)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Alaska turkey drop
[ava] And finally, story eleven. In rural Alaska, some families really do get their Thanksgiving turkey from the sky. Esther Keim and pilot Heidi Hastings call it the Alaska Turkey Bomb. Hastings flies low and slow, and Keim drops bags with a turkey and some treats, often onto a frozen lake or swamp so they're easy to spot. This year they delivered forty-two turkeys across the Susitna Valley, from Alexander Creek to the foothills of Denali. One family had to hunt through the alders for theirs, but no turkeys were lost.
[ava] It's all funded by donations and volunteers, and they've dropped about a hundred sixty turkeys since twenty twenty-two. Keim says the best part is the texts and videos that come in while they're still in the air. For people living off-grid, she says, it brings the community together.
> source: Alaska's News Source (KTUU), "Alaska Turkey Bombers drop Thanksgiving meals right out of the sky" (Nov 24); BBC News video (Nov 2026)
