// Story 2: Birthright citizenship ruling
[ava] Story two. A federal judge in Washington has blocked President Trump's second executive order on birthright citizenship. That August order came after the Supreme Court struck down his first one earlier this year. It would have denied citizenship to some US-born children, including those whose parents work for foreign governments without diplomatic immunity, children born through commercial surrogacy, and children of parents suspected of gang or terrorist ties.
[ava] Judge Christopher Cooper wrote that the president cannot carve out new exceptions for children born here and subject to US law. His order covers a certified class of affected children rather than everyone nationwide, and it can be appealed. The White House called him an activist judge and says the order is consistent with the Supreme Court's own reasoning. Supporters of the order argue it targets birth tourism and security risks. Opponents say the Constitution's text leaves no room for it.
> source: Nathan v. Trump, No. 26-cv-3377 (D.D.C.), order and memorandum opinion (Nov 25); ABC News, "Judge blocks latest Trump effort to restrict birthright citizenship" (Nov 25); Fox News (Nov 25)

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Trump-Rutte statement; Kyiv strike
[ava] Story three, the world. After meeting at Mar-a-Lago last night, President Trump and NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte issued a joint statement urging Russia to avoid any actions, intentional or not, that expand the war in Ukraine, and calling for a peaceful end as quickly as possible. It's seen as a warning about how Moscow retaliates for Ukraine's recent strikes on Russian ships in a Libyan port and the Gulf of Finland.

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Nvidia halts SE Asia shipments; Tesla-SpaceX merger committees
[andrew] Story six, tech. The Financial Times reports Nvidia has told partners like Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. It follows a new US rule that took effect last week requiring export licenses for advanced AI chips to those countries and Brazil, aimed at closing routes Chinese firms allegedly use to get restricted hardware. It's unclear whether the pause is temporary.
[andrew] And on the Musk front, Tesla and SpaceX have each named special committees to negotiate a possible all-stock merger. Tesla's side is chair Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung, advised by Evercore. SpaceX's side is Roelof Botha and Randy Glein, advised by Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs. The FT says they're aiming for year-end but aren't close on an exchange ratio. Critics note all five have long ties to Musk. Supporters say the committees exist precisely to keep the process by the book. Any deal would need a Tesla shareholder vote and antitrust reviews in several countries.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: GPT-6 protein design; Legg; Aaronson crypto rumors
[andrew] Story seven, AI. OpenAI says its GPT-6 models can design working proteins through reasoning alone. In lab tests, thirty-five of three hundred twenty-two designed minibinders stuck to their targets, with at least one hit for each of five targets. The caveat, which OpenAI itself stresses, is that specialist tools still handled key steps, so this is a proof of principle, not a replacement for tools like AlphaFold.
[andrew] DeepMind co-founder Shane Legg has raised his odds of what he calls minimal AGI before twenty twenty-eight to about sixty-five percent, up from the fifty percent he's held since two thousand nine. He calls it provisional.
[andrew] And a rumor worth handling carefully. Computer scientist Scott Aaronson wrote that he's virtually certain, from multiple sources, that the AI labs are sitting on undisclosed results in cryptography, and that he's heard rumors of a fast factoring method that could threaten RSA encryption. He stressed he can't verify the specifics. Neither OpenAI nor Anthropic has confirmed anything, and no practical break has been shown.
> source: OpenAI, "GPT-6 models demonstrate intrinsic protein-design capabilities" (Nov 24); Nature, "Watch out, AlphaFold: Claude and ChatGPT's new biology skills wow scientists" (Nov 24); OfficeChai, Shane Legg (Nov 25); Cointelegraph, "AI labs likely sitting on cryptography 'bombshells,' computer scientist says" (Nov 26); Scott Aaronson, Shtetl-Optimized (Nov 25)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: FIFA breach; Zodia; hashrate hijacking
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. FIFA has confirmed a criminal cyberattack that exposed a limited set of personal and business information. A new group calling itself the Handsome Boy Hacking Club claims it took twelve gigabytes, including identity and financial documents, and posted it after FIFA refused to pay. Sources say the attackers got in by impersonating staff rather than exploiting a technical flaw. FIFA hasn't confirmed the group's claims about what was taken.
[ava] Standard Chartered's crypto custody arm, Zodia Custody, says a breach exposed names and email addresses for a small number of clients and employees. It found no unauthorized transactions.
[ava] And bitcoin miners, take note. Braiins says attackers have been hijacking internet routes to quietly redirect mining power from unencrypted connections, hitting more than a thousand sites on a single day. The fix is switching to Stratum version two or encrypted connections.

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 11: Blessing Baskets
[ava] And finally, story eleven. When Jeannine and Miguel Garcia arrived from Peru in nineteen eighty-nine, they were cleaning houses and had never heard of Thanksgiving. One of their clients explained it, then went shopping and put together a basket for them, with a turkey, mashed potatoes, pie mix and stuffing, which Jeannine had never seen before.
[ava] Decades later, the couple started Blessing Baskets in Northern Virginia. They made a hundred eighteen baskets the first year, nearly three hundred the next, and four hundred sixty-eight last year. This fall the goal was seven hundred fifty. As Jeannine put it, it was never about the food. It was about the thoughtfulness. Before that client passed away, the Garcias made sure she met their children, so she could see the difference she made.
> source: WTOP News, "3 decades after an act of kindness, Fairfax Co. family makes sure hundreds of families can celebrate Thanksgiving" (Nov 9)
