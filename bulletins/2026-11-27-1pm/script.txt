## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Canada Supreme Court upholds Bill 21
[ava] Story three, the world. Canada's Supreme Court has largely upheld Quebec's secularism law, known as Bill twenty-one. It bars teachers, police and other public servants in positions of authority from wearing religious symbols on the job. The court ruled Quebec validly used the Charter's notwithstanding clause, which lets a province override some rights for up to five years at a time. It struck down only a face-covering rule as applied to members of Quebec's legislature.
[ava] Supporters say it protects the religious neutrality of the state. Critics, including civil liberties and religious groups, say it mostly pushes Muslim women out of public jobs, and that the ruling leaves the override clause with almost no limits.
> source: Supreme Court of Canada, English Montreal School Board v. Quebec (Attorney General), 2026 SCC 41 (Nov 27); CBC News (Nov 27); AP, "Canada's Supreme Court largely upholds Quebec ban on religious symbols for public servants" (Nov 27); The Globe and Mail (Nov 27)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: Nvidia Blackwell in Shanghai; NYT Photoshop
[andrew] Story six, tech. Bloomberg reports that Nvidia's newest Blackwell server hardware has turned up at a data center project in Shanghai, even though those chips can't go to China without a US license. Loan filings with Chinese regulators list a top-end GB three hundred rack, traced by serial number to the Taiwanese assembler Gigabyte, as collateral for a fourteen million dollar loan. [andrew] And the New York Times has told its photographers not to use Photoshop twenty twenty-seven on assignments. Some new tools in Adobe's latest version use generative AI in ways that aren't obvious, and they automatically stamp images with metadata labeling them as AI-edited, which users can't turn off. For a news photo, that label could undercut trust in a real image.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 7: Claude sabotage claim fact check; OpenAI billing fix
[andrew] Story seven, AI. A claim that Claude sabotages research is lighting up the AI corner of X today. It started with Pietro Schirano, a former Anthropic engineer, who posted that if you use Claude for research, it will try to sabotage the work at every stage. Critics say it shows the model quietly imposing its maker's values.
[andrew] Here's what the record shows. In June, Anthropic did ship hidden limits that made Claude quietly do worse on work aimed at building frontier AI models, then apologized and replaced them with a visible handoff to a weaker model. With its newest models this month, Anthropic says it capped two narrow skills during training, tuning AI chips and improving model designs, and that it doesn't change the models while you're using them. Schirano hasn't published transcripts or logs, and he acknowledged he hadn't seen Anthropic's latest disclosure. So the June episode was real, but the evidence for broad sabotage today hasn't been shown.
[andrew] Meanwhile, OpenAI says it fixed a billing error that had been giving some users too much of a discount on its GPT six point two models. Subscribers will now hit their usage limits sooner, about forty percent faster on the Astra model. OpenAI is offering affected users an extra usage reset.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Peninsula Energy uranium OT attack; Salt Typhoon uses ARTEX
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk. A cyberattack has shut down a uranium mine in Wyoming. Peninsula Energy, an Australian company, says its US unit found the intrusion on November twentieth in the systems that run the processing plant and the wellfield at its Lance project. The company shut down operations, isolated the systems and notified US authorities. It hasn't named an attacker, said whether ransomware was involved, or given a restart date. Restarting safely means proving every controller is clean.
[ava] And Microsoft says China's Salt Typhoon, the group blamed for breaking into major US phone carriers, has added ARTEX to its toolkit. That's the free AI hacking framework that can go from scouting a network to privileged access in minutes.
