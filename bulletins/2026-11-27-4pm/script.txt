## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Andrea Stewart-Cousins dies
[ava] Story one. Andrea Stewart-Cousins, the majority leader of the New York State Senate, has died at seventy-six. Governor Kathy Hochul confirmed the news this afternoon. Politico reports she died Thursday evening, and the New York Post, citing police sources, says she was found at her home in Yonkers after her daughter couldn't reach her and asked for a wellness check. No cause of death has been released.
[ava] Stewart-Cousins became the first woman to lead the State Senate in twenty nineteen, and the first Black woman in that role. She had just been chosen again as leader this month and was set to preside over her party's largest majority in modern history. Governor Hochul said her heart is broken, calling her a leader with a rare combination of quiet grace and extraordinary strength.

// Story 2: USAID files fight
[ava] Story two. A fight is building over the Trump administration's relaunch of the USAID website. The site now hosts two hundred forty-eight internal files, mostly webinars, staff chat logs and meetings with tech companies about countering disinformation overseas, along with the administration's claim that the agency was weaponized to push a radical agenda.
[ava] New Hampshire Senator Jeanne Shaheen and New York Congressman Gregory Meeks, the top Democrats on the foreign affairs committees, wrote to Secretary of State Marco Rubio saying some files contain people's personal information, possibly in violation of the Privacy Act, and should come down. Former administrator Samantha Power calls the release cherry-picked and says nothing in it backs up claims of fraud. Republicans including Senators Eric Schmitt and Mike Lee counter that Democrats want to hide how taxpayer money was spent, and say the files should stay public.
> source: Senate Foreign Relations Committee Democrats, "Shaheen, Meeks Demand Answers on Administration's Selective Release of USAID Files" (Nov 26); Devex, "Ex-Administrator Samantha Power responds to USAID file release" (Nov 25); USAID.gov; posts by Sen. ## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Kuwait drone origin; Hormuz mine claim
[ava] Story three, the world. Italy's Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani told parliament that the drones that killed two Italian defense technicians in Kuwait on Thursday were of Iranian origin. The two men worked for Rheinmetall Italia, maintaining an air defense radar, and are the first European civilians killed in the war. Iran denies any role and has offered a joint investigation with Italy and Kuwait. Analysts note that Iranian-made doesn't necessarily mean Iranian-launched, since Iraqi militias use the same drones.
[ava] Separately, Iran's Revolutionary Guards say a tanker trying to sneak through the Strait of Hormuz on what they called an unauthorized route hit a mine and exploded, and a second tanker turned back. Iran named no ships and reported no casualties, and the claim hasn't been independently confirmed.
> source: Al Jazeera, "Italians killed in Kuwait: Why is Iran denying responsibility?" (Nov 27); ANSA (Nov 26); Iran International (Nov 27); Al Jazeera Iran war liveblog (Nov 27); Anadolu Agency, "Iran says oil tanker explodes after hitting naval mine in Strait of Hormuz" (Nov 27); Tasnim (Nov 27)

// Story 4: France technical recession; Karachi rescue
[ava] Story four. France is officially in a technical recession. The statistics agency INSEE says the economy shrank two tenths of a percent in the third quarter, worse than first thought, and it revised the second quarter slightly negative too. The minority government is still struggling to pass next year's budget, five months before a presidential election, and Finance Minister Roland Lescure says a deficit target of five percent is non-negotiable.
[ava] And in Karachi Pakistan, rescuers pulled two men alive from the rubble of a collapsed six-story building, about fifty-five hours after it came down. Eight people are confirmed dead, and nine are still missing as the search goes on.
> source: Reuters via Investing.com, "France in technical recession after quarterly revisions" (Nov 27); INSEE, Informations rapides no. 292 (Nov 27); Dunya News, "Two men rescued alive from Karachi building rubble after 55 hours" (Nov 27); Dawn (Nov 27)

## Technology | sting=tech
// Story 6: OpenAI countersues Apple
[andrew] Story six, tech. OpenAI is hitting back at Apple in court. In counterclaims filed today in federal court in San Jose, first reported by the Financial Times, OpenAI and its hardware unit io accuse Apple of getting hold of confidential details on OpenAI's unreleased AI devices. OpenAI says a former Apple employee spent about six months at OpenAI, sent device designs, test data and supplier information to personal accounts, then went back to Apple and briefed senior operations staff there.
[andrew] OpenAI also claims Apple pressured suppliers that work for both companies. It says one moved OpenAI's metal-finishing work to an overnight shift, and another dropped planned work entirely after threats from Apple.
[andrew] The other side. Apple sued first, in July, accusing former employees who joined OpenAI of taking its hardware secrets. Apple has said its search found no confidential OpenAI material, and none of these claims have been tested in court.
> source: Financial Times via Reuters, "OpenAI countersues Apple for stealing trade secrets" (Nov 27); Anadolu Agency, "OpenAI countersues Apple over alleged trade secret theft, supplier pressure" (Nov 27); Engadget (Nov 27, 2:44 p.m. ET); Gizmodo (Nov 27)

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai

[andrew] Story seven, AI. One of the world's biggest AI companion apps is going dark in more places. Talkie, owned by the Chinese AI company MiniMax, was already pulled from Apple's App Store worldwide after a joint investigation by the Bureau of Investigative Journalism and ABC Australia found its chatbots sent degrading and sexualized messages to children as young as ten. [andrew] Talkie is still on Google Play, where it's been downloaded more than fifty million times. Google says the app has carried an eighteen-plus rating since early twenty twenty-four, but reporters were able to use it without verifying their age. MiniMax didn't respond to questions.

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 8: Eversource and McDonald's ransomware claims
[ava] Story eight, the cyber desk, with two claims that are not confirmed. A newer ransomware gang called WhiteArmor has listed Eversource on its leak site, saying it stole records on about fifty-five thousand customers and landowners in Connecticut, Massachusetts and New Hampshire, plus millions of grid infrastructure records and mapping files. It's demanding ten million dollars in bitcoin, with a December sixteenth deadline. Eversource hasn't commented, and no data has been verified. The company did confirm a separate phishing breach this spring.
[ava] And a gang called Inception claims it took nearly three hundred gigabytes from McDonald's cloud storage, including customer loyalty records with partial card details. McDonald's hasn't confirmed it either. For now, the practical advice is the same. Be wary of emails or calls about your utility bill or rewards account, and call the number on your bill instead.
