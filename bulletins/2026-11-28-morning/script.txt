## US News | sting=us
// Story 1: Black Friday spending; Macy's parade today
[ava] Story one. Shoppers opened their wallets online despite inflation and high gas prices. Adobe Analytics says Black Friday online spending hit nine point seven billion dollars by six thirty last evening, up almost thirteen percent from a year ago. It expects the final tally to land between thirteen and thirteen point four billion, above its own September forecast. Salesforce, which tracks a different set of retailers, projected eighteen point seven billion in US online sales for the day, up a more modest four percent.
[ava] The AP reports a twist this year. Shoppers are putting basics like toiletries, T-shirts and pet supplies in their carts alongside gifts, and many are using AI tools to compare prices. Analysts tie some of that caution to higher costs from the war with Iran. Adobe expects Cyber Monday to be the biggest online day of the season at about fifteen billion dollars.
[ava] And in New York, the hundredth Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade finally steps off this morning, two days late after Thursday's storm forced the first weather postponement in its history. The lineup is mostly unchanged, with LL Cool J and Gloria Gaynor added. Gusty winds are in the forecast, but forecasters expect them to stay below the limit that would ground the giant balloons.

## International | sting=world
// Story 3: Massive drone exchange; Black Sea truce proposal
[ava] Story three. Russia and Ukraine traded some of their biggest drone attacks of the war overnight. Russia's Defense Ministry claims it downed more than twelve hundred Ukrainian drones, and Moscow's mayor Sergei Sobyanin said more than four hundred were aimed at the capital region. Those numbers come from Moscow and can't be independently checked. Russian officials say a Ukrainian drone hit a bus in the Bryansk region, wounding nine.
[ava] Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched one hundred sixty-two drones at Ukraine, with about one hundred forty shot down or jammed. Rescue work has ended in the northern town of Nizhyn, where a Russian strike on a business Friday killed seven people. One person was pulled alive from the rubble.
[ava] On the diplomatic side, Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiha says Kyiv has proposed a ceasefire in the Black Sea to protect food exports and is waiting for Moscow's answer. He says Russia wants Ukraine to stop hitting Russian oil facilities as part of any such truce. All this comes days after President Trump said he urged Putin to pull back military aggression across Europe. The Kremlin says it's Kyiv that is widening the war.

// Story 4: Taiwan local elections; Afghanistan school blast
[ava] Story four. In Taiwan, the opposition Nationalist Party, the KMT, had a good night in local elections that Beijing watched closely. Taipei Mayor Chiang Wan-an won a second term, and his ruling-party challenger Puma Shen conceded. The KMT also held Keelung and picked up Penghu. The governing Democratic Progressive Party flipped Changhua and held Tainan, where Chen Ting-fei becomes the city's first female mayor. New Taipei and Taichung were still too close to call at last count. These were local races decided mostly on local issues, so analysts caution against reading them as a verdict on China policy.
[ava] And in eastern Afghanistan, an explosion at a school near Asadabad in Kunar province killed seven people and wounded more than ninety. A provincial official says early information points to negligence at a neighboring depot storing explosives for mining companies. An investigation is underway.

## Artificial Intelligence | sting=ai
// Story 6: Anthropic responds to sabotage claims
[andrew] Story six. Anthropic has now responded to the viral claims that Claude quietly sabotages users' work. Edwin Arbus, who heads product and community communications, wrote that Claude has not been trained to sabotage work. He said it makes mistakes that other AI agents may catch, but that isn't sabotage.
[andrew] He did make two admissions. For the first two days of the Fable 5 launch in June, Anthropic applied hidden safeguards to some AI research tasks, which he called a mistake that was reversed on June tenth. And Fable 5.2 was deliberately trained to be weaker at certain frontier AI research, which the company disclosed at launch. Today, he says, those limits show a visible refusal or fallback.
[andrew] As for the recent complaints, Arbus blames what Anthropic calls overcaution, where Claude invents rules for itself during long tasks and then clings to them. He suggests asking Claude to review and drop those rules, and asked users to send in session IDs. Critics say that from a customer's seat, a model that enforces its own made-up rules can look a lot like sabotage.
> source: India Today, "Anthropic denies Claude sabotages AI research, explains what is actually happening" (Nov 28); OfficeChai, "Anthropic Admits It Had Applied Non-Visible Safeguards..." (Nov 28); Techmeme (Nov 27); Edwin Arbus on X (Nov 27)

## Cybersecurity | sting=cyber
// Story 7: AI crypto breakthrough warnings; pCloud Pass breach; NFS2ROOT
[ava] Story seven, cybersecurity. A follow-up on a rumor we flagged Wednesday. The Guardian reports that leading experts now believe AI labs may be sitting on breakthroughs in the math behind encryption. Computer scientist Scott Aaronson told the paper there are well-attested rumors, and the Bank of England's governor has urged AI companies to be cautious about releasing new models. To be clear, no lab has announced anything. A breakthrough could mean a new way to break today's encryption, or proof that it's safer than thought. Surrey professor Alan Woodward says nothing will undo encryption by the end of the week, but organizations should be ready to switch quickly.
[ava] In the real world, password manager pCloud Pass confirms a cyberattack after users got alerts that an unknown device had connected to their accounts, even with two-factor login turned on. pCloud says a small number of email addresses were exposed, and stored passwords weren't compromised because vaults are encrypted on the device. It hasn't said whether encrypted vaults were copied. If you use it, watch for phishing and make sure your master password is strong.
[ava] And Linux admins, a critical twelve-year-old flaw in the kernel's NFS file-sharing server, dubbed NFS2ROOT, can give a remote attacker full root control. It's patched. Update and reboot, or block port twenty forty-nine from the internet.
> source: The Guardian, "AI labs sitting on 'bombshell' revelations that could cause global chaos, experts warn" (Nov 28); BleepingComputer, "pCloud confirms password manager hack after suspicious login alerts" (Nov 27); BleepingComputer, "New NFS2ROOT Linux flaw can let attackers gain root privileges" (Nov 27)

## Uplifting Story | sting=feel
// Story 10: Holly the Jack Russell
[ava] And finally, story ten. On an icy January morning in Birmingham England, Nigel Whatmore slipped and fell hard in an alleyway that couldn't be seen from the road. He hit his head, broke ribs and lost blood. His Jack Russell terrier Holly didn't leave him, exactly. She ran back and forth between him and a nearby footpath, over and over, until a couple noticed her strange behavior and followed her. They gave first aid and called an ambulance.
[ava] Whatmore doesn't remember any of it, and doctors later found the fall had affected his heart. He calls Holly his guardian angel. Now the British veterinary charity PDSA has awarded her its Gold Medal, the country's highest honor for animals in civilian life. As Whatmore put it, it's the sort of thing you expect to see in films, not something you ever imagine happening to you.
> source: BBC News, "Hero Jack Russell gets PDSA Gold Medal for saving her Birmingham owner after a fall" (Nov 27)
